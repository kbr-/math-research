Simultaneous quotient avoidance

Lemma. Let \(W=\mathbb F_2^L\) have a fixed coordinate basis and disjoint p-dimensional coordinate blocks \(U_1,\ldots,U_M\), with \(M\ge2,p\ge1,Mp\le L\). Let \(\psi_\nu:W\to E_\nu\), \(\nu=1,\ldots,Z\), be a fixed finite family of binary linear maps, \(Z\ge1\). Set \(t=\lceil\log_2M\rceil\), \(z=\lceil\log_2Z\rceil\). Assume \[ p+t+1\le L/16,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\psi_\nu\ge2p+2t+z+3\quad\text{for every }\nu.\tag{1} \] There is \(T\in\mathrm{GL}(L,2)\) such that \(G_a=TU_a\) are a literal direct sum, every nonzero vector in each \(G_a\) has coordinate weight greater than \(L/4\), and \[ (G_a+G_b)\cap\ker\psi_\nu=0 \quad(a\ne b,\ \nu\in[Z]).\tag{2} \] Consequently each \(\psi_\nu\) injects on every \(G_a\), and \(\psi_\nu(G_a)\cap\psi_\nu(G_b)=0\) for distinct parents. The maps may be correlated and may be quotient maps; they must be fixed before T is chosen.

Proof. Choose T uniformly in the finite general linear group. Each fixed nonzero vector has uniform nonzero image, since an invertible basis change carries any one such vector to another. The full original dense-basis proof bounds the number of binary vectors of weight at most \(L/4\) by \(3^{L/4}(4/3)^L\). Its union bound over fewer than \(M2^p\) individual-block vectors gives \[ \Pr[\text{some individual block has a low-weight image}] \le2M2^p(2/3^{3/4})^L \le(2^{17/16}/3^{3/4})^L<1/4. \] The first condition gives \(L\ge16\) and the middle inequality. The last bound follows because the base to the sixteenth power is \(2^{17}/3^{12}<1/4\), exactly as in the original proof.

For a map of rank q, its kernel has \(2^{L-q}\) elements. A uniform nonzero image lands there with probability at most \(2^{1-q}\). There are fewer than \(M^2 2^{2p}\) vectors among all pair-block sums; repeated vectors only overcount. Union over all Z maps therefore gives \[ \Pr[\text{some forbidden pair-map event}] \le Z\,2^{2p+2t+1-\min_\nu\operatorname{rank}\psi_\nu} \le1/4. \] The two bad-event bounds leave a positive proportion of T's. Invertibility preserves the literal direct sum, proving (2). If \(\psi_\nu(g_a)=\psi_\nu(g_b)\), then \(g_a+g_b\) lies in that pair sum and kernel, so is zero; the literal direct sum then gives \(g_a=g_b=0\). Taking one vector zero also gives individual injectivity. No independence among maps or events is used. \(\square\)

Meaning of the obstruction

The structural reason is that each surviving star leaves a large linear image of the whole monomial layer. A single binary basis can therefore separate every small parent pair in every one of these finitely many images. A nonunit localization can create coincidences, but the original admissibility conditions do not force it to do so. The argument depends on the size of a library of maps fixed before T; it does not control an arbitrary source-dependent polynomial weight selected from a much larger class.

This excludes a universal proof of the common-star-input hypothesis from the old source properties. It does not exclude a different actual-error mechanism on the stronger source: a final multiplier can lie outside the review's \(\chi q\) form, and multiple actual rows can annihilate errors without providing that common input class. Nor does (3) prove a lower bound on the completed stacked rank. The original narrow test remains genuinely open at its old lexicographic choice. Neither a high local floor nor a full initial rank is substituted for the missing completed-map statement.

The lemma itself also has an elementary unbounded witness: take \(M=2,p=1,Z=1\), \(L\ge48\), and the identity quotient map on W. Degenerate cases. The lemma excludes empty map families, zero-dimensional parent blocks and a single parent; without the rank condition a zero map would defeat individual injectivity. Repeated maps do not invalidate the conservative Z bound. The source handles zero-class patterns separately, rather than requiring an impossible kernel avoidance for them. At \(\chi=1\) the existing unweighted separation already gives zero common image, as the review noted. Duplicate parents would violate the literal direct-sum hypothesis; duplicate coordinates within a chosen basis do not describe the required basis. A satisfiable old family, non-surviving weight or insufficient residual degree does not meet the weighted-cube hypotheses and is not covered. No globally satisfying charged assignment is assumed.
