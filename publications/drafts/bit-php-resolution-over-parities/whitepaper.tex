\begin{abstract}
We present a proposed proof that the usual bit pigeonhole principle with
\(n+1\) pigeons and \(n=2^\ell\) holes requires superpolynomial-size DAG-like
resolution-over-parities refutations, with no regularity or proof-depth
restriction. The argument converts an arbitrary short refutation into
polynomially many complete affine extension blocks admitting an
ordinary polynomial-calculus refutation of degree \(O(\log n)\).
A simultaneous coefficient substitution, weighted by a nonzero row-linear
bit polynomial, would then produce an old-base consequence of degree
\(O(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2})\).
Matching-moment extension and a cube-supported separating functional exclude
that consequence. All algebraic dependencies are proved in this draft; an
appendix supplies the required chessboard homology argument. We also describe
the open, versioned research framework in which the proof was developed.
This is a working manuscript prepared for review, not an independently
verified or formally certified result.
\end{abstract}
