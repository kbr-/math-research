Recover the existing basis criterion with its scope

The full adapted-basis criterion was read. For free modules over \(k[t]/(t^n)\) it requires compatibility of colon images with intersections and a distributive lattice. Its reproduced field-level fact says that a finite family of subspaces generating a distributive lattice has a common adapted basis; the converse follows because coordinate subspaces form a distributive lattice. The proof selects complements at join-irreducible elements, not merely triplewise checks on the original generators.

For our finite filtered vector space, a basis adapted both to all \(K_i\) and to the filtration would make the lattice generated by the \(K_i\) and all filtration subspaces distributive. The unit line detects the obstruction

Let \(J_D\) be a binary Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal. Let \(\deg w\le b\), \(\deg H\le h\), \(\deg q\le\delta\), with \(H,q,w\) binary polynomials and \(w\notin J_D\). Choose \[ R\ge\max(1,h,\delta),\qquad b+R+h+\delta\le D. \] Assume \[ wHq\notin J_D,\qquad wH(1-q)\notin J_D. \tag{1} \] Put \(V=[wB_{\le R}]\), with induced degree flag \(V_k=[wB_{\le k}]\), and define on this domain \[ K_0=\ker([wf]\mapsto[wHqf]),\qquad K_1=\ker([wf]\mapsto[wH(1-q)f]). \] The same reserve makes these maps representative-independent. Let \(T=V_0=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{[w]\}\), a one-dimensional line.

Proposition (unit flag kernel gap). One has \[ T\cap(K_0+K_1)=T,\qquad (T\cap K_0)+(T\cap K_1)=0. \tag{2} \] Hence no basis can be adapted to both kernels and the degree flag. If also \(w(1-H)\notin J_D\), the nonzero class \([w(1-H)]\) nevertheless lies in \(K_0\cap K_1\), represented by a multiplier of degree at most \(h\). Thus common-basis failure need not obstruct a useful common annihilator.

Proof. Booleanity gives \(q^2-q\) a certificate through \(2\delta\). Consequently \[ [w(1-q)]\in K_0,\qquad [wq]\in K_1, \qquad [w]=[w(1-q)]+[wq]. \] The certificates fit \(b+h+2\delta\le b+R+h+\delta\le D\). Equation (1) says the unit class is in neither kernel, so its one-dimensional line meets each trivially. This proves (2). Coordinate subspaces in a common basis satisfy the distributive law, contradicting (2) if that basis also spans \(T\) by a subset. Any degree-compatible basis has this property at degree zero.

For the positive assertion, use \(H^2-H\) through \(2h\). Multiplying by \(wq\) or \(w(1-q)\) fits \(b+2h+\delta\le D\), proving that \([w(1-H)]\) is in both kernels. Its stipulated survival and degree bound give the claimed useful witness. No unbudgeted outside multiplication or ordinary NS cofactor claim is used. \(\square\)

A realization inside the actual hard-window framework

Suppose \(J_D\) contains an unsatisfiable old Boolean family \(\Gamma\), with \(u=\max(2,\max\deg\Gamma)\), and \(w\) survives with \[ D-b\ge\max(u,4). \] The weighted restriction-cube theorem supplies at least two old coordinates \(x,y\) for which the four classes \([w],[wx],[wy],[wxy]\) are independent. Take \(H=y,q=x,R=1\). Then \([wxy]\), \([wy(1-x)]\), and \([w(1-y)]\) all survive. The proposition's conditions hold through \(b+3\le D\), including its positive conclusion. This is a realization in a proper pseudoideal containing an unsatisfiable base, not evaluation on a global satisfying assignment.

Using the complete block definition, it comes from two fresh accuracy-one blocks on the same actual affine tuple \((x,y)\). In the first block use the constant row \((1,1)\): both companions reduce to \(xy\). In the second use \((1,0)\): the \(x\)-companion is Boolean-zero and the \(y\)-companion is \(y(1-x)\). Thus their actual error kernels are exactly the displayed pair on \(V_1\). All coefficient fields vanish. The single weight \[ W=w(1-y) \] survives and kills every companion through actual degree at most \(b+3\). The original product and companion ceilings remain two and three; a full original degree-\(B\) source replay still pays \(b+1+B\le D\), with \(B\ge\max(3,u)\) when those old axioms are used. No small specialized certificate resets that original charge.

The hard-window counterexample therefore cannot be dismissed solely as an artifact of a satisfiable Boolean base. But it remains a fixed-choice control, not a source-hardness theorem: the displayed weight already removes its two blocks. Changing the weight or coefficient rows changes the kernel family, and may restore compatibility. Those permitted alternatives are retained.

A weaker joint model does not need all kernels to be coordinate

Let \(X=\bigcap_i K_i\) be the actual joint kernel in any finite filtered vector space \(V\). A basis can always be adapted to the degree flag and \(X\) alone. To see this, choose a basis of \(X\) adapted to \(X\cap V_k\). Choose a basis of \(V/X\) adapted to its quotient flag and lift each vector into the first \(V_k\) where its class occurs. The union is a basis of \(V\). For a vector in \(V_k\), subtract the appropriate lifted quotient vectors of degree at most \(k\); the remainder lies in \(X\cap V_k\), proving compatibility with every filtration level. This is elementary two-flag linear algebra, not a new source constructor.

This weaker model preserves exactly the needed witnesses, and the relative-complex lemma already records their degrees. Computing \(X\), or proving that it contains a sufficiently low-degree nonzero class, is still the original mathematical task. The construction supplies neither low-degree multiplication matrices nor a monomial ideal describing all individual kernels. Those additional structures cannot be assumed from the existence of the joint basis.

On the same domain \(V=[wB_{\le R}]\) and under the stated reserve \(b+R+h+\delta\le D\), the present common kernel can be described directly without simultaneous diagonalization: \[ \ker M_{wHq}\cap\ker M_{wH(1-q)}=\ker M_{wH}. \] One inclusion follows by adding the two error components; the reverse follows by bounded multiplication by \(q\) and \(1-q\). This is the elementary common-factor/unit-residual identity behind the existing factor and span tools, not a new approximation rate. It is the relation coefficients that make the direct description work.
