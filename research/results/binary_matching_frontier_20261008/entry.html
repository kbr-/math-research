The graph-constraint model

Let \(G\) be a simple graph on \(n\ge3\) vertices and \(m\ge1\) edges, with maximum degree \(\Delta\ge1\). There is one Boolean variable \(x_e\) for each edge. Each vertex carries a finite family of multilinear polynomials using only its incident edges. Include every Boolean axiom and suppose the full system is unsatisfiable. Equivalently, its local relation may be presented by all forbidden-assignment indicators \(\chi_\alpha=\prod_e \ell_{\alpha_e}(x_e)\), with \(\ell_1=x\), \(\ell_0=1-x\). No constraint may involve edges outside one vertex star. This is not the class of arbitrary bounded-width CNFs.

These local presentations are interchangeable through degree \(2\Delta\). For a forbidden assignment \(\alpha\) to a star of size \(b\), choose an input polynomial \(f\) with \(f(\alpha)\ne0\). Then \[f\chi_\alpha-f(\alpha)\chi_\alpha\in I_{2b}(\mathrm{Bool}).\] For completeness, telescope each monomial \(x_S-\alpha_S\) one variable at a time. Its term for \(i\in S\), after multiplying by \(\chi_\alpha\), uses \[ (x_i-\alpha_i)\chi_\alpha =(2\alpha_i-1)(x_i^2-x_i)\prod_{j\ne i}\ell_{\alpha_j}(x_j). \] The other telescope factors have degree at most \(|S|-1\), so every Boolean-generator multiple has degree at most \(b+|S|\le2b\). Sum with the coefficients of \(f\). Multiplying the input \(f\) by \(\chi_\alpha\), subtracting this certificate and dividing by the nonzero scalar derives \(\chi_\alpha\). Conversely multilinear interpolation expresses each input as a scalar sum of the forbidden indicators, through degree at most \(b\). These are ordinary polynomial certificates in every characteristic.

Eliminate a vertex frontier without losing the degree ledger

Fix any vertex order of cutwidth \(C\). For a prefix \(S\), let \(F_S\) be its crossing edges and let \(R_S\) consist of assignments to \(F_S\) that extend to all edges incident with \(S\), satisfying all constraints at vertices in \(S\). We derive the indicator of every assignment outside \(R_S\). The empty prefix has no forbidden boundary assignment. At the full prefix, unsatisfiability makes the unique empty assignment forbidden, whose indicator is one.

To add vertex \(v\), use the bag \(B_S=F_S\cup E(v)\), with at most \(C+\Delta\) variables. A bag assignment is compatible with the enlarged prefix exactly when its old frontier restriction lies in \(R_S\) and its star restriction satisfies the new vertex. This follows directly by combining an extension inside \(S\) with the assigned new star edges. Therefore any bad bag assignment violates either a previously derived forbidden frontier indicator or a local forbidden star indicator.

Derive that bag assignment's full indicator by multiplying its violated indicator by the literals on the remaining bag variables. No variable is repeated, and every intermediate degree is at most \(|B_S|\). Previously derived indicators are reused as final PC lines; their earlier derivations are not multiplied by the new literals. Multiplication by \(1-x\) is one variable multiplication followed by subtraction. If an assignment \(\beta\) to the new frontier has no admissible completion to the bag, derive all its completion indicators and sum them. The identity \[ \sum_{\alpha:\alpha|_{F_{S\cup\{v\}}}=\beta}\chi_\alpha=\chi_\beta \] is literal polynomial distributivity, since each eliminated variable contributes \(x+(1-x)=1\). This completes the induction. Along with the initial local conversion, it gives PC degree at most \(\max\{2\Delta,C+\Delta\}\). It makes no claim that the exponentially many indicators can be enumerated in polynomial proof size.

Order matched pairs together

Theorem (matched frontier ceiling). For the preceding unsatisfiable graph system and any matching of size \(a\), put \(b=n-a\ge2\). There is an ordinary PC refutation over its original field of degree at most \[ \left\lceil\frac{(m-a)b}{2(b-1)}+ \Delta\sqrt{8b\log(2b)}\right\rceil+2\Delta. \] After deleting satisfiable isolated-vertex constraints, a maximal matching yields, for fixed \(\Delta\), \[ \operatorname{PCdeg}\le \left(\frac12-\frac1{4\Delta-2}\right)m +O_\Delta(\sqrt{m\log m}). \] If instead \(\Delta=O(\log m)\), the bound is \(m/2-\Omega(m/\log m)\) for sufficiently large \(m\), with constants depending on the maximum-degree bound. An unsatisfiable isolated-vertex constraint already gives degree zero.

Proof of the ordering. Make every matched pair one block and every unmatched vertex a singleton, giving \(b\) blocks. There are exactly \(m-a\) inter-block edges. Give the blocks independent uniform keys. For the first \(j\) blocks, the expected number of crossing edges is \[ \frac{2j(b-j)}{b(b-1)}(m-a)\le\frac{(m-a)b}{2(b-1)}. \] Changing one key changes the first \(j\) blocks by at most one block swap, hence at most four vertices, and changes the cut by at most \(4\Delta\). The same bounded-differences inequality used in the earlier ordering proof bounds its upper tail by \(\exp(-u^2/(8b\Delta^2))\). Take \(u=\Delta\sqrt{8b\log(2b)}\) and union-bound over \(1\le j<b\); the total is below one. Thus one ordering simultaneously has every block-boundary cut at most the displayed mean bound plus \(u\).

Expand each matched block into its two vertices consecutively. A prefix inside such a block changes its preceding block-boundary cut by at most \(\Delta\). Thus the full cutwidth is at most the ceiling in the theorem plus \(\Delta\). Apply the complete frontier elimination above. Its result is at least \(2\Delta\), so it also pays the local predicate conversion.

Asymptotic consequences. A maximal matching has \(a\ge m/(2\Delta-1)\): every edge is incident to a matched edge, and each selected edge accounts for at most \(2\Delta-1\) edges. Also \(b\ge n/2\). With isolated vertices removed, \(n\le2m\), while \(m\le\Delta n/2\). The leading term is \[ \frac{m-a}{2}+O(\Delta), \] and the remaining cost is \(O(\Delta\sqrt{n\log n}+\Delta)\). At fixed \(\Delta\), substitute the matching lower bound to obtain the claimed fixed gap. When \(\Delta\le c\log m\), the saved \(a/2\) is at least a constant times \(m/\log m\), whereas the error is \(O_c(\sqrt m(\log m)^{3/2})=o(m/\log m)\). This proves the second range as well. \(\square\)

Positive flow cannot supply the recorded window in these ranges

Corollary (flow window exclusion). Encode the positive signed integer-flow predicate at each graph vertex by all its forbidden Boolean assignments. For any such family of polynomial encoding length in the number \(m\) of edge variables, its PC refutation degree is at most \(m/2-\Omega(m/\log m)\). Thus it cannot satisfy the recorded moving old-degree hypothesis at fixed approximation depth \(\ell\ge3\). At fixed maximum degree it cannot satisfy that hypothesis even for \(\ell=2\). This conclusion is about that sufficient old-degree route, not the Frege proof-size complexity of flow.

Proof. Summing signed net flows over all vertices cancels every edge, so demanding positivity everywhere is inconsistent. Its input predicates are local Boolean relations, so the theorem applies after encoding, over any field. At a vertex of degree \(t\), complementing the appropriate Boolean variables makes the signed net flow \(t-2\sum_{i=1}^t y_i\). At least half of its \(2^t\) assignments have nonpositive net flow. The canonical encoding therefore has at least \(2^{t-1}\) forbidden clauses at that vertex. Polynomial encoding length in \(m\) forces \(\Delta=O(\log m)\). Any isolated vertex instead supplies an immediate contradiction.

For \(\ell\ge3\), the target deficit \(s=o(m/(\log m)^{\ell-2})\) is \(o(m/\log m)\), so the proposed cutoff \(\lfloor m/2-s\rfloor\) eventually exceeds the actual upper degree above. At fixed \(\Delta\), the fixed fractional gap contradicts every target with \(s=o(m)\), including depth two. Growing-degree depth two is not excluded by this estimate, and no lower bound is established there. Noncanonical encodings or formula lengths not polynomial in \(m\) need their own complete parameter ledger; this corollary makes no transfer claim for them. \(\square\)
