Symmetric rank moments

Proposition. Over any field of characteristic two, let \(A\) be \(m\times n\), \(B\) be \(n\times m\), with independent variables and \(m,n\ge2\). Put \(E_{ij}=(AB)_{ij}\) and \(F_{ij}=E_{ij}+\delta_{ij}\). There is no linear functional on polynomials of degree at most four which has \(\Lambda(1)=1\), annihilates every \(qF_{ij}\) with \(\deg q\le2\), and is invariant under every simultaneous permutation of the inner indices of \(A,B\). Boolean axioms are not needed for this obstruction. Hence a normalized NS or PC functional with those properties cannot be made fully inner-permutation invariant for free.

Proof. Let \(H=E_{11}E_{22}+E_{12}E_{21}\). The exact degree-four identity \[ H+1=E_{22}F_{11}+F_{22}+E_{21}F_{12} \] gives \(\Lambda(H)=1\). Expand both products. Terms with the same inner index cancel in pairs. For distinct indices, each monomial type has one orbit indexed by the \(n(n-1)\) ordered pairs, so invariance gives \[ \Lambda(H)=n(n-1)(\alpha+\beta)=0 \] in characteristic two, where \(\alpha,\beta\) are the values on the two monomial types. This contradicts normalization. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For \(m=2\), any \(n\ge2\), set the first two columns of \(A\) and rows of \(B\) to the two-by-two identity and all others to zero. Evaluation there is a normalized functional annihilating every input multiple, but it is not inner-permutation invariant. Thus the obstruction is to the added symmetry, not to consistency or ordinary functionals. On the intended rank contradiction \(m>n\), it remains a warning about the stronger symmetric design requirement, not a low-degree refutation.

Degenerate cases. The two output indices require \(m\ge2\). The displayed permutation control uses \(n\ge2\); for \(n=1\), the two-by-two minor itself is zero and already conflicts with the rank equations. No division by group order, positivity or averaging is assumed. Invariance under smaller odd-order groups is not ruled out.

Odd group averaging

Lemma. Let a finite group \(G\) permute the old variables and the full generator list \(F_i\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), with a specified compatible permutation of its placeholders. If \(|G|\) is odd and \(C(x,Y)\) is a Hilbert-like certificate, then \[ \overline C=\sum_{g\in G}gC \] is an invariant Hilbert-like certificate. A size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) unbounded-fanin arithmetic circuit gives size \(O(|G|(S+1))\), depth at most \(d+1\). Coefficient degree does not increase. This is ordinary Reynolds averaging specialized to odd order, not a new invariant-theory theorem.

Proof. Transform each placeholder with its corresponding generator. Each summand then vanishes at zero placeholders and evaluates to one at \(Y=F(x)\). The sum evaluates to \(|G|=1\) in the binary field. Group multiplication permutes the summands, proving invariance. Copy the circuit for every group element and add the outputs; substitutions are permutations only. Linearity and degree persist. If \(F_0\) and \(Y_0\) are individually fixed by the action, the axiom-cut operation \((1-F_0)C+Y_0\) commutes with this average, so symmetry alone does not remove that old normalization. \(\square\)

An odd cyclic relabeling of the rank system's inner indices is an allowed example when \(n\) is odd, with orbit cost \(n\). Full symmetric-group averaging is not allowed. This lemma concerns certificates; the preceding proposition concerns normalized functionals. They are different objects and are not contradictory.

Nonvacuity. Take a fixed unit input \(F_0=1\), its certificate \(Y_0\), all dummy-variable Boolean axioms, and an odd cyclic group permuting those variables and their Boolean placeholders. Averaging returns \(Y_0\). Degenerate cases. The trivial group changes nothing. For an even-order group this same invariant certificate averages to zero, so oddness cannot be dropped.

Certificate syzygy module

Lemma. For \(R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) over any field and \(F=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)\), suppose a coefficient vector \(c\in R^r\) satisfies \(F\cdot c=1\). Then \(\ker(F:R^r\to R)\) is free of rank \(r-1\), and all coefficient certificates form \(c+\ker F\). No circuit-cost bound follows from this statement.

Proof. The map \(a\mapsto ac\) splits the surjection \(F\), so \(R^r=\ker F\oplus Rc\). Its kernel is a finitely generated projective module; Quillen–Suslin makes it free. Passing to the fraction field gives its rank. The affine-coset assertion follows by subtracting two solutions. We use the field case of Dicks, Theorem 6, with no effective basis-size assertion. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For \(F=(1,0)\) and \(c=(1,0)\), the kernel is \(Re_2\). Degenerate cases. If \(r=1\), the kernel is the zero free module; \(r=0\) cannot satisfy the hypothesis.

This is the full polynomial-ring module. It says nothing about freeness of a truncated PC kernel, its associated graded module or a degree-bounded section.

Binary product coordinates

Lemma. In the central extension \(\mathbb F_4\langle X\rangle=\mathbb F_2\langle X\rangle\oplus\omega\mathbb F_2\langle X\rangle\), with \(\omega^2=\omega+1\), suppose \(L\ge1\) ordered factors \(a_i+\omega b_i\) have their binary coordinates given by noncommutative formulas of size at most \(B\) and depth at most \(e\). Both binary coordinates of their product have formulas of size \(O((B+1)L^2)\) and depth at most \(e+2\lceil\log_2L\rceil+1\). Coordinates of the individual factors are supplied, not extracted for free from arbitrary circuits.

Proof. Represent \(a+\omega b\) by \[ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a+b\end{pmatrix}. \] Matrix multiplication respects multiplication in the central extension even when \(a,b\) do not commute with the other factor's coordinates: direct expansion gives first row \((ac+bd,ad+bc+bd)\), and the second row has the same off-diagonal entry and the sum of the first two. Pad the ordered factor list by identities to a power of two \(L'<2L\), without permuting it, and balance the matrix product. Each desired matrix entry is a sum of two ordered products of child entries. Its formula-size recurrence is \(T(L')\le4T(L'/2)+O(1)\), with leaf cost \(O(B+1)\); thus \(T=O((B+1)L^2)\). Each level adds two layers, with one extra leaf layer for \(a+b\). The first row supplies both coordinates. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. Ordered products of \(x_i+\omega y_i\) have constant-size coordinate inputs and unbounded \(L\). Degenerate cases. An empty product has coordinates \((1,0)\); zero factors and constant factors are permitted. The logarithmic depth is paid and does not contradict the binary fixed-depth descent obstruction. This proves a local product ledger, not the complete Tseitin certificate construction.
