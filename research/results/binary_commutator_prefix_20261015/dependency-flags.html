Binary matrix flags

Lemma. Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_r\in\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb F_2)\), \(r\ge1\). Let \(\mathcal W\) contain the empty word and all words of lengths one through three in the letters \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\). Add a zero symbol, and enumerate all ordered four-tuples from \(\mathcal W\cup\{0\}\), with repetitions. Each tuple defines a polynomial \(T_\ell\) equal to the sum of its four entries. Put \(H=(2+r+r^2+r^3)^4\), \(B_\ell=T_\ell^2+T_\ell\). Then every matrix \((I+B_\ell(X))^{10}\) is either zero or I. If all of them equal I, the old matrices have a common invariant binary line, hence are simultaneously upper triangular in some binary basis. The word and sum bounds are absolute, not dependent on r or the generated algebra.

Proof. For any two-by-two binary matrix T, write \(\tau=\operatorname{tr}T\), \(\delta=\det T\). Cayley–Hamilton gives \(T^2=\tau T+\delta I\). If \(\tau=1\), then \(B=T^2+T=\delta I\). If \(\tau=0\), then \(B=T+\delta I\), and \(B^2=T^2+\delta^2I=0\). Thus \(B^2\) is zero or I, with \(B^2=I\) exactly when \(T^2+T=I\). In particular \((I+B)^{10}=((I+B)^2)^5=I+B^2\), a central binary flag. A single five-line excluded-middle prefix is \((I+B)^5\); its square is this flag.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the unital algebra generated by the matrices. Words of length at most three span it. Indeed let \(V_j\) be the span of words of length at most j, starting with \(V_0=\langle I\rangle\). If \(V_{j+1}=V_j\), the space is stable under right multiplication by every generator, so contains every word. Otherwise dimension increases. Ambient dimension is four, so by j=3 either full dimension has been reached or an earlier stabilization has occurred. Choose a basis of at most four of these words. Every algebra element is the sum of at most four basis words over \(\mathbb F_2\), padding by the zero symbol when necessary. Thus the displayed list of \(T_\ell(X)\)'s covers every element of \(\mathcal A\).

If the action of \(\mathcal A\) on \(V=\mathbb F_2^2\) is irreducible, its commuting endomorphism division ring K has binary dimension dividing two, since V is a vector space over its opposite ring. If that dimension is one, finite-dimensional density gives \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb F_2)\). If it is two, V has dimension one over K, and density gives the four-element division algebra; any nontrivial element of its unit group of order three satisfies \(T^2+T+I=0\). In the first case the explicit matrix \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\) has the same property. For the second case no finite-division-ring classification is required: a nonidentity element of a group of order three has order three, and its difference from I is invertible, so \((T-I)(T^2+T+I)=0\) gives that identity. The density statement and opposite-ring convention are exactly those proved/read in the finite-algebra record. Such an element is one of the \(T_\ell(X)\)'s and makes its flag zero. Consequently if all flags equal I the action is reducible. A proper nonzero subspace of a binary plane is a line; a basis starting on it triangularizes every generator. \(\square\)
