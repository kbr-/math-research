Affine slice normalization

Theorem. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_a\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) have degree at most two, supplied total circuit size \(u\) and depth at most \(\Delta\). Put \(b_i=x_i^2+x_i\). Suppose \(C(x,Y,Z)\) is a Hilbert-like refutation, meaning it is linear in all placeholders \((Y,Z)\), vanishes at zero placeholders, and \[ C(x,F(x),b(x))=1. \] Let \(\psi(t)=Ut+c\), with \(U\in\mathbb F_2^{v\times k}\) of column rank \(k\), \(c\in\mathbb F_2^v\), and \(0\le k\le v\). There is a circuit \(A(x,Y,Z)\), linear in all original placeholders and determined by \(F,\psi\) independently of \(C\), such that \[ M(x)=1+A(x,F(x),b(x)),\qquad C'=A+MC \] is another Hilbert-like refutation, and \[ C'(\psi(t),Y,Z)=A(\psi(t),Y,Z) \] as a formal polynomial with placeholders retained. If \(C\) has size \(S\) and depth \(d\), one may take \(A\) of constant depth and size \(O(v(k+1)+2^k(k+1)^3)\), and \(C'\) of size \[ O(S+u+a+v(k+1)+2^k(k+1)^3) \] and depth at most \(\max(d,\Delta+8)+2\). The model is division-free unbounded-fanin arithmetic circuits counting gates and wires, with binary constants. If every coefficient of \(C\) has degree at most \(L\), those of \(C'\) have degree at most \(L+k+2\). This is a formal coefficient-degree bound, not a PC refutation-degree assertion.

Parameter certificate. Set \(G_i(t)=F_i(Ut+c)\) and \(\beta_j=t_j^2+t_j\). For every \(e\in\{0,1\}^k\), some \(G_{i(e)}(e)=1\): otherwise \(x=Ue+c\) makes all original input and Boolean generators zero, contradicting the displayed certificate identity. Choose the least such index; this fixes the following construction using only \(F,\psi\). Put \[ \delta_e(t)=\prod_j\bigl(e_jt_j+(1+e_j)(1+t_j)\bigr), \quad \delta_{e,-j}=\prod_{l\ne j}\bigl(e_lt_l+(1+e_l)(1+t_l)\bigr). \] Then \(\sum_e\delta_e=1\) and \(\delta_e(t)(t_j+e_j)=\beta_j\delta_{e,-j}\).

Expand each quadratic \(G_{i(e)}\) into monomials. For an ordered monomial \(m=t_{j_1}\cdots t_{j_q}\), \(q\le2\), repeated indices allowed, \[ m(t)+m(e)=\sum_{l=1}^q(t_{j_l}+e_{j_l}) \prod_{hl}e_{j_h}. \] Multiplying by \(\delta_e\) gives explicit terms divisible by the corresponding \(\beta_{j_l}\), with quotient \(\delta_{e,-j_l}\prod_{hl}e_{j_h}\). Sum over monomials and assignments, obtaining polynomials \(q_j(t)\) of degree at most \(k\) such that \[ \sum_e\delta_eG_{i(e)}=1+\sum_jq_j\beta_j. \] Therefore \[ T(t,Y,W)=\sum_e\delta_e(t)Y_{i(e)}+\sum_jq_j(t)W_j \] is linear in \((Y,W)\) and satisfies \(T(t,G(t),\beta(t))=1\) formally. Each quadratic has at most \(\binom{k+2}{2}\) monomials, each contributing at most two terms with at most \(k+1\) factors including its placeholder. Expanded sums of these products give size \(O(2^k(k+1)^3)\), constant depth, and coefficient degree at most \(k\). This proof constructs the Boolean corrections; it does not replace a formal identity by agreement at cube points.

Retain original Boolean placeholders. Choose \(V\in\mathbb F_2^{k\times v}\) with \(VU=I_k\), and put \(\pi(x)=V(x+c)\). Then \(\pi\psi=I_k\). Characteristic two and binary constants give the exact identities \[ b(\psi(t))=U\beta(t),\qquad Vb(\psi(t))=\beta(t). \] Define \(A(x,Y,Z)=T(\pi(x),Y,VZ)\). On the slice its specialization at the original generators is exactly \(T(t,G(t),\beta(t))=1\); hence \(M(\psi(t))=0\). All placeholders remain the original \(Y_i,Z_i\), and every parameter Boolean placeholder is replaced by the explicit linear combination \((VZ)_j\). Thus the conclusion holds even for a correlated slice, not only one keeping a subset of coordinates.

Patch and ledger. Evaluating \(C'\) at zero placeholders gives zero. At \((F,b)\), it gives \(A(x,F,b)+M=1\); linearity is preserved because \(M\) has no placeholders. On the slice \(M\) vanishes, proving the asserted polynomial identity. Shared affine circuits for \(\pi\) and \(VZ\) cost \(O(v(k+1))\) wires. The displayed selector products and sums can then be evaluated in at most six layers. A second copy specialized at the inputs and the quadratic \(b_i\) computes \(M\); sharing all input circuits costs \(O(u+a+v)\) more and depth at most \(\Delta+8\). Reuse \(C\) once, multiply and add. These bounds are conservative and count the actual selector expansion. Since the coefficients of \(A\) have degree at most \(k\), \(\deg M\le k+2\). Multiplying a degree-\(L\) coefficient by \(M\) proves the coefficient-degree bound. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For every \(k\ge1\), take \(v=k+1\), \(\psi(t)=(t_1,\ldots,t_k,t_1)\), inputs \(F_1=x_1,F_2=1+x_1\), and certificate \(Y_1+Y_2\). The embedding is correlated and injective, both Boolean values occur, and the prescribed violated index changes with \(t_1\). The theorem's proof does not need either input to become the constant one on the whole slice. For the rank family \(AB=I_m\), \(m>n\ge1\), all inputs are quadratic and the Boolean system is inconsistent by rank; the theorem applies to every supplied certificate and every such fixed affine embedding at unbounded dimensions.

Degenerate cases. At \(k=0\), there is one Boolean point and one violated input; use \(A=Y_{i(\varnothing)}\), recovering a point version of axiom-cut normalization. A supplied certificate excludes the empty or satisfiable input system on a nonempty Boolean cube. Repeated monomial indices are retained in the telescoping formula, so square terms are not silently multilinearized. A noninjective parameterization can be reduced to a basis of its affine image; the theorem itself uses the stated rank hypothesis. It gives no exact-image realization of the patch and no bound on an initially unknown \(S\).

Consequence and limitation

For the rank encoding, \(v=2mn\) and \(u+a=O(m^2n)\). If a fixed slice has \(k\le c\log_2N\), for constant \(c\), its \(2^k\) term is at most \(N^c\); all other costs are polynomial in the encoding length. A hypothetical small certificate can therefore be normalized to cheap data on that slice at bounded depth. This rules out a strategy requiring every full-coset certificate to have hard data on one such fixed slice. The new certificate depends on the slice. The theorem does not normalize every slice simultaneously, preserve an adaptive choice of slice, or preserve the exact Frege simulation image. For \(k=\omega(\log N)\), the displayed construction has no polynomial guarantee; that is an unpaid cost, not a lower bound.
