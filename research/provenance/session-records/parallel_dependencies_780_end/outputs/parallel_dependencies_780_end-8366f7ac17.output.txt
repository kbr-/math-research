
###two-role-compact-decoder
2. Code labels and the decoder
For a move of row \(j\) with pattern \(p\) a label \(y\in E(\rho)\cap C(p)\) is valid if the row filling it is not pinned to it, \(\rho''^{-1}(y)\notin A_y\), and \(j\) is not pinned to it, \(y\notin X_j\); the pair is \(s\)-rich if every pair \((j,p)\) of a light row and a pattern of a term on \(j\) has at least \(s\) valid labels. The code \((\rho^\ast,\sigma,\beta,\delta)\) is that of the previous entry with this validity.
Lemma B (decoder). The code determines the pair. Proof. The decoder and its evaluation rule are those of Lemma B of the previous entry; its three claims are re-examined where a two-role row \(j\) moved later, at its code label \(c_j\) in \(\alpha\) and unassigned in reality, has pins. (i) Matched pins agree: \(\rho^\ast\) matches \(j\) to \(c_j\notin X_j\), so it matches no pin of \(j\), and \(\mu'\) matches none, \(j\) being residual. (ii) A pin \((j,x)\) with \(x\notin Q\) is dead in both: in \(\rho'\) the label \(x\) is occupied by a row other than \(j\), and \(\alpha(j)=c_j\ne x\). A pin \((j,x)\) with \(x\in Q\) unused is alive in reality and dead in \(\alpha\); a term \(T'\) carrying it that precedes the current term \(T\) is falsified in reality, and not through its pin, so through a tail row assigned before \(m\), on which \(\alpha\) agrees, and \(T'\) is falsified in \(\alpha\) as well; \(T\) itself never carries such a pin, since an alive pin of the current term is resolved by the round at \(m\), which assigns \(j\) at \(m\), so that \(j\) is not moved later. Pins at \(c_j\) of other rows are dead in both, \(c_j\) being occupied in \(\rho'\) by its filling row, which by validity is not pinned to it, and \(\alpha(i)\ne c_j\) for every row \(i\ne j\). (iii) The current term's rows assigned before \(m\) agree, including a pinned row assigned by an earlier round or moved earlier and reassigned to its hole; its tail rows moved at \(m\) carry consistent code labels; a row of \(B\) moved later is not in its tail by the complete-term rule and, by the argument of (ii), not its pinned row. Everything else is as before, rows of \(S\) lying outside \(B\). \(\square\)
Lemma C of the previous entry, the count \(A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\) with \(A''=2rN(N+1)e/(n-N-e+1)\), is unchanged: validity only restricts the encoder's choice.


###two-role-compact-richness
3. Richness with both exclusions
Lemma R. If the reader is sparsely pinned and column-sparse, \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\) and \(2^{w+6}s\le e\), then, for a uniform \(e\)-subset \(S\) of \(A_0\) and a uniform pair over \(\Phi_e(S)\), the pair fails to be \(s\)-rich with probability at most \(n2^{-e/29}+2Mr\,e^{-s}\). Proof. As in Lemma R of the previous entry, outside probability \(n2^{-e/29}\) every non-heavy label \(y\) has \(|A_y\cap S|\le e/16\). Fix \(j\) and a pattern \(p\) of a term on \(j\), and let \(L\) be the non-heavy labels of \(C(p)\cap O\setminus X_j\): \(|C(p)\cap O|\ge|C(p)|/2\), at most a quarter of it is heavy, and \(|X_j\cap C(p)|\le|C(p)|/4\), so \(|L|\ge\tfrac38|C(p)|-\tfrac14|C(p)|=n2^{-w-3}\). Then \(|E(\rho)\cap L|\) is hypergeometric with mean at least \(e2^{-w-3}\ge8s\), so it is at least \(4s\) outside probability \(e^{-e2^{-w-3}/8}\le e^{-s}\), and the sequential revelation of the filling on the first \(m=\min(|E(\rho)\cap L|,\lceil e/2\rceil)\ge4s\) of its labels gives at least \(m/2\ge2s\) valid labels outside probability \(2^{-m/2}\le2^{-2s}\), as before. Over at most \(Mr\) pairs \((j,p)\) this costs \(Mr(e^{-s}+2^{-2s})\le2Mr\,e^{-s}\). \(\square\)

###current-term-rule-decoder
2. The current-term rule and its decoder
Code labels. Fix a pair whose compact tree has a path with at least \(s\) moves and fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move, take the lexicographically first such path, cut after its \(s\)-th move, and let \(m_k\) be the node of its \(k\)-th move. Let \(X_{\le k}\) be the set of pin labels of the current terms of the nodes up to and including \(m_k\); \(|X_{\le k}|\le(k-1)+(\nu_0-1)+1\le s+\nu_0-1\), every node having a move or a round. The code label of the \(k\)-th move, of a row \(j_k\) with pattern \(p_k\) in the current term of \(m_k\), is the smallest label of \(E(\rho)\cap C(p_k)\) that is not a code label of an earlier move and not in \(X_{\le k}\); for a two-role reader also not in \(X_{j_k}\), the set of labels \(j_k\) is pinned to, as in the two-role form. The code is \((\rho^\ast,\sigma,\beta,\delta)\) as before with these code labels.
Decoder. The decoder of Lemma B of the compact composition with one change to its evaluation rule: a row marked "at the \(k\)-th code label" satisfies no pin while the \(k\)-th move is undecoded; when the \(k\)-th move is decoded, its row \(j_k\) being read from \(\beta\) and \(y_k=\rho^\ast(j_k)\) from \(\rho^\ast\), the marked row \(f_k\), which \(\sigma\) names, is assigned \(y_k\) in \(\alpha\), and from then on it satisfies the pin \((f_k,y_k)\) and no other, and the preference counts that pin as matched.
Lemma B (decoder). The code determines the pair. Proof. The decoder simulates \(\mathcal T_c(F,\rho')\) node by node. At a node \(m\) with real current term \(T\), let \(K_m\) be the set of \(k\) with \(m_k\ge m\), the slots undecoded when the decoder selects at \(m\); a marked row of a slot \(k\notin K_m\) is assigned its label in \(\alpha\) and behaves as a row of \(S\) with a value in \(E(\rho^\ast)\). Three claims. (i) A term not falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi_{<m}\) whose pin is not \((f_k,y_k)\) for a \(k\in K_m\) has the same pin-matched status in \(\alpha\) and in \(\rho'\): its pinned row \(i\) lies in \(A\), so it is not moved, and \(\alpha(i)=\rho^\ast(i)=\rho'(i)\) for \(i\notin S\), \(\alpha(i)=\sigma(i)=\rho'(i)\) for \(i\in S\) with a value in \(E(\rho^\ast)\) or with a decoded slot, while a marked row of an undecoded slot \(k\) is matched in reality only to \(y_k\), and the pin \((i,y_k)\) is excluded; the preference counts only pins matched by \(\rho'\), as recorded. (ii) A term falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi_{<m}\) is falsified in \(\alpha\): through a tail row assigned in reality, on which \(\alpha\) agrees as in the recorded proof (matched rows, rows moved earlier and reassigned to their holes, rows assigned by round answers), or through its pin, a hole of \(Q\) used or emptied, which \(\alpha\) shows, or an outside label \(x\) with \(\rho'(i)\ne x\) or \(i\) residual, where \(\alpha(i)\ne x\), or \(\alpha(i)\) is undefined, or \(i\) is a marked row of an undecoded slot, whose real label differs from \(x\) and which satisfies no pin by the rule: dead in both. (iii) A term \(T'\) carrying a pin \((f_k,y_k)\) with \(k\in K_m\) and not falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi_{<m}\) comes after \(T\) in the reader's order: it is pin-matched in reality, so if it preceded \(T\) the preference would select it or an earlier pin-matched term, and \(T'=T\) is excluded because \(m\le m_k\) and \(y_k\notin X_{\le k}\), which contains the pin label of \(T\). Now the decoder scans the terms in order as the preference does. Every term before \(T\) is falsified in reality, hence in \(\alpha\) by (ii), or is not falsified and not pin-matched in reality; the latter has, by (iii), no pin \((f_k,y_k)\) with \(k\in K_m\), so by (i) it is not pin-matched in \(\alpha\) either, and the scan passes it whether or not \(\alpha\) shows it falsified through the code label of a row moved later. \(T\) is not falsified in \(\alpha\): its rows assigned before \(m\) agree, its tail rows moved at \(m\) carry consistent code labels, its other tail rows are unassigned in both by the complete-term rule, and its pin agrees by (i), since \(T\)'s pin is not \((f_k,y_k)\) with \(k\in K_m\). If \(T\) is pin-matched in reality it is pin-matched in \(\alpha\) by (i) and the scan stops at \(T\); otherwise no term is pin-matched and not falsified in reality, and none is in \(\alpha\): a term not falsified in \(\alpha\) is not falsified in reality by (ii), and a pin matched in \(\alpha\) is matched in reality, since \(\alpha\) agrees with \(\rho'\) wherever it assigns a pinned row of \(A\), and for a two-role reader a moved row's code label lies outside its pinned labels; so the scan returns the first term not falsified in \(\alpha\), which is \(T\). The rest, the reading of the round answers and the moves from \(\delta\) and \(\beta\), the return of the moved rows to the residual set and of \(\rho''\) from \(\sigma\), is as recorded. For a two-role reader the additional points of the two-role form's Lemma B, the pins of a row moved later, are unchanged, since a moved row's code label lies outside its own pinned labels and outside every other code label. \(\square\)
Lemma C of the compact composition, the count \(A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\) with \(A''=2rN(N+1)e/(n-N-e+1)\), is unchanged: the code has the same format and the rule only restricts the encoder's choice.


###current-term-rule-richness
3. Richness is a count on \(E(\rho)\)
Call the pair \(s\)-rich if \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)|\ge3s\) for every pattern \(p\) of a tail row, and for a two-role reader \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X_j|\ge3s\) for every pair \((j,p)\) of a light row and a pattern of a term on \(j\). If \(s\ge\nu_0\), the encoder always has a label: at the \(k\)-th move at most \(k-1\le s-1\) labels are picked and at most \(s+\nu_0-1\) excluded, and \(3s-(s-1)-(s+\nu_0-1)=s-\nu_0+2\ge2\).
Lemma R. If \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\) (which implies the recorded \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\)), and for a two-role reader the reader is sparsely pinned, then, for every \(e\)-set \(S\) and a uniform pair over \(\Phi_e(S)\), the pair fails to be \(s\)-rich with probability at most \(Mr\,e^{-s}\). Proof. Given \(S\), the set \(E(\rho)\) is a uniform \(e\)-subset of \(O\), \(|O|=n-N\). For a pattern \(p\) of width at most \(w\), \(|C(p)\cap O|\ge n2^{-w}-N\ge n2^{-w-1}\), as \(N\le n2^{-w-2}\) in the stated range; under sparse pinning \(|X_j\cap C(p)|\le|C(p)|/4\), so \(|C(p)\cap O\setminus X_j|\ge n2^{-w}-N-n2^{-w-2}\ge n2^{-w-1}\). Hence \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)|\), or \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X_j|\), is hypergeometric with mean at least \(e2^{-w-1}\ge8s\), and it is below \(3s\le\tfrac38\cdot8s\) with probability at most \(e^{-(5/8)^2\cdot8s/2}\le e^{-s}\), by the Chernoff bound for a hypergeometric variable. There are at most \(Mr\) patterns of tail rows, or pairs \((j,p)\). \(\square\)

###wide-rounds-tree
1. The wide-round tree
Setting. A reader \(F\) as in Section 1 of the compact composition, role-separated with \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\) and pin-free terms allowed, or a two-role reader as in its two-role form, sparsely pinned on its light rows and with \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\), with one change: the tail rows split, \(B=B_{\rm l}\sqcup B_{\rm w}\), a partition fixed by the reader. A light row \(j\in B_{\rm l}\) carries, in every term, a pattern of at most \(w\) literals (the recorded \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\) is implied by the light range of Theorem W); a wide row \(j\in B_{\rm w}\) carries patterns of any width up to \(\ell\). A pair \((T,j)\) of a term and a wide row of its tail, with pattern \(p_{T,j}\), is a wide incidence. The tree lives on the residual instance of \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\), pigeons \(R'\) and holes \(Q\), all \(N\) holes unused at the root.
The wide graph. For \(\rho'\) let \(G=G(F,\rho')\) be the bipartite graph with the residual wide rows \(R'\cap B_{\rm w}\) on one side and the holes of \(Q\) on the other, and an edge \((j,x)\) whenever \(x\in C(p_{T,j})\) for some term \(T\) on \(j\), whatever the status of \(T\). It depends on \(Q\) and \(R'\cap B_{\rm w}\) only. Let \(C=C_{\rm rows}\sqcup C_{\rm holes}\) be its canonical minimum vertex cover: among the vertex covers of least size one with the fewest rows, and among those the lexicographically first; the tie-breaks only make the choice determinate for the decoder, and any fixed rule would do. By König's theorem \(|C|=\tau(G)=\nu(G)\), the matching number of \(G\).
Rule. A hole of \(Q\) is resolved on a path once it is used by a row assigned on the path or emptied by a hole query. The statuses of the terms at a node are those of the complete-term tree with one more way of counting as falsified: a term with an uncovered tail row \(j\in B_{\rm w}\) of pattern \(p\) none of whose holes in \(C(p)\cap Q\) is unresolved counts as falsified. The current term is chosen by the matched-first preference among the terms neither falsified nor counting as falsified; a 1-leaf is a node where some term is satisfied by \(\rho'\cup\pi\), and a node without a current term is a 0-leaf. At the current term, if its pin is alive, the round of the complete-term rule comes first; then its uncovered tail rows are handled in the term's order. A light row is queried as a pigeon over the unused holes of \(Q\), a light move as before. A wide row \(j\in C_{\rm rows}\) is queried as a pigeon over the unused holes of \(Q\), a wide pigeon query. A wide row \(j\notin C_{\rm rows}\) with pattern \(p\) is sought by hole queries at the unresolved holes of \(C(p)\cap Q\) in increasing order, each with branches over the unassigned rows of \(R'\) and the empty branch as in a round, until an answer is \(j\) or no unresolved hole of \(C(p)\cap Q\) remains, in which case \(j\) stays uncovered. Wide pigeon queries and these hole queries are the wide queries. So every tail row of the current term is assigned at its node or left uncovered with all of its cube's holes in \(Q\) resolved, and after its node the term is satisfied, falsified, or counts as falsified, except at a cut. Any row of \(R'\) may be assigned by the answer of a hole query; \(\mathcal T_w\) differs from \(\mathcal T_c\) only at wide rows, and on a reader without wide rows the two trees coincide.
Lemma W0 (annihilation at a wide row). Let \(T\) be a term with a wide row \(j\in R'\) of pattern \(p\), and let a path leave \(j\) unassigned while every hole of \(C(p)\cap Q\) is resolved on it. Then the product of the image of \(T\) under \(\rho'\) and the path's leaf factor lies in the ideal of the residual axioms at degree two (row axioms, row and column exclusions, Boolean axioms), the ideal the switching lemmas' image step works modulo, which contains the ideal of the hole-query nodes lemma. Proof. The restriction confines the label of the residual row \(j\) to \(Q\), so the image of \([j\in p]\) equals, modulo the row axiom and the row exclusions of \(j\), \(\sum_{x\in C(p)\cap Q}X_{jx}\), the sum of the residual unary variables of \(j\) at the labels of its cube inside the flat, as the image of a pin \([i\to x]\) is \(X_{ix}\); it is a factor of the image of \(T\). Every hole of \(Q\) is unused at the root, so a used \(x\in C(p)\cap Q\) is used by a row \(j'\ne j\) of \(R'\) assigned on the path, the leaf factor contains \(X_{j'x}\), and \(X_{jx}X_{j'x}\) is a column exclusion; for \(x\) emptied at a node with matching \(\pi_0\) the leaf factor contains \(u_x(\pi_0)\), and \(j\) is uncovered by \(\pi_0\), so \(X_{jx}u_x(\pi_0)\) lies in the ideal by the hole-query nodes lemma. \(\square\)
If \(C(p)\cap Q=\varnothing\) the image of \(T\) is zero outright, so the terms with a residual wide row whose cube misses the flat vanish at the root: the static skip of the wide-rows entry is built in. Hence a 0-leaf of \(\mathcal T_w\) falsifies or annihilates every term, a 1-leaf contains a satisfied term, and its nodes are pigeon queries over unused holes and hole-query nodes of the recorded kind; the tree has the leaf properties the switching lemmas use, with leaf degrees at most the height, as recorded for the complete-term tree. The q-skip refutation does not apply: it broke the encoding because a moved row's code label, consistent with its pattern, revived a skipped term for the decoder, while here the skip tests only wide rows, which are never placed at code labels, so the decoder sees them exactly as reality does (Lemma W4).


###wide-rounds-cover
2. Wide queries are bounded by the cover
Lemma W1. On every path of \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) the wide queries number at most \(|C|=\nu(G)\); a wide pigeon query has at most \(N\) answers and a hole query at most \(N+2\). Proof. A wide pigeon query is at a row of \(C_{\rm rows}\), which its answer assigns, so each row of \(C_{\rm rows}\) is queried at most once. A hole query is made for an uncovered wide row \(j\notin C_{\rm rows}\) of the current term, with pattern \(p\), at an unresolved hole \(x\in C(p)\cap Q\). An uncovered row is residual, so \((j,x)\) is an edge of \(G\); \(C\) covers it and \(j\notin C_{\rm rows}\), so \(x\in C_{\rm holes}\); the query resolves \(x\), so each hole of \(C_{\rm holes}\) is queried at most once. The answers are the unused holes of \(Q\), respectively the unassigned rows of \(R'\) and the empty branch. König's theorem gives \(|C|=\nu(G)\). \(\square\)
The bound is deterministic: on the pin-free reader \(F_p\) of the shared-cube lemma, \(G\) is the complete bipartite graph between \(R'\cap B\) and \(C(p)\cap Q\), the cover is \(C(p)\cap Q\) when \(|C(p)\cap Q|\le|R'\cap B|\), and the tree makes at most two hole queries where the complete-term tree made \(\min(|R'\cap B|,N-1)\) pigeon queries; on the single-row reader with the \(n\) terms \([j=z]\) the graph is a star at \(j\), the cover is \(\{j\}\), and the tree makes one pigeon query, the smaller-side rule of the hole-only sparse failure without a per-node decision.


###wide-rounds-matching
3. The wide matching number
For a label \(x\) let the wide degree \(d_{\rm w}(x)\) be the number of wide incidences \((T,j)\) with \(x\in C(p_{T,j})\), and let \(m_{\rm w}=\sum_xd_{\rm w}(x)=\sum_{(T,j)}|C(p_{T,j})|\) be the wide mass; both are fixed by the reader. For \(D>0\) let \(\mathrm{Heavy}=\{x:\ d_{\rm w}(x)>D\}\), so that \(|\mathrm{Heavy}|\le m_{\rm w}/D\).
Lemma W2. For integers \(k\ge2\) and \(t\ge1\), with \(d=\lceil\log_2k\rceil\),
\[\Pr_{\rho'\in\Phi_0}\bigl[\nu(G)\ge k+t-1\bigr]\le\Bigl(\frac{|\mathrm{Heavy}|N}{n}\Bigr)^{d+1}+\Bigl(\frac{eDN(N+1)}{t(n+1)}\Bigr)^{t}.\]
Proof. A matching of \(G\) uses distinct holes, so at most \(|\mathrm{Heavy}\cap Q|\) of its edges have a heavy hole and the others form a matching of the subgraph \(G_{\rm light}\) of the edges at holes outside \(\mathrm{Heavy}\); so \(\nu(G)\le|\mathrm{Heavy}\cap Q|+\nu(G_{\rm light})\), and \(\nu(G)\ge k+t-1\) forces \(|\mathrm{Heavy}\cap Q|\ge k\) or \(\nu(G_{\rm light})\ge t\). By the rank lemma in its independent-points form, \(\Pr[|\mathrm{Heavy}\cap Q|\ge k]\le(|\mathrm{Heavy}|N/n)^{d+1}\). For the second event condition on \(Q\). A matching of size \(t\) in \(G_{\rm light}\) consists of \(t\) holes \(x_1<\dots<x_t\) of \(Q\) outside \(\mathrm{Heavy}\), at most \(\binom Nt\le(eN/t)^t\) choices, and distinct rows \(j_1,\dots,j_t\in R'\cap B_{\rm w}\) with \((j_i,x_i)\) an edge; the rows adjacent to \(x_i\) number at most \(d_{\rm w}(x_i)\le D\), at most \(D^t\) choices, and a fixed set of \(t\) rows is residual with probability \(\prod_{i<t}(N+1-i)/(n+1-i)\le((N+1)/(n+1))^t\), since \(R'\) is a uniform \((N+1)\)-subset of the \(n+1\) rows independent of \(Q\). The union bound gives \(\Pr[\nu(G_{\rm light})\ge t]\le(eDN(N+1)/(t(n+1)))^t\). \(\square\)
Polynomial form. Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) and \(c'>0\); put \(d=\lceil c'/\varepsilon\rceil+2\), \(t=\lceil c'/\varepsilon\rceil+1\), \(D=n^{1-\varepsilon}/(N+1)\). If \(m_{\rm w}\le n^{2-2\varepsilon}/(N+1)\), then \(|\mathrm{Heavy}|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\), the first term is at most \((Nn^{-\varepsilon})^{d+1}\le N^{d+1}n^{-c'-3\varepsilon}\) and the second at most \((eNn^{-\varepsilon}/t)^t\le(eN)^tn^{-c'-\varepsilon}\); so for \(n\) large enough that \(N^{d+1}\le n^{3\varepsilon}/2\) and \((eN)^t\le n^{\varepsilon}/2\), which polylogarithmic \(N\) gives, \(\Pr[\nu(G)\ge2^d+t-1]\le n^{-c'}\). A reader with at most \(n^c\) terms, at most \(r\) wide rows per term and every wide pattern of at least \(w_1\) literals has \(m_{\rm w}\le rn^{c+1}2^{-w_1}\), so the mass condition holds when \(2^{w_1}\ge r(N+1)n^{c-1+2\varepsilon}\): the wide range begins at \((c-1+2\varepsilon)\log_2n+\log_2(r(N+1))\) literals. A pattern of fewer than \(w_1\) literals on a wide row adds at most \(n\) to \(m_{\rm w}\), so with \(2^{w_1}\ge2r(N+1)n^{c-1+2\varepsilon}\) up to \(n^{1-2\varepsilon}/(2(N+1))\) such incidences are absorbed by the same condition.


###wide-rounds-rounds
4. Rounds on the wide-round tree
Lemma W3. Lemma 1 of the complete-term rule holds on \(\mathcal T_w\): on every path the trigger pairs of the rounds are pairwise disjoint pairs \((i,x)\) with \(i\in R'\), \(x\in Q\), whose host is not falsified by \(\rho'\) and has no matched pair. Proof. As recorded, with the new way of counting as falsified added. After a round \(i\) is assigned and \(x\) is used or emptied, and wide queries only assign further rows and resolve further holes, so the pairs are disjoint. At the node the current term neither is falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi\) nor counts as falsified, so its host \(h\) is not falsified by \(\rho'\). If \(h\) had a matched pair \((i',x')\), the term \(T'=[i'\to x']\wedge h\) would be pin-matched; were it neither falsified nor counting as falsified at the node, the preference would choose a pin-matched term, whose pinned row is matched and not residual, and no round would follow; so \(T'\) is falsified or counts as falsified there, its pinned part holds under \(\rho'\), and so its tail \(h\) is falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi\) or has an uncovered wide row with every hole of its cube in \(Q\) resolved, which makes the current term, with the same tail, falsified or counting as falsified, a contradiction. \(\square\)
By Lemma 2 of the complete-term rule the round bounds of the multi-row theorem and its predecessors use of the tree only Lemma 1, so they hold on \(\mathcal T_w\) with their probabilities. The re-reading of the multi-row theorem's proof for this entry confirms it: its facts (a) and (b) are Lemma 1; steps (H1) to (H3) count the trigger pairs of rounds through them and the activated union lemma and the rank lemma, which concern \(\rho'\) alone; and (H4) exhibits a term satisfied by \(\rho'\), which makes the root a 1-leaf of \(\mathcal T_w\) as of every tree with the 1-leaf rule. So the multi-row size corollary holds on \(\mathcal T_w\): a role-separated reader with at most \(n^c\) terms, \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\), \(800\,\mathrm e\,(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), under that theorem's conditions on \(N\), has fewer than \(N/48\) rounds on every path of \(\mathcal T_w\) before a 1-leaf except with probability \(3n^{-c-1}\), whatever its tails. The recorded multi-row setting pins every term, while Section 1 allows pin-free terms: a pin-free term triggers no round and contributes no pair to any host, mass or union, and when satisfied it only makes a 1-leaf, so the bound holds with them, the extension that clause (a) of the current-term corollary already makes. The few-light-rows theorem, the combined theorem and the two-role bounds are not transferred here: Lemma 2 charges a round whose host is falsified at its own node to the residual light row assigned there, and on \(\mathcal T_w\) a host can also leave the family consistent with the exposed rows' labels when a wide row of the current term is left uncovered with its holes exhausted and later assigned elsewhere by a hole query; that accounting is left to the next cycle (Section 7).


###wide-rounds-composition
5. Composition on the wide-round tree
Code. For a pair \((\rho,\rho'')\) of \(\Phi_e(S)\) whose tree \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) has a path with at least \(s\) light moves, fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds and at most \(\nu_1\) wide queries before its \(s\)-th light move, take the lexicographically first such path, cut after its \(s\)-th light move, and let \(m_k\) be the node of its \(k\)-th light move. The code is \((\rho^\ast,\sigma,\beta,\delta)\) of the current-term rule: \(\rho^\ast\in\Phi_{e-s}(S)\) places the \(s\) moved light rows at code labels chosen by that rule, the \(k\)-th in \(E(\rho)\cap C(p_k)\), unpicked, outside \(X_{\le k}\), the pin labels of the current terms of the nodes up to and including \(m_k\), and for a two-role reader outside \(X_{j_k}\); now \(|X_{\le k}|\le k+(\nu_0-1)+\nu_1\le s+\nu_0+\nu_1-1\), every node having a light move, a round or a wide query. Wide rows are never placed. \(\sigma\) is as before; \(\beta\) lists, in path order, one bit per node, whether the node has a light move, and per light move its position among the term's tail rows and a last-of-node bit; \(\delta\) lists, in path order, the rounds' answers, the light moves' holes and the wide queries' answers, a hole of \(Q\) for a wide pigeon query and a row of \(R'\) or the empty branch for a hole query. Richness is that of the current-term rule for the light patterns: \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)|\ge3s\) for every pattern \(p\) on a light row, and for a two-role reader \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X_j|\ge3s\) for every pair \((j,p)\) of a light row and a pattern of a term on \(j\); with \(s\ge\nu_0+\nu_1\) the encoder always has a label, since at the \(k\)-th move at most \(k-1\le s-1\) labels are picked and at most \(s+\nu_0+\nu_1-1\) excluded, and \(3s-(s-1)-(s+\nu_0+\nu_1-1)=s-\nu_0-\nu_1+2\ge2\). Lemma R of the current-term rule, a statement about \(E(\rho)\) alone, bounds the failure of richness by \(Mr\,e^{-s}\) under \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\) and, two-role, sparse pinning.
Lemma W4 (decoder). The code determines the pair. Proof. The decoder first recovers \(G\) and \(C\): \(Q\) is the flat of \(\rho^\ast\), and \(R'\cap B_{\rm w}\) is the set of residual wide rows of \(\rho^\ast\), because \(S\subseteq A\) is disjoint from \(B\) and only light rows are placed. It then simulates \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) node by node from \(\alpha\) as in Lemma B of the current-term rule, whose claims (i) to (iii) hold verbatim: they concern the pins and the statuses of terms, and use of the tree only that every uncovered light row of the current term is moved at its node, which \(\mathcal T_w\) keeps. Three additions. The rows assigned in reality on which \(\alpha\) agrees now include the rows assigned by wide queries, read from \(\delta\). The unresolved holes agree in \(\alpha\) and in reality: \(\alpha\) uses no hole of \(Q\) beyond those the path uses, the code labels and the values of \(\sigma\) lying outside \(Q\), and the emptied holes are read from \(\delta\); with the wide rows unplaced, a term counting as falsified through a wide row does so in \(\alpha\) exactly when it does in reality, so claim (ii) extends to this way of being falsified and the current term's uncovered wide rows are unassigned in both. At the current term the decoder reads the round from \(\delta\) if the pin is alive in \(\alpha\), then handles the tail rows in the term's order: a light row is moved at this node exactly when \(\beta\) lists its position, its hole coming from \(\delta\), and otherwise it is assigned in reality as in \(\alpha\); an unassigned wide row of \(C_{\rm rows}\) takes its hole from \(\delta\); for an unassigned wide row outside \(C_{\rm rows}\) it reads from \(\delta\) one answer per unresolved hole of the row's cube, in increasing order, updating \(\alpha\) with each, until the answer is the row or the holes are exhausted, as the tree does. After the \(s\)-th light move it returns the moved rows to the residual set, which gives \(\rho\), and reads \(\rho''\) off \(\sigma\). For a two-role reader a wide row may carry pins; unplaced, and assigned only through \(\delta\), its pins have the same status in \(\alpha\) and in reality. \(\square\)
Lemma W5 (count). Let \(\theta_A=(N+1)e/(n-N-e+1)\) and \(A_{\rm w}=4rN\theta_A\). Among the \(s\)-rich pairs of \(\Phi_e(S)\), the fraction whose tree has a path with at least \(s\) light moves, fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds and at most \(\nu_1\) wide queries before its \(s\)-th light move is at most \(2A_{\rm w}^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}(2(N+2))^{\nu_1}\). Proof. As in Lemma C of the compact composition with the code above. \(\rho^\ast\) ranges over \(\Phi_{e-s}(S)\), of size at most \(\theta_A^{\,s}|\Phi_e(S)|\) by the recorded ratio, and \(\sigma\) over \(e!\) values. A path with \(\nu<\nu_0\) rounds and \(m_{\rm w}\le\nu_1\) wide queries has at most \(s+\nu+m_{\rm w}\) nodes, so \(\beta\) takes at most \(2^{s+\nu+m_{\rm w}}(2r)^s\) values, and \(\delta\) at most \(N^s(N(N+2))^{\nu}(N+2)^{m_{\rm w}}\) by Lemma W1 and the recorded round costs. Lemma W4 makes the code injective, so the fraction is at most \(\sum_{\nu<\nu_0}\sum_{m_{\rm w}\le\nu_1}A_{\rm w}^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu}(2(N+2))^{m_{\rm w}}\le A_{\rm w}^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\cdot2(2(N+2))^{\nu_1}\), since \(\sum_{\nu<\nu_0}x^{\nu}\le x^{\nu_0}\) and \(\sum_{m\le\nu_1}y^m\le2y^{\nu_1}\) for \(x,y\ge2\). \(\square\)
Theorem W (composition on the wide-round tree). Let \(F\) be a reader of Section 1, \(\nu_0=\nu_1=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\), \(s=\lfloor N/12\rfloor-2r-\nu_1\), \(N\ge48(2r+1)\), and let the light patterns satisfy \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(32rN^4(N+1))\). Suppose that, over \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\), every path of \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) before a 1-leaf has at most \(\nu_0-1\) rounds except with probability \(p_R\), and \(\nu(G(F,\rho'))\le\nu_1\) except with probability \(p_W\). Then \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) has height below \(N/8-2r\), and \(F\) switches within the height budget, except with probability at most
\[p_R+p_W+\tfrac75\Bigl(Mr\,e^{-s}+2^{-N/8+(2r+2)(2+3\log_2N)}\Bigr).\]
Proof. The height of a path is its light moves plus at most two per round plus its wide queries. A path of height at least \(s+2\nu_0+\nu_1\) has at least \(\nu_0\) rounds, or more than \(\nu_1\) wide queries, or at least \(s\) light moves with fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds and at most \(\nu_1\) wide queries before its \(s\)-th light move. The first is the round bound's event; the second is contained in \(\nu(G)>\nu_1\) by Lemma W1; the third is an event on \(\Phi_0\), and by Lemma A0, applicable as \(e(N+1)\le n/8\) and \(b=|A|\ge n/2+N+2\), or \(b=|A_0|\) in the two-role form, its probability is at most \(\tfrac75\) times its average over \(S\) of its probability on \(\Phi_0^S\), at most the richness failure \(Mr\,e^{-s}\), Lemma R's range holding, plus the count of Lemma W5. The bound on \(e\) gives \(\theta_A\le2(N+1)e/n\), as \(N+e-1\le n/2\), hence \(A_{\rm w}\le8rN(N+1)e/n\le1/(4N^3)\); and \(N\ge48(2r+1)\) gives \(s\ge N/16-2r-1\ge\nu_0+\nu_1\), since \(\nu_0+\nu_1\le N/24\). With \(N+2\le2N\) the count is at most \(2^{1-(N/16-2r-1)(2+3\log_2N)+(N/48)(2+2\log_2N)+(N/48)(2+\log_2N)}\), whose exponent is \(-N/24-(N/8)\log_2N+(2r+1)(2+3\log_2N)+1\le-N/8+(2r+2)(2+3\log_2N)\), because \((N/8)\log_2N\ge N/12\) for \(N\ge2\). Outside the three events every path has height below \(s+2\nu_0+\nu_1=\lfloor N/12\rfloor+2\nu_0-2r\le N/8-2r\); the tree is a matching decision tree for \(\rho'\) with pigeon queries over unused holes and hole-query nodes, with the leaf properties of Section 1, so the conclusion is the composition recorded in the height budget. \(\square\)
Scope. The theorem replaces the wide-move composition: no wide-move bound, no factor \((4rN)^{\nu_1}\), no hypothesis \(r\le N^6/16\); the wide queries cost \((2(N+2))^{\nu_1}\) in \(\delta\) and the node bits alone, and the range \(e\le n/(32rN^4(N+1))\) is kept as recorded, although \(N^3\) in place of \(N^4\) would do at the cost of the constant in the exponent. The light range is the current-term rule's up to the factor \(N^{5/2}\), width up to \(\log_2n-\log_2(2^9rsN^4(N+1))\). A reader without light rows needs neither the pair space nor the bound on \(|B|\): outside the two bounds' events its tree has height at most \(2(\nu_0-1)+\nu_1<N/8-2r\).


###wide-rounds-corollaries
6. Two corollaries
Corollary 1 (pin-free readers with split rows). Let \(F\) be a pin-free reader with at most \(M\le n^c\) terms \(\bigwedge_{j\in J}[j\in p_j]\), at most \(r\) rows per term, on rows \(B=B_{\rm l}\sqcup B_{\rm w}\) with \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\) when \(B_{\rm l}\ne\varnothing\) (the Scope note), light patterns of at most \(w\) literals with \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(32rN^4(N+1))\), and wide mass \(m_{\rm w}\le n^{2-2\varepsilon}/(N+1)\), which holds when every wide pattern has at least \(w_1\) literals with \(2^{w_1}\ge r(N+1)n^{c-1+2\varepsilon}\). Let \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), \(d=\lceil(c+1)/\varepsilon\rceil+2\), \(t=\lceil(c+1)/\varepsilon\rceil+1\), \(N\ge\max\bigl(48(2r+1),\ 48(2^d+t),\ 8(c+1)\log_2n+(2r+2)(16+24\log_2N)\bigr)\), \(s\ge(2c+1)\ln n+\ln r\), and \(n\) large enough that \(N^{d+1}\le n^{3\varepsilon}/2\) and \((eN)^t\le n^{\varepsilon}/2\). Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) switches \(F\) within the height budget except with probability at most \(4n^{-c-1}\). Proof. \(F\) has no pins, so no rounds: \(p_R=0\). The polynomial form of Lemma W2 with \(c'=c+1\) gives \(\Pr[\nu(G)\ge2^d+t-1]\le n^{-c-1}\), and \(\nu_1+1=\lfloor N/48\rfloor+1\ge2^d+t-1\) by \(N\ge48(2^d+t)\), so \(p_W\le n^{-c-1}\). In Theorem W, \(Mr\,e^{-s}\le n^{-c-1}\) for \(s\ge(2c+1)\ln n+\ln r\), and \(2^{-N/8+(2r+2)(2+3\log_2N)}\le n^{-c-1}\) for \(N\ge8(c+1)\log_2n+(2r+2)(16+24\log_2N)\); the total is \(n^{-c-1}+\tfrac75\cdot2n^{-c-1}\le4n^{-c-1}\). \(\square\)
Widths. The light range ends at \(\log_2n-\log_2(2^9rsN^4(N+1))\) literals and the wide range begins at \((c-1+2\varepsilon)\log_2n+\log_2(r(N+1))\); they overlap when \(n^{2-2\varepsilon-c}\ge2^9r^2sN^4(N+1)^2\), which holds for \(c<2-2\varepsilon\) and \(n\) large. Then every row whose patterns all have at most \(w\) literals, or all have at least \(w_1\) literals, is covered, with \(w_1\le w\), so every width is covered per row; a row carrying both a pattern of fewer than \(w_1\) literals and one of more than \(w\) literals goes to \(B_{\rm w}\) and is covered as long as such incidences number at most \(n^{1-2\varepsilon}/(2(N+1))\), by the absorption remark of Section 3 with \(2^{w_1}\ge2r(N+1)n^{c-1+2\varepsilon}\). From \(c=2-2\varepsilon\) on, the widths between the two ranges are open per reader.
Corollary 2 (role-separated readers below size \(n^{2-2\varepsilon}\)). Let \(F\) be a role-separated reader of Section 1 with at most \(n^c\) terms, \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\) as the multi-row corollary states (a reader with fewer terms has at most \(n\) terms), whose rows split with the light range and the wide mass condition of Corollary 1 with the same \(\varepsilon\), under Corollary 1's conditions on \(N\), \(s\) and \(n\), the multi-row theorem's conditions on \(N\), and \(800\,\mathrm e\,(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\). Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) switches \(F\) within the height budget except with probability at most \(7n^{-c-1}\). In particular, for \(c<2-2\varepsilon\) and \(n\) large, every role-separated reader of size \(n^c\) whose tail rows split into rows of patterns of at most \(w\) literals and rows of patterns of at least \(w_1\) literals switches, whatever the widths. Proof. The multi-row size corollary on \(\mathcal T_w\) (Section 4) gives fewer than \(N/48\) rounds on every path before a 1-leaf except with probability \(3n^{-c-1}\), and below \(\nu_0\) with the slack recorded in the current-term corollary, since its count \(N/192+2^{d_\varepsilon}\le5N/384\) is at most \(\lfloor N/48\rfloor-1\) for \(N\ge256\); so \(p_R=3n^{-c-1}\), \(p_W\le n^{-c-1}\) as in Corollary 1, and Theorem W gives \(3n^{-c-1}+n^{-c-1}+\tfrac{14}5n^{-c-1}\le7n^{-c-1}\). The width clause is the overlap of the two ranges for \(c<2-2\varepsilon\). \(\square\)
Corollary 2 closes, for role-separated readers with split rows, the window case of the current-term rule at sizes below \(n^{2-2\varepsilon}\): the per-reader failure \(7n^{-c-1}\) is the convention of the round bounds, a union over the \(n^c\) readers of a source costing \(n^c\) times that, against the dichotomy's per-reader failure \(n^{c-2}\) (lemma), which the complete-term tree could not beat on \(F_p\).
