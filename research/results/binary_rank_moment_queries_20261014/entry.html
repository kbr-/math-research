Rank moment expansion

Statement. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(m>n\ge1\), with independent matrices \(A:m\times n\), \(B:n\times m\), entry variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_v\) in any fixed order, and \(v=2mn\). Put \(b_j=X_j^2+X_j\), \(F_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(AB)_{ij}\), and \(P=\prod_{i,j}(1+F_{ij})\). Let \(s\) be a formal independent parameter, \(L_jf=(1+X_j)f|_{X_j=0}+X_jf|_{X_j=1}\), \(D_j=I+L_j\), and \(\Gamma_s=\prod_j(I+sD_j)\), with operator composition. For each legal colouring \(c\) of the \(m^2\) cells by integers \(0,\ldots,n\), require \(c_{ii}\ne0\). A positive colour \(\ell\) selects \(A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j}\); colour zero selects the off-diagonal constant one. Write its selected monomial as \(M_c=\prod_jX_j^{k_j(c)}\), with \(0\le k_j\le m\). Define

\[V_0(X,s)=1,\qquad V_k(X,s)=(1+s)X^k+sX\quad(k\ge1),\qquad h_0=h_1=0,\quad h_k(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}X^r\quad(k\ge2).\]

Then the following are formal polynomial identities, with all sums in characteristic two:

\[P=\sum_c M_c,\qquad \Gamma_sP=\sum_c\prod_j V_{k_j(c)}(X_j,s),\] \[Q_s(X,Z)=\sum_c\sum_j sZ_j h_{k_j(c)}(X_j)\prod_{r<j}V_{k_r(c)}(X_r,s)\prod_{r>j}X_r^{k_r(c)},\qquad Q_s(X,b)=P+\Gamma_sP.\]

In particular \(Q_1(X,b)=P\), with every original Boolean placeholder retained. The sums run over \(n^m(n+1)^{m^2-m}\) legal colourings; coincident monomials are counted with their actual parity. The statement supplies no polynomial-size bound for the whole sum.

Proof. Expanding each diagonal factor \((AB)_{ii}\) and each off-diagonal factor \(1+(AB)_{ij}\) gives exactly the legal colourings. The two incident counts bound every entry multiplicity by \(m\). Different coordinate operators commute, and act on a monomial by the product of their univariate actions. For \(k\ge1\), \(L_jX_j^k=X_j\); for \(k=0\), it is one. Thus the action is the displayed \(V_k\). The geometric sum gives \((X^2+X)h_k=X^k+X\) for \(k\ge2\), with both sides zero at \(k=0,1\) after using the corresponding \(V_k\). Consequently \(X^k+V_k=s(X^2+X)h_k\). Telescoping the difference between the two coordinate products, transforming coordinates in increasing order, gives exactly the marked sum after \(Z=b\). On every Boolean point, \(P=0\): otherwise all \(F_{ij}=0\) and \(AB=I_m\), impossible since \(\operatorname{rank}(AB)\le n<m\). At \(s=1\), \(\Gamma_1=\prod L_j\) is multilinear interpolation. A multilinear polynomial zero on the whole Boolean cube is zero, by induction on its variables; hence \(\Gamma_1P=0\). This proves the endpoint. The marked polynomial is linear in \(Z\), not merely zero on its Boolean values.

Interaction retained. Coordinate interpolation is not multiplicative. For one coordinate, put \(\lambda f=f(1)+f(0)\), \(T_s=I+sD\). Direct expansion using \(Lf=f(0)+X\lambda f\), and characteristic two, gives

\[T_s(fg)=T_s(f)T_s(g)+s(X^2+X)(\lambda f)(\lambda g)+s(1+s)(Df)(Dg).\]

Indeed \(L(fg)+(Lf)(Lg)=(X^2+X)\lambda f\lambda g\); substituting \(f=Lf+Df\), \(g=Lg+Dg\) yields the identity. Thus applying interpolation separately to literal bilinear factors loses interaction terms. The legal-colouring formula retains common row/column counts from the same colouring, rather than selecting their moments independently. It needs no rank-one minor identity or assertion that mixed inner indices vanish.

Rank moment table

Statement and proof. For one row's \(n\) positive-colour counts \(k=(k_1,\ldots,k_n)\), every integer vector \(1\le\sum k_\ell\le m\) occurs in a legal colouring. Choose any positive-count colour on the diagonal, distribute the other occurrences in the remaining cells, and fix other rows' diagonals to a positive colour. Its moment is \(R_k=\prod_\ell V_{k_\ell}(X_\ell,s)\). These \(R_k\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_2(s)\), so the table's dimension is

\[D_{m,n}=\binom{m+n}{n}-1.\]

Weak compositions into the \(n\) positive channels and the zero channel give this number, with the all-zero positive vector removed. Each \(R_k\) has unique highest-total-degree monomial \(X^k\), with nonzero coefficient \((1+s)^{\#\{\ell:k_\ell>1\}}\). Every other term has smaller total degree; terms with \(k_\ell=1\) are exactly \(X_\ell\). In a nonzero linear combination, select the largest degree of a participating \(k\); its distinct leading monomial cannot be cancelled by terms of equal or smaller degree. This proves independence. At \(m=2n\), \(D_{m,n}\ge3^n-1\), since \(\binom{3n}{n}=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+2n/i)\ge3^n\). This is a lower bound on linear registers representing all these row polynomials by fixed linear combination, or on explicitly storing every table entry. It is not a circuit-size lower bound and does not require any algorithm to use such a table.

Controls. At \(s=0\), the same independent row table consists of monomials, while the complete product \(P\) already has a small circuit. At \(s=1\), row moments depend only on nonempty positive support; all \(2^n-1\) supports occur because \(m>n\), and their squarefree monomials are independent. The complete product nevertheless interpolates to zero. Neither raw local dimension nor a zero global remainder bounds the size of the marked correction. This is an ordinary polynomial-vector-space count, not a rank in the paid quotient of the Pang route.

Rank color query

Statement. Encode each integer cell colour in \(b=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil\) Boolean index bits \(\theta_{ijr}\). Independent index bits are new query inputs, not functions of old variables. There is a division-free unbounded-fanin arithmetic circuit over \(\mathbb F_2\), with binary constants, of size \(O(m^3n)\), counting gates and wires, depth at most twelve, outputting that legal colouring's \(\prod_jV_{k_j}\) and its full marked contribution in \(Q_s\). The interpretation holds on legal Boolean indices; the local polynomial identities used to build it hold formally. No integer colour greater than one is treated as a field constant.

Construction. For each integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), form its equality selector \(\eta_{ij,\ell}=\prod_r(1+\theta_{ijr}+\operatorname{bit}_r(\ell))\). On Boolean indices it is one precisely for that colour, including correct rejection of encodings larger than \(n\). For an old coordinate \(X_j=A_{i\ell}\) or \(B_{\ell i}\), collect its \(m\) incident selectors \(\eta_{jt}\), in a fixed order. Set

\[f_{jt}(X_j)=1+\eta_{jt}(1+X_j),\quad a_{jt}=1+\eta_{jt},\quad U_j=\prod_t f_{jt},\quad O_j=\prod_t a_{jt},\] \[V_j=(1+s)U_j+s((1+X_j)O_j+X_j),\] \[H_j=\sum_{1\le\ell<t\le m}\eta_{j\ell}\eta_{jt}\prod_{r<\ell}f_{jr}(X_j)\prod_{r>t}a_{jr}.\]

The outputs are

\[G_\theta=\prod_jV_j,\qquad \mathcal Q_\theta=\sum_j sZ_jH_j\prod_{r<j}V_r\prod_{r>j}U_r.\]

Formal local proof. For arbitrary formal selectors, \(U_j(0)=O_j\), \(U_j(1)=1\). Telescoping \(U_j(X)+U_j(0)\) gives the polynomial first divided difference

\[\frac{U_j(X)+U_j(0)}X=\sum_t\eta_{jt}\prod_{r<t}f_{jr}(X)\prod_{r>t}a_{jr}.\]

In each prefix, a second telescoping at one has quotient \(\sum_{\ell<t}\eta_{j\ell}\prod_{r<\ell}f_{jr}(X)\), since every later prefix factor evaluates to one. The second divided difference of the displayed first difference is therefore \(H_j\), proving \(U_j+L_jU_j=b_jH_j\). Both quotient signs here denote proved polynomial identities, not division gates. It follows that \(U_j+V_j=sb_jH_j\), and product telescoping proves \(\mathcal Q_\theta(X,b)=\prod_jU_j+G_\theta\). On a legal Boolean colouring \(U_j=X_j^{k_j}\), hence \(V_j=V_{k_j}\) and the local cofactor equals \(h_{k_j}\) by the monic polynomial identity. These are precisely its contributions in the expansion.

Cost. All cell selectors use \(O(m^2nb)\) wires and depth two. The \(f\) gates have depth four. Construct every coordinate prefix of \(f\) and suffix of \(a\) by separate unbounded products, costing \(O(m^2)\) wires per coordinate. Each pair term uses two selectors and those shared products, followed by one sum: \(H_j\) has depth at most seven and \(O(m^2)\) size. The \(V_j\) have depth at most eight. All global prefixes of \(V\) and suffixes of \(U\) cost \(O(v^2)\) wires at depth nine; the marked products and output sum have depth at most eleven. Thus twelve is conservative. Total size is \(O(m^2nb+vm^2+v^2)=O(m^3n)\), since \(v=2mn\), \(n<m\) and \(b\le m\). No succinct power gates or conversion of counts into binary integers is used. Grafting an old-variable-dependent choice of indices would not commute with interpolation; no such adaptive substitution is asserted.
