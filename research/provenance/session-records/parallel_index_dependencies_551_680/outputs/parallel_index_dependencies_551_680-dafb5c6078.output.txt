    \dim_K\mathcal L_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom mj\ell^j,
    \qquad \deg p\le k\quad(p\in\mathcal L_k).
  \]

    Full proof.  Index a monomial by \(0\le j\le k\), a \(j\)-element subset \(S\) of the rows, and a function \(a:S\to[\ell]\). Conversely every allowed monomial has exactly this description. Ordinary monomials form a coefficient basis, so the allowed exponent subset gives a basis of \(\mathcal L_k\).

### 669 lem:affine-system-linear-algebra
lean-affine-system-linear-algebra  Proper spans, zero-flat witnesses and linear parts 
    Claim  lem:affine-system-linear-algebra .  Let \(S\) be a subspace of the coefficient space just defined, and let \(u\) denote the constant unit form. If \(u\notin S\), there exists \(x_0\in K^n\) with \(E_a(x_0)=0\) for every \(a\in S\). If such an \(x_0\) exists and an affine form \(f\) vanishes at every common zero of \(S\), then \(f\in S\). 
   Consequently, for a finite input family \((g_i)\), a common zero together with vanishing of \(f\) on the common zero set gives constants \((c_i)\) such that 
   \[
    f=\sum_i c_i g_i.
  \] 
   If the input family has no common zero, there instead exist constants with \(u=\sum_i c_i g_i\). These are equalities of forms, hence literal equalities of their ordinary polynomials under the linear map above. 
   For a proper span \(S\), projection to linear coefficients is injective on \(S\). It therefore preserves the dimension of \(S\), and sends every independent family spanning a proper subspace to an independent family of linear parts. Every nonzero \(a\in S\) has \(\deg P_a=1\). 
    Full proof by finite-coordinate duality.  For a linear functional \(L\) on the affine coefficient space, write \(t=L(u)\) and \(v_i=L(u_i)\), where \(u_i\) is the unit vector at the \(i\)-th linear coordinate. Expanding any coefficient vector in the coordinate basis gives 
   \[
    L(a)=a_0t+\sum_i a_iv_i.
  \] 
   The vector-space annihilator theorem says that an element lies in a subspace exactly when every functional vanishing on the subspace kills that element. If \(u\notin S\), choose such a functional \(L\) with \(t\ne0\). Set \((x_0)_i=v_i/t\). The displayed identity gives \(E_a(x_0)=L(a)/t=0\) for every \(a\in S\), proving consistency of a proper span. 
   Next fix a common zero \(x_0\), and suppose \(f\) vanishes at all common zeros. Let \(L\) be any functional annihilating \(S\); its constant value \(t\) may now be zero. Put \(y_i=v_i+(1-t)(x_0)_i\). Direct expansion yields, for every form \(a\), 
   \[
    E_a(y)=L(a)+(1-t)E_a(x_0).
  \] 
   For \(a\in S\), both terms on the right vanish. Thus \(y\) is another common zero, so \(E_f(y)=E_f(x_0)=0\), and the identity forces \(L(f)=0\). Since every annihilator kills \(f\), the same vector-space theorem gives \(f\in S\). This argument avoids dividing by \(t\) in the vanishing step and therefore includes direction functionals with \(t=0\). 
   For a finite family, membership in its span is equivalent to a finite constant linear combination, giving the first certificate.
