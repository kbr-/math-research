Exact levels for one common annihilator

Proposition (flat shadow limits). Let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal over any field containing \(x_i^2-x_i,y_i^2-y_i,x_iy_i\) for \(1\le i\le m\). Let an error tuple \(E\) have degree at most \(e\), with \(e+K\le D\). Suppose a space \(L\) of affine polynomials annihilates every component of \(E\) in \(J_D\), and \(1\notin L\). Put \(r=\dim L\), \(c(k)=\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_k\mapsto([E_i f])_i)\), and \[ A_{m,r}(j)=[z^j](1+2z)^{(m-r)_+}(1+z)^{\min(r,2m-r)}. \] For a degree-compatible monomial order, the actual error standard family has degree-\(j\) size \(c(j)-c(j-1)\le A_{m,r}(j)\), for \(1\le j\le K\). If \(1\in L\), all the allowed error images are zero instead.

For \(1\le\delta\le K\), suppose one uses these displayed caps to certify the intrinsic-shadow condition \(A_{m,r}(j)\le\binom Xj\) throughout \(K-\delta<j\le K\), with \(X\ge K\). In the charged range \(K<m/4\), if \(r\le m\), this certificate cannot yield the sufficient harmonic condition \(\rho=(X-K+\delta)/(K-\delta+1)\le1+1/\delta\). For \(r>m\), writing \(q=2m-r\), if \(q\ge K\) there exists a certifying \(X\ge K\) satisfying the harmonic condition exactly when \[ q\le 2K-\delta+1+\frac{K-\delta+1}{\delta}. \tag{1} \] If \(q<K\), choose \(X=K\) to majorize the caps, but the harmonic condition must still be checked with that \(X\); emptiness of the degree-\(K\) level alone is not the displayed ratio condition.

Proof of the level bound. Write \(Z_k=\{f\in V_k:E_if\in J_D\text{ for all }i\}\). Bounded absorption gives \(Z_k=Z_K\cap V_k\), multiplication closure through \(K\), and the squarefree downward-closed standard family used by the shadow corollary. Since \(1\notin L\), its linear parts are independent. Echelon elimination in the variable order gives \(r\) different leading pivot variables. Each pivot, and each of its multiples through degree \(K\), lies in the initial subspace of \(Z_K\): the affine relation annihilates \(E\), and its subsequent products fit \(e+K\le D\). Boolean squares are excluded, as are products \(x_i y_i\) for pairs with neither variable pivot. This assertion only uses a quadratic relation at levels at least two.

If \(p_a\) pairs have \(a\) pivots, the remaining standard monomials at level \(j\) are therefore at most \([z^j](1+2z)^{p_0}(1+z)^{p_1}\), with \(p_1+2p_2=r\). Replacing one untouched and one double-pivot pair by two single-pivot pairs increases every coefficient, since \((1+z)^2-(1+2z)=z^2\). Repeating yields \(A_{m,r}(j)\). Additional old or error relations can only decrease the actual standard family. The degree-compatible order identifies its exact levels with the increments of \(c\). This proves the refinement of the cumulative flat-image bound.

Proof of the threshold. For \(r\le m\), the counting polynomial coefficientwise dominates \((1+z)^m\). Since \(K\ge1\), its degree-\(K\) cap is at least \(\binom mK\), so any \(X\ge K\) certifying that cap must have \(X\ge m\). On the other hand, rearranging \(\rho\le1+1/\delta\) gives \[ X\le 2K-\delta+1+(K-\delta+1)/\delta\le3K-1. \] This contradicts \(m>4K\). For \(r>m\), the counting polynomial is \((1+z)^q\). If \(q\ge K\), the degree-\(K\) test forces \(X\ge q\), and \(X=q\) suffices at all levels, proving (1). If \(q<K\), all coefficients are bounded by those of \((1+z)^K\); the separate statement follows. \(\square\)

Why separate flat levels cannot be added

Here is an all-dimensional calibration included in the proposition's scope. In the full Boolean function algebra on \(n\ge1\) variables over \(\mathbb F_2\), set \(P_0=1+x_1\), \(P_1=x_1\), and \(E=P_0+P_1=1\). Use the input-degree filtration for every multiplication image. Each component has cumulative rank \(\sum_{j\le k}\binom{n-1}{j}\), whereas \(E\) has cumulative rank \(\sum_{j\le k}\binom nj\). Indeed the component restricts functions to one coordinate hyperplane, where the remaining squarefree monomials form a basis; the sum is the identity on all Boolean functions. At degree \(n\), the component increments are both zero, while the increment for \(E\) is one. Thus summing valid individual exact-level bounds does not give a valid exact-level bound for their sum. Take \(D\ge n+1\) and \(J_D\) the truncation of the full Boolean ideal if a finite paid polynomial window is desired.

This is the loss of strictness of the input-degree filtration: the two restricted functions can each have low-degree representatives without admitting one simultaneous representative of that degree. It does not contradict rank subadditivity for cumulative images. It is consistent with the older return-rank control, whose full proof was reread; no new general filtered-additivity theorem is claimed. The calibration is a satisfiable local algebra, not a hard instance. Both features are legal accuracy-one bottom products with constant coefficient one, for inputs \(x_1\) and \(1+x_1\); their bottom companions vanish modulo Booleanity. A parent input equal to their sum is the constant one and has a perfect legal row. The example therefore rejects a grading shortcut, not an existential source normalization.
