General statement. lem:factor-span-domination: errors that lie, modulo a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal and inside the window, in the span of products by a fixed set of factors have a stacked map that factors through the factor maps' stacked map, so their stacked rank is at most the factors' stacked rank, however many errors there are; transported killers are its instance for decompositions along linear axioms.

Factor span domination

Statement. Over a field, let \(C\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal (a subspace of the polynomials of degree at most \(D\) closed under multiplying members of degree below \(D\) by variables), \(V_K\) the polynomials of degree at most \(K\), \(Q=V_K/(C\cap V_K)\), and \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) polynomials. Let errors satisfy \(E_\nu=\sum_kc_{\nu k}v_k+m_\nu\) for \(\nu=1,\ldots,S\), with \(m_\nu\in C\), \(\deg m_\nu+K\le D\) and \(\deg c_{\nu k}+\deg v_k+K\le D\) for every term. Write \(T_v:Q\to V_D/C\), \([u]\mapsto[vu]\), for any \(v\) with \(\deg v+K\le D\). Then (a) \(\bigcap_k\ker T_{v_k}\subseteq\bigcap_\nu\ker T_{E_\nu}\); (b) the stacked map \((T_{E_\nu})_\nu\) factors linearly through \((T_{v_k})_k\), and \(\operatorname{rank}(T_{E_\nu})_\nu\le\operatorname{rank}(T_{v_k})_k\le\min\bigl(\dim Q,\sum_k\operatorname{rank}T_{v_k}\bigr)\), whatever \(S\) is. Part (b) uses only the conclusion of (a).

Proof. Each \(T_v\) is well defined, since a member of \(C\cap V_K\) times \(v\), multiplied variable by variable through degrees below \(\deg v+K\le D\), stays in \(C\); the error degrees satisfy \(\deg E_\nu+K\le D\) by the term bounds. (a) If \(v_ku\in C\) for all \(k\), each \(c_{\nu k}v_ku\) is a member multiplied through degrees below \(\deg c_{\nu k}+\deg v_k+K\le D\), and \(m_\nu u\in C\); so \(E_\nu u\in C\). (b) By (a) the kernel of the stacked factor map lies in that of the stacked error map; for linear maps on one space, kernel containment gives a linear factorization and bounds the rank of the second by that of the first.

Transported killers. The reviewer's control of the constructed-kernels entry, monomial axioms \(ab\), \(bz\) and the linear axiom \(\ell=w+a+z\) at \(D=2\), is, for the decomposition along \(\ell\), the case \(E=w\), \(v=(a,z)\), \(w=\ell-a-z\): \(b\in\ker T_a\cap\ker T_z\subseteq\ker T_w\). For several errors transported from the same \(r\) factors, the stacked rank is bounded by the stacked rank of the \(r\) factor maps, not by the sum over the errors. Every \(E\) also has the trivial decomposition \(v=(E)\), so the lemma is a statement about a chosen decomposition, not a characterization of killers.

Relations. The lemma generalizes the first sentence of lem:factor-ideal-domination (b), the containment of \(\bigcap_i\operatorname{Ann}_K(w_i)\) in the common kernel “directly, with no \(s\)”, from single products \(h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}\) to spans; it neither contains nor needs part (a) of that lemma, the principal generator. The single-input killers of lem:two-input-kernel-enlargement are its one-factor case. Its shape is that of lem:joint-word-shortening (a joint space bounded by a sum over generators).

What it gives at Pang's parameters. In Pang's setting, as the calibration entry states it, the error maps are \(u\mapsto([P_\nu g_{\nu,i}u])_i\) with \(P_\nu=\prod_{j=1}^t(1-q_{\nu,j}^{p-1})\), \(q_{\nu,j}\) in the span of the family's inputs of degree at most \(d\), so \(\deg P_\nu\le(p-1)dt=\Delta\); lem:two-input-kernel-enlargement is its two-input instance at \(t=1\). They always lie in the span of products by their inputs: take \(v\) the inputs, \(c=P_\nu\), so the term bound is \(\Delta+d+K\le D\), the calibration's own window (cor:pang-observation-calibration). So the product is no obstruction. But the bound is below the trivial \(\dim Q\) exactly when the factors have a nonzero common killer in \(Q\), and such a killer is already a surviving multiplier for every error; for survival the lemma is therefore no stronger than picking a common killer of the factors directly. And the bound does not involve \(P_\nu\) or \(t\), so it cannot reproduce or improve the contraction \((1-\eta)^t\). Families sharing inputs shrink \(r\), and help only if the shared inputs have a common killer, when the direct pick already works.

The open statement's wording. The factorization in (b) is domination by one coarser map, the stacked factor map. The goal review's restated exclusion names only the annihilator of one element; the first sentence of its own lem:factor-ideal-domination (b) already shows the gap for single products, and this lemma for spans. The next review should either widen the exclusion back to every factorization through one coarser map, or accept such savings, recording that by the paragraph above they add nothing to survival beyond a direct common killer.

Degenerate cases. \(r=0\): every error lies in \(C\) within the window, and both sides of (b) are zero. \(r=S\) with \(v_\nu=E_\nu\), \(c=1\): (b) is an equality. A factor in \(C\) has zero map and drops out. A term with \(\deg c_{\nu k}+\deg v_k+K>D\): the lemma says nothing.

New mathematics. lem:factor-span-domination: over any degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, errors in the span of products by fixed factors have a stacked map factoring through the factor maps' stacked map; transported killers are an instance, and for survival the bound is no stronger than a direct common killer of the factors.

Suggestion checked. The mixed bound's inequality holds: the stacked map of all errors is the stack of the map for group \(A\) and the map for group \(B\), the rank of a stack is at most the sum of the ranks of its blocks, the block for \(A\) has rank at most \(\sum_{\nu\in A}\operatorname{rank}T_{E_\nu}\) by the same subadditivity, and the block for \(B\) has rank at most that of the factor maps of \(B\) by (b).
