Observation horizon closure

Let \(V_b=\mathbb F[x]_{\le b}\), \(0\le b\le D\), and let \(\Lambda:V_D\to W\) be any linear map to a finite-dimensional space, with \(\Lambda(1)\ne0\). It may collect several scalar outputs. Define \[ N_b=\{p\in V_b:\Lambda(pq)=0\text{ for every }q\in V_{D-b}\}, \qquad S_b=V_b/N_b. \] Its observation matrix has columns indexed by a basis of \(V_b\), and rows by suffix monomials of degree at most \(D-b\) together with output coordinates. Thus \(\dim S_b\) is its rank.

Lemma (observation horizon closure). Inclusion and multiplication by \(x_i\) induce linear maps \(S_b\to S_{b+1}\). The terminal kernel \(I=N_D=\ker\Lambda\) is closed under every legal variable multiplication through degree \(D\) if and only if \[ N_b=I\cap V_b\quad(0\le b\le D). \tag{Horizon} \] Equivalently every inclusion-induced map \(S_b\to S_{b+1}\) is injective. If these conditions hold and every source generator of degree at most \(D\) belongs to \(\ker\Lambda\), then \(I\) is a proper source PC pseudoideal. Conversely every proper degree-\(D\) PC pseudoideal is obtained this way by taking the quotient map as \(\Lambda\).

Proof. If \(p\in N_b\), then it pairs to zero with every shorter suffix, proving \(p\in N_{b+1}\). Also \[ \Lambda((x_i p)q)=\Lambda(p(x_iq))=0 \quad(\deg q\le D-b-1), \] so multiplication is well-defined. Products commute when both two-step transitions are paid, by the literal polynomial identity. No same-space extension beyond the horizon is inferred.

The empty suffix gives \(N_b\subseteq I\cap V_b\). If \(I\) is PC-closed and \(p\in I\cap V_b\), multiply successively by each suffix monomial through total degree \(D\); hence \(p\in N_b\). Conversely, under (Horizon), any nonzero \(p\in I\) of degree \(b<D\) lies in \(N_b\), so \(x_ip\in N_{b+1}\subseteq I\). This is closure for the actual degree, including polynomials obtained by cancellation.

The kernel of the inclusion-induced map is \((N_{b+1}\cap V_b)/N_b\). Its vanishing at each adjacent pair gives (Horizon) by iteration to \(D\); the converse follows immediately. Finally \(\Lambda(1)\ne0\) makes \(I\) proper, and generator inclusion plus the proved closure supports every PC inference. Given a proper pseudoideal, its quotient map kills \(pq\) for all allowed suffixes whenever \(p\in I\), while the empty suffix detects every class outside it; thus its horizons recover \(I\cap V_b\). \(\square\)

This is a bookkeeping criterion, not a construction of \(\Lambda\) or a small output dimension. It exposes the exact cancellation/reuse condition that a finite matrix representation alone omits. A proof-local construction need only preserve the operations actually used by its proof; demanding the full pseudoideal remains a stronger sufficient endpoint.

Normalized Hankel rank

Proposition. For every \(n\ge3\), define a binary functional on the Boolean algebra by \[ \lambda(p)=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}^n\setminus\{0\}}p(a)\in\mathbb F_2. \] It is normalized. For \(D=n\), its finite-horizon ranks on squarefree monomial bases are \[ r_0=r_n=1,\qquad r_b=1+\binom nb\quad(1\le b\le n-1). \] Nevertheless the natural map \(S_b\to S_{b+1}\) kills every degree-\(b\) squarefree monomial state for \(1\le b\le n-2\). The full Boolean moment matrix has rank \(2^n-1\), and the word series \(\lambda(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_t})\) has minimum realization dimension over \(\mathbb F_2\) exactly \(2^n-1\).

Proof. For a squarefree support \(A\), \[ \lambda(x_A)= \begin{cases}1,&A=\varnothing\text{ or }A=[n],\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \] The empty support gives \(2^n-1=1\) in the field; a nonempty proper support has \(2^{n-|A|}\) satisfying assignments, all nonzero, an even number. The full support has one.

For \(1\le b\le n-1\), the finite-horizon matrix has an isolated constant entry one. All remaining nonzero entries pair a size-\(b\) prefix support with its size-\((n-b)\) complement: any smaller total support has moment zero, and at total size \(n\) union equals \([n]\) only for complementary disjoint supports. That block is a permutation matrix of size \(\binom nb\), proving the rank formula. A degree-\(b\) monomial is detected by its complement at horizon \(b\); at horizon \(b+1\), every suffix has degree at most \(n-b-1\), so its union has size below \(n\) and its moment is zero. Thus the same low-degree polynomial is lost. Ordinary repeated-variable monomials give the same ranks because Boolean reduction preserves degree and the functional kills all Boolean multiples.

For the full matrix, let \(Z\) be the square monomial-evaluation matrix on all Boolean points. It is the invertible subset-incidence matrix. Then \[ H=Z\,\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\ldots,1)\,Z^{\mathsf T}, \] where the zero weight belongs to the origin, so its rank is \(2^n-1\). Any \(r\)-state word realization factors its prefix/suffix matrix through an \(r\)-dimensional space; the displayed finite submatrix forces \(r\ge2^n-1\). Conversely use one diagonal state for each nonzero point, transitions given by its coordinate bits, and initial/final weights one. This realizes the word series with exactly that many states. \(\square\)

There is a short rule for every moment—test whether its support is empty or full—and the weight function is \(1+\prod_i(1+x_i)\). Neither short description gives a small continuation rank. At \(n=4\), ranks increase from five to seven while the inclusion map already kills all four singleton states: nondecreasing numerical rank alone does not establish (Horizon). The scalar terminal kernel kills \(x_1\), but multiplication by the remaining coordinates yields a monomial with moment one. It is not PC-closed.

This functional is supported on genuine Boolean points, so it is not a hard-system design or an obstruction to selecting a different, much smaller realization. A point evaluation is a one-state alternative on the same Boolean-only base. If desired it extends a complete constant-input block by fixing its active coefficient to one; unused coefficients may carry this law. That shows compatibility with a complete local source, but does not force the bad law or prove a source lower bound. The distinction between one chosen realization and all permitted choices is essential.
