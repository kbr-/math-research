Deflate the aggregate, not the proof

Lemma (deflated error transfer). Let \(E_a\) be a finite list of binary old polynomials, of degree at most \(e\), with \(K+e\le D\). Let \(F_a:\Omega\to\{0,1\}\) be their binary evaluation, represented by those integer bits. Suppose each has a finite rational pointwise expansion \[ F_a=\sum_\nu\lambda_{a,\nu}N_{a,\nu}, \tag{2} \] where \(N_{a,\nu}\) are integer-valued functions (in particular the Boolean narrow tests in the marker expansion). Choose any \(v\ge0\) such that \(c_{a,\nu}=2^v\lambda_{a,\nu}\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\) for all terms. Set \(s=v+1\), reduce these odd-denominator coefficients into \(A_s\), and form the stacked matrix \[ R_{a,j}=\sum_\nu c_{a,\nu}\, \mathscr C_{s,D}(N_{a,\nu}\widetilde f_j). \tag{3} \] Then every entry lies in \(2^vQ_D^{(v+1)}\). Under the canonical additive identification \[ \operatorname{def}_v:2^vQ_D^{(v+1)}\longrightarrow Q_D^{(1)}, \qquad 2^v q\longmapsto q\bmod2, \tag{4} \] its image is precisely \(M_{a,j}=[E_af_j]\), the original binary error matrix extended to \(\mathbb F_4\). There is no \(s\) factor in the condition \(K+e\le D\).

Proof. Let \(\widetilde E_a\) be the ordinary coefficient-zero/one lift of the binary polynomial. On every label assignment, its integer value has parity \(F_a\), although it need not itself be a bit. Hence \[ 2^vF_a\widetilde f_j \equiv 2^v\widetilde E_a\widetilde f_j\pmod{2^{v+1}} \tag{5} \] pointwise. Equation (2), after clearing only the even denominators, is an exact identity over \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\). Linearity of (1) therefore identifies (3) with the compression of the left side of (5). By (5) it equals the compression of the right side. The polynomial there, before its scalar factor, has degree at most \(e+K\le D\), so the preceding Fourier argument gives \[ R_{a,j}=2^v[\widetilde E_a\widetilde f_j]_{Q_D^{(v+1)}}. \tag{6} \] A free \(A_{v+1}\)-module has the identification (4): two possible quotients of a given multiple differ by an element killed by \(2^v\), which has even coefficients. For \(v=0\) this is the identity. Reducing (6) gives exactly the claimed matrix. This proves divisibility, well-defined deflation and the degree assertion. No summand of (3) is required to have low polynomial degree, and no multiplication in the truncated quotient was used. \(\square\)

The integer values of the \(N_{a,\nu}\) may be replaced by elements of \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}[\zeta]\) with the same argument, but (2) only needs the literal rational marker expansion here. No independent theorem about that extension is needed. A finite expansion always admits some common \(v\). Large \(v\) can make writing or computing (3) expensive; this is not a polynomial-time construction or a small ring-PC proof. For a mathematical rank argument its precision does not itself consume old polynomial degree.
