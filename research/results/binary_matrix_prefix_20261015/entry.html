Binary matrix flags

Lemma. Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_r\in\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb F_2)\), \(r\ge1\). Let \(\mathcal W\) contain the empty word and all words of lengths one through three in the letters \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\). Add a zero symbol, and enumerate all ordered four-tuples from \(\mathcal W\cup\{0\}\), with repetitions. Each tuple defines a polynomial \(T_\ell\) equal to the sum of its four entries. Put \(H=(2+r+r^2+r^3)^4\), \(B_\ell=T_\ell^2+T_\ell\). Then every matrix \((I+B_\ell(X))^{10}\) is either zero or I. If all of them equal I, the old matrices have a common invariant binary line, hence are simultaneously upper triangular in some binary basis. The word and sum bounds are absolute, not dependent on r or the generated algebra.

Proof. For any two-by-two binary matrix T, write \(\tau=\operatorname{tr}T\), \(\delta=\det T\). Cayley–Hamilton gives \(T^2=\tau T+\delta I\). If \(\tau=1\), then \(B=T^2+T=\delta I\). If \(\tau=0\), then \(B=T+\delta I\), and \(B^2=T^2+\delta^2I=0\). Thus \(B^2\) is zero or I, with \(B^2=I\) exactly when \(T^2+T=I\). In particular \((I+B)^{10}=((I+B)^2)^5=I+B^2\), a central binary flag. A single five-line excluded-middle prefix is \((I+B)^5\); its square is this flag.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the unital algebra generated by the matrices. Words of length at most three span it. Indeed let \(V_j\) be the span of words of length at most j, starting with \(V_0=\langle I\rangle\). If \(V_{j+1}=V_j\), the space is stable under right multiplication by every generator, so contains every word. Otherwise dimension increases. Ambient dimension is four, so by j=3 either full dimension has been reached or an earlier stabilization has occurred. Choose a basis of at most four of these words. Every algebra element is the sum of at most four basis words over \(\mathbb F_2\), padding by the zero symbol when necessary. Thus the displayed list of \(T_\ell(X)\)'s covers every element of \(\mathcal A\).

If the action of \(\mathcal A\) on \(V=\mathbb F_2^2\) is irreducible, its commuting endomorphism division ring K has binary dimension dividing two, since V is a vector space over its opposite ring. If that dimension is one, finite-dimensional density gives \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb F_2)\). If it is two, V has dimension one over K, and density gives the four-element division algebra; any nontrivial element of its unit group of order three satisfies \(T^2+T+I=0\). In the first case the explicit matrix \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\) has the same property. For the second case no finite-division-ring classification is required: a nonidentity element of a group of order three has order three, and its difference from I is invertible, so \((T-I)(T^2+T+I)=0\) gives that identity. The density statement and opposite-ring convention are exactly those proved/read in the finite-algebra record. Such an element is one of the \(T_\ell(X)\)'s and makes its flag zero. Consequently if all flags equal I the action is reducible. A proper nonzero subspace of a binary plane is a line; a basis starting on it triangularizes every generator. \(\square\)

Matrix vanishing and the binary evaluator

At a binary matrix tuple, every paired seed prefix is zero or I by lem:binary-matrix-flags. If any is zero, the exterior factor in (1) is zero. If none is zero, the old matrices have a common invariant line. In a triangularizing binary basis, each diagonal of P is evaluated at an ordinary Boolean assignment. The used clauses support a refutation, so at least one has falsity one at each diagonal assignment. Thus P is strictly upper triangular and \(P^2=0\). Since \(H\ge1\), \(P^{2H}=0\). This proves erasure for arbitrary original placeholder matrices, without restricting them to triangular matrices or axiom values.

Erasure alone does not make generic matrix evaluation cheap. Split the evaluator according to the paired flags. Before the first zero flag, every completed pair has prefix I; after it, every later contribution is zero. A pair's internal contributions can be evaluated by a fixed-size binary circuit on its at most twelve old matrix values and the original Boolean/commutator placeholders with indices among those variables. To justify this locality, every premise of a seed or bridge lies in that copy's own block. The preceding external prefix is kept on the left in both absorption terms. The tag proofs and bridge contributions are literal zero. In all remaining translated line/local-multiplier expressions, cancellation removes the even parity padding, and \(t(\neg\zeta_j)=1\) removes the inflation before certificate recursion. What remains is one of finitely many bounded four-word templates and its canonical certificate, independent of S,H,r and the padding lengths. There are only boundedly many leaves, possible label-equality patterns and factor orderings. In particular the padding variable supplies no extra nonzero generator. The pair's local formula is homogeneous placeholder-linear, retaining arbitrary left and right matrix factors around its placeholder. Fixed-dimensional binary arithmetic for this bounded formula is a constant-size Boolean circuit; it is not a generic unbounded matrix-product gate.

Compute all H flags from their bounded word-sum templates, and all pair-local values. Their prefix AND flags select precisely the pairs through and including the first zero. XOR of the selected four-coordinate values gives the whole seed segment on that branch. All input and later terms carry the zero seed prefix and vanish. Size is polynomial in H and original coordinate inputs; depth is absolute constant. No product of arbitrarily many general matrices is evaluated in this branch.

On the all-one flag branch, triangularize the old tuple by selection among the fixed binary invertible two-by-two basis matrices. For each candidate, test that every transformed old matrix has bottom-left entry zero, and choose the first passing candidate by constant-depth Boolean conjunctions. The lemma guarantees one. Conjugate every old and placeholder matrix by the same chosen basis. The placeholders remain arbitrary matrices. Now evaluate the known full A using the earlier triangular-path construction, with the placeholder block substitution \[ X_j\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}X_j&0\\0&X_j\end{pmatrix},\quad Y_i\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&Y_i\\0&0\end{pmatrix}. \] These four-dimensional leaves are upper triangular; the upper-right block extracts A because it is homogeneous placeholder-linear. Apply the complete entry expansion, not a scalar analogy. For formula size L and depth h, its bound is \((L+6)^8\), depth \(2h\), plus the paid entry circuits. Recover the two-by-two value and conjugate it back. Simultaneous conjugation commutes with the polynomial evaluation, including all placeholders. Conditional selection can evaluate all candidate branches and mask their outputs; no algorithmic basis search is hidden.

Here \(L=O(R^{576})\), \(h\le19\), giving \(O(R^{4608})\) gates/wires for each of a fixed number of candidates. Word/flag computation, seed-local values, first-zero/all-one selection, conjugation and four-bit XOR fit the stated \(O((R+v+1)^{5000})\) bound and depth sixty-four. Composing paid query entries and Boolean probes gives exactly the stated E,J,U and depth ledger. A matrix-valued or arithmetic outer operation is not silently a Boolean probe.
