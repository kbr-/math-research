Affine axioms can be substituted away

Lemma (affine free dimension). Let \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) contain the Boolean axioms and affine polynomials whose span \(\Lambda\) has dimension \(\varrho\) and does not contain \(1\). After Gaussian elimination write the zero set of \(\Lambda\) as \(z_i=\alpha_i(y)+c_i\), \(1\le i\le\varrho\), with \(y\) the remaining \(n=v-\varrho\) variables and \(\alpha_i\) linear. Let \(\sigma\) be the substitution homomorphism and \(\Gamma^\sigma=\sigma(\Gamma)\) together with the Boolean axioms of \(y\). Then for every \(D\ge2\) at least the degrees of the elements of \(\Gamma\):

  1. \(1\in C_D(\Gamma)\) if and only if \(1\in C_D(\Gamma^\sigma)\).
  2. For any further set \(E\) of polynomials, possibly in additional variables which \(\sigma\) fixes, a PC refutation of \(\Gamma\cup E\) of degree \(B\ge2\) gives one of \(\Gamma^\sigma\cup\sigma(E)\) of degree at most \(B\).

Proof. For 2 apply \(\sigma\) to every line. It is a ring homomorphism that does not raise degree. Elements of \(\Lambda\) go to zero. A Boolean axiom \(z_i^2-z_i\) goes to \((\alpha_i+c_i)^2-(\alpha_i+c_i)=\sum_{j}(y_j^2-y_j)\) over the support of \(\alpha_i\), in characteristic two, a combination of Boolean axioms of \(y\). A multiplication by \(z_i\) becomes multiplication by \(\alpha_i+c_i\), a combination of multiplications by variables and a scalar, at the same degree. Other axioms go to elements of \(\Gamma^\sigma\cup\sigma(E)\). This also gives the forward direction of 1. Conversely, for \(g\in\Gamma\) of degree \(e\le D\), \(g-\sigma(g)\) lies in the ideal of the polynomials \(z_i-\alpha_i-c_i\in\Lambda\) with cofactors of degree at most \(e-1\), replacing one \(z_i\) at a time; so \(\sigma(g)\in C_D(\Gamma)\). The Boolean axioms of \(y\) are in \(\Gamma\). Hence \(C_D(\Gamma^\sigma)\subseteq C_D(\Gamma)\). \(\square\)

The bound \(D\ge\) axiom degrees is used only in the converse of 1, and \(D,B\ge2\) only to admit the Boolean axioms of \(y\).

Consequence for the source theorem. The moving-cutoff theorem takes any polynomial set with the Boolean axioms. Apply it to \(\Gamma^\sigma\) on \(n\) variables: if \(1\notin C_D(\Gamma)\) for \(D=\lfloor n/2-s\rfloor\), \(s=o(n/(\log n)^{\ell-2})\), then by the converse direction of 1, which needs \(D\) at least the axiom degrees, the hypothesis holds for \(\Gamma^\sigma\). The theorem, with all its parameters taken in \(n\) (accuracy \(h=\lfloor n^\epsilon\rfloor\), at most \(n^A\) blocks, large \(n\)), says the augmented system over \(\Gamma^\sigma\) has no refutation through degree \(\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), and by 2 neither has the augmented system over \(\Gamma\) with the same blocks. Here \(\sigma\) of a block is the block on the substituted inputs: a complete binary ENS block consists of fresh coefficient variables, their domain equations and the formal product and companions, all polynomial expressions in the inputs and the fresh variables, as the main notebook's block definition states. Inputs keep their degree bound, and the product-only grammar is untouched. A bound of \(v^{A'}\) blocks is covered when \(v\le n^{C}\). So the first open statement may be read with \(n=v-\varrho\) in place of \(v\), provided accuracy, block count and proof degree are also expressed in \(n\); with \(v\le n^C\) this changes exponents by constant factors only, which the transfer statement must carry. The substituted system need not be a CNF or have bounded width; the theorem does not ask for that, but the Frege transfer statement does and is not touched here.

Relation to the record. The main notebook's residual-coordinates lemma is this elimination for label bits of one base (lem:residual-coordinates-linear-elimination, summary read; not relied on). The thin-kernel count gives the same normalization with a worse window: variables that are leading monomials of affine elements of \(C_D\) are not standard, so level \(j\) has at most \(\binom{n}{j}\) standard monomials. The substitution argument is stronger and makes the Kruskal–Katona route unnecessary for this case.

What it admits. Bounded-width systems in which most variables are parities of a small free set: after substitution the rows are bounded-arity relations on linear forms, hence wide in the free variables. The recorded row ceilings were proved for rows on their own variables and count every variable. For this class a standard monomial may be taken inside any basis of the free variables, and a row constrains it only through the basis variables it contains. Whether shared-pin, polarity or interpolation ceilings in the free dimension follow is not examined in this entry.

Point counts bound the thin endpoint from both sides

Lemma (point-count thinness). Let \(1\notin C_D(\Gamma)\) for an unsatisfiable Boolean system over a field with axioms of degree at most \(D\), \(D\ge2\), and let \(W\subseteq\{0,1\}^v\) have \(N\) points with \(I(W)\cap V_K\subseteq C_D\) for some \(K\le D\). Then the standard monomials of \(C_D\) of degree at most \(K\), for a degree-compatible order, number at most \(N\) and at least \(\sum_{i\le K}\binom{D-1}{i}\).

Proof. Standard monomials of degree at most \(K\) are linearly independent modulo \(C_D\cap V_K\supseteq I(W)\cap V_K\), so they are independent as functions on \(W\). For the lower bound, the kernel dimension theorem gives a set of \(D-1\) variables all of whose subsets are standard. \(\square\)

The upper bound uses neither unsatisfiability nor properness; the lower bound does not use \(W\).

So the thin hypothesis \(m_j\le\binom Xj\) holds for all \(j\) in the window as soon as \(N\le\binom{X}{K-\Delta+1}\), roughly \(\log_2N\le2K\), provided \(\binom Xj\) is non-decreasing on the window, that is \(X\ge2K-\Delta+1\), which holds once \(K\ge\Delta^2+\Delta-1\), and no \(W\) with \(\log_2N\) much below \(D\) exists. In these terms the thin endpoint asks for a barrier at half the number of bits of a relaxation whose low-degree vanishing ideal is derivable, where the barrier can never exceed that number of bits. For one-hot blocks the candidate count alone gives little: \(B\) blocks of \(b\ge3\) variables with exactly one true have at most \(\binom Bj(b-1)^j\) standard monomials at level \(j\), and this bound is below \(\binom{2j}{j}\) only for \(j\ge0.77B\) at \(b=3\), for \(j\ge0.92B\) at \(b=4\), and at no level for \(b\ge5\) (solve \(H_2(\kappa)+\kappa\log_2(b-1)\le2\kappa\)). It is an upper bound on the level sizes, so this does not exclude such encodings.

The attempted counterexample

Take a partition of the cube \(\{0,1\}^n\) into \(R_1,R_2\) with \(|R_2|\) odd, axioms all polynomials of degree at most \(D=n-1\) vanishing on \(R_1\) or on \(R_2\). Every decomposition \(1=f_1+f_2\) has \(f_1\) equal to the indicator of \(R_2\), of degree \(n\), so the static sum \(I(R_1)_{\le D}+I(R_2)_{\le D}\) omits \(1\). But this sum is \(\{f\in V_D:\sum_{\alpha\in R_2}f(\alpha)=0\}\), and it is closed under the legal multiplications if and only if for each variable \(x\) the indicator of \(R_2\cap\{x=1\}\) is a multiple of that of \(R_2\) plus an affine function, since functionals on \(V_{n-2}\) given by sums over sets are determined modulo affine indicators. An affine function supported in a face is zero or the face, so in each direction some face is contained in or disjoint from \(R_2\); for \(n\ge2\) this forces \(R_2\) or \(R_1\) to be a single point, and then the system is satisfiable. Otherwise the hyperplane is not closed and the closure is all of \(V_D\). So this construction fails definitively, as the reviewer completed the argument. It tests only \(D=n-1\). No unsatisfiable example with a proper closure above \(n/2+O(1)\) and axioms within the cutoff is recorded, and no general ceiling either; the proposed ceiling is neither refuted nor proved, also in the cube case.
