Rank support tail

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(n=2^a\), \(a\ge0\), \(m=2n\), and let \(A\) and \(B\) be matrices of independent entry variables of shapes \(m\times n\) and \(n\times m\). Put \[F_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(AB)_{ij},\qquad P=\prod_{i,j=1}^m(1+F_{ij}),\qquad v=2mn=4n^2.\] For every entry variable \(x_j\), write \(b_j=x_j^2+x_j\), and use exactly the recorded operators \[L_jf=(1+x_j)f|_{x_j=0}+x_jf|_{x_j=1},\quad D_j=I-L_j,\quad E_tf=\sum_{|T|=t}\left(\prod_{j\in T}D_j\right)f.\] If a finite list \(t_1,\ldots,t_q\in\{1,\ldots,v\}\) contains no \(4n\), then \[\left(\prod_{r=1}^q(I+E_{t_r})\right)P\ne0\] as a formal polynomial. The empty list is allowed. Consequently a complete annihilating homotopy of this mask form must include the exact order \(4n\); repeating fixed orders, or using larger different orders, does not replace it. This asserts no cost lower bound for computing that operator or the combined correction, and no restriction on arbitrary correction circuits.

Restrict the actual product. Let \(\rho\) set every entry of the inner columns/rows with index \(\ell>1\) to zero, and retain \(u_i=A_{i1}\), \(z_j=B_{1j}\). Then \[\rho(P)=P_{m,1}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^m u_i z_i\right)\prod_{i\ne j}(1+u_i z_j).\] This is the residual of the actual one-inner-column system, not a substituted arbitrary Boolean-zero polynomial. For an erased coordinate, \(\rho L_jf=\rho f\), hence \(\rho D_jf=0\). For a retained coordinate, its endpoint evaluations commute with \(\rho\). Thus \[\rho(E_t f)=E_t^{\rm retained}\rho(f),\] including \(t>2m\), where the right side is zero. Every mask sequence can therefore be tested after this restriction. No hardness preservation is assumed for \(\rho\); it is used only to prove formal nonvanishing of the original masked polynomial.

The surviving module term. In the free Boolean-module basis \(x^\epsilon b^\alpha\), with \(\epsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\), the polynomial \(P_{m,1}\) contains \[W=\prod_{i=1}^m (u_i^2+u_i)^n\prod_{j=1}^m(z_j^2+z_j)^n\] with coefficient one. To see this without circuit component extraction, its unique highest-total-degree ordinary monomial is \[\prod_i u_i^m\prod_j z_j^m,\] of degree \(2m^2\), with coefficient one: every bilinear factor must supply its highest-degree term. Monic division by the separate equations \(x_j^2+x_j=b_j\) does not increase weighted degree, giving weight two to each \(b_j\). Since \(m=2n\), the highest weighted-degree module term of each \(x_j^m\) is \(b_j^n\) with coefficient one. Its remainder has smaller weighted degree. Lower ordinary-degree terms of \(P_{m,1}\) cannot contribute \(W\), whose weighted degree is \(2m^2\). This proves the coefficient assertion. It is a proof witness in the unique free-module expansion, not an assumed cheap coefficient operation.

Apply the exact spectrum. The support of \(W\) consists of all \(2m=4n\) retained coordinates. The support-mask lemma makes \(E_t\) diagonal on this basis, with eigenvalue \(\binom{4n}{t}\bmod2\). Since \(4n\) is a power of two, \[(1+T)^{4n}=1+T^{4n}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[T].\] Thus this eigenvalue is zero for every positive \(t\ne4n\), including \(t>4n\), and one for \(t=4n\). Every allowed \(I+E_t\) leaves the coefficient of \(W\) equal to one; diagonality prevents other terms from changing it. The restricted masked polynomial is nonzero, hence so is the original one. This proves the statement.

Complete Boolean generators are retained. The shared-pivot theorem supplies a full formal certificate class at these unbounded parameters, without a fixed-depth guarantee. The explicit restricted term has positive support and coefficient one at every such \(n\). This is a control on actual members of the candidate family, not a replacement of that family by rank-one instances.

Degenerate cases. At \(n=1,m=2\), the restriction erases nothing and the required order is four, the number of variables. The instance already has a cheap correction. With an empty mask list the product is identity and the same witness proves \(P\ne0\). A zero residual would have no such term. \(n=0\) is excluded. For \(n\) not a power of two, the same top term exists but its binomial spectrum can have other nonzero orders, so the stated exact-order conclusion is not extended to those parameters. All assertions are formal, not modulo the Boolean ideal, where \(P\) itself is zero.
