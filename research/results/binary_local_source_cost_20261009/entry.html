Try a narrow/wide hybrid with one surviving weight

Retain a proper binary \(J_D\), \(D=\Theta(n)\), a surviving weight \(w\) of degree \(b\), and actual old input polynomials of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\). This is a fixed stage after earlier maps, not a change to original source degrees. Let \(I\) be the total number of actual tuple input positions at this stage. The intrinsic weighted nonlinear rank \(q_a\) in (5) of the local criterion is at most a tuple's number of input positions. For an integer threshold \(Q\ge1\), there are therefore at most \(I/(Q+1)\) tuples with \(q_a>Q\). This charges tuple incidences, not just the number of product objects.

There is an existing elementary way to process each exceptional tuple without fixing all of its features. If every current \(wg_{a,i}\) lies in \(J_D\), choose zero coefficient rows and keep the weight. Otherwise choose a coordinate for which \(wg_{a,i}\notin J_D\), replace the weight by \(wg_{a,i}\), select that input in one original row and zero-fill. The new product is \(1-g_{a,i}\); all new weighted companions vanish by the old Boolean identity \(g_{a,i}^2=g_{a,i}\). The added weight degree is at most \(\delta\). Through the complete sequence, a conservative sufficient membership buffer is final weight degree plus \(2\delta\); earlier identities are multiplied only when their final ceilings fit. This is the already used surviving-input clamp, iterated, not a new mechanism.

The resulting worst-case additional weight budget is at most \[ \delta\,\frac{I}{Q+1}. \tag{1} \] The narrow tuples stay narrow in the relevant sense: every original weighted affine-feature relation persists after multiplying the weight, if its final degree fits. Their previous dictionaries remain available; fresh compression could improve them, but no such improvement is assumed. Thus the two methods are compatible on one common weight. This resolves neither the size of (1) nor the narrow tuples' image cost.
