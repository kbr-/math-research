The exact fixed-cutoff test

Let \(B=F[x_1,\ldots,x_v]/(x_j^2-x_j)\), identified with multilinear polynomials and filtered by total degree. Write \(B_{\le d}\) for its degree filtration, with \(B_{\le-1}=0\). Let \(J_1,\ldots,J_m\) be ideals of \(B\); in the intended application each is the full vanishing ideal of the Boolean assignments satisfying a chosen joint member of constraints. Put

\[K_d=\sum_a(J_a\cap B_{\le d}),\qquad H_D=\frac{K_D\cap B_{\le D-1}}{K_{D-1}},\qquad Q_D=B_{\le D}/K_D.\tag{1}\]

All sums and intersections use the same ambient Boolean algebra. \(H_D\) records the lower-degree results of cancellations at the cutoff that cannot be represented by lower-degree pieces of the same members. It is not assumed zero.

Proposition (fixed cutoff transport). For \(D\ge1\), the maps

\[\Delta_j:H_D\longrightarrow Q_D,\qquad [q]\longmapsto[x_jq]\tag{2}\]

are well-defined, using multilinear multiplication. They all vanish if and only if \(K_D\) is closed under multiplication of each of its elements of degree at most \(D-1\) by each variable. If every active input polynomial belongs to \(K_D\), \(1\notin K_D\), and all maps (2) vanish, then the input system has no ordinary degree-\(D\) PC refutation.

Proof. Every element of \(K_{D-1}\) is a sum of \(p_a\in J_a\cap B_{\le D-1}\). Since \(J_a\) is an ideal, \(x_jp_a\in J_a\cap B_{\le D}\). Hence \(x_jK_{D-1}\subseteq K_D\), proving that (2) is independent of the representative. Vanishing is precisely the required closure condition on \(K_D\cap B_{\le D-1}\). An ordinary PC proof can be multilinearized line by line: input Boolean axioms become zero, addition stays addition, and a multiplication step with nonzero predecessor of ordinary degree at most \(D-1\) maps to its Boolean product. The zero predecessor is harmless. Induction puts every line of a degree-\(D\) refutation into \(K_D\), contradicting \(1\notin K_D\). This only uses the direction from ordinary to multilinear proofs; it makes no claim that Boolean reduction has zero original cost in the converse direction. \(\square\)

Lemma (top relation surjection). Let \(G_{a,D}\subseteq B_{\le D}/B_{\le D-1}\) be the degree-\(D\) leading parts of \(J_a\cap B_{\le D}\), and let \(R_D\) be the kernel of the summation map \(\bigoplus_aG_{a,D}\to B_{\le D}/B_{\le D-1}\). Choose linear lifts \(s_a:G_{a,D}\to J_a\cap B_{\le D}\). Then

\[r\longmapsto\left[\sum_a s_a(r_a)\right]\in H_D\tag{3}\]

is well-defined independently of the lifts and is surjective. Indeed, the sum has zero leading part, and changing a lift changes it by an element of \(K_{D-1}\). For any \(q=\sum_a p_a\in K_D\cap B_{\le D-1}\), the leading parts of the \(p_a\) form a relation, and the difference from its chosen lifts lies in \(K_{D-1}\). Thus the test can be performed on these explicit leading relations by composing (3) with (2). It does not require a module-freeness assertion or a prescribed generating list of Koszul relations.

Requiring \(H_d=0\) for every \(1\le d\le D\) is stronger. It implies \(K_D\cap B_{\le d}=K_d\) by repeated descent and hence all the maps (2) vanish. If every \(J_a\) is proper, \(K_0=0\); repeated descent also gives \(1\notin K_D\). This is the familiar no-fall route. At a single cutoff, by contrast, unit survival must be checked separately: transport alone holds even for \(K_D=B_{\le D}\).

A complete analytic separation

Proposition (cutoff transport separation). Over any field on three Boolean variables \(x,y,z\), let \(g=(1-z)(1-x)\), \(h=zy\), \(J_1=(g)\), \(J_2=(h)\). These are the vanishing ideals of the two allowed clause sets. The first ideal has basis \(g,yg\), and the second has basis \(h,xh\): multiplication by its fixed local coordinates is either zero or unchanged, while the one free coordinate has basis \(1,y\) or \(1,x\). Their nonzero supports are disjoint since they require different values of \(z\), so the four vectors are independent.

Consequently \(K_2=\operatorname{span}\{g,h\}\), while \(K_3=J_1+J_2\) is their whole sum ideal. The identity

\[ yg+(1-x)h=(1-x)y=:p \tag{4}\]

puts the degree-two \(p\) in \(K_3\), but not in \(K_2\): its \(xy\) coefficient is \(-1\), whereas both \(g,h\) have zero \(xy\) coefficient. The degree-three leading-part map on the four-dimensional \(K_3\) has rank one (the common \(xyz\) term), so \(H_3\) has dimension one. Nevertheless every \(\Delta_j\) vanishes because \(K_3\) is an ideal and \(B=B_{\le3}\). Also \(1\notin K_3\), for example by evaluation at \(x=1,z=0,y=0\). This proves that a nonzero fall space need not obstruct the fixed-cutoff invariant. It is a satisfiable method calibration, not a non-vacuity witness for an unsatisfiable hard specification.

Adding a free variable \(w\) changes the test. The new truncated ideal pieces have bases \(g,yg,wg\) and \(h,xh,wh\) through degree three. They still contain \(p\), but not \(wp\): its \(xyw\) coefficient is \(-1\), while that coefficient is zero on all six listed basis vectors. Thus multiplication by \(w\) has a nonzero defect. This is an exact symbolic calculation, with no numerical rank assumption.

Every earlier fall has a free-variable obstruction

Proposition (free variable obstruction). In any Boolean algebra \(B_0\) with ideals \(J_a\), suppose \(q\in K_{d+1}\cap(B_0)_{\le d}\) but \(q\notin K_d\), for some \(d\ge0\). Extend all ideals to a larger Boolean algebra by adjoining at least \(D-d\) free variables, where \(D\ge d+1\). Denote its corresponding sums by \(\widehat K_e\). Then \(\widehat K_D\) fails multiplication closure at degree \(D\).

Proof. Take \(D-d-1\) of the free variables and let \(W\) be their product (one if none), with one more free variable \(w\). Multiply a representation of \(q\) as a sum of pieces of degrees at most \(d+1\) by \(W\). This proves \(Wq\in\widehat K_D\), while \(\deg(Wq)\le D-1\). If \(wWq\in\widehat K_D\), extract the coefficient of the exact free-variable monomial \(wW\) from each ideal piece of such a representation. This coefficient belongs to the original \(J_a\) and has degree at most \(D-(D-d)=d\). Membership follows either from the tensor decomposition of the extended ideal or by evaluating the free coordinates and using uniqueness of multilinear interpolation. Summing the coefficients would give \(q\in K_d\), a contradiction. Thus multiplying \(Wq\) by \(w\) leaves \(\widehat K_D\). \(\square\)

Corollary (uniform padding strictness). In particular, demanding fixed-cutoff closure uniformly after adjoining \(D\) free variables forces \(H_{d+1}=0\) for every \(0\le d<D\). The converse follows from repeated descent, together with coefficientwise extension: if all those original equalities hold, the degree filtration of the extended sum is strict through \(D\) as well. Indeed the coefficient of a free monomial of degree \(t\) lies in the original \(K_{e-t}\), so a fall from \(e\) to \(e-1\) would give an original fall from \(e-t\) to \(e-t-1\). This identifies exactly where the weaker endpoint ceases to be weaker under arbitrary free padding. The earlier majority obstruction is a concrete application with a proved original fall. Its special local immunity is needed there to exhibit the fall, not for the present propagation argument.

Method decision and remaining implication

Decision. Retain the fixed-cutoff maps (2) as the weaker proof target, and compare images in the actual quotient \(Q_D\) rather than insisting that all of \(H_D\) vanish. This changes the method, without adding an unproved condition to the accepted formula specification. The gain is that harmless falls are allowed; the cost is a separate proof that one survives and a global analysis of the defect images. The free-variable theorem prevents claiming this flexibility from merely tensoring a local calibration with unused coordinates. On an actual fully constrained family, other members might absorb the images, but that needs proof in their common quotient.

Outcome of the scheduled test. The endpoint is strictly weaker than all-degree no-fall gluing, and its obstruction can be computed from the chosen local ideals without first computing the unknown PC closure. However, as a condition on a cover it is still an exact reformulation of that cover's multiplication closure. No independent structural theorem currently forces its vanishing near half. Thus the test does not license a new hardness specification. The earlier promise to avoid hiding the target in admissibility is enforced here.
