Collision rectangle decomposition

Statement. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with binary constants and unbounded-fanin arithmetic gates, counting gates and wires. Let \(m>n\ge1\), \(A:m\times n\), \(B:n\times m\) have independent original entries, and retain every original Boolean generator \(b_x=x^2+x\) and placeholder \(Z_x\). Put \(E_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^n A_{ia}B_{aj}\), \(P=\prod_i E_{ii}\prod_{i\ne j}(1+E_{ij})\). For each \(1\le p \[u=A_{p\ell},\quad v=A_{q\ell},\quad s=B_{\ell p},\quad t=B_{\ell q},\quad D=uvst,\quad R=\sum_{a\ne\ell}A_{pa}B_{aq},\quad S=\sum_{a\ne\ell}A_{qa}B_{ap}.\]

Define the syntactic product omitting just the two named off-diagonal factors, and the prefix polynomial

\[L_{pq}=\prod_{i\ne j,\ (i,j)\notin\{(p,q),(q,p)\}}(1+E_{ij}),\] \[J_{pq\ell}=\sum_{\substack{\alpha:[q-1]\hookrightarrow[n]\ \alpha(p)=\ell}}\ \prod_{\substack{iq}E_{ii}.\]

Empty products equal one. If the injection set is empty its sum is zero. Put

\[U=vs(tZ_u+u^2Z_t),\qquad V=ut(sZ_v+v^2Z_s),\qquad K_{pq\ell}=(1+vs+S)U+RV.\]

Then, as a formal identity,

\[P=Q(X,b)+\mathcal R(X),\quad Q(X,Z)=\sum_{pThe circuit \(Q\) is linear in the full original placeholder list; unused coefficients are zero. Apart from supplying the \(J\) circuits, this construction has polynomial gate/wire cost and constant added depth. If their combined size is \(H\) and maximum depth is \(e\), both \(Q\) and \(\mathcal R\) have size polynomial in \(H+m+n\) and depth at most \(e+10\). No small fixed-depth bound for \(J\), nor a correction for \(\mathcal R\), is asserted. Since \(P\) is Boolean-zero, so is \(\mathcal R\); that fact does not provide its formal correction.

Proof. Direct product Frobenius telescoping gives \(t b_u+u^2 b_t=ut+(ut)^2\), hence \(U(X,b)=D(1+ut)\). Likewise \(V(X,b)=D(1+vs)\). Expanding the two cross factors proves the local identity

\[D(1+ut+R)(1+vs+S)=K_{pq\ell}(X,b)+DRS.\]

In particular the patch removes the constant and same-colour cross contributions, retaining all other colours through \(R,S\). This is an application of the recorded Frobenius witness, not a new division operation. It remains valid with arbitrary additional old-variable dependence in \(R,S\), and after multiplying by any placeholder-free polynomial.

Expand only the diagonal factors: \(\prod_iE_{ii}=\sum_{\alpha:[m]\to[n]}\prod_i A_{i,\alpha(i)}B_{\alpha(i),i}\). Each assignment has a unique first repeated position \(q\le n+1\) and a unique earlier position \(pq}E_{ii}=T_{pq\ell}\): later colours are unrestricted, so their sums factor. The common off-diagonal product is \(L_{pq}(1+ut+R)(1+vs+S)\). Apply the local identity to every group and sum to obtain the displayed complete identity. This sums all branch parities formally, without treating different coordinates independently.

There are \(n\binom{n+1}{2}\) indexed groups, polynomially many. Build the original \(E\) gates once; every \(L\) is an ordinary product of at most \(m(m-1)\) explicit factors. The \(R,S\) sums have at most \(n-1\) products, and the four-placeholder \(K\) uses a fixed number of sum/product gates. Sharing each supplied \(J\), its suffix product and the \(L\) gates, then making the displayed products and sums costs polynomially many wires and at most ten layers beyond the maximum supplied depth. No succinct powers, extension constants or coefficient extraction occur. Original matrix entries and all Boolean generators are preserved. This is an IPS circuit ledger, not an original-PC-degree improvement.

Collision prefix depth gap

Statement. At the goal's \(m=2n\), the prefix \(J_{1,n+1,1}\) restricts by binary constants to the formal determinant of a generic \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) matrix. Consequently this family of individually computed first-collision prefixes has no polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth binary arithmetic circuits, by cor:binary-determinant-depth-gap. This is a restriction of the prefix, not of a full correction or of the masked sum \(Q\).

Proof. In this prefix, row one is assigned colour one, and the other rows \(2,\ldots,n\) inject into colours \(2,\ldots,n\). Since their numbers agree, these are exactly bijections. Therefore

\[J_{1,n+1,1}=\sum_{\pi:[2,n]\overset\sim\longrightarrow[2,n]}\prod_{i=2}^n A_{i,\pi(i)}B_{\pi(i),i}.\]

Set every \(B_{a i}=1\) for \(2\le a,i\le n\). The remaining independent \(A_{ia}\) form the generic square matrix; the displayed permanent equals its determinant in characteristic two. A constant restriction adds no depth or size, so polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth circuits for these prefixes would contradict the registered determinant-family bound. The prefix appears at every \(m=2n\), with growing square order. This formal restriction does not pay for extracting it from a circuit for the whole sum, and does not survive an arbitrary representative change as a proved hard invariant. The suffix can separately be made one by using row colour one at every \(i>n+1\); no such assignment to the remaining mask or full certificate is inferred.
