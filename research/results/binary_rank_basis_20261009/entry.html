The matrix base has no cheap affine consequences

Let \(\Gamma_{m,q}\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) consist of Boolean equations for \(A\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times q}\), \(B\in\mathbb F_2^{q\times m}\) and \(AB-I_m=0\). Write \(\mathcal C_b\) for its ordinary degree-\(b\) PC consequence space. All polynomials and coefficients in this entry are binary.

Lemma (matrix affine rigidity). For \(m\ge3\), \(q\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), if \(1\notin\mathcal C_b\), then \[ \mathcal C_b\cap\mathbb F_2[A,B]_{\le1}=\{0\}. \tag{1} \] In particular this holds in the recorded hard range \(m>q\), \(q\ge2b-1\).

Proof: symmetry preserves the actual proof ceiling. For \(S\in\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb F_2)\) and \(T\in\mathrm{GL}_q(\mathbb F_2)\), use \[ (A,B)\longmapsto(SA,BS^{-1}),\qquad (A,B)\longmapsto(AT,T^{-1}B). \] These are invertible linear substitutions. In the first, \(AB-I\) becomes \(S(AB-I)S^{-1}\); in the second it is unchanged. The image of any Boolean equation is a binary linear combination of Boolean equations because squares distribute in characteristic two. Thus all axiom images have degree-two derivations. Primitive multiplication and linear combinations replay through \(b\), so \(\mathcal C_b\) is invariant under these substitutions. It is also invariant under \((A,B)\mapsto(B^{\mathsf T},A^{\mathsf T})\), which transposes the matrix equations.

Suppose \[ f=c+\sum_{i,k}C_{ik}A_{ik}+\sum_{k,i}H_{ki}B_{ki}\in\mathcal C_b \] is nonzero. If both linear coefficient arrays vanish, \(f=1\), a contradiction. By the transpose symmetry assume \(C\ne0\). Pick a row index \(a\) with \(C_{a,\cdot}\ne0\), and distinct row indices \(b_0,e\), both different from \(a\). Apply the outer transvection \(S=I+E_{a b_0}\) and add the resulting consequence to \(f\). Its difference is \[ f+f(SA,BS^{-1}) =\sum_k C_{ak}A_{b_0k}+\sum_k H_{k b_0}B_{ka}. \] Here \(S^{-1}=S\). Apply the transvection \(I+E_{b_0e}\) to this difference and add it back. It changes the indicated \(A\)-row but leaves the indicated \(B\)-column fixed, because \(e\ne a\). We obtain \[ \sum_k C_{ak}A_{ek}\in\mathcal C_b. \] Choose \(T\) carrying the nonzero binary coefficient column \(C_{a,\cdot}^{\mathsf T}\) to a coordinate vector. The inner action therefore derives one entry of \(A\). Row and inner-column permutations derive every entry of \(A\), all through \(b\). For a fixed outer row \(i\), multiply the derived \(A_{ik}\)'s by \(B_{ki}\), sum, and add the diagonal axiom \(\sum_k A_{ik}B_{ki}+1\). This derives one through degree at most \(b\), since \(b\ge2\). Contradiction. \(\square\)

This is a statement about the matrix base itself. Adding retained blocks can break its symmetry and supply other consequences; those are not covered by (1). It is also not a rigidity theorem for arbitrary nonlinear quotient directions.

The transformed column inputs

Return to even \(n\), \(m>n\), a fixed pairing of the inner indices, and the paired restriction with live set \(L\) of size \(n'\ge2\). First make the inner substitution \(A_{\rm old}=AT\), \(B_{\rm old}=T^{-1}B\), for any \(T\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\), then fix each dead pair \(\{u,v\}\) to equal vectors as before. Let \(X_{i\ell}\), \(\ell\in L\), denote the live entries on the \(A\)-side. An original column entry becomes \[ \ell_{i,k}=c_{i,k}+\sum_{\ell\in L}T_{\ell k}X_{i\ell},\qquad c_{i,k}=\sum_{\{u,v\}\text{ fixed}}(T_{uk}+T_{vk})a_{\{u,v\},i}. \tag{2} \] Put \(v_k=(T_{\ell k})_{\ell\in L}\) and \(V_k=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{\ell_{i,k}:i\in[m]\}\). All labels in (2) are fixed constants, with no randomness assumption.

Proposition (mixed column core cost). There are \(n'\) original columns whose \(v_k\)'s are linearly independent. For the corresponding spaces \(V_k\): each has dimension \(m\), contains no nonzero constant, and distinct spaces intersect only at zero. For any nonzero \(v_k\), the falsity polynomial of \(\bigvee_i\ell_{i,k}\) has exact full-cube Boolean degree \(m\). Give the selected \(n'\) spaces complete accuracy-\(h\) ENS blocks, \(h\ge1\), with their affine tuples and original companion degree \(2h+1\). For incoming degree \(D\ge2h+1\), the historical affine core–residual criterion has exact optimal certified bound \[ \min\{D+n',\,T_mD\}>n',\qquad T_m=\max\{1,\lceil m/h\rceil-1\}. \tag{3} \] This remains the same after quotienting by affine consequences of the residual matrix base proved through any proper cutoff \(b\ge2\). It is a criterion bound, not a lower bound on the augmented system's actual PC refutation degree.

Proof of the geometry. The rows of \(T\) indexed by \(L\) are independent, so the rectangular matrix \(T_{L,\cdot}\) has rank \(n'\) and contains \(n'\) independent columns. For a nonzero \(v_k\), the homogeneous parts of \(\ell_{i,k}\) use disjoint variable rows. A combination with coefficients \(\alpha_i\) has zero linear part only if every \(\alpha_i=0\). This proves dimension \(m\) and absence of a nonzero constant. For two distinct nonzero binary vectors \(v_k,v_j\), these vectors are linearly independent. Equality of two combinations of their forms forces, in each variable row, \(\alpha_i v_k=\beta_i v_j\), hence both coefficients zero. This proves the intersection statement, regardless of offsets.

The falsity polynomial is \(\prod_i(1+\ell_{i,k})\). Choose \(\ell_0\in L\) with \(T_{\ell_0k}=1\). Its multilinear expansion contains \(\prod_iX_{i\ell_0}\) with coefficient one. No other factor uses the same row variables, so this term cannot cancel or undergo a Boolean degree reduction. All terms have degree at most \(m\), proving exact degree. In particular the restriction has not made this clause a tautology on the free live-variable cube. This is not a claim that computing its exact falsity polynomial is necessary for an ENS approximation.

Proof of the certified cost. Apply the historical spread-cost proof to these \(n'\) affine \(m\)-dimensional spaces. A batch with at least two has zero intersection at its first selected member, so its residual rank is \(m\), forcing factor \(T_m\). A zero-core singleton has the same factor. If no such multiplicative step occurs, all selected spaces are singleton batches with nonzero cores, each charging one. Full-core singletons attain \(D+n'\), and one all-zero-core batch attains \(T_mD\), proving the exact minimum. This reuses the old criterion proof; it is not a new general ordering theorem.

To check the strict comparison, let \(s=\lceil m/h\rceil\). If \(s\le2\), \(T_mD=D\ge2h+1>m\). If \(s\ge3\), then \[ T_mD\ge(s-1)(2h+1)>sh\ge m. \] Since \(m>n'\), both branches in (3) exceed \(n'\). Finally (1) says that every supplied affine matrix-base consequence at a proper cutoff is zero, so that quotient changes none of these spaces. \(\square\)

Source scope and what remains

The tested blocks are legitimate old-affine ENS tuples, and their column OR formulas have polynomial descriptions. The statement does not assert that they occur essentially in a short refutation. The earlier coherent-spread positive control already shows that a bad raw core cost can coexist with a cheap alternative normalization. Nonlinear base relations, nonconstant coefficient maps, a surviving weight, different encodings and actual proof-specific use are not excluded here.

The inner substitution and paired restriction still preserve residual matrix equations exactly. Their maps on matrix variables are affine, and gate values in the costed CNF interface remain of degree at most two. Thus the old hardness and source-degree ledger are unchanged. What fails is the attempted simplification inference: column width has become row-wise affine dependence, while a substantial raw spread remains. The historical core theorem is useful when its hypotheses hold, but these symmetries and restrictions do not force them.
