A full coefficient algebra gives uncharged restrictions

Let \(B=F[x_1,\ldots,x_v]/(x_i^2-x_i)\), let \(D\ge2\), and let \(\Gamma\subseteq B_{\le D}\) be unsatisfiable. A truncated operator certificate consists of a finite-dimensional \(F\)-space \(W\), a linear map \(\Phi:B_{\le D}\to W\), and linear endomorphisms \(T_i\) such that \[e=\Phi(1)\ne0,\qquad \Phi(g)=0\ (g\in\Gamma),\qquad T_i\Phi(f)=\Phi(x_if)\quad(f\in B_{\le D-1}).\tag{1}\] These conditions make the full kernel of \(\Phi\) a proper degree-D pseudoideal. They do not require global commutation or global idempotence on all of \(W\).

Choose a coordinate set \(S\) and put \(A=F[x_i:i\in S]/(x_i^2-x_i)\). Suppose \(W\) is a unital \(A\)-module, \(T_s\) is multiplication by \(x_s\) for \(s\in S\), and every \(T_i\), \(i\notin S\), is \(A\)-linear. Write \(\delta_a\in A\) for the point idempotent of \(a\in\{0,1\}^S\), \(W_a=\delta_a W\), and \(e_a=\delta_a e\).

Theorem (coefficient fibre barrier). For every \(a\) with \(e_a\ne0\), the restricted input \(\Gamma|_{S=a}\) has no ordinary PC refutation through degree \(D\), and \[\dim W_a\ge2^{D-1}.\tag{2}\] At least one such fibre exists. If \(|S|\le D\) and \(\Phi\) is injective on the full coordinate algebra \(A\), then every fibre is nonzero on \(e\), so \(\dim W\ge2^{|S|+D-1}\). In particular a module with polynomially bounded fibre dimensions cannot certify a near-half cutoff, however many Boolean coefficient assignments it indexes.

Proof. The pairwise orthogonal point idempotents sum to one, so \(W=\bigoplus_aW_a\) and \(e=\sum_ae_a\). All outside-variable operators preserve every summand by \(A\)-linearity. On polynomials in the remaining variables define \[\Psi_a(f)=\delta_a\Phi(f).\tag{3}\] Equation (1) and fibre preservation imply \(T_i\Psi_a(f)=\Psi_a(x_if)\) through the same predecessor degree \(D-1\).

For a squarefree monomial \(x_Ux_V\) with \(U\subseteq S\), \(V\cap S=\varnothing\) and \(|U|+|V|\le D\), iterate (1) on the selected variables. Each intermediate multiplication is legal, and their operators are exactly the coefficient actions. Hence \[\delta_a\Phi(x_Ux_V)=a_U\delta_a\Phi(x_V).\tag{4}\] By linearity, \(\Psi_a(g|_{S=a})=\delta_a\Phi(g)=0\) for every original input, including those of degree \(D\). Thus \(\ker\Psi_a\) contains every restricted input and is closed under legal multiplication. When \(e_a\ne0\), it is proper and excludes ordinary PC through \(D\), with Boolean reductions paid as in the preceding kernel theorem. Every restriction of an unsatisfiable formula is unsatisfiable, so that theorem also gives (2).

If the full algebra on \(S\) lies in the domain and \(\Phi\) is injective there, then (1) gives \(\Phi(\delta_a)=\delta_ae\ne0\) for every \(a\). Summing (2) proves the final dimension bound. No global commutation or idempotence is assumed for the outside-variable operators. \(\square\)

The pointwise extension of the last selector recipe falls under this test whenever it is claimed to satisfy (1): all conditional matrices and right inverses would have entries in \(A\), so the outside operators would be \(A\)-linear. Merely building compatible mass-one coefficient families does not establish (1), and the theorem does not assert such families are absent. It says that this full pointwise action cannot turn them into the desired certificate.

The actual source has easier restricted fibres

Let an unsatisfiable canonical modular system on \(v\) variables have an odd-prime modulus \(q\), all supported coefficients nonzero, maximum actual width \(w\), and a fractional exact cover by nonnegative real row weights \(\lambda_a\): \(\sum_{a:i\in X_a}\lambda_a=1\). Let the proof field have characteristic different from \(q\). Pin any set \(S\), leave \(n=v-|S|\) variables, and retain the canonical restricted rows.

Corollary (modular fibre exclusion). Every restricted formula has ordinary PC degree at most \[B(n)=\max\{w+1,3,\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\}.\tag{5}\] Consequently no certificate (1) linear over the full Boolean coefficient algebra on \(S\) exists if \(D\ge B(v-|S|)\), regardless of the dimension of \(W\). In particular, when \(D\ge\max\{w+1,3\}\), a necessary condition for such a certificate is \[|S|\le v-2D+2.\tag{6}\] For the central source at \(D=\lfloor v/2-s_v\rfloor\), this permits only about \(2s_v\) globally frozen coefficient bits, not a coefficient set of size \(D-1\) or near half the variables.

Restricted upper bound. Restricting a canonical forbidden-tuple indicator either makes it zero or gives exactly the corresponding indicator on the remaining row variables. Thus the restricted inputs are the canonical residual relations, with possible duplicates, and maximum width at most \(w\). An empty residual row refutes through its remaining width by summing its indicators; a false constant row is immediate. Discard true zero-variable rows. Otherwise every positive-width row retains its nonzero coefficients, and the same \(\lambda_a\) cover every remaining variable exactly once.

The modular interpolation theorem gives a local interpolation degree \(t_a\le h_a/2\) for each remaining row width \(h_a\), independent of its new target or parity. Hence \(\sum_a\lambda_at_a\le n/2\). Its paid fractional-cover ceiling yields (5) when \(n\ge2\); for \(n\le1\) Boolean point indicators give an immediate smaller bound. No field-consistency assumption is needed. Apply the fibre theorem: every potentially nonzero fibre would exclude a refutation that (5) supplies, so all \(e_a\) vanish, a contradiction. If \(|S|\ge v-2D+3\), then \(n\le2D-3\), and all three terms in (5) are at most \(D\). This proves (6). \(\square\)

The original central source has width \(8l\) and the exact cover \(\lambda_a=1/5\). At its intended cutoff, \(D\) eventually exceeds its width floor and is much larger than one third of \(v\). Any chosen coefficient set of size \(D-1\), or the larger locally shattered set constructed earlier, violates (6). This excludes the full Boolean coefficient-module proposal even with arbitrarily many coordinates per fibre. The proof is uniform over every pin and uses only the source's already verified unsatisfiability, nonzero coefficients and partition cover.

Why this is not an improved original-instance upper bound. Actual PC conditioning on \(S\) pays \(|S|\) degrees, unlike the impossible certificate's uncharged fibre restriction. For an original even central row, if \(r_a\) coordinates are pinned, its residual interpolation loss \(\delta_a=h_a/2-t_a\) is at most \(r_a/2\): it is zero for \(r_a=0\), one half when \(r_a\) is odd, and at most one when a positive even number is pinned. Exact cover gives \[\Delta_{\rm residual}=\sum_a\lambda_a\delta_a\le\frac12\sum_a\lambda_ar_a=|S|/2.\tag{7}\] Whenever these nonempty residual-row interpolation bounds are used in ordinary case analysis, their unfloored expression therefore satisfies \[|S|+(v-|S|)/2-\Delta_{\rm residual}\ge v/2.\tag{8}\] Width floors and the additive constants do not improve it. Thus this particular interpolation-plus-conditioning calculation supplies no gap below the original midpoint. Empty fibres or a different stronger refutation mechanism need separate treatment; (8) is a bound on this upper-bound expression, not a lower bound on actual degree. The central formula is not excluded.

A genuine certificate may violate global coefficient Booleanity

Proposition (truncated action control). Over every field of characteristic different from two and three, the Boolean system \[x+y+z=3/2\tag{9}\] is unsatisfiable and has a proper degree-two operator certificate on a three-dimensional space. Its forced \(x\)-operator is not globally idempotent, although it satisfies every Boolean identity required through degree two. Both restrictions \(x=0\) and \(x=1\) have degree-two refutations. Therefore this certificate cannot be a full Boolean coefficient module on \(x\), but is a valid degree-filtered certificate.

Explicit map. Use the basis \((1,y,z)\) for the target and row-vector coordinates. Define \[ \begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr} f&1&x&y&z&xy&xz&yz\\\hline \Phi(f)&(1,0,0)&(3/2,-1,-1)&(0,1,0)&(0,0,1)&(3/8,0,-1/2)&(3/8,-1/2,0)&(-3/8,1/2,1/2). \end{array}\tag{10}\] Boolean powers use their multilinear reductions. The operator matrices, acting on the right, are \[ T_x=\begin{pmatrix}3/2&-1&-1\\3/8&0&-1/2\\3/8&-1/2&0\end{pmatrix},\quad T_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&1&0\\-3/8&1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad T_z=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\-3/8&1/2&1/2\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.\tag{11}\] For each predecessor \(1,x,y,z\), direct substitution gives \(\Phi(f)T_i=\Phi(x_if)\); these twelve identities imply the assertion for every affine predecessor. The input in (9) has image zero, and \(\Phi(1)\ne0\). Hence (1) holds at \(D=2\), and the kernel is proper and closed at that cutoff. The characteristic assumption ensures (9) has no Boolean solution: its possible integer sums are \(0,1,2,3\), none equal to \(3/2\) in those fields.

Writing \(e=\Phi(1)\), the same identities give \(e(T_x^2-T_x)=0\) and \(e(T_iT_j-T_jT_i)=0\). But \[\Phi(y)(T_x^2-T_x)=(0,-1/8,1/8)\ne0.\tag{12}\] This vector comes from a degree-three Boolean multiple, beyond the certified window. The images of the affine predecessors span the whole target, so (1) already forces the displayed operators on it. No different global action on this same image can repair that idempotence. The control shows why full Booleanity on all state vectors is an extra requirement, even though all legal Boolean equations are respected.

Restricted refutations. At \(x=a\), put \(c=3/2-a\), so \(c\in\{1/2,3/2\}\). From \(f=y+z-c\), derive \(yz+(1-c)y\) and \(yz+(1-c)z\) through degree two using Booleanity. Their difference gives \(y-z\), since \(1-c\ne0\), and then \(2y-c\). Multiplication by \(y\) gives \((2-c)y\). Since \(c\ne0\) and \(2-c\ne0\), these linear consequences contradict \(y=c/2\), still through degree two. This also independently rules out the full coefficient module by the fibre theorem. In characteristic three, the all-zero assignment solves (9), so that characteristic is correctly excluded. \(\square\)

Disposition and next test

The exponential dimension floor does not authorize replacing a filtered space by an arbitrary large algebra. The pointwise Boolean-module enlargement freezes selected coordinates strongly enough to inherit uncharged restricted refutations. A repair must abandon that full coefficient-linearity, or the full Boolean coefficient action itself, outside the legal degree window. The actual kernel endpoint only requires (1), not global idempotence or commutation. The explicit control shows these stronger demands can already fail for a valid small certificate.
