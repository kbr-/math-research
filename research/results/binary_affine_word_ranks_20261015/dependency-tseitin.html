Noncommutative Tseitin formulas

Theorem. Fix a maximum degree bound \(\Delta\). For every finite simple oriented graph with \(n\ge1\) vertices, \(m\) edges and charges \(c_v\in\mathbb F_3\) with \(\sum_vc_v\ne0\), its ternary one-hot Tseitin CNF has a noncommutative IPS formula over \(\mathbb F_2\) of size \(O_\Delta((n+m+1)^4)\) and binary-fan-in depth \(O_\Delta(\log(n+m+2))\). Include all Boolean and variable-commutator generators. Every monomial in placeholders has exactly one placeholder. Formula size counts the entire tree, with no sharing. Constants depend on the fixed local degree bound; no bound uniform in growing \(\Delta\) is claimed.

Exact inputs. Variables are \(x_{e,a}\), \(a=0,1,2\). The domain clauses have ordered falsity polynomials \(\prod_{a=0}^2(1-x_{e,a})\) and \(x_{e,a}x_{e,b}\) for \(a<b\). A vertex has every forbidden-tuple polynomial \(\prod_{e\ni v}x_{e,a_e}\), in a fixed edge order, for \(\sum_{e\ni v}\sigma_{ve}a_e\ne c_v\); the two endpoint signs are opposite. Empty forbidden tuples give the unit generator. Add \(x_i^2-x_i\) and \(x_ix_j-x_jx_i\), \(i<j\). Let this binary polynomial tuple be \(F\) and its corresponding noncommuting placeholders \(Y\). We construct a formula \(C(X,Y)\) with literal free-algebra identities \(C(X,0)=0\) and \(C(X,F)=1\).

Local certificates. Work first in the central extension \(K=\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2(\omega)\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). The following elementary procedure supplies constant-size certificates for every fixed local polynomial used below. On \(k\) variables, sort the letters of each monomial by adjacent swaps, recording each difference as a left/right word multiple of a variable commutator. Reduce powers to exponent at most one using \(x^r-x^{r-1}=x^{r-2}(x^2-x)\). Thus the difference between a given bounded-degree polynomial and its sorted multilinear reduction has an explicit Boolean/commutator certificate. For a multilinear polynomial \(f\), Boolean interpolation gives its commutative equality with \(\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}^k}f(a)\delta_a\), where \(\delta_a=\prod_j x_j^{a_j}(1-x_j)^{1-a_j}\). Sorting both sides lifts this equality to the free algebra, with commutator corrections. If \(f\) vanishes on every satisfying local assignment, each nonzero term has a violated clause \(Q\); its falsity factors occur in \(\delta_a\). Permute these factors to write that term as a word-polynomial multiple of \(Q\), paying each swap. Commutators of literals reduce to signed variable commutators. All factors, monomials and Boolean assignments in this procedure are bounded in number by a constant depending on the local arity and degree. Replacing each generator occurrence by its placeholder yields a finite sum of left/right word multiples of one placeholder. This proves formal ideal membership and its constant formula cost, rather than inferring a certificate from evaluations alone.

Define \(U_e=\sum_a\omega^a x_{e,a}\), \(V_e=\sum_a\omega^{-a}x_{e,a}\), \(\alpha_v=\omega^{c_v}\), and let \(T_v\) be the ordered product of \(U_e\) or \(V_e\) according to the endpoint sign. On the exactly-one edge assignments, \(U_eV_e=1\); on the satisfying vertex assignments, \(T_v=\alpha_v\). The local procedure, on at most \(3\Delta\) variables for a vertex and three for an edge, supplies explicit constant-size placeholder-linear certificates for \(T_v-\alpha_v\) and \(U_eV_e-1\), from precisely the original domain/vertex clauses, Boolean axioms and commutators. Expand these constant-size local certificates into a constant number of scalar-weighted word-placeholder-word terms. Isolated vertices with zero charge give zero; a nonzero isolated charge gives a unit input and an immediate certificate.

Global identity. Order the vertices and write \(W=\prod_vT_v\), \(\Omega=\prod_v\alpha_v\ne1\). The formal telescoping identity \[ W-\Omega=\sum_v\left(\prod_{u<v}T_u\right)(T_v-\alpha_v)\left(\prod_{u>v}\alpha_u\right) \] requires no commutation. Each edge contributes exactly one \(U_e\) and one \(V_e\) to the word \(W\) of length \(2m\). Sort this factor word into \(W'=\prod_e U_eV_e\) by at most \(\binom{2m}{2}\) adjacent swaps. For each swap the difference is \(L(AB-BA)R\). Since \(A,B\) are linear forms of three old variables, \(AB-BA\) is a sum of at most nine scalar multiples of variable commutators (zero same-variable terms are discarded). This pays every swap without silently declaring variables commutative. Finally, \[ W'-1=\sum_e\left(\prod_{f<e}U_fV_f\right)(U_eV_e-1). \] Use the local placeholder certificates in the first and third displays and the explicit commutator placeholders in the second. Subtract the first identity from the sum of the other two: \((W-W')+(W'-1)-(W-\Omega)=\Omega-1\). Multiply by the nonzero scalar \((\Omega-1)^{-1}\). The resulting \(K\)-formula \(D(X,Y)\) is formally zero at \(Y=0\) and one at \(Y=F\).

Formula and field ledger. Put \(s=n+m+1\). After expanding only constant-arity local certificates, \(D\) is a sum of \(O_\Delta(s^2)\) terms. Each term is an ordered product of \(O_\Delta(s)\) factors: scalar constants, linear forms in at most three old variables, single old variables, and exactly one original placeholder. Each factor has explicitly supplied binary coordinates of constant formula size. Apply the balanced product-coordinate lemma separately to each term, and balance the sum. The constant coordinate \(C\) has size \(O_\Delta(s^4)\) and depth \(O_\Delta(\log(s+1))\), with all duplication charged. Substitution of the binary tuple \(F\) commutes with the unique decomposition \(D=C+\omega C_1\); therefore \(C(X,F)=1\), \(C_1(X,F)=0\), and \(C(X,0)=0\). Every expanded term still has one placeholder because coordinate multiplication does not add placeholder occurrences to a factor word. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. Arbitrarily large connected cubic graphs with charge one at one vertex and zero elsewhere satisfy the hypotheses, including the fixed-degree expander family used for old PC hardness. Connectivity and expansion are not needed for this upper bound. A single oriented edge with endpoint charges \(1,0\) is a nontrivial control: the endpoint relations give \(U=\omega\), \(V=1\), while \(UV=1\), and the same three-term identity yields \(\omega-1\). Degenerate cases. A graph with no edges and nonzero total charge has an isolated unit input. On a connected zero-total-charge graph, a spanning-tree assignment solves the vertex equations, so no such certificate exists; correspondingly the final scalar vanishes. Changing orientation merely exchanges the roles of \(U,V\). These are symbolic controls, not reported computer runs.
