Affine elimination and the selector ledger

Corollary (affine slice budget). Let \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) contain Booleanity, with all generators of degree at most \(w\), and suppose \(1\notin C_D(\Gamma)\). Choose \(r\) affine forms with independent linear parts, \(0\le r\le v\), and assume \[ B=D-r\ge\max(2,w). \tag{1} \] There is a fibre of those forms, of free dimension \(n=v-r\), such that \(\Gamma\) together with its fibre equations has no degree-\(B\) PC refutation. Affine elimination on that fibre preserves degree-\(B\) refutability, and does not increase the degree of any complete ENS source under the same substitution. The guaranteed deficit relative to half the free dimension is \[ \frac n2-B=\left(\frac v2-D\right)+\frac r2. \tag{2} \] Thus this guarantee strictly worsens the near-half deficit for \(r>0\), and leaves it unchanged for \(r=0\). It does not bound how hard a specially chosen fibre may actually be.

Proof: select a surviving fibre. Write the forms as \(L_1,\ldots,L_r\). For each \(b\in\mathbb F_2^r\), put \(\chi_b=\prod_{i=1}^r(1+L_i+b_i)\), of degree at most \(r\). Their sum is one as a polynomial: sum over each bit using \((1+L_i)+L_i=1\). Properness of \(C_D(\Gamma)\) therefore gives some \(\chi_b\notin C_D(\Gamma)\). Independent linear parts ensure that every fibre is consistent and has dimension \(v-r\). For \(r=0\), the only selector is one.

Suppose the selected fibre equations \(L_i+b_i=0\), together with \(\Gamma\), had a degree-\(B\) PC refutation. Multiply every proof line by \(\chi_b\). Old axiom lines and all inferences are derivable through \(B+r=D\). For a fibre axiom, its weighted image is \[ (L_i+b_i)\chi_b=(L_i^2+L_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(1+L_j+b_j). \] Since the form is binary affine, \(L_i^2+L_i\) is a linear combination of the old Boolean equations. The displayed certificate has degree at most \(r+1\le D\). Weighted replay would derive the final polynomial \(\chi_b\) in \(C_D(\Gamma)\), a contradiction. This is the existing pattern-selector proof, now used to select one surviving fibre.

Proof: eliminate affine equations. Row-reduce the selected equations and partition variables into pivots \(z_i\) and free variables \(y\), with \(z_i=\alpha_i(y)+c_i\). Let \(\sigma\) be that affine substitution, fixing any fresh ENS coefficients. It is a degree-nonincreasing ring homomorphism. A PC multiplication by \(z_i\) becomes a sum of multiplications by the free variables and a scalar. A pivot Boolean axiom becomes \[ (\alpha_i+c_i)^2+(\alpha_i+c_i) =\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}\alpha_i}(y_j^2+y_j), \] derivable through degree two. Hence a refutation before substitution gives one after substitution at the same cutoff.

Conversely, for any original generator \(g\) of degree at most \(w\), replacing pivots one at a time writes \(g-\sigma g\) as a combination of the affine equations with each product of degree at most \(\deg g\). The substituted generator is therefore derivable from \(\Gamma\) and the fibre equations through \(w\le B\). The free Boolean axioms were already present. Reusing these final polynomials simulates any substituted degree-\(B\) refutation before substitution. This proves equivalence with the explicit original-axiom cutoff in (1).

For a complete ENS source, \(\sigma\) sends each input to its substituted polynomial, fixes every fresh coefficient variable and sends every original product, companion and coefficient-field equation to the corresponding expression. Its degree does not increase. Original carried charges remain valid upper bounds; cancellations do not reset the original source ledger. This proves the asserted forward transfer, without claiming an arbitrary new lower bound for that source. Finally substitute \(n=v-r\) and \(B=D-r\) to obtain (2). \(\square\)

If the affine equations were already axioms, the elimination part costs no hardness: it simply expresses the same problem in \(n\) free coordinates. This differs from imposing new equations on a system known hard only through \(D\). For a particular chosen slice the selector must survive; old hardness alone does not make every fibre survive.

What an improvement would have to preserve

Suppose a separate theorem preserved a cutoff \(D-\ell(r)\) after \(r\) independent affine constraints. Its deficit would be \[ \frac{v-r}{2}-(D-\ell(r)) =\frac v2-D+\ell(r)-\frac r2. \] A gain relative to the old deficit therefore requires \(\ell(r)<r/2\); generic selector replay has \(\ell(r)=r\). The near-half notebook's failed conditioning induction records the corresponding loss in an upper-bound attempt. Neither calculation excludes a special slice-preservation theorem or a different family chosen together with its slice.

Our charged one-hot convention already removes the affine sum-one relation in every edge block and uses \(v=2m\) Boolean degrees of freedom. Counting those equations again would double-count the normalization. In the recorded growing-family window, \(D\le v/16\), well below half. The imported normalization alone does not improve that ratio, and generic additional conditioning makes its certified deficit worse. The alternative is a new preservation theorem or a different hard family; no such theorem is inferred from the dimension count.

It can nevertheless improve how a source problem is formulated. Coherent auxiliary affine equations may simplify all tuples simultaneously, and the normalized-rank proof then uses \(N_n(k)=\sum_{j\le k}\binom nj\). But one must prove properness in that enlarged old system at the required cutoff and recompute every source degree and inventory in \(n\). Polynomial length in \(v\) is polynomial in \(n\) only with a polynomial relation between those parameters. Clauses on affine forms are admitted by the near-half encoding with their width cost; arbitrary sums of such products are not thereby admitted as separate clauses.
