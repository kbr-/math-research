What the primary theorem actually says

Eagon–Reiner, Proposition 1 and Theorem 3, express Betti data using links in an Alexander dual and equate a squarefree ideal's linear resolution with Cohen–Macaulayness of its dual complex over the same field. Their statements, definitions and proofs were read. The cited Hochster and Alexander-duality prerequisites were not independently replayed. The theorem concerns ordinary polynomial-ring monomial ideals, not Boolean PC closure. It also retains the generators' initial degree; “linear” does not mean degree one or a small degree.

Two actual kernels before any topology

Let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) Boolean pseudoideal over a field \(F\), identified with its multilinear image in \[ B=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_i^2-x_i). \] Let \(w\notin J_D\) have degree at most \(b\), let actual error polynomials \(E_1,\ldots,E_s\) have degrees at most \(e\), and choose \(1\le R\le n\) with \[ b+e+R\le D. \] All membership and multiplication statements retain this unreduced product reserve. Define subspaces of \(B_{\le R}\) \[ L_0=\{f:\operatorname{ML}(wf)\in J_D\},\qquad L_1=\{f:\operatorname{ML}(wE_i f)\in J_D\ \hbox{for every }i\}. \] Then \(L_0\subseteq L_1\), and each is closed under multiplication by a variable followed by multilinear reduction when the multiplier's predecessor has degree at most \(R-1\). These follow by bounded absorption; no full ideal beyond the window is asserted.

Fix one degree-compatible multiplicative monomial order in \(F[x]\), restricted to squarefree monomials. For \(j=0,1\), form an ordinary squarefree monomial ideal \(I_j\subseteq F[t_1,\ldots,t_n]\): generate it by \(t_S\) whenever \(x_S\) is the leading monomial of a nonzero element of \(L_j\). The \(t\)'s are auxiliary combinatorial variables. Let \(\Delta_j\) be the Stanley–Reisner complex of \(I_j\). Allow the void complex when \(I_j=(1)\). Beyond degree \(R\) this is a chosen formal monomial completion, not a claim about unbudgeted multiplication in \(J_D\).

Lemma (relative kernel complex). One has \(\Delta_1\subseteq\Delta_0\). For every \(0\le k\le R\), \[ \dim\frac{L_1\cap B_{\le k}}{L_0\cap B_{\le k}} =|\{S\in\Delta_0\setminus\Delta_1:|S|\le k\}|. \tag{1} \] Thus a useful degree-\(\le k\) annihilator exists exactly when this relative face set is nonempty. Every inclusion-minimal relative face \(S\) lifts to \(f\in L_1\setminus L_0\) with leading monomial \(x_S\) and degree \(|S|\le R\). If \(\Delta_j^*=\{T\subseteq[n]:[n]\setminus T\notin\Delta_j\}\), complement gives \[ S\in\Delta_0\setminus\Delta_1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [n]\setminus S\in\Delta_1^*\setminus\Delta_0^*. \tag{2} \] In particular degree at most \(k\) corresponds to a relative dual face of size at least \(n-k\).

For a minimal relative face \(S\), the induced pair on \(S\) is \[ (\Delta_0|_S,\Delta_1|_S)=(2^S,\partial 2^S). \] Its augmented relative chain complex has one generator, in degree \(|S|-1\), and its homology there is \(F\). For \(S=\varnothing\), use \((\{\varnothing\},\varnothing)\) and degree \(-1\). This local relative class detects the first useful leading support; no assertion about nonzero global relative homology is made.

Proof: the paid standard families. Suppose \(x_T\) leads a relation in \(L_j\), and \(i\notin T\), with \(|T|+1\le R\). Multiply by \(x_i\). Terms already containing \(x_i\) drop degree on multilinear reduction, while the leading squarefree term \(x_{T\cup\{i\}}\) retains its higher degree and order. Multiplicativity and degree compatibility show that it still leads. Legal closure puts the resulting relation in \(L_j\). Iterating proves that a squarefree monomial of degree at most \(R\) belongs to \(I_j\) exactly when it is an actual leading monomial of \(L_j\). This is the same divisor-closure argument used in the intrinsic shadow theorem.

Since \(L_0\subseteq L_1\), their leading families and generated ideals are nested, so their standard complexes are nested in the reverse order. Row echelon form with this degree-compatible order makes the standard squarefree monomials through \(k\) a basis of \(B_{\le k}/(L_j\cap B_{\le k})\). Subtracting the two quotient dimensions gives (1). It counts the difference of the actual kernels; neither an ambient denominator nor an independently chosen coordinate change is introduced.

If \(S\) is a minimal relative face, \(t_S\in I_1\setminus I_0\). It contains a generating leading support \(T\subseteq S\), \(|T|\le R\), for \(I_1\). As \(S\in\Delta_0\), also \(T\in\Delta_0\); thus \(T\) is itself a relative face, and minimality forces \(T=S\). Hence \(|S|\le R\) and there is an actual \(f\in L_1\) led by \(x_S\). It is not in \(L_0\), since that leading monomial is standard for \(L_0\); degree compatibility gives \(\deg f=|S|\). Definitions give \(wf\notin J_D\) and \(wE_i f\in J_D\), with the original reserve paid. The lifted polynomial need not be the monomial \(x_S\) itself.

Formula (2) follows directly from the definition of Alexander duality by complementation. For the induced pair, every subset of \(S\) is in \(\Delta_0\), and every proper subset is in \(\Delta_1\) by minimality, whereas \(S\) is not. All proper faces therefore vanish in the relative chain quotient, leaving the single top generator with zero boundary and no group above it. This proves the homology statement, including the augmented empty case. \(\square\)

The lift certifies memberships in the supplied \(J_D\). It is not automatically a low-degree ordinary Nullstellensatz cofactor representation from old axioms. Such a representation would need additional data; when \(J_D\) is actual PC closure, its memberships instead have that PC meaning. A complex guessed from a support pattern supplies neither the actual leading relations nor these witnesses.

Why one global homology group misses the target

Take the Boolean-only quotient on \(n\ge2\) variables, \(w=1\), one error \(E=x_1\), \(R=n\) and \(D\ge n+1\). Its useful annihilator is \(1-x_1\), of degree one. In coordinates \(y_i=1-x_i\), the error is \(1-y_1\) and its kernel is the Boolean ideal generated by \(y_1\). Thus \(\Delta_0\) is the full simplex and \(\Delta_1\) is the simplex on vertices \(2,\ldots,n\). Both form a relative cone with apex 2: adding or deleting that apex gives the usual contracting homotopy on their relative chains. Hence all global relative homology groups vanish, despite the degree-one useful annihilator. The induced pair on \(\{1\}\) has the nonzero relative class described in the lemma. This is an all-\(n\) scope control of the invariant, not a hard old system.
