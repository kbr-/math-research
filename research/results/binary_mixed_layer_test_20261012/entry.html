Paired monomial row gap

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) modulo Booleanity on old coordinates \(y\). Take \(q\ge1\) pairs \(A_j,B_j\), all \(2q\) supports pairwise disjoint, each of size \(r\ge3\); extra old variables are allowed. Put \(a_j=y_{A_j}\), \(b_j=y_{B_j}\), and \(H_j=a_j+b_j+\ell_j\), with arbitrary old-affine \(\ell_j\). Every degree below is total degree before Boolean reduction unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Proposition. (a) If affine \(d_j\) have \(\operatorname{ml}(\sum_jd_jH_j)\) of degree at most two, every \(d_j=0\). Here \(\operatorname{ml}\) is the unique multilinear representative. (b) When every \(\ell_j=1\), any polynomial \(P\) of Boolean-reduced degree less than \(r\) in the ideal generated by \(H_1,\ldots,H_q\) and Booleanity vanishes at the point where every support variable is one. This holds for each fixed value of the extra variables. In particular, a Boolean-only complete normalizer using only row values of reduced degree less than \(r\) must have weight zero at that point. (c) Quadratic coefficients yield the following explicit repair, so (a) is not an obstruction to all polynomial rows.

Proof of (a). Expand \(d_j=c_j+\sum_k c_{jk}y_k\). For \(k\notin A_j\), the degree-\(r+1\) monomial \(y_{A_j\cup\{k\}}\) arising from \(d_ja_j\) cannot occur from any other support: two disjoint \(r\)-sets cannot both fit in an \((r+1)\)-set for \(r\ge2\). Hence \(c_{jk}=0\) for every \(k\notin A_j\). Repeating with \(B_j\) forces every coefficient with \(k\notin B_j\) to zero. Disjointness leaves no linear coefficient. The remaining degree-\(r\) monomials have distinct supports, so \(c_j=0\) since \(r\ge3\). The terms \(d_j\ell_j\) have degree at most two and cannot cancel any of the coefficients used. This verifies the reviewer's suggestion for arbitrary affine offsets.

Proof of (b). Fix all \(B_j\) variables to one and fix the extra variables. On this subcube \(H_j=a_j\). Therefore \(P\) vanishes whenever, in each \(A_j\), the assignment is not the all-one point. On one such punctured \(r\)-cube, monomials indexed by proper subsets form a basis of functions: evaluation on proper subsets is the inclusion matrix, triangular by size with diagonal one. Tensoring these bases gives independence on the product of punctured cubes. A polynomial of total degree less than \(r\) uses a proper subset within every block. Its restriction is consequently identically zero, including at the all-one point. This proves the claim. Every polynomial row \(\sum_jd_jH_j\) lies in this ideal. If every such row has reduced degree below \(r\), all rows are zero at that point, every complementary ENS factor is one, and every \(H_j=1\); thus a weighted companion can vanish there only if the weight vanishes. Old inconsistent axioms can remove this point, so this is not a statement modulo the hard old system.

Quadratic-coefficient repair. For \(s\in A_j,t\in B_j\), set \[d_{j,s,t}=(1+y_s)(1+y_t),\qquad Q_{j,s,t}=d_{j,s,t}\ell_j.\] Then \(d_{j,s,t}H_j+Q_{j,s,t}\) has a Boolean certificate through \(r+2\). Indeed \[d_{j,s,t}a_j=(y_s^2+y_s)y_{A_j\setminus s}(1+y_t),\] and likewise for \(b_j\) with \(t\). Thus every \(Q\), of degree at most three, lifts to a legal quadratic-coefficient row. It need not vanish: for \(\ell_j=1\), its value is one when \(y_s=y_t=0\). Affine-row failure therefore does not justify stopping at (a).

For \(\ell_j=1\), all these \(Q\)'s are quadratic, and their common zeros satisfy \(a_j=1\) or \(b_j=1\). More precisely, order each support and telescope: \[ H_j+a_jb_j=(1+a_j)(1+b_j) =\sum_{s\in A_j,t\in B_j} \left(\prod_{s'\text{ before }s}y_{s'}\right) \left(\prod_{t'\text{ before }t}y_{t'}\right)Q_{j,s,t}. \tag{1} \] Every summand has degree at most \(2r\); this is a literal polynomial identity. Consequently if a paid weighted prefix already kills every \(Q\), it compares the original \(H_j\) with the degree-\(2r\) monomial \(a_jb_j\), at the cost of multiplying the weight/prefix by the certificate in (1). It does not kill that residual. At the all-one point every \(Q=0\), whereas \(H_j=a_jb_j=1\), witnessing the remaining component. This completes the proposition.

Unweighted Boolean cost. A further consequence, using the forced-indicator argument from the coupled-row optimum, is that any unweighted Boolean-only normalizer of this interpreted tuple with \(h\ge1\) rows and coefficient degree at most \(T\ge0\) satisfies \[h(r+T)\ge rq.\tag{2}\] Indeed its product \(P\) must equal \(\prod_{j=1}^q(1+H_j)\) as a Boolean function: at a common zero every row is zero, so \(P=1\), and elsewhere a companion forces \(P=0\). This indicator has multilinear degree exactly \(rq\). Its top part is \(\prod_j(a_j+b_j)\); all selected supports are disjoint, so its distinct degree-\(rq\) monomials cannot cancel. A term using any affine offset has ordinary degree at most \(r(q-1)+1<rq\). On the other hand \(\deg P\le h(r+T)\). This proves (2) for arbitrary affine offsets, and credits the existing indicator-degree principle rather than asserting a new general packing theorem. It fixes the literal bottom interpretation and requires Boolean-only unweighted vanishing; different bottom choices, old-axiom certificates and surviving nontrivial weights are not excluded.

The universal relative code bound fails

The reviewer's suggested test of the next candidate can be decided in this cycle. The existing coded theorem bounds Boolean error rank divided by \(N_v(K)\). Consider the proposed replacement by \(h_w(K)\), for every proper Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, with all its other parameters unchanged. This stronger universal replacement is false.

Proposition. For every integer \(r\ge128\), set \[m=64,\quad v=64r,\quad K=16r,\quad s=1,\quad t=8,\quad D=25r,\quad w=1.\] Partition the \(v\) variables into \(m\) disjoint \(r\)-blocks and let \(z_i\) be their monomials, \(g=(z_1,\ldots,z_{64})\). Let \(J_D\) be the degree-\(D\) consequence space of Booleanity and equations identifying all variables in each block with its first variable. Then \(J_D\) is proper, \(h_w(K)=2^{64}\), and for every binary matrix \(C\) with at most \(t\) rows, \[ c_C^J(K):=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\in V_K\mapsto([fP_Cg_i])_i\bigr) =2^{64-\operatorname{rank}C}-1, \quad P_C=\prod_j(1+(Cg)_j). \tag{3} \] Nevertheless the proposed relative upper bound is below \(2^{-37}\), whereas the actual relative rank exceeds \(2^{-9}\): \[ (v+1)\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(64,\lambda_r)>1\}<2^{-37},\qquad \frac{c_C^J(K)}{h_w(K)}>2^{-9},\qquad \lambda_r=(3/4)^r+(1/4)^r. \tag{4} \] The code allowance is exactly \(\min(64,s\lceil\log_2(65)\rceil+1)=8\), and the reserve \(K+rt+r=D\) holds. This is an all-code counterfamily to the universal estimate, not a counterexample in the charged hard family.

Proof. Substitution of the block representative for each variable, followed by Boolean reduction, has ordinary certificates through the input polynomial's degree: telescope each linear substitution, then divide powers by Boolean equations. It follows that the degree-\(D\) kernel of this reduction equals \(J_D\), is a pseudoideal, and is proper. Its quotient is the algebra of functions of 64 representative bits \(Y_i\). Since \(K\ge64\), every such function is represented in \(V_K\), and \(z_i\) reduces to \(Y_i\). On the full representative cube, the error tuple is nonzero precisely on \(\ker C\setminus\{0\}\). Restriction of the full function algebra onto that set is surjective, so its size gives (3). All original products lie within \(K+rt+r=D\), so this computation is in the paid quotient.

The relative rank in (3) is at least \(2^{-8}-2^{-64}>2^{-9}\). For the other side, the union bound on two successes gives \[\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(64,\lambda_r)>1\}\le\binom{64}{2}\lambda_r^2.\] Because \((3/4)^4<1/2\), \(\lambda_{128}<2^{-32}+2^{-256}<2^{-31}\). Thus at \(r=128\), using \(8193<2^{14}\) and \(\binom{64}{2}=2016<2^{11}\), the claimed right side is below \(2^{-37}\). For \(r\ge128\), \(\lambda_{r+1}\le(3/4)\lambda_r\) and \((64(r+1)+1)/(64r+1)<4/3\), so \((64r+1)\lambda_r^2\) decreases. This proves (4) throughout the family.

Nonvacuity. At \(r=128\) there are 8192 variables, \(K=2048\), \(D=3200\); the displayed exact formulas, not a numerical matrix run, give all ranks. The common-zero assignment proves propriety. The old system here is satisfiable, so no degree-hardness claim is inferred. Degenerate cases. The weight is nonzero, \(1\le K<v/2\), and \(s=1\) lies in the required range. Dependent or fewer code rows only increase the error rank. With 64 independent rows the errors can be killed, but that exceeds the tested eight-row allowance. The construction is not an argument that every proper quotient has this behavior.

The structural reason is degree collapse: the written degree-\(r\) features become independent degree-one coordinates in the quotient. The ambient block inactivity probability no longer describes the quotient's multiplier space. This does not refute the original absolute coded bound or prevent degree-one contraction after reduction. Adding an independent hard component. In this spectator extension, the obstruction persists for proper pseudoideals containing an unsatisfiable system. Let \(r\to\infty\), and let \(\Gamma_r(X)\) in \(L_r\le cr\) variables have a proper Boolean pseudoideal \(J^H_{25r}\) containing all its generators, for some fixed \(c\). Add the 64 disjoint \(r\)-blocks and their within-block identifications on new variables. After identifying copies and multilinearizing their representatives \(Y\), write a polynomial as \(\sum_{S\subseteq[64]}Y_S f_S(X)\). Define \(J_{25r}\) by the condition that every \(f_S\) belongs to \(J^H_{25r}\), restricted to total degree at most \(25r\). This definition includes old Boolean reduction in \(X\), and is independent of its choice modulo the old Boolean equations.

This is a proper pseudoideal containing \(\Gamma_r\), Booleanity and the copy equations. Multiplication by an old \(X\) coordinate uses closure of \(J^H_{25r}\). Multiplication by a copy coordinate acts by \(Y_i\) after reduction: it reindexes or combines the coefficients, which stay in the same old subspace. All coefficient degrees are bounded by total degree, and every allowed product stays within \(25r\). The constant one is excluded because its empty coefficient is not in \(J^H_{25r}\). Thus adding these definitions preserves degree-\(25r\) hardness when \(\Gamma_r\) is unsatisfiable. The resulting pseudoideal need not equal the smallest consequence space, and no such equality is asserted.

Put \(H(k)=\dim(\mathbb F_2[X]_{\le k}/(J^H_{25r}\cap\mathbb F_2[X]_{\le k}))\). With \(v=64r+L_r\), \(K=16r\), the multiplier dimension is \[h_w(K)=\sum_{j=0}^{64}\binom{64}{j}H(K-j)\le2^{64}H(K).\] Restrict the multiplier domain to all functions of \(Y\) tensored with old classes of degree at most \(K-64\). Their products fit degree \(K\). The coefficientwise quotient definition makes this a genuine tensor subspace, and (3) on its \(Y\) factor gives, for every code of at most eight rows, \[c_C^J(K)\ge(2^{56}-1)H(K-64).\tag{5}\] The standard monomials of a degree-compatible echelon basis of a Boolean pseudoideal form a downward-closed family through its cutoff: an allowed multiple of a leading monomial remains a leading monomial, and Boolean squares are already excluded. If \(a_k\) counts its degree-\(k\) monomials, counting inclusions gives \(ka_k\le(L_r-k+1)a_{k-1}\). Therefore \(H(k-1)/H(k)\ge k/(L_r+k)\) (with value one once degrees exceed \(L_r\)). For \(r\ge128\), \[ \frac{c_C^J(K)}{h_w(K)}\ge(2^{-8}-2^{-64}) \left(\frac{15}{c+16}\right)^{64}>0. \tag{6} \] In contrast \(\rho=K/v\ge16/(64+c)>0\) and \(\rho\le1/4\). Thus \(((1-\rho)^r+\rho^r)^2\) decreases exponentially in \(r\), whereas \(v+1=O(r)\). The proposed binomial-tail bound tends to zero, contradicting (6) eventually. The same reserve \(K+rt+r=25r\) holds.
