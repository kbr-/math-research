Question. The recorded ceilings by elimination, prop:cut-rank-ceiling and thm:two-row-parity-ceiling, are stated for rows on variables with parity rows. What do they give for rows that are relations on arbitrary linear forms, in a form that does not depend on the basis, and which systems escape?

General statement. lem:form-cut-ceiling, for every unsatisfiable system of relations on linear forms over \(\mathbb F_2\) and every order of its rows.

Setting

Over \(\mathbb F_2\), on \(\mathbb F_2^n\) with Boolean variables and multilinear polynomials. A row \(i\) is a set of linear forms spanning \(W_i\subseteq(\mathbb F_2^n)^*\), \(\dim W_i\le w\), with a polynomial \(p_i\) in those forms, of degree at most \(\dim W_i\) in them, whose zeros are the allowed points; a clause on forms forbidding one tuple, a flat, is the case \(p_i\) the indicator of that flat. The system is the \(p_i\), \(1\le i\le L\), in a fixed order. Put \(U_{\le t}=\sum_{i\le t}W_i\), \(U_{>t}=\sum_{i>t}W_i\), \(A_t=U_{\le t}\cap U_{>t}\), the cut space, \(A_0=A_L=0\), and \(M_t=A_{t-1}+W_t\). A point of a space \(M\) of forms is a linear functional on \(M\), an assignment of values to its forms; its indicator \(\delta^M_y\) is a product of \(\dim M\) affine forms, the forms of a basis of \(M\) each plus a constant, a polynomial of degree at most \(\dim M\).

The ceiling

Lemma (form cut ceiling). If the system is unsatisfiable, then \(1\in C_D\) for \(D=\max\{w+1,\max_{1\le t\le L}\dim M_t\}\), and \(\max_t\dim M_t\le\max_t\dim A_{t-1}+w\). If every row is the indicator of a flat, \(D=\max_t\dim M_t\) suffices.

Proof. Let \(B_t\) be the set of points \(z\) of \(A_t\) such that no assignment of values to the forms of \(U_{\le t}\) extends \(z\) and satisfies rows \(1,\ldots,t\). \(B_0\) is empty, and \(B_L\) is the single point of the zero space, the system being unsatisfiable. Claim: \(\delta^{A_t}_z\in C_D\) for every \(z\in B_t\); at \(t=L\) this is \(1\in C_D\). Induction on \(t\). First, \(A_t\subseteq M_t\): for \(u\in A_t\) write \(u=a+b\), \(a\in U_{\le t-1}\), \(b\in W_t\); as \(u\) and \(b\) lie in \(U_{>t-1}\), so does \(a\), hence \(a\in A_{t-1}\). And \(U_{\le t-1}\cap M_t=A_{t-1}+(U_{\le t-1}\cap W_t)=A_{t-1}\), by the modular law and \(W_t\subseteq U_{>t-1}\). Call a point \(y\) of \(M_t\) dead if its restriction to \(A_{t-1}\) lies in \(B_{t-1}\) or it violates row \(t\). For a dead point of the first kind, \(\delta^{M_t}_y\) is \(\delta^{A_{t-1}}_{y|A_{t-1}}\) times the affine forms of a basis of \(M_t\) over \(A_{t-1}\), a multiple of a member of \(C_D\) by the induction hypothesis, of degree at most \(\dim M_t\le D\) at every step. For one of the second kind, \(\delta^{W_t}_{y|W_t}=\delta^{W_t}_{y|W_t}\,p_t\) as functions, and \(p_t\) is multiplied by the factors of \(\delta^{W_t}_{y|W_t}\) one at a time: each product, reduced, is a polynomial in the forms of \(W_t\), of degree at most \(\dim W_t\le w\), but a factor is a sum of variables and each variable times a line of degree \(w\) has degree up to \(w+1\) before the sum reduces, which is why \(D\ge w+1\) is asked; when \(p_t\) is the indicator of a flat, \(\delta^{W_t}_{y|W_t}=p_t\) for the one violating point and nothing is multiplied. Then the remaining factors are multiplied in, each line being of degree at most \(\dim M_t-1\) before its multiplication. So every dead point has its indicator in \(C_D\). Now let \(z'\in B_t\) and let \(y\) be a point of \(M_t\) restricting to \(z'\). If \(y\) were not dead, some assignment \(g\) on \(U_{\le t-1}\) would extend \(y|A_{t-1}\) and satisfy the rows before \(t\); \(g\) and \(y\) agree on \(U_{\le t-1}\cap M_t=A_{t-1}\), so they have a common extension to \(U_{\le t-1}+M_t=U_{\le t}\), which satisfies rows \(1,\ldots,t\) and extends \(z'\), against \(z'\in B_t\). So all points over \(z'\) are dead, and \(\delta^{A_t}_{z'}=\sum_{y|A_t=z'}\delta^{M_t}_y\in C_D\). \(\square\)

The derivation multiplies by linear forms, which the closure allows one variable at a time; by lem:affine-coordinate-invariance one may also read the whole argument in a basis adapted to each step. The floor is needed, as the reviewer's example shows: the rows \(ab+1\), \(bc+b\), \(cd+c\), \(de+d+e\) are unsatisfiable with every \(\dim M_t=2\), and their quadratic parts are independent, so the closure at two, in which only lines of degree at most one are multiplied, is their span with the Boolean axioms and omits \(1\); my draft had no floor and was false. It is the standard elimination, as in prop:cut-rank-ceiling, which is stated for rows on variables with parity rows, a frontier counted in variables less the rank of the parity rows inside it, and a floor of \(2\Delta\); here the frontier is a space of forms and no basis is chosen. By the reviewer's reading it is not a consequence of that proposition through prop:form-clause-encoding, which covers single-tuple clauses and whose chain variables change the frontier, nor of prop:transversal-rank-ceiling, which is incomparable. For rows that are flats each line is the indicator of an affine subspace, so the refutation is one in resolution over parities of width at most \(D\), in line with lem:parity-width-degree-bound: the lemma stays inside the step at which the item's attempts by conditioning stop, the second row of the consolidation's table. Unused: that the forms of a row are independent; \(\mathbb F_2\) enters only through the Boolean setting, and I state it there.

Corollary of the statement, for candidates. Let \(\lambda\) be the least, over orders of the rows, of \(\max_t\dim A_{t}\). A system with no refutation at \(D\ge w+1\), or at any \(D\) if its rows are flats, has \(\lambda>D-w\); at \(D=\lfloor n/2-s\rfloor\), every order of the rows has a cut of rank above \(n/2-s-w-1\). With \(r\) the rank of all the forms, \(\dim A_t=\operatorname{rk}U_{\le t}+\operatorname{rk}U_{>t}-r\).

Which systems escape. Rows whose forms are in general position: while \(tw\le r\) and \((L-t)w\ge r\) the first \(t\) rows span \(tw\) dimensions and the rest span everything, so \(\dim A_t=tw\), which reaches about \(r\). So elimination gives no ceiling below \(n\) for forms in general position, and the general ceiling is not reached this way. Systems that do not escape are those with an order of small cut rank: a chain of relations on consecutive forms \(f_i,f_{i+1}\) with \(f_i=x_1+\cdots+x_i\) has cut rank one and degree at most three, two if the rows are flats, though each row has width up to \(n\) as a clause on variables. Conditioning first does not obviously help for general position, by the reviewer's count, which I have not checked: modulo a space \(T\) of \(\rho\) forms the rows stay in general position in \(r-\rho\) dimensions, giving \(\rho+(r-\rho)\) again, and \(T\) meets too few rows to remove them when \(Lw\) is far above \(r\).

Degenerate cases. One row with no allowed point: \(M_1=W_1\), \(p_1=1\); the lemma gives \(\max\{w+1,\dim W_1\}\) where the true degree is zero. A satisfiable system: \(B_L\) is empty and nothing is derived. Rows all on one space \(W\): every \(M_t=W\), degree at most \(\max\{w+1,\dim W\}\), or \(\dim W\) for flats. Rows with disjoint independent forms: every cut space is zero, \(D=w+1\), or \(w\) for flats, and unsatisfiability then lies in one row. A row repeated: the cut spaces grow by nothing, the order putting copies together. Forms that do not span the dual space: the lemma uses only the span of the rows' forms.

Nonvacuity. \(n=3\), forms \(f_1=x_1\), \(f_2=x_1+x_2\), \(f_3=x_1+x_2+x_3\); rows \(f_1+1\); \(f_1(f_2+1)\), the implication from \(f_1\) to \(f_2\); \(f_2(f_3+1)\); \(f_3\). Unsatisfiable. In this order \(A_1=\langle f_1\rangle\), \(A_2=\langle f_2\rangle\), \(A_3=\langle f_3\rangle\), \(M_2=\langle f_1,f_2\rangle\), \(M_3=\langle f_2,f_3\rangle\); all four rows are indicators of flats, so \(D=2\), where the general bound would be three, and the refutation derives \(f_1+1\), \(f_2+1\), \(f_3+1\) and then \(1\) with the last row.
