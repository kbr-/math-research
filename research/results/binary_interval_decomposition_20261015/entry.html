The source operation and its hypothesis

The retrieved and identified ECCC TR21-094 manuscript is titled New Non-FPT Lower Bounds for Some Arithmetic Formulas, by Limaye, Srinivasan and Tavenas. The author's publication list links it as the technical report for the STOC 2022 paper Set-multilinear and non-commutative formula lower bounds for iterated matrix multiplication. Theorem 4 states its homogeneous noncommutative small-product-depth lower bound. This cycle read its definitions, Lemma 6 and proof, the product lemma and proof, and Proposition 15 with its full induction and Corollary 16. The publisher and author-linked HAL conference copies were not retrieved; the claims below use self-contained arguments and the explicitly read ECCC version. No theorem from an unread version is imported. Primary report.

In the source's exact homogeneous model, each gate has a degree label: constants have degree zero, variables degree one, all children of a sum have its degree, and a product's degree is the sum of its child degrees. Lemma 6 starts with an already homogeneous formula computing an ordered set-multilinear polynomial. It labels the root by the full position interval, copies an interval across sums, and partitions it into consecutive child intervals at products. A variable leaf in the wrong original block is replaced by zero. This keeps size and product depth. It does not construct degree labels for an arbitrary inhomogeneous formula.

Proposition 15 fixes a signed word of block cardinalities. Its relative rank divides coefficient-matrix rank by the square root of the row/column inventory. Its induction treats a product as a fixed partition into consecutive intervals. Either some factor contains a full lower-depth pattern, to which induction applies, or many distinct factors contain extremal signed positions and each loses relative rank by imbalance. This is an actual consecutive-factor operation. It depends on fixed intervals, not on evaluating a Boolean coefficient oracle. Subadditivity and multiplicativity are ordinary matrix-rank facts: addition is rank-subadditive, and disjoint position products give a tensor product of coefficient matrices. No assertion about an arbitrary nonhomogeneous gate follows merely from those facts.

Typed word queries

Lemma (typed word queries). Let a binary noncommutative arithmetic formula of gate/wire size \(S\), depth \(h\), on a finite alphabet \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\), \(m\ge1\), have the consistent degree labels just described and output degree \(D\ge0\). Every constant and coefficient is in \(\mathbb F_2\); products have ordered unbounded fan-in. For its output polynomial \(P\), define the commutative positional lift \[ \mathcal L_D(P)=\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_D}[a_{j_1}\cdots a_{j_D}]P\prod_{i=1}^{D}u_{i,j_i}. \tag{1}\] It has an ordered set-multilinear formula of size \(O(S)\), with no increase in depth or product depth. Consequently the coefficient of an encoded length-\(D\) word has an \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\) query circuit of size \(O(S(1+\log m))\), depth at most \(h+2\). The labels are supplied structural data; no algorithm finds them for an untyped input. The construction is a positional relabeling, not a new source-certificate identity.

Proof. Give the root interval \([1,D]\), allowing an empty interval when \(D=0\). At a sum give every child the same interval. At an ordered product with child degrees \(d_1,\ldots,d_t\), split the parent's interval consecutively with those lengths, allowing zero-length intervals. At a variable leaf of degree one, replace \(a_j\) by \(u_{i,j}\), where its assigned interval is \(\{i\}\); constants stay unchanged. A formula tree gives each occurrence one interval, even when the same original letter occurs many times. Induction proves (1) for every gate on its assigned interval: addition is linear, and multiplication concatenates a unique degree-determined split of each word. No convolution over possible child lengths occurs. Copies, gates and wires are unchanged. Each output monomial has exactly one variable from each position block.

For a query word, set \(u_{i,j}\) to its letter-equality indicator. A block's unique one-hot assignment kills every monomial except the queried word, so evaluation is its coefficient. Encode each letter with \(\max(1,\lceil\log_2m\rceil)\) bits. A leaf equality uses literals and one unbounded AND gate, with at most two layers including NOT; invalid codes can give zero. Replace formula sums by XOR and products by AND. Count each leaf's selector wires, obtaining the stated size and depth. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), weighted sums use only zero/one coefficients and do not introduce an uncharged field-coordinate operation. Empty intervals evaluate constants and add no query wires. \(\square\)

The interval induction is the same elementary mechanism as the read Lemma 6, with fresh position copies in place of its original-block pruning. It also shows why whole-output homogeneity alone is not the hypothesis: the degree labels must agree at every sum and product gate.

A hard component of the exact image

Corollary (compiled homogeneous gap). Use the complete original-input binary certificate family \(D_n\) of thm:compiled-word-query-gap, for \(n\ge1\), retaining every old variable and every clause, Boolean and commutator placeholder as a degree-one letter. Let \(H_n=(D_n)_{n+2}\) be its homogeneous degree-\(n+2\) component. There is no polynomial-size fixed-depth family of syntactically homogeneous binary noncommutative formulas computing \(H_n\). Moreover \(\mathcal L_{n+2}(H_n)\) has no polynomial-size fixed-depth binary arithmetic circuits, even allowing inhomogeneous circuits for that positional polynomial. Thus no generic polynomial-size, fixed-depth conversion of these exact images to the selected homogeneous formulas or positional circuits exists. The full \(D_n\)'s themselves remain small constant-depth formulas. No lower bound for all certificates, minimum proofs, or the original CNFs follows.

Proof. The exact-image theorem gives, for every \(b\in\{0,1\}^n\), \[ [z x_{b_1}\cdots x_{b_n}Y_z]H_n=[z x_{b_1}\cdots x_{b_n}Y_z]D_n =\mathbf1\{\textstyle\sum_i b_i=0\pmod3\}. \tag{2}\] The word has precisely the selected degree, so taking that component changes no coefficient in (2). If \(H_n\) had polynomial-size fixed-depth homogeneous binary formulas, the lemma would give polynomial-size fixed-depth \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\) circuits for these queries. Their fixed guard and placeholder positions need constants; each middle position selects \(x_0\) or \(x_1\) with one input bit. This contradicts the recorded MOD-3 consequence.

For the stronger positional-circuit assertion, assign the first position's \(z\) variable and last position's \(Y_z\) variable to one and every other variable in those blocks to zero. In middle block \(i\), set its \(x_0\) variable to \(1+b_i\), its \(x_1\) variable to \(b_i\), and every other original-letter copy to zero. The lifted polynomial is set-multilinear by definition, so this assignment evaluates it to (2). Any supplied binary arithmetic circuit, whether homogeneous or not, becomes an AND/XOR Boolean circuit with at most one additional input-negation layer. Hence a polynomial-size fixed-depth circuit for the positional lift is impossible as well. This proves the corollary.

Original resource ledger. The source CNF remains \(z,\neg z,x_0,\ldots,x_n\), with original encoding \(N=\Theta(n\log(n+2))\). The full tuple has \(n+2\) old variables and \((n+3)+(n+2)+\binom{n+2}{2}\) original placeholders. Thus its free alphabet has \(O(n^2)\) letters; the lift has \(O(n^3)\) variables. The original used/distinct sequent graph has size \(O(n^4)\), depth seven, and its full certificate has size and joint-degree ceiling \(O(n^{48})\), depth seventeen. The analysis object \(H_n\) has degree exactly \(n+2\), since its all-zero-bit word in (2) has nonzero coefficient. Polynomial size in \(N\) or in these parameters is polynomial in \(n\). The reductions to \(n\)-bit MOD-3 use constants and single bits or their negations, with no hidden hard preprocessing. The component and its positional lift are analysis polynomials, not asserted certificates for the original tuple; their smaller degree does not replace the full source-degree ledger.

Nonvacuity. The typed-query budget is witnessed, for example, by ordered products of arbitrarily many letters; degree-zero constants and typed zero polynomials are also covered. The entire explicit \(D_n\) family verifies the original-image budget, for every \(n\ge1\). Its source is an easy unit contradiction, but all nodes in the chosen proof are used and cedents distinct. This proves that the conversion's input class is nonempty at unbounded original encodings, not that the CNF is hard.

Degenerate cases. With \(D=0\), (1) is the scalar constant coefficient and the query is empty. With one alphabet letter the query is fixed. A typed zero formula gives zero on all queries, irrespective of its consistent declared degree. The guard construction requires \(n\ge1\); the zero-length guard exception in its original theorem is not used. An untyped sum such as \(x_0+x_0x_1\) is outside the lemma. Extension-field constants are excluded from its binary query bound, and a homogeneous output computed through inhomogeneous intermediates is excluded from its structural conversion premise. The positional-circuit lower bound has no such homogeneity premise on a hypothetical circuit.

Try the direct rank argument

Conversion attempt. For an inhomogeneous product, a length-\(D\) word can split among children in several ways. Introducing every degree label gives the exact identity \((P_1\cdots P_t)_D=\sum_{d_1+\cdots+d_t=D}(P_1)_{d_1}\cdots(P_t)_{d_t}\), but supplies neither a polynomial-size fixed-depth homogeneous formula nor inexpensive component circuits. The corollary disproves a generic such constructor even on the exact used/distinct compiler images. Relabeling gate degrees by their maxima instead keeps only a formal top component and loses the queried terms. Extending the alphabet by position copies first still needs to sum over these length choices; (2) applies to that proposed output too. These test two versions of the conversion, not all structural measures.

Direct-measure attempt. A rank proof need not construct the component. Define its candidate measure by taking the selected component, positionally lifting it and retaining the prescribed position alphabets, then forming the source's relative-rank matrix. The earlier affine component control already supplies a precise base-case failure if one applies the homogeneous formula inequality at the original product depth. Let \(G_t=\prod_{j=1}^{3t}(1+X_j)\). Retain two letters in each odd position, \(X_{3i-2},X_{3i-1}\), and in each even position \(X_{3i-1},X_{3i}\), with fresh position copies. The retained degree-\(2t\) matrix across odd/even positions is exactly \(B^{\otimes t}\), \(B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\), by the recorded strict-subsequence proof. Each block has two variables; for the source word \((1,-1)^t\), normalized rank is therefore one. For \(2t\ge8\) the depth-one comparison is within Proposition 15's range. The original \(G_t\) has product depth one and size \(O(t)\), while the source's homogeneous bound at that depth would tend to zero, \(O(t)\exp(-2t/10)\). The retained positional restriction actually has a product-depth-two formula, a product of the displayed three-term pair polynomials; no such formula for the full positional lift is asserted. Thus this example shows why applying the rank induction at unchanged product depth is invalid; it does not exclude every costed depth increase.

Taking absolute values and summing over all length allocations avoids that invalid step, but the available estimate pays their inventory. Already \(G_t\)'s degree-\(2t\) expansion has \(\binom{3t}{2t}\) factor selections. The previous rank-one-factor calculation cannot count just the original product gate; its added length choices can overwhelm the homogeneous imbalance discount. This is an identified failure of the attempted bound, not a lower bound on every improved rank analysis. No cancellation-sensitive, source-dependent replacement is established.
