Explicit generators for a unit row

Lemma (unit certificate generators). Let \(A\) be a commutative ring with identity, \(r\ge1\), and let \(F=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)\), \(c\in A^r\) satisfy \(F\cdot c=1\). Put \(K_{ij}=F_j e_i-F_i e_j\) for \(i<j\). For every \(h\in\ker F\), \[h=\sum_{i<j}(h_i c_j-h_j c_i)K_{ij}.\tag{1}\] Hence the pairwise Koszul vectors generate all syzygies. Also, in \(A[Y_1,\ldots,Y_r]\), \[(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)\cap(Y_1-F_1,\ldots,Y_r-F_r) =(Y_i(Y_j-F_j):1\le i,j\le r).\tag{2}\] No Noetherian hypothesis is needed for these explicit unit-row identities.

Proof. Each \(K_{ij}\) is killed by \(F\). At coordinate \(i\), the right side of (1) is \[\sum_{j\ne i}(h_i c_j-h_j c_i)F_j =h_i(1-F_i c_i)-c_i(-F_i h_i)=h_i.\] For (2), call the two left-side ideals \(I,J\). Their sum contains \(Y_i-(Y_i-F_i)=F_i\), hence contains \(1=\sum_i c_iF_i\). Comaximal ideals satisfy \(I\cap J=IJ\): if \(a+b=1\), \(a\in I,b\in J\), then for \(z\in I\cap J\), \(z=za+zb\in IJ\); the reverse inclusion is immediate. Here one explicit choice is \(a=\sum_i c_iY_i\), \(b=-\sum_i c_i(Y_i-F_i)\). Products of the displayed generators of \(I,J\) generate \(IJ\). \(\square\)

For \(A=K[x]\), the vectors \(K_{ij}\) have at most two nonzero coordinates, each an input polynomial up to sign. If the input degrees are at most \(d\), their coordinate degrees are at most \(d\); the scalar generators in (2) have total degree at most \(\max(2,d+1)\). The lists have polynomial-size circuit presentations from the input circuits. These are generator lists, not a claim of a free basis or small relation coefficients. Formula (1) explicitly uses the unknown or supplied certificate \(c\); if \(\deg h_i\le H\), \(\deg c_i\le C\), its coefficients have degree at most \(H+C\), not a bound independent of \(C\).

The kernels in (1) belong to the full polynomial ring with a unit input ideal. This does not contradict the historical graded Koszul counterexample, whose full proof was read: its homogeneous row-sum ideal is not a supplied unit row, and it concerns a fixed graded strand. Nor does (1) give bounded-degree PC closure, a filtered contraction, or cheap coefficients for arbitrary input syzygies. The earlier freeness result and unit-slice moves retain those same resource distinctions.

A constant filling can be the original certificate problem

Proposition (constant syzygy filling). Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_r\in K[x]\) over any field, \(r\ge1\). Adjoin \(F_0=0\) and define \(K_{ij}=F_j e_i-F_i e_j\) for \(0\le i<j\le r\). Consider a polynomial \[H(x,Z)=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}(x)Z_{ij}\] linear in its placeholders and with vector specialization \(\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}K_{ij}=e_0\). Circuits for such \(H\) and Hilbert-like certificates \(C(x,Y)\) for the original tuple \(F\) convert in both directions by placeholder renaming and specialization alone. These operations do not increase gate/wire size, depth, or the maximum degree of old-variable coefficients. The equivalence includes nonexistence on either side.

Proof. From \(C=\sum_{j=1}^r c_jY_j\), use \(H=\sum_j c_jZ_{0j}\), realized by renaming placeholders in the supplied circuit. Since \(F_0=0\), \(K_{0j}=F_j e_0\); the vector specialization is \(e_0\sum_jc_jF_j=e_0\).

Conversely, the coordinate-zero component of any filling identity is \(\sum_{j=1}^r a_{0j}F_j=1\); pairs with \(i>0\) contribute nothing there. Set \(Z_{0j}=Y_j\), and every \(Z_{ij}\) with \(i>0\) to zero in the circuit for \(H\). Its polynomial is \(C=\sum_j a_{0j}Y_j\), a certificate for \(F\). No syntactic placeholder-linearity is needed: polynomial Hilbert-likeness suffices. These are input substitutions by variables and constants, so they have the stated circuit and coefficient-degree costs. Unused placeholders need not be instantiated; declaring a full placeholder list would add only its polynomial listing cost. All original generators, including Boolean axioms when present, remain in \(F\). \(\square\)

The equation \(F_0=0\) adds no constraint, and the target \(e_0\) is a constant syzygy. Thus even a constant target and an explicit low-degree generator list can retain the original certificate circuit problem. This is a reduction, not a hard family: it proves no lower bound and makes no statement that every unpadded filling problem is difficult. It also leaves the source's proposed final invariance step open; it does not refute that research program.

Nonvacuity. For \(F=(x,1-x)\), the certificate \(Y_1+Y_2\) gives the filling \(Z_{01}+Z_{02}\) over every field; its vector specialization is \(e_0\). Adding unused variables gives unbounded presentations. For the actual matrix and CNF tuples the full unit-ideal hypothesis holds because Boolean inconsistency supplies a formal certificate, but no small circuit for it is assumed. Their original degrees are bounded, so the explicit generator lists meet the advertised small-presentation condition at unbounded instance sizes.

Degenerate cases. With \(r=1\) and a unit \(F_1\), the first kernel is zero and the empty pair-generator list is correct. The zero syzygy uses zero coefficients. If every \(F_j=0\) over a nonzero field, neither a certificate nor a filling of \(e_0\) exists, so the second proposition does not assume its conclusion. The first lemma requires a unit row; mere finite generation or a consistent input tuple does not meet that premise. Dummy zero generators are allowed in the second proposition and do not remove any original Boolean equation.
