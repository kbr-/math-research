Conditional seed contraction

Statement: seed law. Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional binary vector space, \(R\) uniform on \(V\), and \(\pi_i:V\to G_i\) surjective linear maps to literal binary polynomial input spans. In a fixed parent order, expose \(\pi_iR=y_i\) for \(i<t\), on a nonempty fibre. Put \(B_{<t}=(\pi_i)_{i<t}\), choose \(R_0\) in this fibre, and set

\[p=\pi_tR_0,\qquad H_t=\pi_t\ker B_{<t},\qquad W_i=\operatorname{im}\pi_i^*\subseteq V^*.\]

Then the conditional next row is uniform on \(p+H_t\), and

\[\operatorname{codim}_{G_t}H_t=\dim\bigl(W_t\cap\sum_{i<t}W_i\bigr).\]

In particular this codimension is at most the cut rank \(\dim((\sum_{i<t}W_i)\cap(\sum_{i\ge t}W_i))\). These spaces are coefficient observations, not the old-variable input spans themselves; \(W_i\) is defined by the actual projection map. For the canonical echelon retractions, it consists exactly of the seed-coordinate functionals at that parent's pivot positions.

Statement: paid gain. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J\) containing the old Boolean axioms. Let \(A\) have degree at most \(a\), target tuple \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), and let \(G=\operatorname{span}(g_i)\). Take ordinary multiplier spaces \(U_{K-\delta}\subseteq V_{K-\delta}\), \(U_K\subseteq V_K\), with \(K\ge\delta\), satisfying \(G U_{K-\delta}\subseteq U_K\), and assume \(a+K+2\delta\le D\). No nesting or intermediate-cutoff closure is required. Fix an affine fibre \(p+H\subseteq G\), with basis \(h_1,\ldots,h_s\) of \(H\), and sample \(q=p+\sum_\ell\xi_\ell h_\ell\) uniformly. For the two endpoints write

\[c_A(k)=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\in U_k\mapsto([Ag_if])_i\bigr),\qquad \mathcal R=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\in U_{K-\delta}\mapsto([Ag_idf])_{i,d}\bigr),\quad d\in\{p,h_1,\ldots,h_s\}.\]

With \(A'=A(1+q)\), the conditional expectation satisfies

\[\mathbb E_q c_{A'}(K)\le c_A(K)-\tfrac12\mathcal R.\]

The covered-direction rank \(\mathcal R\) need not equal \(c_A(K-\delta)\). The conditional estimate is for this same conditioned domain and fibre; it provides no separate optimized choices for different histories.

Proof of the seed law. The fibre is \(R_0+\ker B_{<t}\). A linear map sends its uniform measure to uniform measure on its image coset, since every image point has the same number of preimages. A linear functional on \(G_t\) annihilates \(H_t\) precisely when its pullback under \(\pi_t\) belongs to \((\ker B_{<t})^\perp=\sum_{i<t}W_i\). The injective map \(\pi_t^*\) identifies this annihilator with the displayed intersection, proving its dimension formula. Since \(W_t\subseteq\sum_{i\ge t}W_i\), the cut inequality follows. Canonical retractions read pivot coordinates and expand the chosen basis; their duals span exactly those coordinate functionals. This is the elementary cut-space decomposition isolated from the near-half proof, not a new general linear-algebra principle.

Generic affine pencil. On \(U_{K-\delta}\), let \(M_df=([Ag_idf])_i\), and form \(M(\lambda)=M_p+\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell M_{h_\ell}\) over \(E=\mathbb F_2(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)\). Scalar extension preserves the pseudoideal's paid multiplication closure by finite-dimensional linear algebra. The pencil kernel equals the common kernel of all \(M_d\) after scalar extension. The common kernel is contained in the pencil kernel. Conversely, take \(f\in U_{K-\delta}\otimes E\) with \(Ag_iL_0f\in J_E\) for every \(i\), where \(L_j=p+\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2^j}h_\ell\). The same rational-coefficient \(f\) is used throughout, without applying Frobenius to it. Each binary polynomial \(d\) in this span has a Boolean certificate for \(d^2+d\) through \(2\delta\). Multiplying the current relation by \(L_j\), and subtracting \(Ag_if(L_j^2+L_{j+1})\), therefore gives the next relation. Every step has degree at most \(a+(K-\delta)+\delta+2\delta=a+K+2\delta\); completed relations are reused, so the degree does not grow with the number of Frobenius steps. For \(j=0,\ldots,s\), the coefficient matrix has columns \(1,\lambda_1^{2^j},\ldots,\lambda_s^{2^j}\). Its determinant is nonzero: each permutation chooses a distinct exponent assignment to the independent \(\lambda\)'s, with the omitted exponent row identifying the constant column, so its monomials are distinct. Inversion recovers all \(Ag_idf\in J_E\). This also covers \(s=0\), where the matrix is \((1)\); zero or dependent \(p\) causes no difficulty. Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}_E M(\lambda)=\mathcal R\).
