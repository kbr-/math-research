Marked curve recovery

Statement. Let \(X/k\) be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve over a finite field, of genus \(g\). Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) be distinct rational points and \(A,G\) divisors of nonnegative degrees \(a,m\), whose supports avoid those points. Write \(L(B)=H^0(X,\mathcal O(B))\) as rational functions with \((u)+B\ge0\), together with zero. Let \(t\ge0\), and assume

\[a\ge t+g,\qquad n>a+m+t.\tag{1}\]

Given \(y_i=f(P_i)+e_i\), with unknown \(f\in L(G)\) and at most \(t\) nonzero \(e_i\), solve the homogeneous linear system

\[\lambda\in L(A),\quad N\in L(A+G),\qquad N(P_i)=\lambda(P_i)y_i\quad(1\le i\le n).\tag{2}\]

There is a nonzero solution, every nonzero solution has \(\lambda\ne0\), and every such solution satisfies \(N=\lambda f\) as rational functions and \(\lambda(P_i)e_i=0\) at every coordinate. Recover \(f\) by solving the second linear system

\[h\in L(G),\qquad \lambda(P_i)h(P_i)=N(P_i)\quad(1\le i\le n).\tag{3}\]

This system has the unique solution \(h=f\). If a further rational point \(R\) avoids the evaluation points and the supports of \(A,G\), and \(a\ge t+2g\), one may instead impose \(\lambda(R)=1\) in (2). That affine system is consistent, and every solution has the same recovery properties and satisfies \(N(R)=f(R)\). The value \(f(R)\) is recovered without receiving it. With bases and evaluation matrices for \(L(A),L(G),L(A+G)\) supplied, both variants use \(O(n^3)\) field operations and \(O(n^2)\) field elements of memory. Basis and evaluation construction is a separate input cost until an explicit family is supplied below.

Proof: existence. Let \(E=\sum_{e_i\ne0}P_i\), of degree \(e\le t\). Riemann–Roch gives \(\ell(A-E)\ge a-e+1-g\ge1\); this is cor:curve-locator-budget, whose full proof and defining hypotheses were reread. A nonzero \(\lambda\in L(A-E)\) vanishes as a rational function at the erroneous coordinates, since the support of \(A\) avoids them. Taking \(N=\lambda f\in L(A+G)\) solves (2). In the marked case, the same recorded locator lemma gives \(\lambda(R)=1\), since \(a\ge e+2g\). Its proof uses \(H^1(A-E-R)=0\) and the evaluation exact sequence. The algorithm need not know \(E\); it solves (2) using only \(y\) and the supplied spaces.

Proof: every solution works. A nonzero \(u\in L(B)\), with \(\deg B=b\) and with the evaluation points outside its support, has at most \(b\) zeros among those points: its effective divisor \((u)+B\) has degree \(b\). If \(\lambda=0\) in (2), then \(N\) vanishes at all \(n>a+m\) points, hence \(N=0\). For any solution, \(N-\lambda f\in L(A+G)\) vanishes at all \(n-e\ge n-t>a+m\) correct positions. It is therefore zero identically. Substitution in (2) proves \(\lambda(P_i)e_i=0\). If \(h\) also solves (3), the function \(\lambda(h-f)\in L(A+G)\) vanishes at all \(n>a+m\) points, so is zero. The function field is a domain and \(\lambda\ne0\), hence \(h=f\). This is injectivity on the clean message space, even though multiplication by \(\lambda\) need not be invertible on all evaluation vectors. In the marked case, evaluating \(N=\lambda f\) at \(R\) gives the asserted protected value.

What is repaired and what survives

Set \(U=\operatorname{ev}L(A)\), \(C=\operatorname{ev}L(G)\), \(H=\operatorname{ev}L(A+G)\) on the \(n\) received coordinates, and let \(Q:k^n\to k^n/H\) be the explicit quotient tester obtained by elimination. Then \(U*C\subseteq H\). For an error supported on at most \(t\) positions, \(H\) has no nonzero vector on those positions, because its minimum distance is at least \(n-a-m>t\). Thus \(Q\) detects every possible nonzero product \(\lambda*e\). Its error-action rank is at most \(t\), whereas evaluation on \(L(A)\) is injective and \(\dim U=\ell(A)\ge a+1-g>t\). Multiplication by every nonzero locator is injective on \(C\), by the same zero bound. These verify each hypothesis of thm:bilinear-error-recovery from divisor data, without assuming a successful locator.

Equation (2) is the explicit lift of \(Q(\lambda*y)=0\): \(N\) witnesses membership in \(H\). In the marked case use the evaluation algebra on \(P_1,\ldots,P_n,R\) to view \(\lambda\) as one multiplier annihilating the defect vector \((e_1,\ldots,e_n,0)\) while fixing the \(R\)-coordinate idempotent. It fixes the protected value and permits full message recovery. The extra coordinate is not supplied to the decoding algorithm; it is evaluated after (3).

The multiplicative budget is \(L(A)L(G)\subseteq L(A+G)\), with total pole-divisor degree \(a+m\); the observable detection margin is \(n-t>a+m\). On a one-point curve \(F_b=L(bP_\infty)\), multiplication obeys \(F_bF_c\subseteq F_{b+c}\). Consequently a fixed multiplier of pole order at most \(a\) carries a source expression of pole budget \(B\) through \(a+B\), using the same completed-product argument as certified repair composition. This is the geometric provider of the repair principle, with the bilinear theorem as its precise existing interface. It is not an identification of pole filtration with total polynomial degree or of this evaluation algebra with an unsatisfiable bounded-PC quotient. The arbitrary-error finite-grid contraction theorem is not invoked on the curve.

An explicit positive-genus family

Statement. For every \(s=2^r\ge4\), work in a supplied representation of \(k=\mathbb F_{s^2}\) and take the projective Hermitian curve

\[Y^sZ+YZ^s=X^{s+1},\qquad P_\infty=(0:1:0),\qquad R=(0:0:1).\]

Use all affine rational points except \(R\) as the received coordinates. Put

\[n=s^3-1,\quad g=s(s-1)/2,\quad m=s^3/4-1,\quad t=s^3/8-1,\quad a=t+2g,\quad G=mP_\infty,\quad A=aP_\infty.\tag{4}\]

The marked algorithm recovers every \(f\in L(mP_\infty)\) after arbitrary errors at at most \(t\) coordinates and produces a locator with \(\lambda(R)=1\). The message dimension is \(m-g+1>0\). Points, bases and evaluation matrices can be constructed in \(O(n^3)\) field operations with \(O(n^2)\) memory, including the subsequent two-system decoder. Thus no Riemann–Roch-space oracle is hidden for this family. Field construction itself is excluded by the supplied-field representation; these are field-operation, not bit-complexity, bounds.

Geometry and point count. The three homogeneous partial derivatives are respectively \(X^s,Z^s,Y^s\) up to nonzero signs, so they never vanish simultaneously at a projective point. The curve is smooth over the algebraic closure. A smooth plane curve is geometrically integral: distinct positive-degree components would meet by projective Bézout and create a singularity, and a repeated component would also be singular. Its degree is \(s+1\), so the plane-curve genus formula gives \(g=s(s-1)/2\). Both formulas are standard imported curve facts, also stated for this family in Høholdt–van Lint–Pellikaan, Example 2.74; their specific smoothness and count checks are supplied here.

The only point at infinity is \(P_\infty\). For each \(x\in\mathbb F_{s^2}\), \(x^{s+1}\) lies in \(\mathbb F_s\). The trace \(y\mapsto y^s+y\) is a nonzero \(\mathbb F_s\)-linear map onto \(\mathbb F_s\), with kernel of size \(s\): it is not the zero map since the polynomial \(Y^s+Y\) has at most \(s\) roots in a field of size \(s^2\). Thus there are exactly \(s\) affine \(y\)'s for each \(x\), hence \(s^3\) affine points. Remove \(R\) to obtain \(n\).

Explicit bases and filtration. In the chart \(Y=1\) at infinity put \(u=X/Y,v=Z/Y\). The equation is \(v+v^s=u^{s+1}\). Since its derivative with respect to \(v\) is one, \(u\) is a uniformizer; \(1+v^{s-1}\) is a unit and \(\operatorname{ord}_{P_\infty}v=s+1\). Therefore the affine coordinates \(x=X/Z=u/v\), \(y=Y/Z=1/v\) have pole orders \(s,s+1\). The affine coordinate ring is \(k[x,y]/(y^s+y-x^{s+1})\), free over \(k[x]\) on \(1,y,\ldots,y^{s-1}\), by monic division. A rational function with poles only at infinity is regular on this smooth affine complement, hence lies in that coordinate ring. The weights \(si+(s+1)j\), for \(i\ge0,0\le j

Budget and construction. The identity \(a=t+2g\) supplies marked locator existence. The detection margin is

\[n-(a+m+t)=s^3/2-s^2+s+2>0\qquad(s\ge4).\tag{6}\]

Also \(m>2g-2\), since \(m-(2g-2)=s^3/4-s^2+s+1>0\) for \(s\ge4\). Riemann–Roch therefore gives the stated message dimension. List field elements in the supplied representation and enumerate the at most \(s^4\) affine pairs, testing the equation by repeated powering in \(O(\log s)\) operations per pair. This costs \(O(s^4\log s)\subseteq O(n^3)\), and only the accepted points need storage. Enumerate the integer pairs in (5) for \(b=a,m,a+m\). Each list has at most \(b+1=O(n)\) elements. At each accepted point, precompute powers of \(x\) and \(y\) through the largest needed exponents, then form all basis evaluations; this takes \(O(n^2)\) field operations and storage. Forming (2), its normalization row and (3) and solving them takes \(O(n^3)\). No numerical test is used to establish these asymptotic statements.

Nonvacuity. For the general recovery budget and explicit Hermitian budget, (4) gives a family for every \(s=2^r\ge4\). At \(s=4\), symbolic substitution gives \(n=63,g=6,m=15,t=7,a=19\), message dimension ten and margin \(63-(19+15+7)=22\). These are arithmetic consequences of (4), not reported computer output. The message space includes one, and every error pattern of the stated size is covered. The genus and length grow unboundedly, and \(s^3-1>s^2+1\), so this is not the previous projective-line evaluation construction on the same alphabet. No claim of a new code or superior decoding radius is made.

Degenerate cases. At \(t=0\), the proof still works; the homogeneous variant has a nonzero solution and the marked variant has its normalized solution. The zero message is recovered uniquely because the product argument uses \(\lambda\ne0\), not \(f\ne0\). Empty coordinate sets cannot satisfy (1). Repeated points violate the distinct-point hypothesis and cannot be counted as extra zeros. A protected point in the error support is excluded; the algorithm does not promise normalization there. Divisors meeting evaluation or protected points are excluded because ordinary rational-function evaluation would not supply the stated coordinates. Genus zero specializes to the recorded polynomial locator mechanism rather than a new result. A nonzero locator may vanish on some correct coordinates; recovery still follows from the whole product identity, not coordinatewise division.
