The exact certificate system

Use binary matrices \(A\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times n}\), \(B\in\mathbb F_2^{n\times m}\), \(m>n\ge1\), with generators \(F_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-(AB)_{ij}\) and every entry's Boolean generator. Index this full list by \(F_0,\ldots,F_q\), choosing \(F_0=1-(AB)_{11}\). A Hilbert-like refutation is a polynomial \[ C(x,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^q c_i(x)Y_i, \qquad \sum_{i=0}^q c_i(x)F_i(x)=1 \tag{1} \] as a formal identity. Placeholders for Boolean equations are included among the \(Y_i\), not discarded.

Garlík–Gryaznov–Lu–Santhanam–Tzameret, Definition 13, explicitly uses Boolean IPS and formal identities. Definitions 16 and 24 specify the rank systems. Normalize a unit slice by explicit syzygies

Lemma (unit slice certificates). Let \(R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) over any field, and let \(\rho:R\to R\) be a \(k\)-algebra retraction, so \(\rho^2=\rho\). Suppose \(\rho(F_0)=1\) and (1) is a Hilbert-like certificate for the full list \(F_0,\ldots,F_q\). Then the same certificate coset has a representative \(C'\) with \[ \rho(C')=Y_0, \tag{2} \] where \(\rho\) fixes the placeholders. If \(\rho\) fixes some variables and sets the others to field constants, a size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) circuit for \(C\), and a shared generator description of size \(G\), depth \(\Delta\), yield a circuit for \(C'\) of size \(O((q+1)S+G+q+1)\) and depth at most \(\max(d,\Delta)+4\), in the unbounded-fanin arithmetic model. Under that coordinate-restriction hypothesis, if the original coefficient degrees are at most \(L\) and generator degrees at most \(g\), the new coefficient degrees are at most \(L+g\).

Proof. For \(i>0\), set \(h_i=\rho(c_i)\) and define \[ c'_i=c_i-h_iF_0,\qquad c'_0=c_0+\sum_{i=1}^q h_iF_i. \tag{3} \] The two new contributions for each \(i\) cancel in \(\sum_i c'_iF_i\), so the formal identity remains one. Equivalently, \[ C'=C+\sum_{i=1}^q h_i(F_iY_0-F_0Y_i) \tag{4} \] adds explicit Koszul syzygies and preserves linearity in placeholders. Because \(\rho(h_i)=h_i\), \(\rho(c'_i)=0\) for \(i>0\). Applying \(\rho\) to (1) gives \(\rho(c'_0)=\sum_i\rho(c_i)\rho(F_i)=1\), proving (2).

Even if the given circuit is not syntactically linear in placeholders, its polynomial is Hilbert-like, so evaluating \(Y=e_i\) computes \(c_i\). Make at most \(q\) such copies, then substitute the coordinate restriction to compute \(h_i\); these steps do not increase depth. Formula (4) combines them with the generator circuits using a bounded number of addition/multiplication layers. Shared generator gates give the asserted polynomial size and the conservative four-layer increase. Under a coordinate restriction, degrees of \(h_i\) do not exceed \(L\); (3) proves the coefficient degree bound. This degree claim is not asserted for a general high-degree retraction. \(\square\)

Matrix application. Let \(\rho\) set every entry of \(B\) to zero and leave \(A\) formal. Then \(\rho(F_0)=1\), so every Hilbert-like refutation can be changed at the stated cost to one whose whole restriction is \(Y_0\). All other matrix and Boolean coefficient restrictions vanish, and the chosen coefficient is one. This statement includes the \(A\)-Boolean coefficients even though their generators remain nonzero formal polynomials on the slice. Their effects are moved by the same syzygies; they are not thrown away.

Thus extracting a determinant from a designated coefficient, or from the full restricted coefficient vector at \(B=0\), cannot be justified by saying that every certificate must visibly retain it there. The normalization uses the circuit one is given and has only polynomial overhead and constant extra depth. It does not construct a small certificate from nothing, or rule out a feature of the full coset that remains hard away from that slice. An exact-depth statement must of course retain the displayed additive depth cost.

Boolean coefficients cannot simply be killed by a formal path

Proposition (Boolean path rigidity). Let \(R\) be an integral domain, and suppose a polynomial substitution sends each original variable to \(\phi_i\in R\). If it makes every Boolean generator vanish individually, \(\phi_i^2-\phi_i=0\), then every \(\phi_i\) is zero or one. In particular a substitution into a polynomial ring over a field with that property carries no nonconstant matrix indeterminates.

Proof. For each \(i\), \(\phi_i(\phi_i-1)=0\). The integral-domain property forces one of the two factors to vanish. \(\square\)

This controls only the demand that a multiplicative formal substitution remove every Boolean term individually. A valid full certificate may have cancellation among its Boolean and matrix contributions. Targets with nontrivial idempotents, such as a Boolean quotient algebra, also evade the domain hypothesis; then formal polynomial circuit hardness is not automatically the same question. The preceding finite-jet selector control separately shows why bounded-order tests on cube points do not recover the missing full formal identity. We do not elevate these convenient-test failures into a theorem against all extractions.
