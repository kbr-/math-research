The source and the appropriate budget

Pang v1, Section 8 identifies a favorable near-middle regime, while Lemmas 4.1–4.2 give the precise rank recurrence. Here we use the binary specialization, already proved in the weighted adaptive interface, with initial weight one. Put \[ N_v(k)=\sum_{i=0}^k\binom vi,\qquad \eta_K(\delta)=\frac12\frac{N_v(K-\delta)}{N_v(K)}. \] For a degree-\(\delta\) tuple and \(t\) factors, its error rank is at most \(H(1-\eta_K(\delta))^t\), where \(H=\dim V_K/(C_D\cap V_K)\), provided \(K+\delta t+\delta\le D\). All groups will use this same domain and ceiling. Choose coefficients in acyclic order and choose the common multiplier only once after every group. Repeated rank-retaining multipliers, and their \(3K\) reserve, are not required for this construction.

A bound for the binomial lower tail

We first prove the estimate needed for the numerical budget. Let \(K=\lfloor v/2-u\rfloor\), \(u\ge0\), let \(\delta\ge1\) be integral, and assume \(a=u+\delta+2=o(v)\). Uniformly in such sequences, for all sufficiently large \(v\), \[ \frac{N_v(K-\delta)}{N_v(K)} \ge \exp\left[-32e\,\delta\frac{a+\sqrt v}{v}\right]. \] To see this, for each integer \(k\in[K-\delta+1,K]\), write \(b_j=\binom vj\) and put \(q=\lfloor v/(16(a+\sqrt v))\rfloor\). Then \(q\to\infty\), \(q\le\sqrt v/16\), and \(k-q\ge0\) eventually. For \(0\le i\le q\), \[ \frac{b_{k-i}}{b_k} =\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{k-t}{v-k+t+1} \ge\left(\frac{v/2-a-q}{v/2+a+q+1}\right)^q\ge e^{-1}. \] For the last inequality, write the fraction as \(1-z\). Eventually \(z\le1/2\), \(z\le(4a+4q+2)/v\), and \(-q\log(1-z)\le2qz\le1\), since \(qa/v\le1/16\), \(q^2/v\le1/256\), and \(q/v\to0\). Consequently \[ \frac{b_k}{N_v(k)}\le\frac{e}{q+1} \le16e\frac{a+\sqrt v}{v}=o(1). \] Use \(N_v(k-1)/N_v(k)=1-b_k/N_v(k)\), apply \(\log(1-z)\ge-2z\) when \(z\le1/2\), and multiply over the \(\delta\) consecutive cutoffs. This proves the displayed bound. It is a lower bound on an ambient dimension ratio, not an identification with the actual quotient's Hilbert function.

The moving cutoff budget

Lemma (moving cutoff budget). Fix integers \(\ell\ge2\), \(r\ge1\), and a constant \(A>0\). Let \(1\le M(v)\le v^A\), and let \(s(v)\ge0\) satisfy \[ s(v)=o\left(\frac{v}{(\log v)^{\ell-2}}\right). \] Set \(D=\lfloor v/2-s(v)\rfloor\), \(K=D-\lceil v^{1/3}\rceil\), \(\Delta_0=r\), and recursively \[ t_j=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2\ell(M+1))}{\eta_K(\Delta_{j-1})}\right\rceil, \qquad \Delta_j=\Delta_{j-1}t_j\quad(1\le j\le\ell). \] For all sufficiently large \(v\), these parameters are defined, \(\Delta_j=O_{A,r,\ell}((\log v)^j)\) for \(j<\ell\), and \(t_\ell,\Delta_\ell=v^{o(1)}\). They satisfy \[ \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}M_j(1-\eta_K(\Delta_{j-1}))^{t_j}<\frac12 \quad\text{whenever }0\le M_j\le M, \] and \(K+C\Delta_\ell\le D\) for every fixed \(C>0\), eventually. The asymptotic threshold may depend on \(A,r,\ell\) and the specified deficit sequence.

Proof. The cutoff has \(u=s+\lceil v^{1/3}\rceil+O(1)\). Inductively, for \(j\le\ell-1\), assume \(\Delta_{j-1}=O((\log v)^{j-1})\). In the tail estimate the exponent is \[ O\left(\frac{\Delta_{j-1}(s+v^{1/3}+\Delta_{j-1}+\sqrt v)}{v}\right)=o(1), \] because \(j-1\le\ell-2\). Thus the ratio tends to one, \(\eta_K(\Delta_{j-1})\to1/2\), and \(t_j=O(\log v)\). This proves the induction through \(\ell-1\). At the final layer, \(\Delta_{\ell-1}=O((\log v)^{\ell-1})\), so the same exponent is \(o(\log v)\). Therefore \(\eta_K(\Delta_{\ell-1})\ge v^{-o(1)}\), whence \(t_\ell,\Delta_\ell=v^{o(1)}\). All input degrees are eventually below \(K\), justifying each step of the recursion in order. The definition of \(t_j\) gives each summand at most \(M/[2\ell(M+1)]\), so their sum is strictly below one half. Finally \(C\Delta_\ell=o(v^{1/3})\), proving the reserve. \(\square\)

For depth two this permits any \(s=o(v)\); for depth three it permits \(s=o(v/\log v)\). For general fixed depth, one example is \(s=v/((\log v)^{\ell-2}\log\log v)\). This is much larger than \(\sqrt v\), yet the last approximation degree is subpolynomial. The fixed-fraction obstruction is unchanged: its positive constant gap does not meet this hypothesis. The lemma makes no sharpness claim about the moving threshold.

One common multiplier for the complete source

Theorem (moving cutoff source exclusion). Keep the preceding regime and choose fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/(3\ell)\), with \(h=\lfloor v^\epsilon\rfloor\). Let \(\Gamma_v\subseteq F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) contain the Boolean axioms and satisfy \(1\notin C_D(\Gamma_v)\). Augment it by at most \(v^A\) complete binary ENS blocks in levels \(1,\ldots,\ell\), all with accuracy \(h\) and distinct fresh Boolean coefficient variables. Every input must be a degree-at-most-\(r\) old polynomial plus a constant linear combination of products belonging to blocks in strictly lower levels. Earlier coefficients may occur through those products, not separately. Shared products refer to the same block. Then the augmented system has no PC refutation through degree \(B=\lfloor v^{1/3}\rfloor\), for sufficiently large \(v\). No unproved old degree lower bound is asserted by this hypothesis.

Proof of coefficient selection. Choose the numerical lemma's \(M=\max(1,\#\text{blocks})\), and let \(H=\dim V_K/(C_D\cap V_K)>0\). Process the blocks by level. Once the earlier coefficient variables have been assigned binary constants, each current input has degree at most \(\Delta_{j-1}\): old terms have degree at most \(r\), and sums do not raise the maximum. Apply the already proved rank-contraction step to the current tuple for \(t_j\) rows. Set those coefficient rows to its chosen binary combinations and every unused row to zero. The lemma gives \(t_j\le h\) eventually. The resulting product has degree at most \(\Delta_j\), and its error map on \(V_K\) has rank at most \(H(1-\eta_K(\Delta_{j-1}))^{t_j}\). The contraction ceiling \(K+\Delta_j+\Delta_{j-1}\le D\) follows from the reserve. A zero or empty tuple has zero error rank and causes no exception.

All choices are global constant substitutions for the actual source variables. Later choices never alter earlier input tuples or errors. Each error map annihilates \(C_D\cap V_K\) by bounded multiplication, and their summed ranks are below \(H/2\). Choose a nonzero class in their joint kernel. Multilinearize its representative to obtain one \(f\), \(\deg f\le K\), with \(f\notin C_D\), and with \(f\) times every specialized companion in \(C_D\). The Boolean multilinearization certificates fit the same degree bound. This is a single final dimension count; no repeated weight selection is used.

Proof replay and original costs. Suppose the original augmented source had a degree-\(B\) PC refutation. Apply the one constant assignment to its entire proof, keeping old variables fixed. Degrees cannot increase. Every specialized coefficient-domain axiom vanishes. For an active old axiom \(Q\), \(fQ\in C_D\) because \(Q\in C_D\) and \(\deg f+\deg Q\le K+B\le D\). For each active companion this membership was just arranged. Multiply every specialized proof line by \(f\). Linear combinations are preserved; multiplication by an old variable stays through \(K+B\le D\), while multiplication by an assigned coefficient is scalar multiplication. Inductively every weighted line lies in \(C_D\). The final line gives \(f\in C_D\), a contradiction.

Original carried degrees are not reset by padding. Before specialization, the product-degree recurrence is \(W_0=r\), \(W_j\le h(W_{j-1}+1)\), so \(W_j\le(r+j)h^j\); companions add at most \(W_{j-1}\). At fixed depth their maximum is \(O_{r,\ell}(h^\ell)=o(v^{1/3})\) under the stated strict bound on \(\epsilon\). Thus every original helper axiom fits the claimed source scale. The replay argument uses the original proof ceiling \(B\), not an artificially reduced cofactor budget after coefficients are zeroed. \(\square\)

Completeness is retained to specify the canonical source, not because the proof requires every companion to be present. The upper bound on \(\epsilon\) ensures all original helper degrees fit below \(B\); the contradiction itself only replays axioms active in the supposed degree-\(B\) proof. The theorem requires no input-slot bound for its algebraic argument, only finite tuples and polynomially many blocks; a polynomial proof encoding supplies those finiteness conditions. It permits arbitrary overlap and actual affine parent mixtures within its declared product-only grammar. It does not assert that every presentation of Frege automatically has this grammar or coherent boundary. The existing binary compiler can be used only with its recorded hypotheses and with all of its original costs at most \(B\).
