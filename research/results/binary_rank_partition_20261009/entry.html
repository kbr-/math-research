The scalar identity and its scope

Sokal, equation (1.3) and Sections 4.3–4.4, defines the multivariate rank-generating polynomial and proves deletion–contraction and parallel reduction. For one-dimensional homogeneous feature spaces, the formal sum \(\sum_S t^{\dim L_S}\prod_{e\in S}v_e\) is that invariant at \(q=t^{-1}\). General higher-dimensional feature spaces instead give a subspace polymatroid on feature labels. These scalar identities count subsets with multiplicity; they do not assert an identity of our polynomial image spaces.

Actual image deletion

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J_D\) containing Booleanity. Let \(w\) have degree at most \(b\), a fixed parent prefix \(Q\) have degree at most \(p_0\), the actual tuple \((g_i)_{i=1}^r\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), and the binary polynomial features \(P_e\), \(e\in E\), have degree at most \(\delta\). Let \(U\subseteq V_K\), and \(h\ge1\), with \[ b+p_0+K+(h+1)\delta\le D. \tag{1} \] For a set \(S\), put \(P_S=\prod_{e\in S}P_e\), including \(P_\varnothing=1\). Define \[ \Lambda(v)=([wQg_i v])_{i=1}^r\in(V_D/J_D)^r,\qquad \mathcal A_j(E)=\operatorname{span}\{\Lambda(P_Sf):S\subseteq E,\ |S|\le j,\ f\in U\}. \tag{2} \] Only products within (1) are used. These are actual feature-word images. If the parent inputs are affine combinations of the features, (2) is an enlargement containing the input-word image, not necessarily equal to it.

Lemma (filtered word deletion). For \(e\in E\), let \(E'=E\setminus\{e\}\). The map \[ C_e:\mathcal A_{h-1}(E')\longrightarrow (V_D/J_D)^r/\mathcal A_h(E'),\qquad \Lambda(v)\longmapsto\Lambda(P_ev)+\mathcal A_h(E') \tag{3} \] is well-defined when \(v\) is represented by the indicated words of length at most \(h-1\). It gives \[ \dim\mathcal A_h(E)=\dim\mathcal A_h(E')+\operatorname{rank}C_e. \tag{4} \] In particular the contraction term is the additional feature image modulo the deletion image, not its unquotiented dimension or simply the rank of a contracted affine arrangement.

Proof. The representative space for \(v\) has degree at most \(K+(h-1)\delta\). If two representatives have the same \(\Lambda\)-image, each coordinate difference \(wQg_i(v-v')\) belongs to \(J_D\) and has degree at most \(b+p_0+K+h\delta\le D-\delta\). Multiplication by \(P_e\) is therefore legitimate by bounded absorption through (1). It proves descent before even taking the extra quotient in (3).

Partition the generating sets in (2) according to whether they contain \(e\). This gives \[ \mathcal A_h(E)=\mathcal A_h(E')+ \operatorname{span}\{\Lambda(P_eP_Sf):S\subseteq E',\ |S|\le h-1,\ f\in U\}. \] The second summand projected modulo the first is exactly \(\operatorname{im}C_e\); the dimension formula for a quotient proves (4). No idempotence, orthogonality or same-cutoff invariance of \(\mathcal A_h(E')\) is assumed. Binary Booleanity is needed only when comparing set words with arbitrary repeated input-word expansions: reducing repetitions then fits their original product degree. \(\square\)

The source shortening theorem still requires its own extra Booleanity reserve \(b+p_0+K+(h+2)\delta\le D\); using equal-span prefix compression retains its stated \((2h+1)\delta\) reserve. Formula (4) lowers neither. It also leaves the denominator \(\dim[wU]\) unchanged. Replacing the quotient in (3) by an independently chosen proper old quotient would be an additional theorem, not deletion–contraction bookkeeping.

A complete coordinate calibration

Proposition (coordinate word saturation). In the full Boolean function algebra on \(v\ge1\) variables, take \(U=V_K\), \(K\ge0\), the scalar identity map for \(\Lambda\), and features \(P_i=x_i\) on a set \(E\) of size \(s\). Then \(\mathcal A_h(E)\) has the monomial basis \[ \{x_R: |R|\le K+h,\ |R\setminus E|\le K\}, \] and hence dimension \[ d_h(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(K,v-s)}\binom{v-s}{j} \sum_{i=0}^{\min(s,K+h-j)}\binom{s}{i}. \tag{5} \] For \(s<v\) and \(h\ge1\), adding one coordinate feature has exact increment \[ d_h(s+1)-d_h(s)=\binom{v-s-1}{K} \sum_{i=0}^{\min(s,h-1)}\binom{s}{i}, \tag{6} \] with out-of-range binomial coefficients zero. If \(v-s\le K\), the image already equals \(V_{\min(v,K+h)}\), and each additional independent feature has zero increment.

Proof. Multiplying a squarefree degree-at-most-\(K\) monomial by at most \(h\) coordinate features produces a monomial meeting both stated inequalities. Conversely, from a support \(R\) obeying them, place at most \(h\) of its coordinates in \(R\cap E\) into the feature word, enough that the remaining monomial has degree at most \(K\). This is possible because both \(|R|-K\le h\) and \(|R|-K\le|R\cap E|\). Distinct squarefree monomials are independent Boolean functions, giving (5).

A newly admitted support after adding feature \(e\notin E\) must contain \(e\), have exactly \(K\) coordinates outside \(E\cup\{e\}\), and at most \(h-1\) coordinates inside \(E\). These choices give (6), and account for every support that was previously disallowed. Finally if \(v-s\le K\), the outside-support condition is automatic, so the claimed saturation and zero increment follow. \(\square\)

This is a filtration control for every \(v,s,K,h\), not a sequence of finite cases or a hard-source construction. For example the feature annihilator spaces \(\langle1-x_i\rangle\) are independent, with exact prefix \(1-P_i=1-x_i\); nevertheless the increment vanishes after saturation. With \(U=\langle1\rangle\) instead, the image has dimension \(\sum_{i\le h}\binom{s}{i}\) and an independent added feature has positive increment before saturation of that feature algebra. Thus the feature matroid alone, without the initial multiplier domain and its quotient, does not determine the relevant recurrence. No contradiction with Sokal's scalar identity is asserted.
