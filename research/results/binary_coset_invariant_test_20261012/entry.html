Bounded-degree coset probes

Proposition. Let \(A=\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\), \(v\ge1\), and \(F=(F_1,\ldots,F_r)\in A^r\) generate the unit ideal. Suppose one indexed generator, denoted \(F_b\), is \(B=x_1^2+x_1\). Let \(H(x,Z_1,\ldots,Z_r)\) have total degree at most \(k\ge0\) in \(Z\), and be computed by a division-free binary arithmetic circuit of size \(S\) and depth \(d\), with unbounded fan-in and size counting gates and wires. Assume \[ H(x,c(x))=h(x)\quad\text{for every }c\in A^r\text{ with }F\cdot c=1, \] where \(h\) is one common polynomial. Then \(h\) has a circuit constructed from \(H\) and \(B\), without a supplied certificate, of size \[ O_k\!\left((S+r+v+1)^{(k+2)4^k}\right) \] and depth at most \(4^k(2d+8)\), over \(\mathbb F_2\). In particular fixed \(k\) preserves polynomial size and bounded depth. The constant may depend on \(k\); the statement supplies no polynomial bound for growing \(k\).

Eliminating the unknown certificate. Choose any polynomial certificate \(c\), solely for the proof of the identity. For every \(i\ne b\), put \(K_i=B e_i-F_i e_b\). These are explicit Koszul syzygies, so \[ c+\sum_{i\ne b}t_iK_i \] is a certificate for every tuple \(t_i\in A\). The hypothesis makes the polynomial \[ G(T)=H\!\left(x,c+\sum_{i\ne b}T_iK_i\right)-H(x,c) \] zero on every tuple from \(A\). The domain \(A\) is infinite, even though its constant field is binary. Over its fraction field, induction on the number of variables and the univariate root bound therefore imply \(G=0\) formally. No inference from two field-point evaluations or from vanishing derivatives is used.

In the fraction field substitute \(T_i=-c_i/B\). Every coordinate other than \(b\) becomes zero, and coordinate \(b\) becomes \[ c_b+\sum_{i\ne b}F_ic_i/B=1/B. \] Consequently \(h=H(x,e_b/B)\) as a rational identity. This rational expression is a polynomial because it equals \(H(x,c)\). The proof uses an existing certificate, but the construction below does not compute it. Neither a small certificate nor cheap Koszul filling is assumed.

A small dividend. Set all placeholders other than \(Z_b=z\) to zero and write \[ H(x,ze_b)=\sum_{j=0}^k a_j(x)z^j,\qquad g(x)=\sum_{j=0}^k a_j(x)B^{k-j}. \] The preceding identity gives \(g=B^kh\) in \(A\). Obtain the \(a_j\) by keeping coefficients of \(z^0,\ldots,z^k\) at every circuit gate. This is truncated polynomial arithmetic, not interpolation over an extension field. Discarding higher powers cannot change a lower coefficient, since all powers are nonnegative, including when intermediate cancellations occur.

At a product gate of fan-in \(q\), its coefficient of degree \(j\le k\) is the sum over \(e_1+\cdots+e_q=j\) of products of the corresponding input coefficients. There are \(\binom{q+j-1}{j}\) such terms when \(q>0\). An empty product has constant coefficient one. One product layer and one sum layer per original product suffice. Counting all coefficients, product wires, and gates gives the conservative size \(O_k((S+r+v+1)^{k+2})\) and depth at most \(2d+2\). The powers of \(B\) are products of at most \(k\) copies, and the final weighted sum leaves the depth at most \(2d+6\), with the same size bound. At \(k=0\), simple placeholder substitution already computes \(h\).

Remove the known denominator. The divided-difference construction computes \[ \mathcal D_a f=\frac{f(x_1)-f(a)}{x_1-a} \] as a division-free circuit over the same field: its product telescoping uses at most two layers per original layer and quadratic size. It applies to arbitrary old-variable coefficient polynomials. Apply \(\mathcal D_0\) to \(g\) exactly \(k\) times, then \(\mathcal D_1\) exactly \(k\) times. The identity \(g=x_1^k(x_1+1)^kh\) guarantees at each step that the endpoint value is zero, so these operations are exact divisions, leaving \(h\). Iterating the quadratic-size and depth recurrence \(d'\le2d+1\) for \(2k\) passes yields the asserted size and depth bounds. No division gate, field change, or arbitrary growing-degree component extraction remains. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. Use \(F=(x_1,1+x_1,B)\), with certificate \((1,1,0)\), and add unused old variables to obtain unbounded ambient dimension. For any fixed \(k\), the probe \(H=a(x)(\sum_iF_iZ_i)^k\), with any supplied small circuit for \(a\), has common value \(a\). The theorem recovers that value without choosing certificate coefficients. More generally every inconsistent Boolean input system with its Boolean axioms has a polynomial certificate by the finite-grid ideal argument, but no small certificate is assumed; the proposition concerns any observable meeting its invariance hypothesis on that class.

Degenerate cases. Zero \(H\) gives zero common value. Degree-zero probes are already independent of the certificate. A unit row with only the generator \(B\) cannot exist; the assumptions exclude it. Without a nonempty certificate class, invariance would be vacuous, so the unit-ideal premise is essential. Without an old variable the infinite-domain and Boolean-pivot argument is unavailable. A probe such as \(Z_b\) is not invariant: Koszul changes can alter it. In characteristic two zero formal derivatives do not imply translation invariance; the proof uses polynomial identities obtained from an infinite domain, not derivatives.
