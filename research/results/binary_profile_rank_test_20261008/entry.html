A rectangular annihilator cannot have very small rank

Lemma (rectangular annihilator rank). Let \(F\) be any field, let \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), \(n\ge1\), and let \(\Gamma\subseteq F[x]\) be a finite system with no Boolean solution, containing every \(x_i^2-x_i\). Its original generator degrees are at most an integer \(d\ge2\). Fix an integer \(L\ge d\). Let \(0\ne\mu: F[x]_{\le L}\to F\) be linear and satisfy \[\mu(gu)=0\qquad(g\in\Gamma,\ \deg u\le L-\deg g).\tag{1}\] Zero generators can be discarded. No assumption \(\mu(1)\ne0\) is made. Form the matrix \[M_\mu=(\mu(x_i f))_{i,f},\tag{2}\] with rows indexed by variables and columns a monomial basis of \(F[x]_{\le L-1}\). Then \[\operatorname{rank}M_\mu\ge L-d+1.\tag{3}\] The bound is sharp for such unnormalized ordinary annihilators. Existence of one alone is not a refutation-degree lower bound.

Proof: eliminate the row relations. Suppose the rank is \(r\le L-d\). Its left kernel gives an \((n-r)\)-dimensional space \(\mathcal R\) of homogeneous linear forms \(\ell(x)\) such that \(\mu(\ell f)=0\) for every \(f\) of degree at most \(L-1\). Gaussian elimination chooses \(r\) of the original variables, denoted \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\), as free coordinates, and writes every other variable as a linear combination of them modulo \(\mathcal R\). Let \(\phi:F[x]\to F[y]\) be that substitution, fixing the chosen coordinates. For every polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(L\), monomial telescoping gives \[P-\phi(P)=\sum_j\ell_j H_j,\qquad \deg H_j\le L-1,\] using a basis of \(\mathcal R\). Therefore \(\mu(P)=\mu(\phi(P))\). This is an equality of the given functional's values, not a claim that the new linear forms belong to the old ideal or are PC consequences.

Proof: exclude every free Boolean point. For \(a\in\{0,1\}^r\), let \(\delta_a(y)\) be its point indicator, of degree \(r\). The substituted old coordinates \(\phi(x_i)(a)\) form a point of \(F^n\). If it is not Boolean, an old Boolean generator is nonzero there; if it is Boolean, some generator is nonzero by unsatisfiability. Hence choose \(g_a\in\Gamma\) with \(\phi(g_a)(a)\ne0\). Since \(r+d\le L\), (1) and the substitution identity apply to \(\delta_a g_a\), giving \[0=\mu(\delta_a g_a)=\mu(\delta_a\phi(g_a))=\phi(g_a)(a)\mu(\delta_a).\] The last equality uses Boolean reduction on the selected old variables. Its certificate fits the original product degree \(r+\deg g_a\le L\): repeatedly replacing a power \(y_j^m\) by \(y_j\) subtracts a multiple of \(y_j^2-y_j\) without increasing total degree. The reduced product is exactly \(\phi(g_a)(a)\delta_a\). Thus every \(\mu(\delta_a)=0\). If \(r=0\), the empty indicator is one and the same argument applies to the single empty assignment.

Finally substitute any \(P\) of degree at most \(L\) and reduce in the \(r\) selected Boolean variables. Its multilinear remainder has degree at most \(r\) and is the linear combination \(\sum_a\phi(P)(a)\delta_a\). All these reductions fit degree \(L\), so \(\mu(P)=0\). This contradicts \(\mu\ne0\) and proves (3). The original degree \(d\) was not reset after substituting the row relations. \(\square\)

Sharpness and hypothesis controls

For every \(d\ge2,L\ge d\), set \(r=L-d+1\ge1\), and use \(r\) Boolean variables. Take \[\Gamma=\{x_j^2-x_j:1\le j\le r\}\cup\{1+x_1^{d-2}(x_1^2-x_1)\}.\] The last generator has original degree \(d\) and equals one on the Boolean cube, so the system is unsatisfiable. Let \(\mu(P)\) extract the coefficient of \(x_1\cdots x_r\) after Boolean multilinear reduction. It is nonzero and \(\mu(1)=0\). Boolean-generator multiples reduce to zero. A legal cofactor of the last generator has degree at most \(L-d=r-1\); its product reduces to that cofactor, whose top coefficient is zero. Thus (1) holds.

There are exactly \(r\) rows in (2). The columns \(f_j=\prod_{i\ne j}x_i\) give the identity matrix: \(\mu(x_i f_j)=1\) for \(i=j\) and zero otherwise. Hence the rank is exactly \(r\). This family has a degree-\(d\) NS refutation already, namely subtracting the displayed Boolean multiple from the last generator. It is a sharpness control for an unnormalized annihilator, not a proper-PC-quotient example. Confusing those two notions would give a false lower-bound claim.

Both structural hypotheses matter. On a satisfiable Boolean system, evaluation at a nonzero Boolean solution has rectangular rank one at every cutoff. If Boolean generators are omitted, the polynomial \(x^2+x+1\) has no Boolean root but evaluation at a root in \(\mathbb F_4\) gives rank one at every cutoff. These are exact all-cutoff controls, not sampled evidence. The degree reduction and Gaussian substitution used in the proof are standard; no independent novelty claim is made for them.

Every complete output flag has a wide last fiber

Corollary (wide terminal fiber). Let \(\Gamma,F,d\) be as above, let \(J\subseteq F[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing \(\Gamma\), and let \(A\notin J\) have degree at most \(a\). Take integers \(K\ge d-1\), \(a+K+1\le D\). In \(Q=F[x]_{\le D}/J\), define \[H_A=\operatorname{span}\{[Af]:\deg f\le K+1\},\qquad W_A=\{([Ax_i f])_{i=1}^n:\deg f\le K\}\subseteq F^n\otimes H_A.\tag{4}\] For every complete flag of \(H_A\), the final fiber of \(W_A\) has dimension at least \(K+2-d\). For a complete flag of the larger \(Q\), its last nonzero fiber has that same lower bound. Consequently every scalar matrix \(C\) of rank at most \(h\) has the flag-intersection upper expression \[\sum_j\dim((W_A)_j\cap\ker C)\ge\max\{0,K+2-d-h\}.\tag{5}\] This is a lower bound on that sufficient upper expression, not on the actual intersection \(W_A\cap(\ker C\otimes Q)\).

Proof. Let \(0\ne\lambda\in H_A^*\), extend it linearly to \(Q^*\), and set \(\mu(P)=\lambda([AP])\) for \(\deg P\le K+1\). It is nonzero by the definition of \(H_A\). For every legal old generator multiple through \(K+1\), bounded absorption gives \(Agu\in J\) through \(a+K+1\le D\). Thus \(\mu\) satisfies (1) with \(L=K+1\). Contracting (4) by \(\lambda\) has matrix \(M_\mu\), so its rank is at least \(K+2-d\).

For a complete flag of \(H_A\), choose a functional whose kernel is its penultimate member. The contraction rank is exactly the final fiber dimension, by rank-nullity. For a flag of \(Q\), take the first member containing \(H_A\); its predecessor meets \(H_A\) in codimension one. The corresponding contraction again has positive rank at least \(K+2-d\), and all later fibers are zero. Finally, intersecting that fiber with a codimension-at-most-\(h\) scalar kernel loses at most \(h\) dimensions. This proves (5). No positivity, normalization or particular quotient-coordinate basis is used. \(\square\)

Actual input-word application. For raw inputs \(g_i=x_i\) and original domain \(U=V_K\), the domain generated by at most \(h\) input factors is exactly \(U^+=V_{K+h}\): every monomial of degree at most \(K+h\) factors into at most \(h\) variables times a monomial of degree at most \(K\). Apply (4) with \(K+h\) in place of \(K\). If \(K+h\ge d-1\) and \(a+K+h+1\le D\), every complete flag for the resulting input-word image has a final nonzero fiber of dimension at least \(K+h+2-d\). Thus every rank-at-most-\(h\) scalar matrix gives a flag-intersection sum at least \(\max(0,K+2-d)\). In particular, when \(K\ge d-1\), no flag has all fibers of dimension at most \(h\), let alone the small-field construction's range \(h/4\). This rules out that uniform maximum-fiber premise for the actual fixed input-word space; it is still not a lower bound on actual chosen-prefix errors or on their rank relative to the surviving weight space. Smaller conditioned domains are not covered.

A wide flag need not mean a hard block

Suppose additionally that \(1-\sum_i c_i x_i\in J\), with a nonzero legal binary coefficient vector \(c\) in the binary application. The one-row scalar map \(c\otimes\mathrm{id}\) is injective on \(W_A\): if \(\sum_i c_i[Ax_if]=[Af]=0\), bounded absorption gives every \([Ax_if]=0\). Thus its actual intersection with the kernel is zero. Nevertheless (5) forces every complete flag to give a scalar-intersection sum at least \(\max(0,K+1-d)\). For \(K\ge d\), no flag makes this particular bound exact.

With the extra actual-product reserve \(a+K+2\le D\), the legal prefix \(P=1-\sum_i c_i x_i\) itself has all \(APx_i f\in J\), since \(P\in J\). This is allowed: only the final common weight must survive, not \(AP\). The control assumes the displayed old linear relation and does not claim it for every Boolean base. It illustrates exactly why a bad raw profile is not a source-exclusion theorem.

For canonical unsatisfiable CNFs there is also a familiar cheap control, without requiring such a linear relation: the all-zero assignment violates some positive clause of width \(t\) at most the CNF width. Selecting its variables in separate rows makes the prefix the old clause polynomial. The sufficient actual-product reserve \(a+K+t+1\le D\) puts every \(APx_i f\) in the old pseudoideal. The formal source must also pay its full original row/product budget. The raw-variable tuple therefore should not be used as an intrinsically difficult source family. It is used here to test a proposed profile property on a source-admissible tuple.

What the test changes

The earlier small-field construction's scalar range \(s\le h/4\), after writing its codimension allowance as \(h\), cannot be combined with an assumed maximum fiber width \(s\) when \(K+2-d>s\) in (4). This is a real old-quotient obstruction under every complete flag, not failure of one coordinate order. The comparison is about that stated parameter range and that tuple/domain; no theorem about every possible design is claimed.

More importantly, one wide final fiber does not establish that its mass is large compared with \(\dim[wU]\). A tail-sensitive bound might tolerate it. The argument also uses the full multiplier domain \(V_K\) and the raw-variable input tuple. It does not apply automatically to a conditioned domain, arbitrary nonlinear parent mixtures, or an actual chosen prefix that has already made its error zero. Those distinctions preserve the actual lower-bound question.
