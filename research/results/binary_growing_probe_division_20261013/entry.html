Reciprocal probe dividend

Lemma. Let \(H(x,Z)\) have a division-free unbounded-fan-in arithmetic circuit over \(\mathbb F_2\), of size \(S\ge1\) counting gates and wires and depth \(d\ge0\). Fix a placeholder index \(b\), an old variable \(x_1\), and \(B=x_1^2+x_1\). There is an integer \(0\le k\le(S+1)^d\) and a division-free binary circuit computing \[ g=B^k H(x,e_b/B) \] in the fraction field, of size \(O((S+2)^{d+2})\) and depth at most \(2d+4\). The right-hand side is a polynomial by construction. If the full-certificate invariance hypotheses of bounded coset probes hold, with unit-generating \(F\) including \(B\), then \(g=B^kh\) for the common polynomial value \(h\), also for growing placeholder degree. This does not construct a division-free circuit for \(h\).

Proof. First set all placeholders except \(Z_b\) to zero. Associate an integer denominator weight \(k_v\) and polynomial numerator \(g_v\) to every gate. At an old-variable or constant input take \(k_v=0\), \(g_v\) equal to that input. At \(Z_b\), take \(k_v=1,g_v=1\). At a sum gate take \(k_v=\max_u k_u\), with empty maximum zero, and \[ g_v=\sum_u B^{k_v-k_u}g_u. \] At a product gate take \(k_v=\sum_u k_u\) and \(g_v=\prod_u g_u\); an empty product has weight zero and numerator one. Induction gives the rational gate value \(g_v/B^{k_v}\). Every exponent used is nonnegative. No interpolation, coefficient truncation, or division gate is used.

At input depth zero the weights are at most one. Each gate has fan-in at most \(S\), so at depth \(j\) they are at most \((S+1)^j\), including shared subcircuits and repeated wires. For each sum input wire needing a positive exponent, compute its power of \(B\) with one unbounded product of at most \((S+1)^d\) copies. There are at most \(S\) such wires. Computing \(B\) once and all these powers costs \(O(S(S+1)^d+S+1)\) gates and wires, with powers at depth at most three. Each original sum adds a scaling-product layer and a sum layer; each product adds one layer. Numerators therefore have depth at most \(2d+4\), with the stated conservative size bound. Repeated powers need not be shared to meet it. At the output take \(k=k_v,g=g_v\).

For the invariant case, the full earlier proof uses the explicit Koszul moves \(B e_i-F_i e_b\), an infinite polynomial coefficient domain, and the fraction-field substitution \(T_i=-c_i/B\) in their formal identity. It proves \(h=H(x,e_b/B)\) without any degree restriction; only its former circuit extraction bound required fixed placeholder degree. Applying the construction just proved gives \(g=B^kh\). An unknown certificate is used only to justify this rational identity, not as a circuit input. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For \(H=Z_b^q\), \(q\ge1\), a single product of \(q\) input wires gives \(k=q,g=1\); this tests clearing at unbounded weight but is not invariant. For invariant examples use \(F=(x_1,1+x_1,B)\), certificate \((1,1,0)\), and \(H=a(x)(F\cdot Z)^q\) with supplied small \(a\). The common value is \(a\), and growing \(q\) and unused old variables give unbounded parameters. No hard common value is inferred from this witness.

Degenerate cases. Zero circuits and zero substituted outputs give zero dividends. A constant or old-only input has \(k=0\). Empty sums and products follow the displayed conventions. Cancellation can make the actual pole order much smaller than the syntactic weight; the claim is an upper construction and does not assert minimality. Without full-class invariance there need not be a polynomial quotient after removing \(B^k\). With no unit-generating \(F\), invariance can be vacuous; that premise is retained only for the corollary.

Exact division and the source check

The earlier divided-difference proof divides by a known split linear factor with quadratic size and depth recurrence \(d'\le2d+1\). Applying it \(k\) times at zero and \(k\) times at one removes \(B^k=x_1^k(x_1+1)^k\), but that recorded bound grows like \(4^k\) in depth. The dividend above has polynomial size for fixed \(d\), while \(k\) may grow polynomially with \(S\); this recurrence consequently supplies no fixed-depth quotient bound. It is not a proof that no other division algorithm works.

Bhattacharjee, Kumar, Rai, Ramanathan, Saptharishi and Saraf, Closure under factorization from a result of Furstenberg, state closure over the base field in characteristic zero or sufficiently large characteristic.
