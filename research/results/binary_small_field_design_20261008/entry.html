Independent legal rows

Corollary (independent row shortening). Let \(F\) have prime characteristic \(p\), let \(J\subseteq F[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, let \(A\) have degree at most \(a\), and let \(g_1,\ldots,g_k\), \(k\ge1\), have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), with \(g_i^p-g_i\in J\). Let \(U\subseteq F[x]_{\le L}\) be an \(F\)-linear subspace. All degree bounds and cutoffs are nonnegative integers; \(k\ge1\), \(\delta\ge1\), and \(0\le h\le k\). Assume \[a+L+p\delta\le D.\] Write \(Q=F[x]_{\le D}/J\), \(Tf=([Ag_if])_i\), and \(W=T(U)\). For \(0\le h\le k\), let \(C\) be uniform among full-row-rank matrices in \(\mathbb F_p^{h\times k}\). Then \[\mathbb E_C\dim_F\bigl(W\cap(\ker_F C\otimes_F Q)\bigr)\le\beta_p(k,h)\dim_F W,\qquad \beta_p(k,h)=\frac{p^{k-h}-1}{p^k-1}.\tag{1}\] For \(h=0\), interpret the empty matrix as giving factor one. The coefficient field \(F\) may extend the prime field; input identities and legal row entries retain their stated prime-field scope. The positive \(\delta\) restriction is retained for the prefix application; the direct argument also covers degree-zero inputs.

Proof. Sample ordered rows sequentially, each uniform outside the span of its predecessors. This produces the uniform full-row-rank ensemble, because each ordered independent tuple has probability \(\prod_{j

The full arbitrary-domain shortening proof, applied conditionally to \(U_j\), says that an unrestricted next uniform row leaves expected intersection dimension at most \(d_j/p\). A row in the span of the previous rows leaves all \(d_j\) dimensions. That event has probability \(p^{j-k}\). If \(E_j\) is the conditional mean for a row outside that span, then \[p^{j-k}d_j+(1-p^{j-k})E_j\le d_j/p, \qquad E_j\le\frac{p^{k-j-1}-1}{p^{k-j}-1}d_j.\] For \(j\le h-1\le k-1\), these factors are nonnegative. Iterate conditional expectation and telescope their product to obtain (1). The same Frobenius degree window is used each time; this argument does not multiply \(h\) prefix factors for free. \(\square\)

The probability \(\beta_p(k,h)\) is exactly \(\pi_1\) in the earlier full-rank moment lemma. That lemma concerned expected kernel cardinality and showed why its logarithm can fail even when a good prefix exists. Here the conditional Frobenius argument bounds expected dimension on actual multiplication images. The old obstruction to a cardinality-moment certificate remains unchanged.

A floor for the generic scalar guarantee

Proposition (uniform design floor). Let \(0\le h\le k\), \(k\ge1\), and let any probability distribution choose subspaces \(H_C\subseteq\mathbb F_p^k\) of codimension at most \(h\). Some nonzero vector \(v\) satisfies \[\Pr(v\in H_C)\ge\beta_p(k,h).\tag{3}\] Consequently, if for every nonzero scalar subspace \(Z\) of dimension at most \(s\ge1\) one has \(\mathbb E\dim(Z\cap H_C)\le\gamma\dim Z\), then \(\gamma\ge\beta_p(k,h)\). In particular a family of \(N\) kernels with a uniform strong-design bound \(\sum_C\dim(Z\cap H_C)\le B\dim Z\) needs \(B/N\ge\beta_p(k,h)\).

Proof. Average the containment probability over all \(p^k-1\) nonzero vectors. The result is \[\frac{\mathbb E(|H_C|-1)}{p^k-1}\ge\frac{p^{k-h}-1}{p^k-1}.\] At least one vector attains that average. On its one-dimensional span, intersection dimension is one exactly when it is contained. The conclusions follow, also for nonuniform distributions and kernels of smaller codimension. This is elementary incidence counting, not a lower bound on a particular source's optimized choice. \(\square\)

Actual-image saturation. Over the Boolean function algebra on \(k\) bits, take \(A=1\), \(g_i=x_i\) and the full multiplier domain. Its tuple image is \[W=\bigoplus_{x\in\mathbb F_2^k\setminus\{0\}}\langle x\otimes\mathbf1_{\{x\}}\rangle,\qquad \dim W=2^k-1.\] For every rank-\(h\) coefficient matrix, its row-kernel intersection and actual full-domain prefix error both have dimension \(2^{k-h}-1\). Pointwise multiplication and the binary zero-test prefix prove this directly: precisely the nonzero points in \(\ker C\) survive. Thus (1) is attained, even by every full-rank choice, on this actual multiplication image. This is the already recorded linear-tuple side of the block-prefix comparison, reused rather than introduced as a new hard instance. It is satisfiable and uses the full Boolean cutoff. The weight denominator is \(2^k\), so it is not an obstruction to finding a surviving weight.

What the design comparison decides

In the descended prime-field setting, for a fixed coefficient flag, the filtered tensor lemma transfers a scalar guarantee with factor \(\gamma\) to \(\mathbb E\dim(W\cap(H_C\otimes Q))\le\gamma\dim W\), provided its fiber-dimension hypotheses hold. Any guarantee uniform over all scalar subspaces up to a positive dimension has \(\gamma\ge\beta_p(k,h)\). On actual prime-valued multiplication images, (1) already provides that factor without a flag-profile hypothesis. Thus replacing its uniform factor by a black-box scalar-design constant cannot improve this certificate in the common degree window. This does not compare every individual matrix, tailored distribution or exact flag profile. A prime-field design inequality is not automatically a guarantee for arbitrary extension-field fiber spaces.

For binary \(h<k\), \[\frac{\beta_2(k,h)}{2^{-h}}=\frac{1-2^{h-k}}{1-2^{-k}}\in(1/2,1].\] For positive \(h<k\) the upper inequality is strict. The conditioning removes less than a factor two from the old rate and introduces no new exponential decay; at \(h=k\), exact elimination was already available. This cannot be promoted to the missing estimate of \(d_a/H\). The purpose of proving the exact comparison was to test the proposed generic design mechanism before investing in its explicit construction.

Preserved exceptions. The filtered scalar-design route can use the smaller prefix-identity window \(b+p_0+K+(h+1)\delta\le D\); (1) used the extra Frobenius reserve \(\delta\). Therefore the domination statement is restricted to the common window. Near a tight boundary, that difference must still be checked. More substantially, a family adapted to the actual input directions can beat its worst-case scalar guarantee: the nonlinear tuple in the existing block-prefix comparison has a perfect chosen row despite the same optimized uniform-flag value as the linear tuple. The present floor does not exclude such source-sensitive use, additional coefficient features, or rank control of the actual stacked errors instead of a sum of upper bounds.
