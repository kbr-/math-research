Binary polynomial multiplicity degree

Determine, or bound tightly, the minimum degree \(D(n,k)\) of a polynomial \(P\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with Hasse multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) and at most \(k-1\) at the origin, below the threshold \(k\ge2^{n-2}\) of the published question: is the multiplicity bound \(2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) attained there, and if not, what is the true value?

Origin: starting research record.

Starting point

The thread starts from the binary multiplicity note (audit:binary-multiplicity-cover-question, preprint draft in publications/binary-polynomial-multiplicity/): the multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel lemma of Dvir, Kopparty, Saraf and Sudan gives \(D(n,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) for all positive \(n,k\), answering the bound asked in Question 4.3 of Bishnoi, Boyadzhiyska, Das and Mészáros (CPC 2023). In correspondence with an author of that paper (24 September 2026, paraphrased), the question's threshold was explained as the range where hyperplane covers attain this value, and two directions were suggested: decide whether the algebraic bound is tight for smaller \(k\), where extremal polynomials need not be products of linear forms, or improve it; and consult the open questions of Sauermann and Wigderson, who study the algebraic problem.

Where we stand

Current state. The exact small table (\(n\le7\)) and three out-of-sample predictions support the conjectured formula \(D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\) for \(1\le k<2^{n-1}\): the multiplicity bound is not attained below the threshold, and each dimension beyond \(m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\) costs one degree. Upper half: the power-of-two reduction derives it for all \(k\in[2^j,2^{j+1})\) from one factor \(Q_{2^j}\), and dimension-free certificates for \(j\le3\) prove \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-m(k)\) for every \(n\) and \(k\le15\) (small-k upper bound). Lower half: proved for every \(k\) and \(n\ge m(k)\) by the one-dimension step (\(S=P(x',0)+P(x',1)\) after a coordinate change) and the multiplicity bound at \(n=m(k)\) (lower bound). So the formula holds for every \(k\le15\) (exact value), and in general it is equivalent to the power-of-two factor conjecture. Recorded results used as inputs:

  • Lower bound. \(D(n,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) (the note; working source-audit corollary, independent review outstanding).
  • \(k=1\). \(D(n,1)=n\): Alon–Füredi Theorem 5 gives the lower bound over any field, and \(\prod_i(1-x_i)\) attains it. So the multiplicity bound is not tight at \(k=1\), \(n\ge3\).
  • Upper bound from high origin orders. The high-origin cover theorem (thm:finite-field-high-origin-cover, working proof in the odd-field thread, every prime power including \(q=2\)) forces degree \(\ge n+2k-2\) when the origin order is exactly \(k-1\) and \(\ge n+2k-3\) when it is \(k-2\), both attained by affine covering products. Hence \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-3\) for \(k\ge2\), and any improvement needs origin order at most \(k-3\).
  • A low-origin construction. The characteristic-two polynomial in Sauermann–Wigderson (arXiv 2010.00077v2, Section 4.2) has multiplicity \(\ge4\) at nonzero points, value one at the origin and degree \(n+4\), below \(n+2k-3=n+5\). It meets the lower bound at \((n,k)=(3,4),(4,4)\) (degrees 7, 8), recorded in the note's source checks.

Only polynomials with origin order \(\le k-3\) can beat \(n+2k-3\); the computed minima use orders \(0\), \(1\) and occasionally higher.

The remaining route

  1. Prove the formula \(D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\) for all \(k\). Lower half: proved for all \(k\) (lower bound). Upper half: reduced by the power-of-two reduction to factors \(Q_{2^j}\) (degree \(2^{j+1}-j-2\), value one at the origin, multiplicity \(\ge2^j-|a|\) at weights \(1..2^j-1\)); certificates exist for \(j\le3\), a uniform construction for all \(j\) is open (conj:power-of-two-factor-existence). Work with formal polynomials and Hasse multiplicity; reduction modulo \(x_i^2-x_i\) does not preserve multiplicities.
  2. Place the answer in the literature. Read Sauermann–Wigderson (Theorem 1.1, Section 4.2 and their open questions) and the covering results of Bishnoi–Boyadzhiyska–Das–Mészáros before naming any result; distinguish real, odd-characteristic and binary statements, and hyperplane covers from polynomials.
  3. Decide what reaches the note. A tightness theorem or improved bound could turn the note into a publishable paper; otherwise it stays a short observation. Publication or correspondence needs the author's explicit decision.

Working mathematical context

Conventions. Degree is total degree of the formal polynomial. \(\operatorname{mult}_a(P)\) is the least \(|\alpha|\) with a nonzero coefficient of \(z^\alpha\) in \(P(a+z)\); equivalently all Hasse derivatives of order below it vanish at \(a\). The origin condition \(\operatorname{mult}_0(P)\le k-1\) excludes \(P=0\) and is not linear, so minimum degree is a comparison of two linear spaces rather than one solve. Never reduce modulo the field equations.

Linked results. The multiplicity lower bound: binary multiplicity note. The origin-order bounds: high-origin cover theorem and punctured first-jet lemma (\(q=2\), \(k=2\): degree \(\ge n+1\) with nonzero origin). The odd-field thread's higher-jet obstruction concerns \(3\le k\le q\) and is vacuous at \(q=2\). Reuse these by link and keep their working-proof status; the odd-field thread's hyperplane-cover goal is a different question.

External sources. Alon–Füredi, Covering the cube by affine hyperplanes (1993), Theorem 5; Dvir–Kopparty–Saraf–Sudan (SICOMP 2013), Definition 2.2 and Lemma 2.7; Sauermann–Wigderson, arXiv 2010.00077v2 (JLMS); Bishnoi–Boyadzhiyska–Das–Mészáros, CPC 32(5) 2023, Question 4.3 (arXiv v1 Question 5.2). Read the primary text before relying on a statement.

24 September 2026 — Exact small values point to one extra degree per dimension above the threshold

Finite check for \(n\le7\) and a conjectured formula; no general proof. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. Below the published threshold \(k\ge2^{n-2}\), is the multiplicity bound \(2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) attained, and if not, what is \(D(n,k)\)? The missing implication for the thread is a formula for \(D(n,k)\) valid for all \(n,k\). This cycle reads the Sauermann–Wigderson statements, computes exact values in the range they leave open and stops at a stated general conjecture supported by predictions made before the larger cases were run.

What Sauermann–Wigderson settle over \(\mathbb F_2\)

Read from arXiv 2010.00077v2 (Theorems 1.3–1.5 and Section 4.2). Over \(\mathbb R\), for \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge2k-3\): origin order at most \(k-2\) forces degree at least \(n+2k-3\), and origin order exactly \(k-1\) forces \(n+2k-2\), both attained. Theorem 1.5 (origin order \(k-1\)) holds over every field and every \(n\ge1\). Their proof of the other bounds uses characteristic zero only through the nonvanishing of the Catalan numbers; in characteristic two \(C_2=2\) vanishes, so their Theorem 1.3 holds for \(k=2,3\) and fails first at \(k=4\), where their explicit \(\mathbb F_2\) polynomial \(\prod_\ell(x_\ell+1)\cdot Q\) has degree \(n+4\) and value one at the origin. They pose no problem about \(\mathbb F_2\) beyond this discussion; their concluding remarks concern real hyperplane covers and fixed \(n\) with large \(k\). So over \(\mathbb F_2\) the large-\(n\) values are \(D(n,2)=n+1\) and \(D(n,3)=n+3\), far above the note's bound, and the open range is \(k\ge4\), in particular \(k>(n+3)/2\), where their theorem does not apply at all.

Exact values

Method. At the origin the Hasse conditions only say that \(P\) has no monomials of degree below its order. Hence a polynomial of degree at most \(d\) with multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point and origin order exactly \(\ell\) exists if and only if \(r(\ell,d)<r(\ell+1,d)+N_\ell\), where \(r(\ell,d)\) is the \(\mathbb F_2\)-rank of the nonzero-point conditions restricted to monomials of degree in \([\ell,d]\) and \(N_\ell\) is the number of degree-\(\ell\) monomials. The condition for point \(a\) and \(|\beta|<k\) at monomial \(x^\alpha\) is \(\prod_i\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}a_i^{\alpha_i-\beta_i}\bmod2\), evaluated by Lucas' theorem. The kernel research/tools/bmd_min_degree.cpp computes the rank profiles by bitset elimination with columns in ascending degree and extracts witnesses by one Gauss–Jordan pass in descending degree: in reduced form a free degree-\(\ell\) column gives a kernel vector vanishing on all lower degrees, hence a witness of origin order exactly \(\ell\).

Validation. A brute-force enumeration (research/tools/bmd_reference.py) agrees on \((n,k)=(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\), including per-order minima. The kernel reproduces every known value: \(D(n,1)=n\) (Alon–Füredi), \(D(n,2)=n+1\) and \(D(n,3)=n+3\) for \(n\ge3\), origin order \(k-1\) at exactly \(n+2k-2\), and \(D(3,4)=7\), \(D(4,4)=8\). Every witness is re-checked by direct evaluation of its Hasse coefficients with exact binomials, and for \(n\le5\) a direct solve also confirms nonexistence one degree below each minimum. The faster three-elimination mode agrees with the per-order mode on all 21 cases where both ran.

The conjectured formula

With \(m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\), the least dimension at which \(k\) reaches the published threshold, every computed value satisfies

\[ D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor=2k+\bigl(n-m(k)\bigr)\qquad(1\le k<2^{n-1}). \]

For \(2^{n-2}\le k<2^{n-1}\) the right side is \(2k\), the multiplicity bound, which the question's authors report is attained there by hyperplane covers (the exact covering theorem was not re-read this cycle; the computed threshold cases \((3,2),(3,3),(4,4),(5,8)\) agree). Below the threshold it says that each dimension beyond \(m(k)\) costs exactly one degree. For large \(n\) it reads \(n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\), recovering \(n+1,n+3,n+4\) for \(k=2,3,4\), and it lies below the real answer \(n+2k-3\) exactly when \(k\ge4\). The formula was stated after the \(n\le5\) and \(n=6,k\le4\) data and then tested with the degree cap set to the prediction: the new cases \((6,5),(6,6),(7,4)\) gave 12, 14, 11 as predicted, while \((7,2),(7,3)\) were already fixed by Sauermann–Wigderson.

A reduction for the upper half. \(\prod_i(1+x_i)\) has multiplicity exactly \(|a|\) at each point \(a\). So if a single polynomial \(Q_k\), independent of \(n\), has degree \(2k-m(k)\), order below \(k\) at the origin and multiplicity at least \(k-|a|\) at every point of weight \(1\le|a|<k\), then \(\prod_i(1+x_i)Q_k\) gives \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-m(k)\) for every \(n\). This holds for \(k=2\) with \(Q=1+\sum_ix_i\) and for \(k=4\) by Sauermann–Wigderson. The \(D(5,5)\) data show \(Q_k\) must be allowed a positive origin order.

General statement. The all-parameter claim of this cycle is the conjecture conj:binary-multiplicity-degree-formula; the finite evidence is check:binary-multiplicity-degree-exact-table. Neither half is proved beyond the recorded cases.

Verification gate. Dependencies re-read from their sources: the note's deduction, the high-origin cover theorem and the Sauermann–Wigderson Theorems 1.3–1.5 and Section 4.2. The claim registry has no equivalent statement (searched by content words). The computation checks are listed above. No new lemma is claimed, so no reviewer pass was needed for this cycle.

Assessment. The missing implication is now a precise formula rather than an open window, with the answer to the co-author's question: the multiplicity bound is not attained below the threshold, and the excess is \(n-m(k)\). Both halves need proofs, most promisingly the one-dimension steps \(D(n,k)\ge D(n-1,k)+1\) and a product construction \(Q_k\). 24 September 2026 — The upper half reduces to powers of two and holds for every k up to 15

Working proofs of a general reduction and of the upper bound for k≤15 from finite certificates; powers of two from 16 open. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The conjectured formula needs an upper-bound construction of degree \(n+2k-m(k)\), \(m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\), for all \(n,k\). The previous entry reduced this to an \(n\)-independent factor \(Q_k\) with \(P=\prod_i(1+x_i)Q_k\). This cycle looks for such \(Q_k\), proves what they give for all \(n\), and stops at a proved bound or a precise reduction.

Facts used. (A) For nonzero \(P,Q\), \(\operatorname{mult}_a(PQ)=\operatorname{mult}_a P+\operatorname{mult}_a Q\): the lowest homogeneous parts of \(P(a+z)\) and \(Q(a+z)\) multiply to a nonzero form. (B) \(F_n=\prod_i(1+x_i)\) has \(\operatorname{mult}_a F_n=|a|\), since \(1+a_i+z_i\) is \(z_i\) or \(1+z_i\). (C) \(g=x_1^2+x_1\) satisfies \(g(a+z)=z_1+z_1^2\), so \(\operatorname{mult}_ag=1\) at every point.

The power-of-two reduction

Working lemma. Let \(j\ge0\), \(n\ge1\) and \(Q\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with (i) \(\deg Q\le2^{j+1}-j-2\), (ii) \(\operatorname{mult}_0Q=t<2^j\), (iii) \(\operatorname{mult}_aQ\ge2^j-|a|\) whenever \(1\le|a|<2^j\). For \(0\le r<2^j\) and \(k=2^j+r\), the polynomial \(P=F_nQg^r\) has degree at most \(n+2k-m(k)\), origin order \(t+r<k\) and multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point. Hence \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. Here \(m(k)=j+2\) and \(\deg P\le n+2^{j+1}-j-2+2r=n+2k-m(k)\). By (A)–(C), \(\operatorname{mult}_0P=t+r<2^j+r\). For \(1\le|a|<2^j\), \(\operatorname{mult}_aP\ge|a|+(2^j-|a|)+r=k\); for \(|a|\ge2^j\), \(\operatorname{mult}_aP\ge|a|+r\ge k\). The factor \(g^r\) is what makes the computed origin orders equal \(k-2^{\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor}\).

Certificates valid in every dimension

Working lemma. For a finite set \(L\) of partitions let \(Q^{(n)}=\sum_{\lambda\in L}m_\lambda^{(n)}\), the monomial symmetric polynomials in \(n\) variables (zero when \(\lambda\) has more than \(n\) parts). For \(w\ge1\), a vector \(b\in\mathbb N^w\) and a multiset \(c\) of positive integers, let \(N(\lambda;b,c)\) be the number of maps from the \(w+\#c\) positions to values, with equal parts of \(\lambda\) not distinguished, that use each nonzero part of \(\lambda\) exactly once, put at a "one" position with value \(b_i\) either nothing (only if \(b_i=0\)) or a part \(p\) of which \(b_i\) is a bit-submask, and put at a "zero" position with value \(c_l\) exactly the part \(c_l\). Then for every \(n\), every point \(a\) of weight \(w\) and every \(\beta\) that restricts to \((b,c)\) on the coordinates with \(a_i=1\) and on those with \(a_i=0,\beta_i>0\), the coefficient of \(z^\beta\) in \(Q^{(n)}(a+z)\) is \(\sum_{\lambda\in L}N(\lambda;b,c)\bmod2\). So if these sums are even for all \(1\le w<K\) and all \(\Sigma b+\Sigma c<K-w\), then \(\operatorname{mult}_aQ^{(n)}\ge K-|a|\) for every \(n\) and every \(1\le|a|<K\).

Proof. The coefficient of \(z^{\beta_i}\) in \((a_i+z_i)^{\alpha_i}\) is \(\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\), odd exactly when \(\beta_i\) is a bit-submask of \(\alpha_i\) (Lucas), if \(a_i=1\), and \([\alpha_i=\beta_i]\) if \(a_i=0\). So the coefficient in \(m_\lambda^{(n)}(a+z)\) is the parity of the number of distinct exponent vectors \(\alpha\) in the orbit of \(\lambda\) with every factor odd. Such \(\alpha\) vanish at zero positions with \(\beta_i=0\), equal \(\beta_i\) at the other zero positions, and at one positions are \(0\) (only when \(\beta_i=0\)) or a nonzero part satisfying the submask condition. These \(\alpha\) correspond exactly to the maps counted by \(N\), and the zero parts fill the remaining coordinates uniquely, so the count does not involve \(n\). If \(N>0\) then \(\lambda\) has at most \(w+\#c\le n\) nonzero parts; if \(\lambda\) has more than \(n\) parts both sides are zero. A pair \((b,c)\) with \(w+\#c>n\) does not occur in dimension \(n\).

The upper bound for every \(k\le15\)

Working theorem (finite certificates). For every \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le15\), \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. A symmetric search (research/tools/bmd_symmetric_q.py, the conditions of the certificate lemma over all partitions of size at most the degree bound) found sets \(L_0,\ldots,L_3\) for \(K=1,2,4,8\): \(Q_1=1\), \(Q_2=1+e_1\), \(Q_4\) equal to the Sauermann–Wigderson characteristic-two factor \(1+\sum_i(x_i^3+x_i^2+x_i)+\sum_{i\ne j}(x_i^3+x_i^2)x_j+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j+\sum_{i<j<l}x_ix_jx_l\), and \(Q_8\), a sum of 72 monomial symmetric functions of degree at most 11 (listed in research/results/binary-multiplicity-degree/c2-symmetric-q/symmetric-q-k1-11.json). research/tools/bmd_check_certificates.py verifies directly that each \(L_j\) contains the empty partition (so \(Q^{(n)}(0)=1\) in every dimension), has sizes at most \(2^{j+1}-j-2\), and satisfies all 216 parity conditions (certificates-check.json). By the certificate lemma, \(Q_{2^j}^{(n)}\) satisfies (i)–(iii) of the reduction for every \(n\); the reduction with \(j=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\le3\) and \(r=k-2^j\) gives the bound.

Cross-check, not part of the proof. Independently of the parity conditions, the products \(F_nQ_k\) for symmetric \(Q_k\), \(k\le11\), were expanded in \(n=3,\ldots,7\) and their multiplicities computed by shifting each monomial (bmd_min_degree verify, cross-checked against the direct Hasse evaluation where cheap): every case has degree exactly \(n+2k-m(k)\), multiplicity \(\ge k\) off the origin and origin order \(k-2^{\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor}\) (verification.json). Symmetric \(Q_k\) of the target degree exist for every \(k\le11\).

What remains of the upper half. By the reduction, the upper half of the conjecture for all \(k\) is equivalent to the existence, for every \(j\), of a factor \(Q_{2^j}\) satisfying (i)–(iii) in all dimensions; this is recorded as the power-of-two factor conjecture (conj:power-of-two-factor-existence). The certificates show no closed form yet: \(Q_4\) and \(Q_8\) contain every \(m_\lambda\) of degree below \(2^j\), so \(Q_{2^j}\) agrees with the truncation of \(\prod_i(1+x_i)^{-1}\) below degree \(2^j\) and \(F_nQ_{2^j}=1+(\text{terms of degree}\ge2^j)\), plus a correction of 1 and 27 terms. Products of the "bit" functions \(1+e_{2^i}\) cannot supply it, since weight-one points already need multiplicity \(2^j-1\) from the bit-zero factor.

General statement. The all-parameter results of this cycle are lem:power-of-two-factor-reduction and lem:dimension-free-symmetric-certificates; the finite-certificate theorem thm:binary-multiplicity-upper-bound-small-k applies them for \(k\le15\), and conj:power-of-two-factor-existence states the remaining upper-half obligation.

24 September 2026 — A discrete derivative proves the whole lower half; the formula holds for every k up to 15

Working proofs of the lower bound for all k and of the exact value for k≤15; the upper half beyond 15 remains the power-of-two factor conjecture. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The lower half of the conjectured formula, \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-m(k)\), had no progress. It would follow from a one-dimension step \(D(n-1,k)\le D(n,k)-1\) and the multiplicity bound at the threshold dimension \(m(k)\). This cycle proves that step, tested first against every adjacent pair of the exact table (16 pairs, all differing by exactly one).

Admissible polynomials exist. With \(h=\prod_{y\ne0}(1+y(x))\) over the nonzero linear forms \(y\), \(h(0)=1\) and \(h\) has multiplicity at least \(2^{n-1}\) at every nonzero point (the \(2^{n-1}\) forms with \(y(a)=1\) each vanish there), so \(h^k\) is admissible and \(D(n,k)\) is finite.

The one-dimension step

Working lemma. For \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le k<2^n\), \(D(n-1,k)\le D(n,k)-1\).

Proof. Let \(P\) attain \(D(n,k)\), with degree \(d\), origin order \(\ell\le k-1\) and lowest homogeneous component \(L\ne0\) at the origin. At most \(\ell\le2^n-2\) distinct linear forms divide \(L\), fewer than the \(2^n-1\) nonzero forms, so some nonzero form \(y\) does not divide \(L\). An invertible linear substitution \(A\) with \(y\circ A=x_n\) preserves degree, permutes the nonzero points and preserves multiplicities (\(\operatorname{mult}_a(P\circ A)=\operatorname{mult}_{Aa}P\), since an invertible linear substitution in \(z\) keeps the lowest component nonzero); so assume \(x_n\nmid L\). Write \(P=\sum_{t\ge0}x_n^tP_t(x')\) and set

\[ S(x')=P(x',0)+P(x',1)=\sum_{t\ge1}P_t(x'). \]

(a) \(\deg S\le d-1\), since \(\deg P_t\le d-t\). (b) For \(a'\ne0\) both \((a',0)\) and \((a',1)\) are nonzero; setting \(z_n=0\) in \(P((a',c)+z)\) sends each monomial to itself or to zero, so \(P(x',c)\) has multiplicity at least \(k\) at \(a'\), and so has \(S\). (c) \(P(z',1)\) is the restriction at the nonzero point \((0',1)\), so it has no terms of degree below \(k\). \(P(z',0)\) has lowest component \(L(z',0)\), nonzero of degree \(\ell\) because \(L=x_nL'+L(x',0)\) and \(x_n\nmid L\). Hence \(\operatorname{mult}_{0'}S=\ell\le k-1\). So \(S\) is admissible in dimension \(n-1\).

The lower bound

Working theorem. For every \(k\ge1\) and \(n\ge m(k)\), \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. With \(m=m(k)\), \(2^{m-2}\le k<2^{m-1}\), so the multiplicity bound gives \(D(m,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{m-1}\rfloor=2k\). For \(m<n'\le n\), \(k<2^{m-1}<2^{n'}\), so the step gives \(D(n',k)\ge D(n'-1,k)+1\); summing, \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-m\).

This is the lower half of conj:binary-multiplicity-degree-formula for every \(k\) (its range \(1\le k<2^{n-1}\) is exactly \(n\ge m(k)\)). At \(k=1\) it is the Alon–Füredi bound \(D(n,1)\ge n\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(n\ge2\), by their own induction; \(n=1\) is immediate. It explains the answer to the co-author's question: below the threshold the multiplicity bound is never attained, because each extra dimension costs a full degree.

The exact value for \(k\le15\)

Working corollary. For every \(1\le k\le15\) and every \(n\ge m(k)\), \(D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\). This settles the conjectured formula for \(k\le15\), combining the lower bound with the small-k upper bound. For example, over \(\mathbb F_2\) and large \(n\): \(D(n,4)=n+4\), \(D(n,8)=n+11\), \(D(n,15)=n+25\), while the real-field answer is \(n+2k-3\).

General statement. The all-parameter results are lem:one-dimension-degree-step and thm:binary-multiplicity-lower-bound; cor:binary-multiplicity-degree-small-k gives the exact value for \(k\le15\). The full formula is now equivalent to conj:power-of-two-factor-existence.

Assessment. The lower half of the missing implication is proved for all \(k\) by an elementary argument, and the formula is proved for \(k\le15\). The whole question is now the uniform construction of the power-of-two factors \(Q_{2^j}\). The reverse step fails for simple lifts: \(S(x')(1+x_n)+x_nS(x'+v)\) loses at \((v,0)\) or \((0',1)\), where only order \(\ell+1\) is available, so the product vehicle \(\prod(1+x_i)Q\) remains the route. 24 September 2026 — Two natural routes to the power-of-two factors fail

Refutation of a multiplicative recursion for j=2,3 and finite checks of two ansätze; the factor conjecture stays open. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The formula is now equivalent to conj:power-of-two-factor-existence: for each \(j\), a factor \(Q_{2^j}\) with value one at the origin, degree \(2^{j+1}-j-2\) and multiplicity \(\ge2^j-w\) at weights \(1\le w<2^j\). This cycle tests the two most natural structured constructions and stops at a construction or precise obstructions.

Observations that shaped the tests

The unique top-degree term of the certificates is the staircase \(m_{(2^j-1,2^{j-1}-1,\ldots,3,1)}\): \(m_{(3,1)}\) in \(Q_4\) and \(m_{(7,3,1)}\) in \(Q_8\), whose size \(\sum_{i\le j}(2^i-1)\) is exactly the degree budget. At the threshold dimension \(j+2\) a factor is available from coding theory: the generator columns \(V\) of the first-order Reed–Muller code \(\mathrm{RM}(1,j+1)=[2^{j+1},j+2,2^j]\), in systematic coordinates, give \(\prod_{v\in V}(1+v\cdot x)=\prod_i(1+x_i)\cdot\prod_{w\in W}(1+w\cdot x)\) with \(|W|=2^{j+1}-j-2\); but this factor lives in \(j+2\) variables and fails at points outside them. A product of hyperplanes of the target degree in dimension \(n\) is a binary \([n+2^{j+1}-j-2,\,n,\,2^j]\) code, exactly at the Griesmer bound, and such codes stop existing as \(n\) grows (no \([9,5,4]\) code); so for large \(n\) the factors are not products of linear forms, as the co-author anticipated. Incidentally \(D(n,2^j)\) coincides with the Griesmer length for dimension \(n\) and distance \(2^j\).

The bit-function ansatz

Since \(e_{2^i}(a)\) is bit \(i\) of \(|a|\) by Lucas, polynomials in \(e_1,e_2,e_4,\ldots\) are the natural candidates for weight-dependent conditions. research/tools/bmd_bit_ansatz.py expands each product \(e_\mu\) in the monomial basis (parity of 0–1 matrices) and imposes the dimension-free conditions: no factor exists in \(\mathbb F_2[e_1,e_2,e_4]\) for \(K=4\) (10 unknowns) or in \(\mathbb F_2[e_1,e_2,e_4,e_8]\) for \(K=8\) (74 unknowns). Indeed \(Q_4\) in the elementary basis involves \(e_3\).

No multiplicative recursion

The staircase suggests \(Q_{2^j}=Q_{2^{j-1}}R_j\) with \(\deg R_j=2^j-1\), the exact degree increase. By multiplicity additivity this works for every choice of \(Q_{2^{j-1}}\) meeting its bounds exactly only if \(R_j(0)\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{mult}_aR_j\ge r_j(|a|)\), where \(r_j(w)=2^{j-1}\) for \(1\le w<2^{j-1}\) and \(r_j(w)=2^j-w\) for \(2^{j-1}\le w<2^j\).

Working proposition. For \(j=2,3\) and every \(n\ge2^j-1\), no polynomial \(R\) (symmetric or not) of degree at most \(2^j-1\) with \(R(0)\ne0\) satisfies these bounds.

Proof. Restrict \(R\) to the coordinate subspace of dimension \(N=2^j-1\); restriction does not lower multiplicity and keeps \(R(0)\ne0\). The multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel lemma gives \(\sum_{a}\operatorname{mult}_a\le N\,2^{N-1}\), while the bounds force \(\sum_{w\ge1}\binom Nw r_j(w)\): \(13>12\) for \(j=2\) and \(470>448\) for \(j=3\). With \(h=2^{j-1}\) the excess is \(\tfrac32\,4^{h-1}-\tfrac{2h-1}2\binom{2h-2}{h-1}-h\) (checked against the direct sums), positive for \(h=2,4\) but negative from \(h=8\), so this counting says nothing for \(j\ge4\). For \(j=4\) the symmetric solver finds no symmetric \(R_4\) (recursive-R.json, 684 unknowns, 2030 conditions).

General statement. The all-parameter claim tested is conj:power-of-two-factor-existence; it is neither proved nor refuted. The obstructions obs:bit-algebra-factor-ansatz-fails and obs:multiplicative-factor-recursion-obstruction exclude two constructions of it.

Verification gate. The counting proof reuses the multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel lemma exactly as in the note (with \(S=\mathbb F_2\)) and the restriction fact already reviewed in the one-dimension step; the sums were computed by script. No new lemma is used elsewhere, so no reviewer pass was run for this negative result.

Assessment. The missing implication (a uniform \(Q_{2^j}\)) is unchanged; two natural routes are closed, and any construction must be additive rather than a product of per-level factors. The line's prospects are uncertain: the certificates exist for \(j\le3\) and the lower bound is sharp there, but no structure beyond the staircase top term is visible. Next, study the solution space itself (its dimension and a canonical element) or look for a non-symmetric additive construction built from the Reed–Muller threshold factor. Process: the ansatz tests were seconds; the symmetric \(R_4\) search took 51 s in Python, acceptable here, but a \(j=5\) test would need the compiled kernel.
