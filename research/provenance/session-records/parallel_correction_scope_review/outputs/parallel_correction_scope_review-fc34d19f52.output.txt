
###activation-bounded-theorem
3. Corollary: two-tier or activation-bounded labels
  Theorem. Call a label \(x\) two-tier if \(|I^{\rm sp}_x|<D^\ast\), and activation-bounded if \(m_x(\zeta)<D^\ast\) for every \(\zeta\), with \(\mathcal T\) the sparse classes. If every label is two-tier or activation-bounded, the conclusion of the two-tier theorem holds with the same constants.
  Proof. Only step (G0) changes. In the lemma's count, split \(X\) into its two-tier and activation-bounded labels; for a two-tier label drop the tail event, which can only increase the probability, and count \(|I^{\rm sp}_x|\) choices of \(i_x\); the product-measure expectation then factors as \(\prod_{\text{two-tier}}|I^{\rm sp}_x|\cdot\mathbb E_{\zeta'}\prod_{\text{bounded}}m_x(\zeta')<(D^\ast)^k\). Steps (G1) to (G6) concern dense classes only. \(\square\)
  This is a proper extension: a label with many sparse classes that share few tail rows, or whose patterns are pairwise inconsistent, is activation-bounded without being two-tier. It still excludes labels whose sparse classes sit on many tail rows with compatible patterns, where \(m_x\) can reach \(\Theta(n)\).


###multirow-size-corollary
3. Corollary: every role-separated mixed reader of size below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\)
  Corollary. Under the theorem's conditions on \(N\), if \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\) and \(800e(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), every reader as in Section 1 with at most \(n^c\) terms, whatever its tails, has fewer than \(N/48\) heavy rounds on every path before any 1-leaf with probability at least \(1-3n^{-c-1}\), and switches within the height budget when its tails lie in the light lemma's range.
  Proof. Each term contributes one pair to one host, so \(\Omega_{\rm L}=\sum_x|U^{\rm L}_x|\le\sum_{h\text{ light}}m_h\le n^c\), and \(|X_{\rm L}|\le\Omega_{\rm L}/D_2^\ast\le800e(N+1)n^{c-1}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\); apply (i). \(\square\)
  Beyond size \(n^{4/3}/\mathrm{polylog}\), up to which the general random-flat theorem covers all readers, this is the first switching statement in the window that assumes nothing about the reader's pinned pairs, labels, hosts, or tail shape: the recorded column and host theorems each restrict one of them, and the final clause of the pattern-bounded theorem covers single-row tails of width up to \(\ell/(2\log_2(2\ell+1))\) at every polynomial size. The only width restriction is the light lemma's, on each tail row separately; a tail of total width above \(\tfrac13\log_2n\) must therefore spread over several rows, which the corollary allows.


###crowded-size-corollary
3. Corollary: size below \(n^{3-2\varepsilon}\)
  Corollary. Under the theorem's conditions on \(N\) and \(J_0\), every reader as in Section 1 with \(1600e\,t(N+1)N^4J_0\,n^c\le n^{3-2\varepsilon}\) has fewer than \(N/48\) heavy rounds on every path before any 1-leaf with probability at least \(1-6n^{-c-2}\), and switches within the height budget when its tails lie in the light lemma's range.
  Proof. A host with \(k\le t\) rows is counted in the light mass of \(k\) rows, so \(\sum_jm^{\rm L}_j\le t\sum_hm_h\le tn^c\), and the crowded rows number at most \(tn^c/M_\varepsilon=1600e\,t(N+1)J_0n^{c-2+\varepsilon}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4=b_{\max}\). \(\square\)
  With \(N\) and \(J_0\) polylogarithmic the condition reads \(c\le3-2\varepsilon-o(1)\), against \(2-\varepsilon\) for the previous corollary; the tails must still satisfy \(J_0\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\), that is \(W+4t+\log_2t\le(1-\varepsilon)\log_2n-O(\log\log n)\), and the light lemma's per-row range for switching.


###two-role-few-rows-theorem
3. Theorem: two-role readers with few light rows
  Theorem. Let \(\varepsilon\), \(c\), \(r_0\), \(c'\), \(d\), and \(N\) be as in the few-light-rows theorem, and let a reader have light rows \(B\) with \(b=|B|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\), two-role rows allowed, tails of any shape, any pinned pairs, any size. On the complete-term tree it has fewer than \(N/48\) heavy rounds on every path before any 1-leaf with probability at least \(1-4n^{-c-2}\); if it is sparsely pinned and its light part satisfies the light lemma's hypotheses with \(e\ge2^{w+3}s\), it switches under one restriction within the height budget.
  Proof. Write \(A_0\) for the rows outside \(B\). Condition on \(\zeta=\mu'|_B\), which fixes the labels and the residual status of every row of \(B\), including \(C\); outside the event \(|R_B|\ge r_0\), of probability at most \(n^{-c-2}\), fewer than \(r_0\) light rows are residual. Given \(Q\) and \(\zeta\), the rows of \(A_0\) form a uniform bijection onto the labels outside \(Q\cup\zeta(B)\) and the remaining slots, and \(R_{A_0}=R'\cap A_0\) is a uniform subset of \(A_0\) of size at most \(N+1\), with \(|A_0|=n+1-b\ge n/2+N+2\). For a bad path let \(y\) be its final matching on \(R_B\), extended to the rows a cut last node leaves unassigned consistently with that node's current term and arbitrarily otherwise, and \(\mathcal H_y\), \(U_x(y)\subseteq A\), \(m(y)\), \(X(y)\) as in the few-light-rows setting. Its rounds are of three kinds: (1) rounds whose host is falsified by the path at a residual light row assigned at the round's node, fewer than \(r_0\) by the charging of Lemma 2; (2) rounds whose pinned row lies in \(C\), hence in \(C\cap R_B\), fewer than \(r_0\), one per row; (3) rounds with host in \(\mathcal H_y\) and pinned row in \(R_{A_0}\). Fact (b) holds as before: a host of \(\mathcal H_y\) with a matched pair has a term never falsified on the path, so the preference forbids every round; for a pair at a row of \(C\) the matched status is fixed by \(\zeta\), and on a bad path no such pair of a host of \(\mathcal H_y\) is matched. Fix \(y\), one of fewer than \(n^{r_0}\) labelings. (K1) If \(m(y)\ge(4N+2b+3)(n+1)\): the pairs at rows of \(C\) carry at most \(b(n+1)\) of \(m(y)\), the labels in \(Q\cup\zeta(B)\) at most \((N+b)(n+1)\), so the rows of \(A_0\) carry at least \(3(N+1)(n+1)\) at labels outside \(Q\cup\zeta(B)\), and the product over those columns of the \(A_0\)-bijection gives \(\Pr[\text{no host of }\mathcal H_y\text{ matched}\mid Q,\zeta]\le e^{-3(N+1)}\le n^{-c'}\). (K2) Otherwise \(|X(y)|\le(4N+2b+3)(n+1)/D_2^\ast\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\) for \(N\ge25\); rounds of kind (3) at labels outside \(X(y)\) are fewer than \(N/192\) outside probability \(2^{-N/192}\) by the union-lemma count over \(R_{A_0}\), and \(|X(y)\cap Q|<2^d\) outside probability \(2(Nn^{-\varepsilon})^{d+1}\le n^{-c'}\) by the rank lemma conditioned on \(\zeta\). In all, at most \((N/192-1)+(2^d-1)+2(r_0-1)<N/48\) rounds outside a total probability of \(4n^{-c-2}\), as \(2^d\le N/128\) and \(2r_0\le N/128\) for \(N\ge2^{d+7}\) and \(N\ge256r_0\). The switching clause is Lemma 3 with Lemma 4 and the height budget. \(\square\)


###two-role-many-corollary
3. Corollaries
  Corollary 1 (size below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\)). Under the multi-row theorem's conditions on \(N\), if \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\) and \(800e(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), every two-role reader with at most \(n^c\) terms, whatever its tails, has fewer than \(N/48\) heavy rounds on every path of the complete-term tree before any 1-leaf with probability at least \(1-3n^{-c-1}\), and switches within the height budget under sparse pinning when its tails lie in the light lemma's range. Proof. Steps (H1) to (H3) of the multi-row theorem are role-independent, as the complete-term scope records: given \(Q\) alone the residual rows form a uniform set of at most \(N+1\) of all rows and the occupant products run over all rows, so the union lemma of (H2) applies to \(R'\cap U^{\rm L}_x\) with all \(n+1\) rows in place of \(A\). Hence part (i) of that theorem holds for two-role readers, on the complete-term tree by Lemma 2 with no added rounds, as no light row is exposed; and \(|X_{\rm L}|\le800e(N+1)n^{c-1}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\) as in the multi-row size corollary, since each term contributes one pair to one host whatever the roles of its rows. \(\square\) The theorem above gives the same conclusion with probability \(1-6n^{-c-2}\) for tails on at most \(t\) rows with \(J_0\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/(2N^4)\) and \(3200e\,t(N+1)n^c\le n^{2-\varepsilon}\), taking \(B_{\rm e}\) to be the crowded rows: \(\sum_jm^{\rm L}_j\le tn^c\), so there are at most \(tn^c/M_\varepsilon=1600e\,t(N+1)J_0n^{c-2+\varepsilon}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) of them, and \(\sum_{j\in C_0}m^{\rm L}_j\le tn^c\le M_C\).
  Corollary 2 (size below \(n^{3-2\varepsilon}\)). Under the same conditions, every reader as in Section 1 with \(3200e\,t(N+1)N^4J_0\,n^c\le n^{3-2\varepsilon}\) and either at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/(2N^4)\) two-role rows or \(\sum_{j\in C}m^{\rm L}_j\le M_C\) has the same conclusion. Proof. The crowded rows number at most \(tn^c/M_\varepsilon\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/(2N^4)\). In the first case take \(B_{\rm e}\) to be the crowded rows together with \(C\), so \(C_0=\varnothing\); in the second take the crowded rows, so \(C_0\subseteq C\). \(\square\)


###composition-gap-statement
2. The gap
(a) Two families. The round bounds are statements about \(\Phi_0\) and use its structure throughout: the products over columns run over occupied outside labels, the residual set has exactly \(N+1\) rows, the union lemma's probability that \(k\) given rows are residual is \(((N+1)/(n/2))^k\), and the light bijection of the exposed steps is onto \(Q^c\) and \(N+1\) slots. The extension encoding needs \(e\ge2^{w+3}s\) free outside labels and does not exist on \(\Phi_0\). No recorded argument applies a round bound to the slack tree \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\), \(\rho\in\Phi_e\), or the encoding to the compact tree \(\mathcal T(F,\rho')\), or relates the two trees: the restriction of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) by \(\rho''\) is a tree for \(\rho'\), but it is not \(\mathcal T(F,\rho')\), since the matched-first preference and the falsification tests of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) are evaluated on \(\rho\), which matches \(e\) fewer rows.
(b) The compact proofs do not survive on the slack family, and the slack tree is not defined at pins with free holes. The round-bound proofs use \(|R'|=N+1\) essentially: the sparse count of the union lemma sets \(((N+1)/(n/2))^k\) against \((D_2^\ast)^k\) choices of rows, which on \(\Phi_e\), with \(N+1+e\) residual rows and \(e=n^\theta\), becomes about \((n^{\theta}/(400k))^k\); the residual-row bound of the exposed steps, \(((N+1)b/n)^{r}\), becomes \((eb(N+1+e)/(rn))^{r}\), useless for \(\theta\ge\varepsilon\); and the products over columns lose the \(e\) unoccupied labels as dormant mass. So no recorded round bound is a statement about the slack tree, at any size. Moreover, for \(\rho\in\Phi_e\) a term \([i\to x]\wedge h\) with \(i\in R(\rho)\) and \(x\in E(\rho)\) is neither falsified nor satisfied. Under the recorded rule its pinned pair is not alive, because \(x\notin Q\); the node queries the uncovered tail rows, and at the next node the same term is current with no uncovered tail row and no alive pair, so the node has no query. On \(\Phi_0\) the case does not arise, which is why the theorems are stated there. The tool resolves it by treating the pair as alive, with a round whose pigeon answer is a hole of \(Q\cup E\) and whose hole answer is a residual row; then even the trivial count fails, since trigger holes may lie in \(E\) and the rounds number up to \(N+e\). For a reader of \(n^c\) terms with spread pinned pairs, a pair has \(i\in R(\rho)\) and \(x\in E(\rho)\) with probability \(((N+1+e)/(n+1))(e/(n-N))\), so about \(n^{c-2}e^2\) pairs are alive at free holes, and for \(c>2-2\theta\) their matching number exceeds \(N\); below that size they are negligible in expectation and the residual count alone is the obstruction.
(c) The arithmetic of the sketch. Even for rounds at holes of \(Q\), the slack tree's pigeon answer ranges over \(Q\cup E\), and its hole answer over the \(N+1+e\) residual rows of \(\rho\), so a round costs \((N+e)(N+e+2)=n^{2\theta+o(1)}\) in \(\delta\) (the recorded count \((N+e)(N+2)\) of the both-endpoints lemma and Lemma 1 pairs a slack pigeon count with a compact hole count, an instance of the same mix), while a moved row gains \(A=2r(N+e)e(N+e+1)/(n-N-e+1)=n^{-(1-3\theta)+o(1)}\). With \(\nu<N/48\) rounds and height \(s+2\nu\le N/8-r\) the sum is below \(n^{-c}/8\) only if \((1-3\theta)s\ge2\theta\nu+c+o(N)\) with \(s\le N/12\), that is \(\theta<2/7\); the observation's arithmetic, \(A^s=n^{-s/3+o(s)}\) against a heavy factor \(n^{k/3+o(N)}\), takes \(e\le n^{2/9}\) for the first and \(e\le n^{1/3}\) for the second. So the sketch, where it applies, covers per-row widths up to \((2/7-o(1))\log_2n\), not the light lemma's \(\tfrac13\log_2n\). On the compact tree the answers of a round take \(N(N+2)\) values, polylogarithmic; the width range would be the light lemma's if the composition were carried out there.


###composition-gap-routes
4. Repair routes and their obstacles
(R1) Compose on the slack family, moving pinned rows. Define the slack tree at a term whose pin \((i,x)\) has \(x\in E(\rho)\) by a pigeon query on \(i\) alone (an answer \(x\) satisfies the pin, any other answer falsifies the term); restricted by \(\rho''\), the query is answered by the filling when \(i\) is filled and is a real query into \(Q\) otherwise. Encode the pinned row of every such pigeon query as a moved row with its pin \(x\) as code label, free in \(\rho\), and the pinned row of a round at a hole of \(Q\) whose pigeon answer \(y\) lies in \(E(\rho)\) as a moved row with code label \(y\): the family ratio then pays the answer as for a light row, each such round costs a factor \(A\), no bound on their number is needed, and the rounds whose answers lie in \(Q\) cost \(N(N+2)\) each, polylogarithmic, so the width range is the light lemma's. Obstacle: the decoder's matched test on \(\rho^\ast\) sees a moved pinned row as a matched pair; a term whose pinned row is moved at a later node is a fake matched-pair term at every earlier node and, when it precedes the true current term in the reader's order, the matched-first preference chooses it. A light answer \(j\mapsto x\) recorded in \(\delta\) may also collide with a pin \(x\) used as a code label, so that a term killed in reality shows a satisfied pin in \(\alpha\); a collision rule (a row at its pin in \(\rho^\ast\) while that label is a hole of \(\delta\) counts as killed) is needed. Under the plain earliest-term rule with that collision rule the decoder is correct, because a fake term earlier than the current term would itself be current, but fact (b), which every round bound uses through the preference, is lost. A preference that the decoder can compute and that keeps fact (b) is the missing device.
(R2) Compose on the compact family by a swap. Fix an auxiliary decomposition \(\rho'=\rho\cup\rho''\); move \(j\) to \(y\in E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X_j\) and make the occupant of \(y\) residual, so the code stays in \(\Phi_0\); counting pairs \((\rho,\rho'')\), the family ratio is \(\binom{n-N}{e-s}/\binom{n-N}{e}\le(e/(n-N-e+1))^s\), the displaced row is one of \(N+1\) residual rows of the code, and the light answers are holes of \(Q\), so a moved row costs about \(2rN^2e/n\), which would widen the light range. Obstacle: before the moved row's node the decoder sees the displaced row as residual, while in \(\rho'\) it is matched at \(y\), and a term falsified only through that row is misjudged; recording the displaced row's label costs \(n\) per row, and the set \(E(\rho)\) is not known to the decoder. If the count held with a decoder, the light range would pass the recorded intrinsic bound (code obstruction, window structure), whose argument covers extension codes with free pools; the displaced row's invisible label is where that entropy reappears, so the route is recorded as an obstacle, not a prospect.
(R4) Narrow tails on the slack family. For tails of polylogarithmic cube size, \(2^w\) and hence \(e\) polylogarithmic, the compact proofs use \(|R'|\) only through factors \((N+1)/n\) and the mass at unoccupied labels, so re-running them on \(\Phi_e\) with \(N+1+e\) residual rows, \(D_2^\ast\) reduced by the factor \((N+1+e)/(N+1)\), and \(e(n+1)\) extra dormant mass looks routine; the free-hole pins then have expected count \(n^{c-2}(N+1+e)e\), negligible below size \(n^{2-o(1)}\), and a slack round costs polylogarithmically. With a restriction-by-filling argument for trees with hole queries, also unwritten, this would recover switching for narrow tails below size \(n^2\) without moving pinned rows; it does not reach the light lemma's width range.
(R3) Bound the extra rounds on the slack family directly. Rounds at free holes have unbounded matching number for \(c\ge2-2\theta\) by Section 2(b), and rounds at holes of \(Q\) whose pinned row is filled cost \(n^{\theta}\) each; neither can be absorbed without moving the pinned row.
A per-node mark of whether the current term's pin is matched is not enough for (R1): a term whose pinned row is moved at a later node, with its pin free and its tail alive, is unmatched in reality, never preferred, and can precede a marked current term in the reader's order. The device to test first records instead, at each node whose current term has a matched pin, the rank of that pin among the \(\rho^\ast\)-matched pins carrying a term not falsified by \(\alpha\), in the order of their first terms: the fake pins are pins of rows moved at later nodes, at most one per later round, so the rank is at most \(\nu+1\le N+1\), and among the terms with the identified pin the earliest one not falsified by \(\alpha\) is the current term, since an earlier term unfalsified in reality with the same matched pin would itself be current. With the collision rule this is a candidate preference, not a proof; the round-trip tool, extended to move pinned rows, decides it on small boards before any proof.
