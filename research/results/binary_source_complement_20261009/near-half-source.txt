The graded ring
Let \(F\) have characteristic two, \(V=F^n\) with coordinates \(x_i\), and \(A=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_i^2)\), graded, \(\dim A_j=\binom nj\). Every linear form has square zero in \(A\), so \(A\) is the exterior algebra of the space of linear forms, which in characteristic two is commutative, and every invertible linear change of coordinates is an automorphism of \(A\). For linear forms \(\ell,\ell'\) the product \(\omega=\ell\ell'\) depends only on the plane \(W=\langle\ell,\ell'\rangle\), up to a nonzero scalar, and is zero when they are dependent. If \(W\) is a plane, choose coordinates with \(\ell=x_1\), \(\ell'=x_2\): then \(\omega A=x_1x_2A'\), \(A'\) the algebra on the other variables, and the annihilator of \(\omega\) is the ideal \((\ell,\ell')\). So \(\omega A\cong(A/(\ell,\ell'))(-2)\), with series \(z^2(1+z)^{n-2}\).
For planes \(W_1,\ldots,W_m\) with two-vectors \(\omega_i\) let \(I_m=(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m)\) and \(H_m(z)\) the Hilbert series of \(A/I_m\). The product series is \(P_{n,m}(z)=(1+z)^{n-2m}(1+2z)^m\).
Lemma (decomposable colon recursion). For each \(m\ge1\) and each degree \(j\), with \(\bar A=A/(\ell_m,\ell_m')\), the exterior algebra on \(n-2\) variables, and \(\bar I_{m-1}\) the image of \(I_{m-1}\) in it: \[\dim(A/I_m)_j=\dim(A/I_{m-1})_j-\dim(\bar A/\bar I_{m-1})_{j-2}+\dim\Bigl(\frac{(I_{m-1}:\omega_m)}{I_{m-1}+(\ell_m,\ell_m')}\Bigr)_{j-2}.\] Consequently, suppose that for every \(m'\le m\) and every set \(K\subseteq\{m'+1,\ldots,m\}\) the sum of the planes \(W_k\), \(k\in K\), is direct and meets each \(W_i\), \(i\le m'\), trivially, and that the last term vanishes for the configuration \(W_1,\ldots,W_{m'}\) taken modulo that sum, in degrees at most \(j-2|K|-2\). Then the coefficients of \(H_m\) through degree \(j\) are those of \(P_{n,m}\).
Proof. \((A/I_m)_j\) is the cokernel of multiplication by \(\omega_m\) from \((A/I_{m-1})_{j-2}\) to \((A/I_{m-1})_j\), whose kernel is \((I_{m-1}:\omega_m)/I_{m-1}\) in degree \(j-2\). That kernel contains \((I_{m-1}+(\ell_m,\ell_m'))/I_{m-1}\), since \(\ell_m\omega_m=\ell_m'\omega_m=0\), and \(A/(I_{m-1}+(\ell_m,\ell_m'))=\bar A/\bar I_{m-1}\). Counting dimensions gives the display. For the consequence: \(\bar I_{m-1}\) is generated by the images of the \(\omega_i\), which are two-vectors of planes in the \((n-2)\)-dimensional quotient space by the hypothesis, and induction on \(m\) through the configurations named applies with \(P_{n,m-1}-z^2P_{n-2,m-1}=(1+z)^{n-2m}(1+2z)^{m-1}\bigl((1+z)^2-z^2\bigr)=P_{n,m}\). \(\square\)
The last term of the display is what a proof of exactness has to kill: the new row must be a zero divisor on the quotient by the earlier ones only through its own two forms. It is injectivity of multiplication by \(\omega_m\) from \(\bar A/\bar I_{m-1}\) to \(A/I_{m-1}\). Only sufficiency is proved; the vanishing is equivalent to exactness for the new row once the earlier rows and their images are exact.
One dependency
Proposition (one dependency series exactness). Let \(n=2m-1\), \(m\ge2\), and let the space of linear forms be \(P_1\oplus\cdots\oplus P_{m-1}\oplus\langle t\rangle\) with planes \(P_i\). Let \(W_i=P_i\) for \(i<m\), and let \(W_m\) be a plane not contained in \(P=\bigoplus P_i\), so that \(W_m\cap P\) is a line \(\langle\lambda\rangle\), and suppose the component of \(\lambda\) in every \(P_i\) is nonzero. Then \[H_m(z)=\frac{(1+2z)^m+z^{m+1}}{1+z},\] which agrees with \(P_{n,m}=(1+2z)^m/(1+z)\) in every degree at most \(m\); the coefficients of \(P_{n,m}\) are positive through degree \(m\) and the next is \(-1\). If instead the component of \(\lambda\) is nonzero in exactly \(s\) of the planes, \(1\le s\le m-1\), then \(H_m=\bigl((1+2z)^m+z^{s+2}(1+2z)^{m-1-s}\bigr)/(1+z)\), agreeing with \(P_{n,m}\) exactly in degrees at most \(s+1\).
Proof. Choose \(\ell\in W_m\setminus P\) and change the coordinate \(t\) to \(\ell\), which fixes \(P\) and the \(\omega_i\), \(i<m\); then \(\omega_m=t\lambda\) up to a scalar. In \(P_i\), every pair of independent forms has the same product up to a scalar, so a basis \(u_i,v_i\) of \(P_i\) may be chosen with \(u_i\) the component of \(\lambda\) when it is nonzero; \(\omega_i=u_iv_i\). So \(A/I_{m-1}=Q\otimes F[t]/(t^2)\), \(Q=\bigotimes_{i<m}Q_i\), \(Q_i=F[u_i,v_i]/(u_i^2,v_i^2,u_iv_i)\) with basis \(1,u_i,v_i\), and \(\omega_m=t\sigma\), \(\sigma=\sum_{i\in S}u_i\), \(S\) the set of planes met, \(|S|=s\). Then \(\omega_m(A/I_{m-1})=t\,\sigma Q\), so \(\dim(I_m/I_{m-1})_j=\dim(\sigma Q)_{j-1}\). Split \(Q\) by the set \(T\subseteq S\) of factors in \(S\) taken as \(v_i\) and by the basis element taken in each factor outside \(S\): on such a summand \(\sigma\) acts as multiplication by \(\sum_{i\in S\setminus T}u_i\) on \(E=\bigotimes_{i\in S\setminus T}F[u_i]/(u_i^2)\), an exterior algebra on \(N=s-|T|\) variables, the terms \(u_i\) with \(i\in T\) killing \(v_i\). For \(N\ge1\) a change of coordinates makes this form a variable \(u\), and multiplication by a variable on \(E=F[u]/(u^2)\otimes E'\) has image \(uE'\), of series \(z(1+z)^{N-1}\); for \(N=0\) it is zero. Summing over \(T\) with \(|T|=r\le s-1\), weight \(z^r\), and over the factors outside \(S\), series \((1+2z)^{m-1-s}\): \[\sum_j\dim(\sigma Q)_jz^j=(1+2z)^{m-1-s}\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}\binom srz^r\,z(1+z)^{s-1-r}=(1+2z)^{m-1-s}\,\frac{z\bigl((1+2z)^s-z^s\bigr)}{1+z}.\] Hence \(H_m=(1+2z)^{m-1}(1+z)-z^2(1+2z)^{m-1-s}\bigl((1+2z)^s-z^s\bigr)/(1+z)\), which is the stated series, using \((1+z)^2-z^2=1+2z\). The difference from \(P_{n,m}\) is \(z^{s+2}(1+2z)^{m-1-s}/(1+z)\), whose lowest term is \(z^{s+2}\). For \(s=m-1\): the coefficients \(c_j\) of \(P_{n,m}\) are the dimensions of \((A/I_m)_j\) for \(j\le m\); \(c_m=\sum_i(-1)^{m-i}\binom mi2^i=1\) and \(c_{m+1}=-c_m=-1\). Since \(A/I_m\) is generated in degree one, \((A/I_m)_m\ne0\) forces \((A/I_m)_j\ne0\) for all \(j\le m\), so \(c_j>0\) there. \(\square\)
In the lemma's terms, the proposition says that for one dependency in general position the last term of the display vanishes below the top, where it is the class of \(v_1\cdots v_{m-1}\), and it shows what the term is otherwise: the part of the quotient on which \(\sigma\) is zero for want of a factor to act on.

From graded to Boolean. The rows of a formula are products of affine forms in \(F[x]/(x_i^2-x_i)\); their top forms generate the ideal studied here, and the static span's associated graded space contains its part of degree at most \(D\), so the Boolean quotient is at most the graded one, with equality when every fall of degree has its top form in the graded ideal; falls there always are, a form times its own row. I have not proved equality in any case, and the runs do not show it, the graded quotient not having been computed.
Whether the linear relations of products of affine forms account for the excess of the computed degree over the index of the generic series at \(n=20\) and \(32\). The generic series ignores that a product of two forms is killed by each of them; the product series is the series of the recursion above and has index four at \((20,60)\) and at \((32,96)\).
