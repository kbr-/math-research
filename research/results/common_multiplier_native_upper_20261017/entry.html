Native rectangle upper bound

Setting. Over a field \(k\), \(J_{D'}\) is the degree-\(D'\) part of the ideal of the Boolean axioms \(x_i^2-x_i\) in \(n\) variables, so that within degree \(D'\) membership is vanishing on the cube \(\{0,1\}^n\); an old functional is a linear functional on \(V_{D'}\) vanishing on \(J_{D'}\). Following lem:native-detection-count, for errors \(E_1,\dots,E_s\) of degree at most \(e\), \(U=V_K\) and slack \(L\) with \(e+K+L\le D'\), old functionals \(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_m\) detect the stacked kernel \(\ker T\), \(T(u)=([E_iu])_i\), when \(\ker T\) equals the set of \(u\) with \(\mu_j(E_iuv)=0\) for all \(i\), \(j\) and \(v\in V_L\). \(N_a(d)=\sum_{k\le d}\binom ak\), and \(B_d\subseteq\{0,1\}^a\) is the set of points of weight at most \(d\).

Lemma (native rectangle upper bound). Let each \(E_i\in V_e\) be a polynomial whose function on the cube has as support a nonempty subcube \(A_i\) of dimension \(a_i\), that is, the set where some coordinates take fixed values; over \(\mathbb F_2\) an affine subspace with an affine coordinate system serves as well (as for Pang's errors \(E_i=Pg_i\) with linear inputs, \(A_i=\{q_1=\dots=q_t=0,\ g_i=1\}\), in lem:native-functional-growth's proof). Let \(\phi_i:\{0,1\}^{a_i}\to A_i\) be the coordinate map (over \(\mathbb F_2\), an affine one), and for each \(y\) of weight at most \(\max(K-L,0)\) let \(\lambda_{i,y}=\sum_{z\in y\oplus B_L}E_i(\phi_i(z))^{-1}\delta_{\phi_i(z)}\), \(\oplus\) the coordinatewise sum modulo \(2\). These \(\sum_iN_{a_i}(\max(K-L,0))\le s\,N_n(\max(K-L,0))\) functionals are old and detect \(\ker T\). For \(s=1\) and \(A=A_1\) of dimension \(a\), the least number \(m_{\min}\) of old functionals detecting \(\ker T\) at slack \(L\) satisfies \(N_a(K)/N_a(L)\le m_{\min}\le N_a(\max(K-L,0))\); for \(K\ge L\), \(N_a(K)\le N_a(L)N_a(K-L)\), and the ratio of the two bounds tends to \(\binom KL\) as \(a\to\infty\).

Proof. A point evaluation vanishes on the Boolean ideal, so each \(\lambda_{i,y}\) is old. On the cube, \(E_iu\) vanishes exactly when \(u\) vanishes on \(A_i\), so \(\ker T=\{u:u|_{A_i}=0\text{ for all }i\}\), and for \(u\in\ker T\) every form is zero (each \(E_iuv\) has degree at most \(e+K+L\le D'\)). Conversely suppose all the forms vanish. Then \(\lambda_{i,y}(E_iuv)=\sum_{z\in y\oplus B_L}(u\circ\phi_i)(z)(v\circ\phi_i)(z)\), the weights cancelling \(E_i\). The map \(\phi_i\) preserves degree (it substitutes constants for the fixed coordinates; over \(\mathbb F_2\) an affine map), and the restrictions of \(V_L\) to \(A_i\) are all functions of degree at most \(L\) on \(\{0,1\}^{a_i}\).

  • The set \(y\oplus B_L\) is independent for degree \(L\): the evaluation of the multilinear monomials \(x_T\), \(|T|\le L\), at the points \(1_R\), \(|R|\le L\), is \([T\subseteq R]\), unitriangular over any field, so evaluation on \(B_L\) is bijective; the map \(z\mapsto y\oplus z\) is the substitution \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\) for \(i\) in the support of \(y\), which preserves degree. So as \(v\) ranges over \(V_L\), \((v\circ\phi_i(z))_{z\in y\oplus B_L}\) takes every value, and the forms give \(u\circ\phi_i=0\) on \(y\oplus B_L\).
  • The union of the \(y\oplus B_L\) contains \(B_K\): a point of weight \(w\le K\) is a centre when \(w\le K-L\), and otherwise lies in \(y\oplus B_L\) for \(y\) obtained by removing \(w-(K-L)\le L\) of its coordinates; when \(K\le L\), \(B_K\subseteq B_L\) and the one centre \(y=0\) suffices.
  • \(B_K\) determines degree-\(K\) functions, by the same unitriangularity; this is lem:all-field-approximate-majority-certifier-barrier's statement that a nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most \(K\) meets the radius-\(K\) Hamming ball, there over any field. So \(u|_{A_i}=0\) for every \(i\).
For \(s=1\), the lower bound is lem:native-detection-count (a), \(r\le m\dim Z_L\), with \(r=\dim V_K|_A=N_a(K)\) and \(Z_L\), the span of \(Ev\) modulo \(J_{e+L}\), isomorphic through \(v\mapsto Ev\) to \(V_L|_A\), of dimension \(N_a(L)\). Every set of size at most \(K\) is a union of one of size at most \(L\) and one of size at most \(K-L\), so \(N_a(K)\le N_a(L)N_a(K-L)\); the leading terms \(a^L/L!\cdot a^{K-L}/(K-L)!\) against \(a^K/K!\) give the limit \(\binom KL\). \(\square\)

Hypotheses, each with its step.

  • The cube (the Boolean axioms in \(J\)): membership as vanishing, and point evaluations as old functionals. The field: any, with subcube supports; \(\mathbb F_2\) only for affine supports, which need affine coordinates of \(\{0,1\}^n\).
  • Each \(E_i\) supported on a nonempty subcube: the weights \(E_i^{-1}\) on the support and the coordinate map preserving degree.
  • \(e+K+L\le D'\): the forms are defined on \(E_iuv\). Nothing else is used: the lemma holds at every \(K\), \(L\), \(n\), \(s\).

Degenerate cases. \(L=0\): \(B_0=\{0\}\), the functionals are weighted point evaluations at the \(N_a(K)\) points of \(B_K\), and the bounds agree, \(m_{\min}=N_a(K)\). \(K\le L\): one functional per support, so for \(s=1\), \(m_{\min}=1\). An empty \(A_i\) (over \(\mathbb F_2\), an input in the span of the \(q_k\)): \(E_i=0\) and no functional is needed for it.

Nonvacuity. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(n=5\), \(E=x_5\) (support the subcube \(x_5=1\), \(a=4\)), \(e=1\), \(K=2\), \(L=1\), \(D'=4\), so \(e+K+L=4\le D'\): the lemma gives \(N_4(1)=5\) functionals against the lower bound \(\lceil11/5\rceil=3\); native_upper.json gives rank \(11=N_4(2)\) for the five ball translates on \(\{0,1\}^4\), and exact_k2.json rank \(11\) with three sets. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) every old functional is a sum of point evaluations, and its forms on \(E\cdot\) depend only on its points in \(A\), so searching sets in \(\{0,1\}^a\) loses nothing.
