Define the restricted model precisely

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be an oriented graph on \(n\ge4\) vertices, with edge expansion \(\varepsilon>0\): every \(S\) with \(0<|S|\le n/2\) has \(|\delta S|\ge\varepsilon|S|\). Positive expansion implies connectedness, since a smallest component of a disconnected graph would have size at most \(n/2\) and empty boundary. Give vertex \(v\) charge \(c_v\in\mathbb F_3\), with \(\sigma=\sum_vc_v\ne0\). Its equation is divergence equal to \(c_v\), on independent ternary edge labels.

A charge derivation starts with these vertex equations and optionally \(0=0\). Each later equation is a scalar multiple or a linear combination of at most two earlier equations over \(\mathbb F_3\). It ends in \(0=b\), \(b\ne0\). The derivation may be a DAG with arbitrary reuse and length. Each line is an equation, not a disjunction of equations or an arbitrary Boolean formula. For a vertex coefficient vector \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_3^V\), its line is \[ E_\lambda:\quad\sum_{e=u\to v}(\lambda_u-\lambda_v)x_e=\sum_v\lambda_vc_v. \tag{1} \] Label the vertex equations by the unit vectors, assign the optional \(0=0\) the zero vector, and propagate labels by the same scalar/binary operations. This supplies labels to every line; no syntactic charge annotation of arbitrary Frege lines is assumed.

Proposition (charge annotation barrier). Every such derivation contains a line \(E_\lambda\) with \[ \frac n3\le |\operatorname{supp}\lambda|<\frac{2n}3, \qquad |\{e=u\to v:\lambda_u\ne\lambda_v\}|\ge\frac{\varepsilon n}{6}. \tag{2} \] For a bounded-degree graph family with expansion bounded below by a positive constant, if every line predicate is represented exactly on all valid one-hot edge assignments by an AND/OR/NOT/MOD2 circuit of one fixed depth, some line has superpolynomial circuit size in \(n\). No bound on the number of derivation lines is needed for this conclusion.

Find a balanced vertex coefficient support

Proof of (2). At the final equation the edge coefficient vector is zero, so connectedness forces its label to be a constant vector \(t\mathbf1\). Its right-hand side is \(t\sigma\ne0\), hence \(t\ne0\) and its support has size \(n\). Every initial label has support at most one, below \(n/3\) because \(n\ge4\).

In a topological ordering, take the first label whose support has size at least \(n/3\). It cannot arise from scalar multiplication of a smaller-support label. It is a binary combination of two labels with support less than \(n/3\), so its support lies in their union and has size less than \(2n/3\). Cancellation can only reduce that union, establishing the first part of (2), without a tree-like or monotonicity assumption.

Among the two nonzero level sets of \(\lambda\), choose a larger one \(S\). Then \(|S|\ge n/6\) and \(|S|<2n/3\). If \(|S|\le n/2\), use it; otherwise use its complement, whose size is greater than \(n/3\) and at most \(n/2\). In either case the smaller side of this cut has at least \(n/6\) vertices. Every crossing edge has unequal endpoint labels, so expansion gives the second part of (2). \(\square\)

Nonzero total charge is needed to make the final constant label nonzero; connectedness identifies the kernel of the edge-gradient map. In fact the map \(\lambda\mapsto(\partial\lambda,\lambda\cdot c)\) is injective here, since a constant vector in its kernel is zero. Thus replacing a label by a global additive constant is not a free normalization of the same charged equation.

An exact charge predicate encodes MOD3

Proof of the circuit consequence. Suppose a line has \(q\ge\varepsilon n/6\) nonzero edge coefficients \(a_e\in\{1,2\}\) and right-hand side \(b\). Fix one of these edge labels to \(b/a_e\). For each of the remaining \(q-1\) active edges, restrict its label to \(a_e^{-1}u_e\) for a Boolean input \(u_e\). Set inactive edges to label zero. These are constant-size substitutions into one-hot indicators: each variable indicator becomes a constant, \(u_e\), or \(1-u_e\). Under this restriction, (1) is exactly \[ \sum_{e=1}^{q-1}u_e=0\pmod3. \] Thus a polynomial-size fixed-depth binary-modular representation of the line would give one for MOD3 on \(q-1=\Omega(n)\) bits, contradicting Smolensky's Theorem 2. Initial vertex equations have bounded arity on fixed-degree graphs; their easy representation is consistent with the forced intermediate line. \(\square\)

The exactness requirement is on the entire valid one-hot cube, not merely on models of the inconsistent old system. The latter requirement would be vacuous. The circuit field/gates remain binary-modular: a short arithmetic expression over \(\mathbb F_4\) is not the asserted Boolean circuit representation.

Compare the existing positive derivation

The complete balanced character-cut proof was reread. It derives cut binomials over a root-of-unity extension with ordinary PC degree bounded by half the cutwidth plus its local reserve. That proof already allows balanced products and inverse-character certificates. It neither supplies a small fixed-depth binary Boolean circuit for each exact cut predicate nor extracts a charge-only annotation from an arbitrary Frege proof. Its degree upper bound and the present circuit representation lower bound concern different resources and are compatible.

The ladder is the negative graph control. Its running linear charge relations have a constant-size rail boundary along the natural order, while its expansion tends to zero; (2) gives no growing boundary there. Its small Frege proof and hard split separator therefore do not contradict the present restricted-derivation statement. They do forbid transferring a depth guarantee merely from the local form of an inference.

The still-missing extraction theorem

An additional theorem would suffice: from every size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) Frege refutation of the fixed expander family, construct a charge derivation whose every line has an exact binary-modular circuit of depth bounded independently of \(n,S\), and size polynomial in \(n,S\). For \(S\le N^A\), fixed degree makes the original encoding length polynomial in \(n\), so the proposition contradicts that representation for every fixed \(A\). This is a conditional consequence, not an available extraction result.

Arbitrary Frege lines contain disjunctions, conjunctions and MOD2 compositions of mixed private variables. Their truth functions are not individual ternary charge equations. No rule here maps a general MP inference into the binary linear-combination rule, nor bounds the size/depth of any such map. Allowing arbitrary Boolean combinations or new existential witness variables would change the annotation model and invalidate the direct application of (2). The needed source coverage is therefore substantial; the proposition cannot be promoted to the requested Frege bound.
