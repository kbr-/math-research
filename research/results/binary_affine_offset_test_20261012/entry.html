Monomial exchange certificates

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with Boolean coordinates \(y_1,\ldots,y_v\). For every \(r\)-subset \(S\subseteq[v]\), \(1\le r\le v\), let \(m_S=\prod_{i\in S}y_i\), let \(\ell_S\) be affine, and put \(G_S=m_S+\ell_S\). Define the quadratic list \(\mathcal Q\) by \[ A_{S,i}=(1+y_i)\ell_S\quad(i\in S),\qquad E_{S,i,j}=y_j\ell_S+y_i\ell_{S-i+j}\quad(i\in S,j\notin S). \] Every member has degree at most two. Zero members are retained harmlessly. We use \(\mathcal I_c\) for ordinary ideal certificates all of whose displayed generator multiples have degree at most \(c\).

Lemma. For all \(S,T\) of size \(r\), \[\ell_S G_T\in\mathcal I_{r+2}(\mathcal Q,\mathrm{BOOL}),\] with coefficients on the quadratic generators of degree at most \(r\). Moreover these quadratic generators lift to affine coefficient rows on the original \(G\) tuple: \[ (1+y_i)G_S=A_{S,i}+(y_i^2+y_i)m_{S\setminus i},\qquad y_jG_S+y_iG_{S-i+j}=E_{S,i,j}. \tag{1} \] The first comparison has a Boolean certificate through \(r+1\); the second is a literal identity.

Proof. List \(S\setminus T=\{i_1,\ldots,i_k\}\) and \(T\setminus S=\{j_1,\ldots,j_k\}\) in any order and set \(S_a=S-\{i_1,\ldots,i_a\}+\{j_1,\ldots,j_a\}\). Write \(s=\ell_S\), \(b_a=\prod_{q\le a}y_{j_q}\) and \(D_a=s\ell_{S_a}+sb_a\). The exact recurrence is \[ D_0=s^2+s,\qquad D_a=y_{j_a}D_{a-1}+sE_{S_{a-1},i_a,j_a}+\ell_{S_a}A_{S,i_a}. \tag{2} \] Indeed the two \(sy_{i_a}\ell_{S_a}\) terms cancel. It matters that each removed \(i_a\) belongs to the original \(S\), so the support relation in (2) is available. Iterating gives \[ D_k=b_k(s^2+s)+\sum_{a=1}^k\left(\prod_{q>a}y_{j_q}\right) \left(sE_{S_{a-1},i_a,j_a}+\ell_{S_a}A_{S,i_a}\right). \tag{3} \] Order \(C=S\cap T\). The further exact identity \[ \ell_S G_T=D_k+b_k\sum_{i\in C}\left(\prod_{c\in C,\ c\text{ before }i}y_c\right)A_{S,i} \tag{4} \] follows by telescoping \(1+\prod_{i\in C}y_i\). Since \(s\) is binary affine, \(s^2+s\) is a constant combination of the Boolean equations. The first term of (3) costs \(k+2\); each summand in its sum costs at most \(k-a+3\le r+2\); the terms added in (4) cost at most \(r+1\). These are written certificates, not only equalities on points. The formulas (1) follow by expansion. An invertible affine change from old coordinates \(x\) preserves all bounds and transports each \(y_i^2+y_i\) to a constant combination of \(x_j^2+x_j\). This proves the lemma.

Affine monomial cleanup

Let an unsatisfiable binary Boolean old system \(\Gamma(x)\) in \(v\) variables have generator degrees at most \(u\ge2\). Let \(J_D\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a proper degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing \(\Gamma\) and Booleanity, closed under multiplication whenever the resulting degrees stay at most \(D\). For each of \(M\ge1\) parents choose any predetermined invertible affine old coordinate system \(y_a\), and arbitrary affine \(\ell_{a,S}\). Include genuine complete accuracy-\(h\) affine-bottom blocks on \((1-y_{a,i})_{i\in S}\) for every \(|S|=r\), and a complete accuracy-\(h\) parent with inputs their genuine products plus \(\ell_{a,S}\). Identical defining bottom tuples may share a block. No parent reads another parent or raw coefficient variables.

Theorem. Suppose \(2\le r\le v\), \(2\le K\le v\), \(t\ge1\), \(h\ge\max(r,t+1)\), and \[ T=u-1,\quad B\ge\max(u,2h^2+3h),\quad K+TB\le D, \qquad M(1-\eta)^t<1,\quad \eta=\frac{N_v(K-2)}{2N_v(K)},\quad N_v(k)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom vj. \tag{5} \] Then there is a common old polynomial \(W\notin J_D\), \(\deg W\le K\), and one coherent assignment of all fresh coefficients to old polynomials of degree at most \(T\), such that all weighted mapped source axioms belong to \(J_D\). The complete source has no degree-\(B\) PC or ordinary NS refutation. Exactly \(t+1\) potentially active rows per parent suffice; unused original rows are zero-padded. No small-description bound for the polynomial coefficient maps is claimed.

Quadratic stage. Set each bottom to its literal \(r\) rows and zero-pad, so its product is exactly \(m_{a,S}\) and its companions have Boolean certificates through \(r+1\). Form \(\mathcal Q_a\) for each resulting tuple. Apply prop:legal-row-centering with no high directions, incoming weight one, \(d=\delta=2\). Its reserve is \(K+2(t+1)\le D\), implied by (5). It supplies constant rows on each quadratic tuple, with product \[P_a=\prod_{b=1}^t(1+q_{a,b}),\qquad \deg q_{a,b}\le2,\] and one \(W\notin J_D\), of degree at most \(K\), such that \(WP_aQ\in J_D\) for every \(Q\in\mathcal Q_a\). The common denominator in that proof is \([V_K]\); no survival of any individual \(P_a\) is required. The zero quadratic tuple has zero error rank and causes no exception.

Lift each \(q_{a,b}\) by (1) to a row \(q^*_{a,b}=\sum_S d_{a,b,S}G_{a,S}\), with \(\deg d_{a,b,S}\le1\). Each comparison \(q^*_{a,b}+q_{a,b}\) has a Boolean certificate through \(r+1\). Therefore \(P_a^*=\prod_{b\le t}(1+q^*_{a,b})\) agrees with \(P_a\) by a telescoping Boolean certificate through \(t(r+1)\); the other factors have degree at most \(r+1\). These are legal actual parent coefficient rows, not new quadratic source axioms.

Last row. The full thm:hamming-ball-unit-rows gives, for each basis, explicit \(c_{a,S}\) of degree at most \(u-1\) and \[R_a=1+\sum_S c_{a,S}m_{a,S}\in\mathcal I_{u+r-1}(\Gamma).\] Use \(c_{a,S}\) in parent row \(t+1\), giving \(L_a=\sum_Sc_{a,S}G_{a,S}\), and zero-pad thereafter. Then \[1+L_a=R_a+\sum_Sc_{a,S}\ell_{a,S}.\tag{6}\] For every parent input \(G_{a,U}\), multiply (6) by \(WP_aG_{a,U}\). Its \(R_a\) term belongs to \(J_D\) through \(K+2t+u+2r-1\). Each remaining term is \(c_{a,S}WP_a\ell_{a,S}G_{a,U}\); (3)–(4), the quadratic-stage memberships and bounded absorption put it in \(J_D\) through \(K+2t+u+r+1\). Here an already-derived \(WP_aQ\) has actual degree at most \(K+2t+2\), and its extra cofactor has degree at most \(u-1+r\); no multiplication outside the paid pseudoideal is used. Boolean summands have the same bound. For \(r\ge2\) this is at most the preceding \(R_a\) bound.

Replace \(P_a\) by the actual \(P_a^*\). Multiplying its comparison certificate by \(W(1+L_a)G_{a,U}\) costs at most \[K+t(r+1)+u+2r-1.\tag{7}\] Thus every actual weighted parent companion is in \(J_D\). All fresh fields map to Boolean-zero polynomials, with weighted certificates through \(K+2T\). Weighted bottom companions cost at most \(K+r+1\); weighted old generators cost at most \(K+u\).

Original ledger and replay. For \(r\le h\), \(t\le h-1\), the nonweight part of (7) is at most \(h^2+2h+u-2\). The nonweight part of the other main ceiling is at most \(4h+u-3\), which is no larger since \((h-1)^2\ge0\). For \(u\ge2\), \[(u-1)(2h^2+3h)\ge h^2+2h+u-2,\] as seen first at \(u=2\), with margin \(h^2+h\), then by increasing \(u\). These bounds and every field/bottom/old obligation fit \(K+TB\). The original bottom and parent companion ceilings remain \(2h+1\) and \(2h^2+3h\); none is recomputed after cancellation.

The global assignment fixes old variables and maps each fresh variable to degree at most \(T\ge1\). Replay an alleged degree-\(B\) PC proof as \(W\Phi(f)\in J_D\). Initial images have just been established; linear combinations commute. For a nonzero predecessor in a multiplication step, its original degree is at most \(B-1\). Expanding the substituted variable into old monomials keeps each intermediate weighted degree at most \(K+TB\). Bounded absorption therefore replays the step. The last line would give \(W\in J_D\), a contradiction. Ordinary NS has its same-degree PC replay. This proves the theorem.

For completeness, \(N_v(K-2)\ge\binom v{K-2}\) and \(N_v(K)\le\binom vK/(1-K/(v-K+1))\); the ratio is bounded below since \(K/v\to\epsilon\).
