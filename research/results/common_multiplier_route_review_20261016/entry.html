Joint-savings review: the coordinate count matches the kernel ceiling to first order

16 October 2026 · Review with a working proof (coordinate blocks only) · Joint-savings route review.

Question. Six research entries since the last review worked on effective-selection, the last four on joint-error-savings with goal progress unchanged. This review asks what the line has established about joint savings, what joint-error-savings still needs, whether the line's evidence can be falsified, and which test comes next.

General statement. The line's general claim is that joint error actions can be bounded below the sum of their ranks by structure of the errors, in the protected quotient, with a gain in a sufficient multiplier budget or admissible instance range. Established: thm:overlapping-curve-recovery (support counting in an evaluation model), lem:exact-common-kernel-selection (an exact selection criterion), lem:restriction-rank-overlap, thm:shared-span-affine-exclusion and thm:increment-count-exclusion (containment and increment savings in Braun's kernel step), and prop:common-kernel-ceiling (a ceiling on any certificate of that step at a fixed degree). This review adds prop:coordinate-count-window: for coordinate blocks the exact summed count reaches the ceiling's main term up to a factor \(1+O((r+k)/v)\).

Obstacle

Every saving found so far is either support counting in an evaluation model or domination: blocks whose restrictions factor through one coarser restriction, or a union of a few. The line has not produced a saving on a structured family that is neither, and its worst-case results do not decide whether one exists. For coordinate blocks the common kernel exists exactly when a row-linear monomial meets every pinned set; the exact summed count is a union bound for that event, and the coordinate count and the ceiling sandwich the threshold between \(-k\ln(1-r/v)\) and \(rk/(v-r-k)+\ln(k\ln(v+1)+1)\). At \(\ell=16\), \(D=107\) the window is about \(8.4\) in \(\ln M\), nearly all logarithmic; at the minimal degree \(D=6\ell+1\) the main-term part grows, to about \(341\) at \(\ell=32\), against a logarithmic part of about \(19.5\). So there is room for a saving below the summed ranks on unrestricted coordinate families: a correlation certificate could enter the logarithmic part, while the main-term part would need a certificate selecting exactly \(k\) rows; nothing in this notebook tests either. Pang's adaptive stage is untested too: lem:joint-word-shortening leaves its word-space dimension \(\dim Z_h\) open with only \(\dim Z_h\le\sum_ad_a\).

Coordinate count window

Statement. In Braun's setting (\(\mathbb F_2\), \(m\) rows of \(\ell\) bits, \(v=m\ell\), row-linear \(\mathcal L_k\) of dimension \(H\)), let proper coordinate blocks pin sets \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\) of bits to constants, each set of size at least \(r\), with \(x=1-r/v\), \(\rho=(m-k+1)\ell/k\) and \(\rho x>1\). Then each restriction rank is at most \(H\,x^k\,\rho x/(\rho x-1)\), so if

\[M\,x^k\,\frac{\rho x}{\rho x-1}<1,\quad\text{that is}\quad\ln M<-k\ln x-\ln\frac{\rho x}{\rho x-1},\tag{1}\]

the summed exact ranks are below \(H\) and a nonzero \(f\in\mathcal L_k\) has zero restriction to every flat. Here \(rk/v\le-k\ln x\le rk/(v-r)\). By prop:common-kernel-ceiling, at the same \(k\) and \(r\) some family with \(\ln M\le rk/(v-r-k)+\ln(k\ln(v+1)+1)\) has no such \(f\). The main terms \(-k\ln x\) and \(rk/(v-r-k)\) agree to a factor \(1+O((r+k)/v)\); their difference is about \((rk/v)(r/(2v)+k/v)\).

Proof. A pin \(x_i=1\) becomes a pin to zero under the translation \(x_i\mapsto x_i+1\), which preserves row-linearity and degree, so assume all pins are to zero. As in the ceiling proof, the restriction to \(x_P=0\) then has rank equal to the number of row-linear monomials of degree at most \(k\) avoiding \(P\). With \(\ell_i'=\ell-|P\cap\text{row }i|\), the number of degree \(j\) is \(e_j(\ell_1',\ldots,\ell_m')\). Maclaurin's inequality for nonnegative reals, \((e_j/\binom mj)^{1/j}\le e_1/m\), gives \(e_j\le\binom mj\bar\ell^{\,j}\) with \(\bar\ell=\ell-|P|/m\le\ell x\). Put \(a_j=\binom mj\ell^j\); then \(a_{j+1}/a_j=(m-j)\ell/(j+1)\ge\rho\) for \(j<k\), so \(a_j\le a_k\rho^{-(k-j)}\), and the rank is at most \(\sum_{j\le k}a_jx^j\le a_kx^k\sum_{i\ge0}(\rho x)^{-i}=a_kx^k\rho x/(\rho x-1)\le Hx^k\rho x/(\rho x-1)\). Summing over the blocks and using rank–nullity on the stacked map gives (1). Finally \(y\le-\ln(1-y)\le y/(1-y)\) at \(y=r/v\), and \(rk/(v-r)\le rk/(v-r-k)\), so the certified range lies below the ceiling at every admissible parameter.

Numbers. The saved check coordinate_window.json gives, at \(\ell=16\), \(D=107\), \(k=K_D=303\), \(r=r_D=14593\): \(\rho x\approx3397\), certified \(\ln M<4.2461\), ceiling family at \(12.62\) with main term \(4.2775\), published reach \(1.40\). The sweep coordinate_window_sweep.json at the minimal degree \(D=6\ell+1\) gives main-term gaps \(0.04\), \(0.34\), \(3.16\), \(31.8\), \(341\) at \(\ell=16,20,24,28,32\), against logarithmic terms \(8.4\), \(11.2\), \(14.0\), \(16.7\), \(19.5\). For coordinate blocks the published count's loss against the ceiling is in its single-flat estimate; for Braun's general affine flats this is not shown.

Nonvacuity. The window ledger is realized at \(\ell=16\), \(D=107\) by any \(M\le69\) coordinate blocks of rank \(r_D\), for example pinning disjoint or overlapping sets of \(14593\) bits; \(e^{4.246}\approx69.8\), and the hypotheses \(\rho x>1\) and \(r+k<v\) are checked in the script.

Degenerate cases. \(M=0\) needs nothing. If \(r\ge v\) no proper coordinate block of that size exists. If \(\rho x\le1\) the geometric bound is void and the statement says nothing. Duplicated blocks count twice in (1), as in any summed count.

Architecture review

Necessary target. For joint-error-savings: some independently checkable structural hypothesis on actual errors, an all-parameter bound on their joint action strictly below the summed ranks on a nontrivial unbounded family, in one protected quotient, with a gain in a sufficient multiplier budget or admissible instance range after all composition costs; duplication, coordinate changes and re-presentations of one kernel excluded. Downstream. Through lem:exact-common-kernel-selection, any certificate of the exact stacked criterion feeds thm:certified-repair-composition at unchanged later cost; through thm:shared-span-affine-exclusion it already enlarges Braun's admissible range for families clustered by shared spans. That consequence is an exclusion for a structured class of affine families; whether it meets the statement's wording, or only a domination case the reviewers have twice judged too close to re-presenting one kernel, is the open question of this line.

Extra requirements. The line has demanded a saving that is not domination. That demand is not in the open statement's words; it came from the reviewers' reading. The worst-case results do not show it unattainable on structured families, and the window above shows room even on unrestricted coordinate families. Phrased by containment it could never be met on coordinate families, because every common kernel there is spanned by monomials meeting every pinned set and so yields a cover afterwards. The demand is therefore stated in terms of the certificate: its hypothesis is checkable without being given a cover, and it does not follow from the cover bound \(\sum_cr(K_c)<H\) of lem:restriction-rank-overlap for any cover the hypothesis supplies.

Independent alternative. Before seeing the route, the medium reviewer received the goal, the open statements and the registered result statements only (review record). It proposed probabilistic existence certificates for the coordinate kernel, joint savings in Pang's adaptive stage, and several glued multipliers, ranking Pang's stage first. Compared with the route, which tested only Braun's kernel step: the symmetric local lemma is falsified below for that step; Pang's stage remains untested, since \(\dim Z_h\) is open and the innovation codimension of lem:conditional-seed-contraction measures per-round contraction, not the word space; gluing pays a degree shift linear in the number of clusters. In the second stage the same reviewer proposed a correlation (Harris) certificate on coordinate families, adopted as the decisive test below.

Rank normalization. All ranks in this line are measured in one fixed space: ordinary restriction images of \(\mathcal L_k\), or the evaluation quotient of the curve model. A correlation certificate is a relative statement: it bounds the probability that a random monomial meets every pinned set, not a sum of absolute ranks; it must be compared with the summed count in the same space, \(\mathcal L_k\) at one \(k\).

Decision. Keep joint-error-savings open, with the non-domination requirement stated in terms of the certificate, and keep the route on it. The next entry is lemma-first: prove the correlation (Harris) certificate for coordinate kernels, whose missing steps the reviewer supplied, and evaluate it across the sweep. It targets the logarithmic part of the window; the growing main-term part would need a certificate selecting exactly \(k\) rows. Pang's word-space dimension and the affine-flat extension of the coordinate count stay queued.

Decisive test. Trivial cases. Duplicated pinned sets change neither the event “a monomial meets every set” nor a certificate's product over distinct events; a translation of pins does not change the event; one pinned set needs no certificate. Power. The summed count certifies \(\ln M<-k\ln x\) by (1); the ceiling forbids beyond \(rk/(v-r-k)+\ln(k\ln(v+1)+1)\); at \(\ell=16\), \(D=107\) these are \(4.25\) and \(12.62\). In the Bernoulli row-selection model the chance of avoiding a pinned set is about \(e^{-\mu r/v}\), so the certificate's main term is at most \(kr/v\), below the count's \(-k\ln x\) by about \((kr/v)(r/2v)\), and it gains only an additive term of about \(\ln k\); by the sweep's figures that loss exceeds the gain at minimal degree from \(\ell=28\) on. Attempt. Fix one bit \(b_i\) per row and select each row independently with probability \(\pi=\mu/m\); the selected bits form a row-linear monomial. Given \(b\), each event “the monomial meets \(P_a\)” is increasing in the selection indicators, so Harris's inequality gives \(\Pr[\text{all meet}\mid b]\ge\prod_a(1-(1-\pi)^{c_a(b)})\), with \(c_a(b)\) the number of rows whose chosen bit lies in \(P_a\). Writing \(\varphi(c)=-\ln(1-(1-\pi)^c)=\sum_{j\ge1}(1-\pi)^{cj}/j\), the generating function of the independent Bernoulli\((s_i)\) indicators gives \(E_b(1-\pi)^{c_aj}\le\exp(-(1-(1-\pi)^j)|P_a|/\ell)\); a Chernoff lower tail makes every \(c_a\ge r/(2\ell)\) except with probability at most \(Me^{-r/(8\ell)}\); and \(\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(m,\pi)>k]\le e^{-\mu h(k/\mu)}\), \(h(t)=t\ln t-t+1\). The remaining step is the sum over large \(j\) on the good event, which this review does not write. Test disposition. New operation. Operation. Prove the certificate: some \(b\) gives \(\sum_a\varphi(c_a(b))\le(1+o(1))M(-\ln(1-e^{-\mu r/v}))\), so a common kernel exists when this is below the selection mass beyond the degree tail; evaluate it with the optimal \(\mu\) at \(\ell=16\), \(D=107\) and across the minimal-degree sweep. Prediction. It beats the summed count where \((kr/v)(r/2v)<\ln k\) roughly, as at \(\ell=16\) with the reviewer's estimate \(\ln M<6.1\) against \(4.25\), and falls below it at \(\ell\ge28\) at minimal degree. A win would be a joint saving whose hypothesis needs no cover. If the large-\(j\) sum loses a factor of order \(k\), the certificate collapses to the union bound. Record search. A search for Harris and FKG correlation inequalities with random monomials, increasing events and kernels returned, in the near-half-pc-degree notebook, prop:same-sign-saddle-bound (biased product measures of sets containing no member of a family) and lem:avoiding-sets-fraction (fractions of \(j\)-sets avoiding a family), which bear on the same event; the next entry reads both before proving anything.

A note for effective-annihilator-degree

A first draft of this review posed a Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity test for the third open statement. The reviewer answered its stopping step from the cited statements, and the author checked the answer: \(k[x]/(x_i^2-x_i)\cong k^{2^n}\), so every ideal quotient of the Boolean ring is a product of copies of \(k\), and a defect that is a genuine ideal there is a reduced set of cube points, with no nilpotents and filtration degree equal to multilinear degree. Nilpotent behaviour arises only modulo a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, which defines no scheme; and an unsatisfiable source system generates the unit ideal, so its scheme is empty. A regularity bound therefore needs a pseudoideal analogue, such as the Hilbert function of the truncation, or a non-Boolean encoding. This is recorded for that statement's development and is not a test of this route.

Outside leads

Non-coordinate flats, where the restriction image has no monomial basis; the queued development is the affine-flat extension.
  • The elementary greedy transversal bound. It targets joint-error-savings on coordinate families. Answers. Whether a covering argument certifies the kernel beyond the summed count. Test. Falsified as an improvement: if every pinned set has at least \(r\) bits and at most one bit per row is chosen, after \(t\le k\) choices each remaining set keeps at least \(r-\ell t\) bits outside used rows, so some bit meets a fraction at least \((r-\ell k)/v\ge2k/m\) of the remaining sets when \(r\ge3\ell k\); \(k\) choices meet all sets once \(M(1-2k/m)^k<1\), that is about \(\ln M<2k^2/m\), below the summed count's \(-k\ln x\approx3k^2/m\). The fractional-cover version (Johnson, Lovász) was not read or tested; the one-bit-per-row constraint makes it a cover under a partition matroid, where the classical factor does not transfer directly.
  • Lovász Local Lemma (Erdős–Lovász; algorithmic form Moser–Tardos), the reviewer's first alternative. It targets the coordinate kernel without the union bound. Answers. Existence of a transversal monomial when the avoidance events have sparse dependence. Test. Falsified as a main-term improvement, for the symmetric lemma with the natural dependency graph: events for pinned sets \(P_a,P_b\) are dependent when the sets meet. By Cauchy–Schwarz on the bit degrees, \(\sum_{a\ne b}|P_a\cap P_b|\ge(Mr)^2/v-Mr\), and \(|P_a\cap P_b|\le r\), so on average a set meets at least \(Mr/v-1\) others. The condition \(e\,p\,(\Delta+1)\le1\) then gains over the union bound \(Mp<1\) at most a factor about \(v/(er)\), which is \(26\) at \(r=r_D=14593\), \(v=1048592\): an additive \(\ln26\approx3.3\) in \(\ln M\). The lopsided form was not tested.
