Fermat family killer

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_p\), let \(C\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing \(x^2-x\) for every variable. (a) If \(\deg q\le d\), \(P=(1-q^{p-1})R\) and \(\deg h+\deg P+(p-1)d\le D\), then \(q^{p-1}Ph\in C\). (b) For a Pang family with \(t\ge1\) choices \(q_1,\ldots,q_t\) of degree at most \(d\), \(P=\prod_j(1-q_j^{p-1})\), \(\Delta=(p-1)dt\), and errors \(E_i=Pg_i\) with \(\deg g_i\le d\), every \(q_j^{p-1}\) kills every \(E_i\) when \((p-1)d+\Delta+d\le D\). (c) For \(S\) families and any choice \(j_\nu\) in each, the product \(k=\prod_\nu q_{\nu,j_\nu}^{p-1}\), of degree at most \(S(p-1)d\), kills every error of every family when \(S(p-1)d+\Delta+d\le D\); if \(C\) is the degree-\(D\) part of the vanishing ideal of the cube, \(k\notin C\) exactly when some cube point has every \(q_{\nu,j_\nu}\ne0\).

Proof. First, \(C\) contains every polynomial \(f\) of degree at most \(D\) that vanishes on the cube: replacing \(x^2\) by \(x\) in a monomial of \(f\) subtracts \((x^2-x)m\), a member of degree at most \(\deg f\), obtained from \(x^2-x\) by multiplying variable by variable within degree \(\deg f\le D\); the multilinear remainder vanishes on the cube and is therefore zero. (a) On a cube point \(q\) takes a value in \(\mathbb F_p\), so \(q^{p-1}\in\{0,1\}\) and \(q^{p-1}(1-q^{p-1})=0\) there; hence \(q^{p-1}Ph\) vanishes on the cube, with degree at most \(\deg h+\deg P+(p-1)d\le D\), and lies in \(C\). (b) is (a) with \(R=\prod_{l\ne j}(1-q_l^{p-1})\) and \(h=g_i\). (c) The product contains the factor \(q_{\nu,j_\nu}^{p-1}\) for each family, so (a) applies with that factor and the rest of the product in \(h\); in the vanishing-ideal model membership is vanishing on the cube, and \(k\) is the indicator of the points where every \(q_{\nu,j_\nu}\) is nonzero.

Against Pang's multiplier. The errors \(P_\nu g_{\nu,i}\) are Pang's own, so both constructions pay the same \(\Delta\); the product killer plays the role of Pang's multiplier, of degree \(K\). Its degree bound \(S(p-1)d\) is below \(K\) exactly when \(S<K/((p-1)d)\), and the actual degree can be smaller, for example when choices repeat across families; the lemma applies when \(S(p-1)d\le D-\Delta-d\), against Pang's \(K\le D-\Delta-d\), with \(1\le d\le K\le n\), \(S(1-\eta)^t<1\) and the separate simulation condition \(K+\max(2\Delta+2d,\Delta+w)\le D\) (cor:pang-observation-calibration). The comparison is one-sided: the product is not shown to survive in Pang's proper closure, only in the cube's vanishing ideal, while Pang's multiplier survives by counting. And Pang's multiplier is itself a common killer of degree \(K\), independent of \(S\), so no construction needs degree linear in \(S\) in general.

Degenerate cases. \(S=0\): \(k=1\). A choice \(q_{\nu,j_\nu}=0\): \(k=0\), which survives nowhere. \(t=0\) is excluded: there is no factor \(1-q^{p-1}\). \(S(p-1)d+\Delta+d>D\): (c) says nothing.
