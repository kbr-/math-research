Exact monomial-pair spaces

All spaces in this section are over \(\mathbb F_2\) modulo Booleanity only, a satisfiable control rather than a hard old system. Let disjoint variable sets be \(H_0,A_0,B_0,Y_0\), of sizes \(h,a,b,s\), where \(h,s\ge0\) and \(a,b\ge1\). Write \(H=\prod_{x\in H_0}x\), \(U=\prod_{x\in A_0}x\), \(V=\prod_{x\in B_0}x\), with \(H=1\) if \(h=0\). Put \(g=(HU,HV)\), \(d=h+\max(a,b)\), \(D=K+2d\), \(J=I_{\mathrm{Bool}}\cap F[x]_{\le D}\), \(Q=F[x]_{\le D}/J\), and \[ W_L=\{([g_1f],[g_2f]):\deg f\le L\},\qquad L=K+d. \] For a row \(C\), let \(\mathcal E_C(K)\) be the actual image with prefix \(P_C=1+Cg\) and multiplier degree at most \(K\); let \(\mathcal I_C(L)=W_L\cap(\ker C\otimes Q)\). Put \(N_s(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(s,k)}\binom sj\).

Theorem (monomial pair intersection). For \(C=(1,1)\), \(K\ge0\), \[ \mathcal E_C(K)=\{(HUV\,r,HUV\,r):r\in F[Y_0]_{\le K}\},\qquad \mathcal I_C(K+d)=\{(HUV\,r,HUV\,r):r\in F[Y_0]_{\le K+h}\}. \] In particular the ranks are \(N_s(K)\) and \(N_s(K+h)\). If the enlarged domain is instead \(K+\max(a,b)\), the same chosen intersection equals the actual image.

For comparison, in the disjoint equal-size case \(h=s=0,a=b=d\), the selecting row \(C=(1,0)\) has \[ \dim\mathcal E_C(K)=N_d(K)-\mathbf1_{\{K\ge d\}},\qquad \dim\mathcal I_C(K+d)=2^d-1. \] At \(K=0\) the actual rank is one for both selecting and parity rows, but the selecting intersection has rank \(2^d-1\) and the parity intersection rank one. Thus one must compare the same row before attributing a loss to its representation.

Proof for the parity row. Boolean reduction gives \(P_Cg_1=P_Cg_2=HUV\); hence the actual image is restriction of degree-\(K\) multipliers to all variables in \(H_0\cup A_0\cup B_0\) equal to one, proving its stated description.

An enlarged image belongs to the row kernel precisely when \(H(U+V)f=0\) as a Boolean function. Then \(HUf=HVf\), and this common function is supported where \(H=U=V=1\). Write it uniquely as \(HUV\,r(Y_0)\). Set all \(H_0\) and \(A_0\) variables to one in \(f\); the remaining polynomial \(f''\) in \(B_0,Y_0\) obeys \((1+V)f''=0\), so its multilinear representative is \(Vr(Y_0)\). If \(r\ne0\), its degree is \(b+\deg r\le L\). Reversing \(A_0,B_0\) similarly gives \(a+\deg r\le L\). Thus \(\deg r\le L-\max(a,b)\).

Conversely every such \(r\) is realized by \(f=(1+U+V)r\), of degree at most \(L\): \(H(U+V)f=0\), and both input products equal \(HUVr\). This proves both the usual and reduced-enlargement assertions. Restrictions and multilinear reduction do not raise degrees; all products involved are through \(D\). In particular the largest displayed image degree is \(h+a+b+K+h\le K+2d\).

Proof for the selecting row. Its actual nonzero coordinate is \(V(1+U)f\). Restriction to \(B_0=1\) gives degree-\(K\) functions on \(A_0\), restricted away from the all-one point. The only possible restriction kernel is the point indicator \(U\), whose degree is \(d\). This gives \(N_d(K)-\mathbf1_{\{K\ge d\}}\). The enlarged condition is \(Uf=0\); the other coordinate on \(B_0=1\) can be any function on \(A_0\) vanishing at its all-one point, because \(L\ge d\). Every such function is already realized by an \(f\) using \(A_0\) alone. This space has dimension \(2^d-1\). \(\square\)

The actual disjoint-block ranks agree with the already proved monomial-code formula; no new claim about those actual ranks alone is intended. The new comparison concerns the enlarged intersections. For \(h>0,K=0,s>0\), their parity-row gap is genuine: \(N_s(h)>1\). Neither \(HU\) nor \(HV\) by itself annihilates all errors with one selecting prefix, since \(A_0,B_0\) are nonempty. This differs from the shared-factor calibration, which included the common factor itself as an input.

Normalize the error map using its factor

Lemma (factored prefix image). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(J\subseteq F[x]_{\le D}\), \(D\ge2\), be a degree-\(D\) pseudo-ideal containing Booleanity. Let \(w,H,h_i\) be polynomials of degrees at most \(b,a,\delta\), and suppose the actual inputs are exactly \(g_i=Hh_i\). Let \(C\) be any binary \(t\)-row matrix, \(t\ge1\), \(q_\ell=\sum_iC_{\ell i}h_i\), and \[ P_g=\prod_{\ell=1}^t(1+Hq_\ell),\qquad P_h=\prod_{\ell=1}^t(1+q_\ell). \] For \(K\ge0\) and \(b+K+(t+1)(a+\delta)\le D\), every \(f\) of degree at most \(K\) satisfies \[ [wP_g g_i f]=[wH P_h h_i f]\quad\text{in }Q=F[x]_{\le D}/J. \] Consequently the actual error image is contained in \[ \widetilde W\cap(\ker C\otimes Q),\qquad \widetilde W=\{([wHh_i u])_i:\deg u\le K+t\delta\}. \] For any complete flag of \(Q\), fixed before a uniform random \(C\), let \(\widetilde r_j\) be the induced fiber dimensions of \(\widetilde W\). Then \[ \mathbb E_C\dim\mathcal E_C(K)\le\sum_j\kappa(t,\widetilde r_j) \le\widetilde\beta:=\sum_j\min\{\widetilde r_j,2^{-t}(2^{\widetilde r_j}-1)\}. \] Here \(\kappa(t,r)\) is the expected nullity of a uniform binary \(t\)-by-\(r\) matrix. For finitely many tuples with common \(w,J,K\) and their stated budgets, \(\sum_\nu\widetilde\beta_\nu<h_w(K):=\operatorname{rank}(f\mapsto[wf],\deg f\le K)\) gives chosen matrices and a common \(f\) with \([wf]\ne0\) and all original weighted errors zero. No survival of \(wH\) is required for the error-rank bound. If using the common-kernel criterion across tuples, its denominator remains \(h_w(K)\), and \(w\) must survive.

Proof. Expand both products by subsets. The difference between \(HP_g\) and \(HP_h\) has, for each nonempty subset \(S\), term \[ (H^{|S|+1}+H)\prod_{\ell\in S}q_\ell. \] For \(|S|\ge1\), \(H^{|S|+1}+H\) is a multiple of \(H^2+H\), by telescoping consecutive powers. Since \(H\) has binary coefficients, \(H^2+H\) has a Boolean-axiom certificate through \(2a\), by squaring monomials and reducing their squares. If \(H\) is constant the difference is identically zero. After multiplying by \(wh_if\), every summand has a certificate through \[ b+K+(|S|+1)(a+\delta)\le D. \] This proves the identity in \(J\). The reduced product \(P_hf\) has degree at most \(K+t\delta\), giving containment in \(\widetilde W\). Multiplying its coordinate tuple by a row of \(C\) yields \(wHf q_\ell P_h\), which vanishes by Booleanity through \(b+a+K+(t+1)\delta\le D\). Filter the intersection by the complete flag of \(Q\). Each successive quotient injects into the intersection of the corresponding fiber with \(\ker C\); summing gives its dimension at most the sum of these fiber intersection dimensions. On a fixed \(r\)-dimensional fiber, restriction of uniform \(C\) is uniform, and expected nonzero kernel-vector count is \(2^{-t}(2^r-1)\), bounding expected kernel dimension along with \(r\). This proves the displayed estimate. Choose matrices whose summed actual ranks are at most the summed expectations. Every original error map kills \(\ker(f\mapsto[wf])\) by pseudo-ideal closure within its original budget. On that quotient of dimension \(h_w(K)\), the stacked map has rank at most the sum of individual ranks. A strict smaller-rank inequality leaves a nonzero kernel class, which is the desired \(f\). \(\square\)

The restriction \(t\ge1\) focuses on nonempty prefixes; the identity and containment also hold for empty products. This is a costed error-map restatement of the common-idempotent argument already used in shared-bit normalization, not a new algebraic identity or a replacement of that all-prime complete-source theorem. Here \(H\) may have growing degree and residual inputs need not be affine, but the conclusion is only an error-image identity and bound. The coefficients still act on the original \(g_i\): no illegal access to \(h_i\) has been introduced. The smaller representative degree does not erase the original certificate cost \(b+K+(t+1)(a+\delta)\). It does not prove a smaller complete source-simulation degree.

For the monomial pair, take \(a=h\) and residual degree \(\delta=\max(|A_0|,|B_0|)\). The normalized domain is \(K+\delta\), and the theorem shows its parity-row intersection is exactly the actual image. Thus a concrete enlargement loss is repaired without requiring \(H\) to be an allowed input or a surviving weight.
