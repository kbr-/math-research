Feature row shortening

All degree cutoffs and bounds are nonnegative integers, and \(s\ge1,m\ge0,t\ge0\) are integer counts. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(J\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing old Booleanity, and \(Q=\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}/J\). Fix \(\deg w\le b\), inputs \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), and old binary features \(\phi_0=1,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m\) of degree at most \(r\ge0\). No independence of the features, inputs or their products is assumed. Put \(d=\delta+r\). For a finite-dimensional subspace \(U\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le L}\), define \[ T_gf=([wg_if])_i,\qquad T_*f=([wg_i\phi_j f])_{i,j}. \] Choose \(t\ge0\) independent uniform binary arrays \(c_{u,i,j}\), and set \[ a_{u,i}(x)=\sum_jc_{u,i,j}\phi_j(x),\quad q_u=\sum_i a_{u,i}g_i,\quad U_C=\{f\in U:[wq_uf]=0\text{ for every }u\}. \] These are old polynomial coefficient images, not independent new input variables.

Corollary (feature row shortening). If \(b+L+2d\le D\), then \[ \ker T_g=\ker T_*,\qquad \mathbb E_C\dim T_g(U_C)\le2^{-t}\dim T_g(U). \] For the prefix application specialize to \(U=V_L\). For \(t\ge1\), \(K\ge0\), \(L=K+td\), and \(P_C=\prod_{u=1}^t(1-q_u)\), let \[ E_C(K)=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\in V_K\mapsto([wg_iP_Cf])_i\bigr),\quad m_w(L)=\operatorname{rank}(T_g|_{V_L}). \] In the same window, \[ \mathbb E_C E_C(K)\le2^{-t}m_w(K+t(\delta+r)). \] The source coefficient images have degree at most \(r\) and satisfy their binary fields with the paid Boolean certificates. For many tuples, summed expected actual ranks below \(h_w(K)=\operatorname{rank}(f\mapsto[wf],f\in V_K)\) give one common surviving weighted multiplier, provided \(w\notin J\). This last step does not assume a separately large feature image.

Proof. The component \(\phi_0=1\) gives \(\ker T_*\subseteq\ker T_g\). If \(wg_if\in J\), multiplication by \(\phi_j\) stays within \(b+L+\delta+r\le D\), giving the reverse inclusion. Thus projection from the expanded image to its \(j=0\) coordinates is an isomorphism, also after restricting the domain to any subspace of \(U\).

Every \(\psi_{i,j}=g_i\phi_j\) is a binary polynomial of degree at most \(d\); \(\psi_{i,j}^2-\psi_{i,j}\) has a Boolean certificate through \(2d\). Apply input row shortening to this expanded tuple and the arbitrary domain \(U\), with prefactor \(w\). Its scalar row equations are precisely \([wq_uf]=0\). It bounds the expected expanded image of \(U_C\) by \(2^{-t}\dim T_*(U)\). The image isomorphisms give the asserted estimate for the original tuple. Redundant feature coordinates cause no problem: the theorem requires neither input independence nor independent multiplication coordinates.

For the prefix use \(U=V_L\), so \(P_Cf\in U\) when \(f\in V_K\). Also \[ wq_uP_Cf=wf(q_u-q_u^2)\prod_{v\ne u}(1-q_v)\in J, \] with Boolean certificate through \(b+K+(t+1)d\), below the assumed \(b+K+(t+2)d\le D\). Thus the actual error image lies in \(T_g(U_C)\), and the expectation follows. Each \(a_{u,i}\) is binary on the old cube, with field certificate through \(2r\), or identically zero when \(r=0\). These certificates also fit after weighting in the displayed window.

For many tuples choose their arrays so the sum of actual ranks is at most the sum of their expectations. Each actual error map kills the degree-\(K\) weight-map kernel, by bounded absorption through \(b+K+td+\delta\le D\). Therefore they descend to a common space of dimension \(h_w(K)\). A strict smaller sum of ranks leaves a nonzero class in their joint kernel. This is exactly the recorded common-kernel argument; no new rank-retaining multiplier operation is invoked. \(\square\)

A positive control already explained by factor packing

On three Boolean bits take \(g_1=xy\), \(g_2=yz\). The affine row \(a_1=1\), \(a_2=1-x\) gives \[ q=xy+(1-x)yz,\qquad g_1(1-q)=g_2(1-q)=0\pmod{I_{\rm Bool}}. \] Each identity follows by Boolean reduction; the row detects whether either input is one. No constant row has both companions zero: the tuple takes values \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\), and the first two would force both constant coefficients to be one, which fails at the third value. This is a satisfiable Boolean control, not a hard-quotient lower bound.

The improvement is a rediscovery of the two-factor affine pairing on residual inputs \(x,z\), together with the factored-prefix identity for common idempotent \(H=y\). More generally that identity removes a known common factor from the error representation while retaining its original certificate charge. It does not cover affine mixtures of unrelated products. Thus the control validates the extra expressive power of chosen coefficients but supplies no new architecture or source coverage.
