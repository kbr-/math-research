Partial graph exclusion

Theorem. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) on \(m\) pairs \((x_e,y_e)\), with local axioms \(x_e^2+x_e,y_e^2+y_e,x_ey_e\). Let a proper degree-\(D\) PC-closed old space \(J_D\) contain these and the old axioms. Let \(w\notin J_D\), \(\deg w\le b\), and let \(U\) consist of local normal polynomials through degree \(k\ge1\), with \(H=\dim[wU]>0\). There are \(M\ge1\) complete terminal ENS blocks, with private coefficients, positive original accuracies and old-only inputs. Block \(a\) has disjoint core and exposed edge sets \(S_a,E_a\), of sizes \(s_a,t_a\), and its tuple contains \[ x_e+F_{a,e}(x_{S_a},y_{S_a})\qquad(e\in E_a). \] The offsets are arbitrary binary polynomials; swaps of the two indicators per exposed pair and arbitrary extra old inputs are allowed. The remaining \(r_a=m-s_a-t_a\) pairs are omitted from this requirement, not deleted from the old domain. Put \(s=\max_a s_a\), \(T=k+s\), and \[ L_a(k)=3^{s_a}\sum_{i+j\le k}2^i\binom{r_a}{i}\binom{t_a}{j}. \] Let \(B\ge2\) cover all original source and old generators and the hypothetical proof, retaining every companion ceiling \(e_{a,i}=h_a(\delta_a+1)+d_{a,i}\). If \[ \sum_aL_a(k)<H,\qquad b+k+TB\le D, \] there are old coefficient maps of degree at most \(T\) and a common \(Q\in U\) with \(wQ\notin J_D\), killing all weighted mapped fresh axioms. Hence no degree-\(B\) PC or NS source refutation exists. The maps need not have polynomial size.

Graph rank. At a fixed one of the \(3^{s_a}\) local core states, each exposed indicator is a fixed bit, leaving at most one free partner bit. An omitted pair still has its full degree polynomial \(1+2z\). The conditional restriction image of \(U\) is therefore at most the cumulative coefficient count of \((1+2z)^{r_a}(1+z)^{t_a}\). Multiplying by the number of core states gives \(L_a(k)\). Choose a complement \(U_0\) of the kernel of \(f\mapsto[wf]\) in \(U\). The joint graph restriction has rank below \(\dim U_0=H\), so a nonzero kernel element \(Q\) survives after multiplication by \(w\).

Complete maps. At core state \(\sigma\), specialize \(Q\) to \(Q_\sigma\), put \(c_e=F_{a,e}(\sigma)\), and project only exposed pairs by \[ x_e\mapsto c_e,\qquad y_e\mapsto(1+c_e)y_e. \] Leave every omitted pair unchanged. This preserves the local domain and maps onto the zero graph. Telescoping normal monomials, using \(y_e=(x_e+1)y_e+x_ey_e\) when \(c_e=1\), gives \[ Q_\sigma=\sum_{e\in E_a}\gamma_{\sigma,e}(x_e+c_e)+R_\sigma,\qquad \deg\gamma_{\sigma,e}\le k, \] where \(R_\sigma\) is local-zero through degree \(k+1\). Unchanged factors cause no additional term. The fully projected polynomial is local-zero because \(Q\) vanishes on the graph; degree-nonincreasing local reduction supplies its certificate.

Interpolate with the core's one-hot cell selectors: \(\beta_{a,e}=\sum_\sigma\chi_\sigma\gamma_{\sigma,e}\), with \(\deg\chi_\sigma\le s_a\). Thus \(\deg\beta_{a,e}\le k+s_a\). Use these in the first coefficient row and zero every other row and extra-input position. On local points the first row value \(H_a=\sum_e\beta_{a,e}g_{a,e}\) equals \(Q\). Hence each weighted companion \(wQ\,g_{a,i}(1+H_a)\) is local-zero, including extra inputs; all mapped fields are Boolean-zero. A substituted original companion has weighted degree at most \(b+k+Te_{a,i}\), and fields at most \(b+k+2T\). Local reduction certifies them at those ceilings. Weighted old generators and every original PC multiplication fit \(b+k+TB\), including reuse after cancellation. A refutation would put \(wQ\) in \(J_D\). This also excludes NS through its PC replay. When \(r_a=0\), this is exactly the preceding finite theorem. \(\square\)

Sublinear exposure exclusion

A uniform mixed-count estimate. Suppose every \(t_a\ge q\ge1\), and put \(\mu=2-q/m\in[1,2)\). The conditional degree-\(j\) count is the elementary symmetric polynomial in \(m\) nonnegative weights: \(s_a\) zeros, \(r_a\) twos and \(t_a\) ones. Their average is \(2-(t_a+2s_a)/m\le\mu\), so it is at most \(\binom mj\mu^j\). To verify this inequality, write the elementary symmetric polynomial as \(A+(u+v)B+uvC\), \(C\ge0\), for two weights. Replacing them by their average cannot decrease it. Repeated averaging converges to equal weights; continuity gives the bound. This is an integer/real count, not evaluation of \(2\) in \(\mathbb F_2\).

For \(k\le m/3\), the terms \(\mu^j\binom mj\) decrease backwards with ratios at most \(k/(\mu(m-k+1))\le1/2\). Therefore \[ \sum_aL_a(k)\le2M3^s\mu^k\binom mk. \]

Corollary. Use the same fixed bounded-degree binary charged graph family, with expansion \(\varepsilon>2\), nonzero total ternary charge and proper \(D=\Theta(m)\) in its recorded window. Fix \[ 0<c_0<1,\quad 0<\beta\le1,\quad 0\le\alpha<\beta-2/\varepsilon, \] and constants \(A,c\). The partial-graph sources with \(M\le m^A\), \(s_a\le m^\alpha\), \(t_a\ge c_0m^\beta\), and original ceiling \(B\le(\log m)^c\) have no degree-\(B\) PC or NS refutations for all sufficiently large \(m\). The graph family is independent of \(A,c\).

Proof. Set \(q=\min_a t_a\), \(s=\max_a s_a\), take \(w=1\) and the full old local-normal degree-\(k\) domain, and choose \[ k=\left\lceil\frac{2m}{q}\ln(8M3^s)\right\rceil,\qquad T=k+s. \] Then \(k=O(m^{1-\beta+\alpha}\log m)\); both \(k\) and \(T\) have exponent below one, so \(k+TB=o(m)<D\). The collision bound and binary descent give \[ H=h(k)\ge \frac{B_m(k)}{1+2\eta},\qquad \eta=\left(1+2(2\sqrt{k/m})^\varepsilon\right)^n-1=o(1), \] since \(n=\Theta(m)\) and \(1-(\beta-\alpha)\varepsilon/2<0\). Thus \(H\ge2^{k-1}\binom mk\) eventually. The preceding numerator bound gives \[ \frac{\sum_aL_a(k)}{H} \le4M3^s\left(1-\frac q{2m}\right)^k \le4M3^s e^{-qk/(2m)} \le\frac12. \] The finite theorem applies. At \(\beta=1,c_0=1/2\), full outside exposure is at least \(m/2\) for all large \(m\), recovering the previous small-core range. The new case \(\beta<1\) needs only sublinear exposure. \(\square\)

Concrete hard-family range. Take \(\varepsilon=8,\beta=1/2,\alpha=1/8,c_0=1/2\). Then \(k=O(m^{5/8}\log m)\), the collision factor tends to zero and every fixed polylogarithmic \(B\) fits \(k+TB=o(m)\). The recorded fixed-degree spectral existence statement permits degree \(128\): \((128-(2\sqrt{127}+1))/2>8\), supplying the required edge-expansion margin. For example \(D=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) is eventually in \(\max(4,256)\le D<8n/8\), with \(m=64n\). This is an unbounded witness family, not a new graph-existence theorem.

Where the numerical method stops

Let \(A_t(k)=[z^{\le k}](1+2z)^{m-t}(1+z)^t\). Replacing the core factor \((1+2z)^{s_a}\) by its total coefficient sum \(3^{s_a}\) only increases the cumulative count, so \(L_a(k)\ge A_{t_a}(k)\). This is the single-pivot profile in affine collision budget. Using even its better of the two expansion-only denominator estimates, the resulting certified normalized bound is \(1-o(1)\), uniformly in allowed \(k\), when \(t_a=o(m^{2/\varepsilon})\). Our numerator is no smaller and the collision denominator used above is no better. Thus those displayed estimates cannot certify a summed deficit for two such low-exposure blocks.

This comparison retains the old \(k+t_a\le D\) window. In the present asymptotic setting, \(k\le D/B\le D/2\), \(D=\Theta(m)\), and \(t_a=o(m)\), so that window holds eventually. The conclusion concerns the numerical certificate only: actual graph ranks may be smaller, the exact old dimension may be larger, and other coefficient choices or source methods may work. In particular this does not prove that polylogarithmic exposure is intrinsically inadequate for source removal.

At zero exposure the graph is the whole local domain, so no surviving local polynomial can vanish on it. Yet if all extra inputs are zero, the block is removed trivially by zero coefficients. This distinguishes a failed graph-target demand from a proof-system obstruction.
