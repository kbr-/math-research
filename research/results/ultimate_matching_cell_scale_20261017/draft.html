General statement. thm:cell-scale-reduction: for exponents \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[2,\infty)\), a fixed \(\beta\ge\frac12\) with \((\beta-\frac12)(\max_k\alpha_k-2)<1\), \(L\ge260\) and dyadic cell sides \(\ell\le(\log N)^\beta/\sqrt N\), with probability tending to one the minimal matching cost is at most \(\frac N8\sum_k\int\psi_{\alpha_k}(8|\Phi_{c,k}|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))+0.03N\) for every bounded field \(\Phi_c\) carrying the cell-smeared \(X\) to \(N\) times Lebesgue measure; so the goal at such a pair follows from such a \(\Phi_c\) with that integral at most \(7\).

Setting. As in thm:dyadic-flow-square-exponential's proof (read this cycle): \(X_1,\dots,X_N\) i.i.d. uniform on \(\Omega=[0,1]^2\), \(Y\) evenly spread, \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\) (natural logarithms), \(\psi_\alpha(u)=e^{u^\alpha}-1\), \(C(z)=\sum_k\psi_{\alpha_k}(|z_k|/s)\), \(h=\frac14\); \(J\) the least integer with \(\delta=2^{-J}\le s/32\), so \(\delta\ge s/64\); \(\rho_j=2^{-j}\); \(m(S)\) the number of \(X_i\) in a dyadic square \(S\); \(E_1\): every dyadic square of level \(j\le J\) has \(N|S|/2\le m(S)\le2N|S|\), \(P(E_1)\to1\) for \(L\ge260\); \(\nu^{\rho}_X\): each \(X_i\) smeared uniformly over its dyadic square of side \(\rho\); \(\nu_Y=N\,\mathrm{Leb}\), each \(Y_j\) smeared over its rectangle (within \(20/\sqrt N\)); \(\Phi_j=\sum_S\Phi^S\) the dyadic square flows of that proof, \(\Phi^S\) built from the child excesses \(e_c=m(S_c)-m(S)/4\) of a level-\(j\) square \(S\).

Theorem (cell-scale reduction). Let \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[2,\infty)\), \(\beta\ge\frac12\) fixed (independent of \(N\)) with \((\beta-\frac12)(\max_k\alpha_k-2)<1\), \(L\ge260\), and for each \(N\) let \(j_c\le J\) with \(\ell=2^{-j_c}\le(\log N)^\beta/\sqrt N\). With probability tending to one: for every bounded field \(\Phi_c\) on \(\Omega\) with \(\int\nabla g\cdot\Phi_c=\int g\,d(\nu^{\ell}_X-\nu_Y)\) for every Lipschitz \(g\), \[\min_\pi\sum_{i\le N}\sum_k\psi_{\alpha_k}\Big(\frac{|X^k_i-Y^k_{\pi(i)}|}s\Big)\le\frac N8\sum_k\int_\Omega\psi_{\alpha_k}\Big(\frac{8|\Phi_{c,k}|}{L\sqrt{N\log N}}\Big)dx+0.03N.\] In particular, if with probability tending to one such a \(\Phi_c\) exists with \(\sum_k\int\psi_{\alpha_k}(8|\Phi_{c,k}|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))\le7\), the conclusion of conj:ultimate-matching holds at \(\alpha\) with the constant \(L\) (each coordinate's sum of \(\psi\) at most \(0.905N\), its sum of exponentials at most \(2N\)). At \((4,4)\) the condition is \(\beta<1\): cells of side \((\log N)^{3/4}/\sqrt N\), the Leighton–Shor side, and coarser ones up to \((\log N)^{1-\varepsilon}/\sqrt N\) are allowed.

Proof. The inner flow. Put \(m'=J-j_c\) and \(\Phi_{\rm in}=\sum_{j_c\le j<J}\Phi_j\). The telescoping identity of thm:dyadic-flow-square-exponential's proof, run over the levels \(j_c\le j<J\) only, gives \(\int\nabla g\cdot\Phi_{\rm in}=\int g\,d(\nu^{\delta}_X-\nu^{\ell}_X)\): each level moves the smearing of the points from their level-\(j\) squares to their level-\((j+1)\) squares. So \(\Phi=\Phi_{\rm in}+\Phi_c\) satisfies \(\int\nabla g\cdot\Phi=\int g\,d(\nu^\delta_X-\nu_Y)\). The flow bound. On \(E_1\) the density of \(\nu^\delta_X\) is at least \(N/2\); lem:flow-matching-bound (2) with \(\lambda=N/2\), \(\lambda'=N/4\), \(h=\frac14\), \(\delta_X=\delta\), \(\delta_Y=20/\sqrt N\) gives cost at most \(\frac N4\int C(4\Phi/N)+\frac N4\sum_k[\psi_{\alpha_k}(4\delta/s)+\psi_{\alpha_k}(80/(L\sqrt{\log N}))]\); with \(4\delta/s\le\frac18\) and \(\alpha_k\ge2\), \(\psi_{\alpha_k}(1/8)\le e^{1/64}-1<0.02\), and the \(Y\) term tends to \(0\), so the error is at most \(0.02N\) for large \(N\), as in that proof. Each \(z\mapsto\psi_{\alpha_k}(|z_k|/s)\) is convex, so \(C(4(\Phi_{\rm in}+\Phi_c)/N)\le\frac12C(8\Phi_{\rm in}/N)+\frac12C(8\Phi_c/N)\), and \(Ns=L\sqrt{N\log N}\): the cost is at most \(\frac N8(I_{\rm in}+I_c)+0.02N\), with \(I_\bullet=\sum_k\int\psi_{\alpha_k}(8|\Phi_{\bullet,k}|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))\). It remains to show \(I_{\rm in}\le0.08\) with probability tending to one. The tail. Fix \(x\) and a coordinate \(k\), and put \(U=8|\Phi_{{\rm in},k}(x)|/(L\sqrt{N\log N})\). The supermartingale step of that proof, started at level \(j_c\) instead of \(j_0\) (given \(\mathcal F_j\) each coordinate of \(\Phi_j(x)\) is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy \(4m(S_j(x))/\rho_j^2\le8N\) on \(E_1\), by Hoeffding's lemma), gives \(P(|\Phi_{{\rm in},k}(x)|\ge t,E_1)\le2e^{-t^2/(16Nm')}\), that is \(P(U\ge u,E_1)\le2e^{-Au^2}\) with \(A=L^2\log N/(1024m')\). The cap. The square-flow lemma of the flow-bound entry gives \(|\Phi^S|\le6\max_c|e_c|/\rho_j\), and on \(E_1\) \(|e_c|\le m(S)\le2N\rho_j^2\), so \(|\Phi_j(x)|\le12N\rho_j\) and \(|\Phi_{{\rm in},k}(x)|\le12N\sum_{j\ge j_c}\rho_j\le24N\ell\); hence on \(E_1\), \(U\le u_{\max}=192\sqrt N\ell/(L\sqrt{\log N})\). The integral. \(\mathbb E[\psi_\alpha(U)1_{E_1}]=\int_0^{u_{\max}}\alpha u^{\alpha-1}e^{u^\alpha}P(U>u,E_1)\,du\le\int_0^{u_{\max}}2\alpha u^{\alpha-1}e^{u^\alpha-Au^2}du\). If \(u_{\max}^{\alpha-2}\le A/2\), then \(u^\alpha\le u^2A/2\) on \([0,u_{\max}]\) and the integral is at most \(\int_0^\infty2\alpha u^{\alpha-1}e^{-Au^2/2}du=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha/2)(2/A)^{\alpha/2}\). The condition. \(m'=\log_2(\ell/\delta)\le\log_2(64(\log N)^{\beta-1/2}/L)\le(\beta-\frac12)\log_2\log N\) for \(L\ge64\); \(u_{\max}\le(192/L)(\log N)^{\beta-1/2}\le(\log N)^{\beta-1/2}\) for \(L\ge192\); so \(u_{\max}^{\alpha-2}\le(\log N)^{(\beta-1/2)(\alpha-2)}\), while \(A/2\ge L^2\log N/(2048(\beta-\frac12)\log_2\log N)\) (for \(m'\ge1\); \(m'=0\) is the degenerate case below); the ratio of the first to the second tends to \(0\) when \((\beta-\frac12)(\alpha-2)<1\), so the condition holds for large \(N\), for both coordinates since \(\alpha_k\le\max\alpha\). Markov. Then \(\mathbb E[I_{\rm in}1_{E_1}]\le\eta_N=\sum_k\alpha_k\Gamma(\alpha_k/2)(2/A)^{\alpha_k/2}\to0\) as \(A\to\infty\), and \(P(I_{\rm in}>0.08,E_1)\le\eta_N/0.08\to0\). On \(E_1\cap\{I_{\rm in}\le0.08\}\) the cost is at most \(\frac N8I_c+0.01N+0.02N\). The particular case: \(I_c\le7\) gives cost at most \(0.905N\), so each coordinate's \(\sum_i\psi_{\alpha_k}\le0.905N\) and \(\sum_i\exp[(|X^k_i-Y^k_{\pi(i)}|/s)^{\alpha_k}]\le1.905N\le2N\). At \((4,4)\), \((\beta-\frac12)\cdot2<1\) is \(\beta<1\). \(\square\)
