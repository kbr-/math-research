Surviving common killer

Statement. Over a commutative ring of coefficients, let \(C\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal (a submodule of the polynomials of degree at most \(D\) closed under multiplying members of degree below \(D\) by variables), and \(w_1,\ldots,w_r\) polynomials. (a) Let \(s\) have degree at most \(\sigma\), with \(sw_i-w_i\in C\) for every \(i\), and let \(p\) satisfy \(sp\in C\). Then \(f=1-s\) satisfies \(fw_i\in C\) for every \(i\) and \(fp-p\in C\); so \(f\) kills every \(w_i\) and preserves \(p\) modulo \(C\). A sufficient case for \(sp\in C\): \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\) with \(w_ip\in C\) and \(\deg a_i+\deg w_i+\deg p\le D\). Errors \(E_\nu=h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}+c_\nu\), \(c_\nu\in C\), are killed by \(f\) when \(\sigma+\deg w_{i(\nu)}+\deg h_\nu\le D\) and \(\deg c_\nu+\sigma\le D\). When \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\) and \(\deg a_i+\sigma+\deg w_i\le D\), also \(s^2\equiv s\), which is why \(s\) is called idempotent. (b) Over a field, let \(X\) be finite and \(C\) the polynomials of degree at most \(D\) vanishing on \(X\), let \(Z\subseteq X\) be the union of the sets \(\{w_i\ne0\}\cap X\), and let \(k\le\min(D-\max_i\deg w_i,D-\deg p)\). A polynomial \(f\) of degree at most \(k\) kills every \(w_i\) and has \(fp\notin C\) exactly when \(f\) vanishes on \(Z\) and not at every point of \((\{p\ne0\}\cap X)\setminus Z\). So, as long as it is at most that bound, the first degree of such an \(f\) is the least degree of a function on \(X\) vanishing on \(Z\) and nonzero at some point of \((\{p\ne0\}\cap X)\setminus Z\).

Proof. (a) \(fw_i=-(sw_i-w_i)\in C\), and \(fp-p=-sp\in C\). In the sufficient case each \(a_i(w_ip)\) is a member multiplied through degrees below \(\deg a_i+\deg w_i+\deg p\le D\). For the errors, \(fE_\nu=h_\nu(fw_{i(\nu)})+fc_\nu\), members by the degree bounds. If \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\), then \(s^2-s=\sum_ia_i(sw_i-w_i)\), a member under the stated bound. (b) A polynomial of degree at most \(D\) lies in \(C\) exactly when it vanishes on \(X\). So \(fw_i\in C\) exactly when \(f=0\) on \(\{w_i\ne0\}\cap X\), and \(fp\notin C\) exactly when \(fp\ne0\) at some point of \(X\), that is, \(f\ne0\) at a point of \(\{p\ne0\}\), necessarily outside \(Z\). The degree bound on \(k\) keeps \(fw_i\) and \(fp\) inside degree \(D\). Only the definition of \(C\) is used in (b), not the pseudoideal axioms.

Between (a) and (b). In the domain model, (a) forces \(fp\equiv p\), so \(f=1\) on all of \(\{p\ne0\}\setminus Z\), while (b) needs \(f\ne0\) at one point only; the degree of (a)'s killer can exceed the exact first degree of (b): on \(X=\{0,1,2\}\subset\mathbb F_3\) with \(w=1-x^2\) (so \(Z=\{0\}\)) and \(p=x^2\) (support \(\{1,2\}\)), (b) gives degree \(1\) through \(f=x\), while (a) needs \(f(0)=0\) and \(f=1\) on \(\{1,2\}\), which forces \(f=x^2\). That gap is where a cheaper surviving killer than \(1-s\) would have to be found.

Against the recorded constructions. When \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\) is in the span of \(0/1\)-valued factors on the full cube, \(s\) vanishes off \(Z\) and equals the indicator of \(Z\), and multilinear representation is unique, so \(1-s\) is the reduced form of the product of denominators \(\prod_i(1-w_i)\): no gain. The general form of (a) asks less of \(s\), only \(s=1\) on \(Z\) and \(s=0\) on the support of \(p\), and may do better. For the pigeonhole hole factors \(x_{ij}\), \(i\in I\), with \(s=\sum_{i\in I}x_{ij}\) as in lem:factor-ideal-domination (d), the killer \(1-\sum_{i\in I}x_{ij}\) has degree \(1\) and preserves every \(x_{kj}\), \(k\notin I\); but when \(|I|\le D\) every cross monomial of \(\prod_{i\in I}(1-x_{ij})\) contains a hole axiom, so the product is congruent to the same degree-\(1\) element, and the gain is only against the formal degree \(|I|\), or where \(|I|>D\) and the product cannot be formed in the window. The element is the empty-state indicator \(e_{j,0}\) of historical lem:columnconditioning, restricted to \(I\): this instance is a rediscovery, read as a common denominator, not an improvement. What is new is the survival clause of (a) in general form and the exact characterization (b); (b) matches, in the domain model, the recorded locator control of prop:multiplier-transfer-limits, where \(t\) points on a line need degree \(t\).
