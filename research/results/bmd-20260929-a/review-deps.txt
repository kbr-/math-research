===== EXCERPT: cube-character-principal-parts =====
Character principal parts
Setup. Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, with distinct finite branch points \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\). Let \(C\) be the smooth projective curve whose function field is generated over \(k(z)\) by \[ w_A^2=\prod_{i\in A}(z-a_i),\qquad |A|\text{ even}. \] Choose a point \(P\) above \(\infty\), and normalize \(w_A\sim z^{|A|/2}\) there. Products satisfy \(w_Aw_B=\prod_{i\in A\cap B}(z-a_i)w_{A\triangle B}\). The square classes of the even subsets are distinct, by valuations at the branches, and form a vector space of dimension \(N-1\); hence the deck group has order \(D=2^{N-1}\). Infinity is unramified and its \(D\) points are indexed by the characters of this even-subset group. Put \(L=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)\). This is the quadratic line bundle \(2H\) on the original root curve, after changing the base coordinate to make its mark infinity. Write \([F]_+\) for the nonnegative-power part of a Laurent series in \(z^{-1}\).
Theorem. The complete quadratic section space is \[ V=H^0(C,L)=\langle1,z,w_{ij}:i<j\rangle, \qquad \dim V=\binom N2+2. \tag{QV} \] For \(M\ge3\), define \[ W_M=\{f\in k[z]_{\le M-3}:[z^{-1}](w_{ij}f)=0\text{ for every }i<j\}. \] Trace induces an isomorphism \[ H^0(C,K_C\otimes L^{-1}(MP))/H^0(C,K_C\otimes L^{-1})\ \cong\ W_M, \qquad \eta\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\eta)/dz. \tag{CP} \] Here \(\eta\) is written in the affine base frame of \(L^{-1}\). An explicit inverse modulo regular sections is \[ \eta_f=\frac1D\sum_{|A|\text{ even}}\frac{[w_Af]_+}{w_A}\,dz. \tag{CC} \] This is also the exact dual of the cokernel of the length-\(M\) jet map \(J_M:V\to L|_{MP}\), with functional \[ \lambda_f(\sigma)=-[z^{-1}](f(z)s(z)),\qquad \sigma(t)=t\,s(1/t),\quad t=1/z. \tag{JF} \] In (JF), \(\sigma\) denotes a local section modulo \(t^M\), and \(s=z\sigma(1/z)\); the expression depends only on those jets.
Proof of (QV). Split sections into deck characters. A character component has the form \(w_Aq(z)\). It has no finite pole only if \(q\) is polynomial: at a branch in \(A\), one base pole gives order \(-1\) upstairs, while at other points a pole is even worse. If \(|A|=2a\), regularity as a section of \(L\) at infinity says \(\deg q+a\le1\). The trivial character therefore contributes \(1,z\), pairs contribute \(w_{ij}\), and all larger even subsets contribute nothing. Independence follows from the character decomposition. This proves completeness even when \(H^1(L)\ne0\).
Global completion and its regularity. At a finite branch, \(dz/w_A\) is regular: when that branch is in \(A\), a parameter \(u\) gives \(z-a_i=u^2\), \(w_A=u\) times a unit and \(dz=2u\,du\); otherwise there is no denominator zero. Thus (CC) has no finite pole. At infinity use the affine frame \(e_0\) and the regular frame \(e_\infty=z e_0\) of \(L\). A differential written with \(e_0^{-1}\) represents a regular section of \(K_C L^{-1}\) precisely when its differential coefficient in \(t\) has order at least one, since \(e_\infty^{-1}=t e_0^{-1}\).
For \(|A|=2a\ge4\), polynomial truncation gives \[ \frac{[w_Af]_+}{w_A}=f+O(z^{-a-1})=f+O(z^{-3}). \] For \(a=1\), the defining coefficient condition of \(W_M\) improves the omitted tail from \(O(z^{-1})\) to \(O(z^{-2})\), so division by \(w_A\) again gives \(f+O(z^{-3})\). For \(A=\varnothing\) the equality is exact. At another lift of infinity these terms acquire their character signs. Character orthogonality makes their leading sum \(D f\) at \(P\), and zero at every other lift. Multiplication by \(dz=-t^{-2}dt\) turns the error \(O(z^{-3})\) into order at least one. Hence \(\eta_f\) is regular as \(K_C L^{-1}\) away from \(P\); at \(P\) its coefficient in the regular inverse frame has order at least \(-M\), because \(\deg f\le M-3\). Its trace is \(f dz\), proving that (CC) gives the asserted classes.
Trace is exact on the quotient. Conversely let \(\eta\) have only the permitted pole at \(P\). Its trace is regular at every finite point. Tame local trace of a regular differential is regular: summing the expansions under \(u\mapsto-u\) leaves a regular multiple of \(dz\). At infinity its pole order as an ordinary differential is at most \(M-1\), giving \(\operatorname{Tr}\eta=f dz\) with \(\deg f\le M-3\). At the other lifts its affine coefficient has order at least one, so \(\eta\) and \(f dz\) have the same principal part at \(P\), including the order-zero coefficient. If the trace is zero, \(\eta\) is regular as \(K_C L^{-1}\) also at \(P\). Conversely a global regular section has trace regular everywhere on \(\mathbb P^1\), hence zero. This proves the kernel assertion.
The ordinary differential \(w_{ij}\eta\) is regular away from \(P\). Its residue there is zero by the residue theorem. As \(\eta-fdz\) has coefficient order at least one at \(P\), multiplication by \(w_{ij}\), of pole order one, cannot change that residue. Hence \([z^{-1}]w_{ij}f=0\), and the trace belongs to \(W_M\). The explicit construction proves surjectivity. Finally the jet functionals annihilating the sections \(1,z\), whose regular local representatives are \(t,1\), are exactly (JF) with \(\deg f\le M-3\): the coefficient of \(z^j\) in \(f\) pairs with the \(t^{j+2}\) coefficient. Annihilating the remaining \(w_{ij}\) is exactly the definition of \(W_M\). This proves the dual-cokernel statement directly, without replacing it by the unrestricted space of differentials. \(\square\)
===== EXCERPT: cube-marked-normal-polynomial =====
Marked normal polynomial
Lemma. Work over a characteristic-zero field \(K\), with \(N\ge3\) distinct elements \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\). Put \(P(z)=\prod_i(z-a_i)\), \(R=\binom N2\), \[ H_j(X,Y)=[T^j]((1+XT)(1+YT))^{-3/2},\quad V_j=(H_j(a_i,a_j))_{i<j},\quad A_L=(V_0,\ldots,V_L). \] For any \(L\ge0\), left null row \(\omega A_L=0\), and right null vector \(A_Lu=0\), freeze \(u\) and set \[ h=\sum_{j=0}^L u_jH_j,\qquad b_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\omega_{\{i,j\}}h_X(a_i,a_j),\qquad r_h(z)=\sum_i b_i\frac{P(z)}{z-a_i}. \tag{MN} \] Then \(\deg r_h\le N-3\), \(r_h(a_i)=P'(a_i)b_i\), and \[ [z^{N-3}]r_h=-(L+1)u_L\,\omega V_{L+1}. \tag{MC} \] For a branch velocity \(v=(v_i)\), the actual matrix normal pairing is \[ \omega\,dA_L(v)u=\sum_i v_i\frac{r_h(a_i)}{P'(a_i)}. \tag{MP} \] The polynomial space \(K[z]_{\le N-3}\) is perfectly paired by this formula with \(K^N/\langle(1)_i,(a_i)_i\rangle\), of dimension \(N-2\). Its subspace \(K[z]_{\le N-4}\) annihilates the further velocity \((a_i^2)_i\), and has dimension \(N-3\) (zero when \(N=3\)). Thus dropping the marked point discards precisely the coefficient (MC).
Proof. Direct differentiation of the generating function gives, with \(H_{-1}=0\), \[ (\partial_X+\partial_Y)H_j=-(j+2)H_{j-1},\quad (X\partial_X+Y\partial_Y)H_j=jH_j, \] \[ (X^2\partial_X+Y^2\partial_Y)H_j=-(j+1)H_{j+1}-\tfrac32(X+Y)H_j. \] Pair these identities with \(\omega\). All columns through \(L\) are killed by \(\omega\), and \(h(a_i,a_j)=0\) for each pair. Symmetry of \(h\) therefore gives \[ \sum_i b_i=\sum_i a_i b_i=0,\qquad \sum_i a_i^2 b_i=-(L+1)u_L\omega V_{L+1}. \] In \(\sum b_iP(z)/(z-a_i)\), the two highest coefficients are \(\sum b_i\) and \(\sum a_i b_i-(\sum a_i)(\sum b_i)\), hence zero. The next coefficient is \(\sum a_i^2 b_i\), proving (MC). Evaluation at \(a_i\) proves the interpolation identity. Differentiating the actual pair entries proves (MP).
For completeness the perfect pairing is elementary. If \(\deg f\le N-2\), Lagrange interpolation and comparison of its \(z^{N-1}\) coefficient give \(\sum_i f(a_i)/P'(a_i)=0\); for \(f=z^{N-1}\) that sum is one. Apply this to \(f=r,zr\). Evaluation is injective for \(\deg r\le N-3\), so its \(N-2\)-dimensional image is the whole annihilator of the two independent vectors \(1,a\). Applying the same identity to \(z^2r\) gives its value equal to the highest coefficient of \(r\). The vectors \(1,a,a^2\) are independent because a quadratic cannot vanish at \(N\ge3\) distinct points unless it is zero. This proves the last assertion. \(\square\)
Geometric meaning, with the mark retained. The polynomials correspond to quadratic differentials \[ q_h=\frac{r_h(z)}{P(z)}(dz)^2 \] on the base projective line, with at most simple poles at the branch points and at the distinguished point \(\infty\). Indeed, in \(t=1/z\), a term of degree \(N-3\) has a simple pole at \(t=0\); degrees at most \(N-4\) are regular there. Coordinate changes preserving \(\infty\) have infinitesimal velocities \(1,a\). Allowing the mark to move adds the third projective vector field \(a^2\). A choice of one lift of an unramified base mark is finite étale and changes no tangent dimension. This is a marked-cover cotangent model, not an identification with the entire moduli space of genus-\(g\) curves.
At \(L=R\) and \(\operatorname{rank}A_R=R-1\), fix nonzero \(\omega\); the kernel has dimension two. The normal-rank criterion becomes linear independence of the two polynomials \(r_h\). If \(\omega V_{R+1}=0\), all these polynomials lie in degree at most \(N-4\); the marked direction supplies no rank there. This is exactly a possibility that must be retained at a component surviving the next prefix. If instead the functional \(u\mapsto u_R\omega V_{R+1}\) is nonzero, it supplies one direction; rank two still requires a nonzero polynomial on its one-dimensional kernel. Neither requirement is proved uniformly. Higher corank must also be excluded independently, as the all-rank control demonstrates. At collisions the root map is ramified; none of these ordinary root derivatives replaces the coefficient-ring criterion.
===== EXCERPT: rectangular-cyclic-normal-map =====
The two-dimensional normal map
Lemma. Let \(S=k[t_1,\ldots,t_d]\), with \(k\) of characteristic zero, and let \(A\) be an \(r\times(r+1)\) polynomial matrix. Put \(J=I_r(A)\). For a height-two prime \(P\supset J\), write \(K=\kappa(P)\). Then \(J_P=P S_P\) if and only if both of the following hold:
\(A(P)\) has rank \(r-1\);
the map \[ \mathcal N_P:\operatorname{Der}_k(S,K) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_K(\ker A(P),\operatorname{coker}A(P)), \qquad \xi\longmapsto[\xi(A)] \tag{NM} \] has rank two.
At corank one, choose a nonzero row \(\ell\) spanning the left kernel and a basis \(u^{(1)},u^{(2)}\) of the right kernel. In these coordinates (NM) is \[ \xi\longmapsto \bigl(\ell\,\xi(A)u^{(1)},\ \ell\,\xi(A)u^{(2)}\bigr). \tag{NP} \] If \(A=(v_0,\ldots,v_r)\), \(v_i=D^iv\), for a connection \(D=\delta+C\), its transport direction is \[ \mathcal N_P(\delta) =\ell v_{r+1}\,\bigl(u^{(1)}_r,u^{(2)}_r\bigr). \tag{TR} \] The intrinsic normal map and its rank or vanishing are independent of the chosen bases; the displayed coordinates transform with those bases. A zero transport pairing may come from either factor; it does not say that every parameter direction has zero pairing.
Proof. At corank one, an \((r-1)\)-minor is a unit in \(S_P\). Polynomial operations after localization put the matrix in the form \[ \begin{pmatrix}I_{r-1}&0&0\\0&a&b\end{pmatrix}, \qquad a,b\in P S_P. \] Thus \(J_P=(a,b)\). Under a parameter derivation, the derivatives of the changing source and target frames vanish after restriction to the kernel and projection to the cokernel: they have factors \(A(P)\) on the corresponding side. Therefore (NM) is exactly \(\xi\mapsto(\xi(a),\xi(b))\) in this local frame. In characteristic zero the conormal space of \(P\) has dimension two, and its map into \(\Omega_{S/k}\otimes K\) is injective. Hence (NM) has rank two precisely when \(a,b\) span \(P S_P/(P S_P)^2\). Nakayama gives \(J_P=P S_P\).
If \(A(P)\) instead has corank at least two, every \((r-1)\)-minor vanishes at \(P\). Differentiating an \(r\)-minor expresses its differential through these \((r-1)\)-minors, so every gradient is zero at \(P\). The ideal cannot generate the two-dimensional conormal space and cannot equal \(P S_P\). This proves necessity of the first condition as well. Finally \(\delta A=(v_1,\ldots,v_{r+1})-CA\). Pairing with \(\ell,u\), the \(CA\) term vanishes, and all shifted columns except the last already lie in the image killed by \(\ell\). Only \(u_r\ell v_{r+1}\) remains, proving (TR). \(\square\)
If \(J\) is proper and unmixed of height two, checking these two conditions at all its height-two primes is equivalent to radicality. Applied to the cube's boundary core, the invariant-prime theorem already handles the double-pair component. Every remaining component needs the two conditions above, not only a nonzero determinant at generic parameters. Formula (TR) is a refinement of the earlier square transport pairing, not a new proof of the required nonvanishing.
===== EXCERPT: cube-marked-ore-deflation =====
Removing the first block preserves the exact marked degree problem
Theorem (thm:cube-marked-ore-deflation). Work in the characteristic-zero centered block presentation, for \(n\ge2\), \(N=n+1\), \(\mathscr A_0=S[D;\delta_0]\), \(S=k[c_2,\ldots,c_N]\). Put \(q=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), \(h_r=2r\), \(\ell_r=2N-4r+1\), and \(w_r=h_r+\ell_r\). Write its complete relations as \[ R_1=L_1b_1-v(b_2,\ldots,b_q),\qquad R_r=H_r(b_2,\ldots,b_q)-C_rb_1\quad(2\le r\le q), \] where \(L_1\) is monic of order \(\ell_1=2N-3\). Define \(F\) on the remaining generators with only the relations \(H_r=0\): the feedback into \(b_1\) is removed. Then \(F\) is a polynomial free \(S\)-module with basis the remaining block strings, of rank \[ \binom N2-(2N-3)=\binom{N-2}{2}. \] Write \(V\) for the full pair target. The marked vector of \(F\) is the actual first off-diagonal row \(v\), of weight \(w_1=2N-1\), and \(F/\mathscr A_0v=C^c\).
There is an explicit left-\(\mathscr A_0\)-linear bijection \[ \Psi:\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathscr A_0,F}(v) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathscr A_0,V}(b_1), \qquad U\longmapsto UL_1-\sum_{r=2}^qV_rC_r, \tag{MD} \] where the \(V_r\) are the unique polynomial Ore coefficients satisfying \(Uv=\sum_{r=2}^qV_rH_r\). For every nonzero \(U\), \[ \operatorname{ord}\Psi(U)=\operatorname{ord}U+2N-3, \qquad \operatorname{lc}\Psi(U)=\operatorname{lc}U. \] For homogeneous \(U\), its total operator degree increases by the same \(2N-3\). Consequently the original degree-two threshold at order \(m\ge2N-1=2n+1\) equals the threshold of \(\operatorname{Ann}_F(v)\) at order \(m-(2n+1)\), with exactly the same excess and highest-coefficient ideal under the established centered dictionary. There is no nonzero original relation below order \(2n+1\). This is an exact reduction over the same parameter ring and derivation; \(F,v\) is not asserted to be a smaller cube instance.
Polynomial normal forms in the retained blocks. Each \(H_r\) has leading word \(D^{\ell_r}b_r\). Every other word has strictly smaller weighted generator order \(h_s+j\), with a coefficient in \(S_+\). The reduction from the block theorem therefore applies after deleting \(b_1\) too, and terminates without dividing a parameter. To see that no hidden relation among normal words is introduced, note that a nonzero combination \(\sum V_rH_r\) has generator order \[ \max_{V_r\ne0}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}V_r+w_r\bigr). \] At the maximum, leading words for different \(r\) involve distinct generators, while all replacement words are smaller. They cannot cancel. In particular the rows \(H_r\) are left-linearly independent. The reduction thus presents a free \(S\)-module on the normal words, and also proves uniqueness of any representation \(Uv=\sum V_rH_r\). Its connection is the polynomial action induced by \(D\). Quotienting the full block presentation by \(\mathscr A_0b_1\) leaves these relations and \(v=0\), proving \(F/\mathscr A_0v=C^c\).
The annihilator bijection. If \(Uv=\sum V_rH_r\), substitute the full relations \(L_1b_1=v\) and \(H_r=C_rb_1\). This proves that (MD) annihilates \(b_1\). Conversely, a polynomial annihilator of \(b_1\) is, by completeness of the full block relations, a combination \(UR_1+\sum_{r\ge2}V_rR_r\). Its coefficients on the other generators vanish precisely when \(Uv=\sum V_rH_r\), giving (MD). If \(U=0\), row independence forces every \(V_r=0\), and the annihilator is zero. The same maximum-leading-word argument proves independence of the full rows \(R_r\), so the coefficients and \(U\) are unique. Addition and left multiplication preserve their defining polynomial identities, giving the stated left-module bijection.
Highest order cannot be lost in feedback. Let \(p=\operatorname{ord}U\). Every coefficient in the off-diagonal row \(v\) has weight at least two, so its largest generator order is at most \(w_1-2\). Applying \(U\) raises that order by at most \(p\). By the preceding no-cancellation formula, \[ \operatorname{ord}V_r+w_r\le p+w_1-2. \] Likewise, every term of \(C_rb_1\) has generator order at most \(w_r-2\). Since \(h_1=2\), this gives \(\operatorname{ord}C_r\le w_r-4\) when \(C_r\ne0\). Thus \[ \operatorname{ord}(V_rC_r)\le p+w_1-6=p+\ell_1-4. \] All feedback terms lie strictly below the leading order \(p+\ell_1\) of \(UL_1\). This proves both exact order and leading-coefficient preservation, without generic localization.
If \(U\) is homogeneous of operator degree \(d\), then \(Uv\) has degree \(d+w_1\), so uniqueness and homogeneity give \(\deg V_r=d+w_1-w_r\) for nonzero \(V_r\). Every summand in (MD) has degree \(d+w_1-h_1=d+\ell_1\). This proves the total-degree shift. The divided-power prefix dictionary and original order-two reduction add two to both order and degree. The original shift is therefore \(\ell_1+2=2N-1\), and excess and highest-coordinate ideals are preserved. Below that order, the first block's independent initial string already excludes a relation. When \(q=1\), the remaining module is zero, the sums are empty and \(\Psi(U)=UL_1\); the same statement includes that endpoint. \(\square\)
The reduction does not automatically iterate as a smaller cube. Every coefficient of \(v\) lies in \(S_+\), and the new connection preserves \(S_+F\). Thus \(v\) and all its iterates vanish in the zero-parameter fibre. They cannot start the constant-pivot string used for \(b_1\) in the original block theorem. The equal rank \(\binom{N-2}{2}\) does not identify this new marked connection with the cube on fewer roots. The residual polynomial parameter obstruction is retained, not removed by the change of rank.
===== EXCERPT: cube-marked-ore-block-presentation =====
A polynomial block presentation with linearly many generators
Theorem (thm:cube-marked-ore-block-presentation). In the preceding centered setting, put \(q=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\). There are explicit constant vectors \(b_r\), of pair-hull weight \(2r\), for \(1\le r\le q\), with \(b_1=1\wedge Z\), such that \[ \bigl\{\nabla_0^jb_r:1\le r\le q,\ 0\le j<\ell_r\bigr\}, \qquad \ell_r=2N-4r+1, \tag{BF} \] is a polynomial \(S\)-basis of the full pair target. Its change-of-basis determinant is a nonzero constant. In particular the target has a complete \(q\)-generator, \(q\)-relation presentation over \(\mathscr A_0\), with marked generator \(b_1\) and relations \[ D^{\ell_r}b_r=\sum_{s=1}^q\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_s-1}a_{r,s,j}D^jb_s, \quad \deg a_{r,s,j}=2N-2r+1-2s-j. \tag{BR} \] The degree equality in (BR) applies to every nonzero coefficient. Every nonzero coefficient lies in \(S_+\), so has weight at least two; all have weight at most \(2N-3\). The full cyclic defect has exactly \(q-1\) minimal homogeneous Ore generators, in weights \(4,6,\ldots,2q\). This does not reduce its free parameter-ring rank to \(q\), nor determine the scalar annihilator of \(b_1\); the cross-block coefficients retain that problem.
Constant block heads. On \(E_0=k[Z]/Z^N\), let \[ RZ^u=-(u+\tfrac12)Z^{u+1},\qquad L_0Z^u=-\frac{u(N-u)}{u-1/2}Z^{u-1}, \] with zero at the endpoints. Then \([L_0,R]Z^u=(N-1-2u)Z^u\), giving the usual finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) raising/lowering relations. Use additive actions on the exterior square. If \(\gamma_u=-u(N-u)/(u-1/2)\), set \[ b_r=\sum_{u=0}^{r-1}\alpha_u Z^u\wedge Z^{2r-1-u},\quad \alpha_0=1,\quad \alpha_{u+1}=-\frac{\gamma_{2r-1-u}}{\gamma_{u+1}}\alpha_u \quad(0\le u<r-1). \] All denominators are nonzero constants. The two contributions to each coefficient of \(L_0b_r\) cancel, so \(L_0b_r=0\). Its Cartan weight is \(w_r=2N-4r\), and its polynomial hull weight is \(2r\).
The zero-fibre basis. The commutator identity gives \(L_0R^jb_r=j(w_r-j+1)R^{j-1}b_r\). Thus all vectors through \(j=w_r\) are nonzero. The next vector is a highest-weight vector of negative weight if nonzero; repeated raising of such a vector could never terminate by the same identity, contradicting nilpotence of \(R\) on the finite exterior space. Hence \(R^{w_r+1}b_r=0\). Each chain is the irreducible string of length \(w_r+1=\ell_r\). For completeness, these strings are independent: the Casimir \(H^2+2H+4RL_0\), with \(H=[L_0,R]\), acts on the \(r\)-th string by \(w_r(w_r+2)\); these scalars are distinct in characteristic zero. Finally \(\sum_{r=1}^q\ell_r=\binom N2\), the dimension of the whole exterior space. The strings therefore form a basis. This is the elementary exterior-square Clebsch–Gordan decomposition, with its actual graded heads specified.
Lifting the whole basis polynomially. The new connection is homogeneous of weight one and preserves \(S_+\). Its zero-fibre action is \(R\), so the vectors in (BF) specialize to the preceding basis. Each column has weight \(2r+j\). Since the zero-fibre basis is homogeneous, this list has the same weight multiplicities as the original pair basis \(Z^u\wedge Z^v\). The determinant of the full square change-of-basis matrix is consequently homogeneous of weight zero. Its nonzero specialization makes it a unit in \(k\), proving (BF) over \(S\), with no parameter denominators.
Express each next iterate in this basis. Homogeneity gives (BR) and the displayed coefficient weights. At zero parameters, that next iterate is the endpoint zero of its string. Thus all coefficients vanish in the zero fibre and lie in \(S_+\). The largest endpoint weight is \(2N-1\), and the smallest basis weight is two, giving the upper bound \(2N-3\).
Completeness of the Ore presentation. Reduce words by (BR), using the weighted generator order \(\operatorname{ord}_{\rm gen}(D^jb_s)=2s+j\), not ordinary differential order. Every nonzero replacement word has strictly smaller generator order, because its coefficient has positive weight. After left multiplication by a power of \(D\), Ore expansion differentiates some coefficients and only lowers the corresponding word order further. Reduction terminates and gives an \(S\)-linear combination of the words in (BF). Their images are a basis, so a relation maps to zero if and only if its reduced form is zero. The stated \(q\) relations are therefore complete, including all cross-block terms.
The graded augmentation quotient of the full target by \(S_+\) and \(D\) has precisely the \(q\) block heads. Killing the marked cyclic module generated by \(b_1\) removes exactly its first zero-fibre string. Hence \(C^c/(S_+C^c+DC^c)\) has one basis vector in each weight \(4,6,\ldots,2q\). Graded Nakayama for the positively graded Ore ring proves both generation and minimality of those remaining heads. This is a statement about the entire cyclic defect, not the smaller normalization kernel or its earlier weight-eight scalar class. \(\square\)
===== EXCERPT: cube-first-step-ext-degree =====
An exact degree formula from the actual prefix cokernel
Theorem. Let \(k\) have characteristic zero, \(N\ge5\), \(S=k[c_2,\ldots,c_N]\), with \(\deg c_j=j\). Put \[ q=\binom{N-2}{2},\quad w=2N-1,\quad p=w+q=\binom N2+2,\quad \lambda=3\binom N4. \] In the free marked core \(F\), let \(v_i=D^iv\), of weight \(w+i\), and define the actual finite prefix \[ C^+=\operatorname{coker}\left[ E=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{q+1}S(-w-i)\xrightarrow{(v_0,\ldots,v_{q+1})}F\right]. \] Write \(e_N=\operatorname{indeg}\operatorname{Ext}^2_S(C^+,S)\), with initial degree \(+\infty\) for the zero module. Then the original first-step degree is \[ \boxed{\ \alpha_N:=\ell_0(3,p+1) =\min\{\lambda,\ \lambda+p+e_N\}\ }. \tag{ED} \] In particular \[ 2\le\alpha_N\le \min\left\{\lambda,\lambda+\binom N2-4N+7\right\}. \tag{UB} \] If the kernel of the natural surjection \(C^+\to D_{22}(-4)\) has codimension at least three, then \[ \alpha_N=\min\left\{\lambda,\lambda+\binom N2-4N+7\right\}. \tag{CE} \] The condition is sufficient, and is not assumed proved. It predicts twelve at \(N=5\), forty-three at \(N=6\), and \(\lambda_N\) for every \(N\ge7\).
Rank-two grading and determinant. Let \(K=\ker(E\to F)\), and use the original excess grading \[ K_{\mathrm{ex}}=K(p+1),\quad E_{\mathrm{ex}}=E(p+1),\quad F_{\mathrm{ex}}=F(p+1),\quad C^+_{\mathrm{ex}}=C^+(p+1). \] The exact leading-coordinate sequence is \[ 0\longrightarrow S(-\lambda+1)\xrightarrow{G}K_{\mathrm{ex}} \longrightarrow I_N^c\longrightarrow0. \tag{KS} \] Here \(G\) is the primitive core boundary relation with a zero last coordinate; its original boundary excess \(\lambda\) decreases by one when regarded at the next order. The centered threshold dictionary identifies \(\operatorname{indeg}I_N^c=\alpha_N\). The proved first-step theorem gives height two for this proper ideal. This is the all-dimensional instance of the earlier rank-two solution-module construction, over the weighted coefficient ring.

The two effectivity conditions in every dimension
Lemma (lem:cube-polar-effectivity-union). Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic different from two, \(n\ge3\), \(d>n-3\), and \[g=1+2^{n-2}(n-3),\qquad N=2^{n-1}d+1-g.\] Assume \(N\ne0\) in \(k\). At collision-free parameters, take the normalized root curve \(C\) with \(w_i^2=1+a_iT\), marked point \(p_0\) given by \(T=0,w_i=1\), and \(H=H_\infty\). Let \(A_m\) be its coefficient matrix through column \(m\), \(\Delta=\det A_{N-1}\), and \(\delta=-\sum a_i^2\partial_{a_i}\). Then the polar locus \(\Delta=\delta\Delta=0\) is, set-theoretically, the union \[\left\{h^0\bigl(dH-(N+1)p_0\bigr)\ge1\right\} \cup \left\{h^0\bigl((n-3-d)H+(N-1)p_0\bigr)\ge1\right\}.\] Both displayed divisor classes have degree \(g-2\). This includes points where the square matrix has corank at least two. For \(n=4\), characteristic zero and every \(d\ge2\), the same statement applies to \((F,\delta F)\), since \(\Delta\) differs from the primitive boundary polynomial by a collision unit.
Proof. The recorded root-curve result gives \(K_C\sim(n-3)H\) and \(V_d=H^0(C,\mathcal O(dH))\) of dimension \(N\). Set \(L=dH-Np_0\), of degree \(g-1\). The coefficient matrix is the Taylor truncation of this complete space, so its left nullity is \(h^0(L)\). Riemann–Roch gives \(h^0(K_C-L)=h^0(L)\). Thus either \(L-p_0\) or \(K_C-L-p_0\) effective implies \(\Delta=0\).
For completeness the earlier dimension-four deformation pairing extends directly to this range. For a basis element \(f=T^s w_S\), with \(D=-\delta\), \[Df=\frac12\left(\sum_{i\in S}a_i\right)f+sT^{s-1}w_S-\partial_Tf.\] The first two terms define a row operator \(R\) on \(V_d\); lowering \(s\) stays in the space. Writing \(b_c\) for the coefficient column, this gives \(Db_c=Rb_c-(c+1)b_{c+1}\) in every characteristic different from two. If \(A=A_{N-1}\) has corank one, choose nonzero left and right null vectors \(\lambda,\mu\), and write \(\operatorname{adj}A=\gamma\mu\lambda^{\mathsf T}\), \(\gamma\ne0\). Pairing the column identity gives \[\delta\Delta=\gamma N(\lambda^{\mathsf T}b_N)\mu_{N-1}.\] The product vanishes exactly when one of the two factors vanishes, since \(N\) is invertible.
The first factor vanishes precisely when the unique section of \(L\) vanishes one additional time, namely \(h^0(L-p_0)\ge1\). The second factor vanishes precisely when the first \(N-1\) columns are dependent. Their rank is then at most \(N-2\), equivalently \(h^0(L+p_0)\ge2\). Since \(\deg(L+p_0)=g\), Riemann–Roch reads \[h^0(L+p_0)=1+h^0(K_C-L-p_0),\] which gives the second effectivity condition. If instead the square matrix has corank at least two, its adjugate is zero and hence both \(\Delta\) and \(\delta\Delta\) vanish. Both \(L\) and \(K_C-L\) have at least two sections, so subtracting one point leaves each effective. This proves the union at every point. Each degree is \(g-2\) by the displayed formula for \(N\). Finally, off collisions \(\Delta=uF\) for a unit \(u\); \(\delta\Delta=u\delta F+F\delta u\) identifies the two polar ideals there. \(\square\)

Character principal parts
Setup. Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, with distinct finite branch points \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\). Products satisfy \(w_Aw_B=\prod_{i\in A\cap B}(z-a_i)w_{A\triangle B}\). The square classes of the even subsets are distinct, by valuations at the branches, and form a vector space of dimension \(N-1\); hence the deck group has order \(D=2^{N-1}\). Infinity is unramified and its \(D\) points are indexed by the characters of this even-subset group. Put \(L=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)\). This is the quadratic line bundle \(2H\) on the original root curve, after changing the base coordinate to make its mark infinity. Write \([F]_+\) for the nonnegative-power part of a Laurent series in \(z^{-1}\).
Theorem. The complete quadratic section space is \[ V=H^0(C,L)=\langle1,z,w_{ij}:i<j\rangle, \qquad \dim V=\binom N2+2. \tag{QV} \] For \(M\ge3\), define \[ W_M=\{f\in k[z]_{\le M-3}:[z^{-1}](w_{ij}f)=0\text{ for every }i<j\}. \] Trace induces an isomorphism \[ H^0(C,K_C\otimes L^{-1}(MP))/H^0(C,K_C\otimes L^{-1})\ \cong\ W_M, \qquad \eta\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\eta)/dz. \tag{CP} \] Here \(\eta\) is written in the affine base frame of \(L^{-1}\). Proof of (QV). Split sections into deck characters. A character component has the form \(w_Aq(z)\). It has no finite pole only if \(q\) is polynomial: at a branch in \(A\), one base pole gives order \(-1\) upstairs, while at other points a pole is even worse. If \(|A|=2a\), regularity as a section of \(L\) at infinity says \(\deg q+a\le1\). The trivial character therefore contributes \(1,z\), pairs contribute \(w_{ij}\), and all larger even subsets contribute nothing. Independence follows from the character decomposition. This proves completeness even when \(H^1(L)\ne0\).
Global completion and its regularity. At a finite branch, \(dz/w_A\) is regular: when that branch is in \(A\), a parameter \(u\) gives \(z-a_i=u^2\), \(w_A=u\) times a unit and \(dz=2u\,du\); otherwise there is no denominator zero. Trace is exact on the quotient. Conversely let \(\eta\) have only the permitted pole at \(P\). Its trace is regular at every finite point. Tame local trace of a regular differential is regular: summing the expansions under \(u\mapsto-u\) leaves a regular multiple of \(dz\).
