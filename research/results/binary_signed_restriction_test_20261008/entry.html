Categorical extension

Lemma (categorical restriction bound). Fix an integer \(q\ge2\) and the alphabet \([q]\). A clause forbids exactly one pattern on its scope, equivalently it is an OR of coordinate inequalities \(x_i\ne a_i\). Empty false clauses are allowed. Let \(k\ge0\) and \(b\ge1\) be integers, and let \(H\) be a CNF of such clauses of width at most \(k\). Retain each coordinate independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\), assigning the rest independent uniform alphabet values. If \[\theta_q=\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}\left(\frac{qp}{1-p}\right)^\ell<1,\] then each restricted \(H\) has a finite real signed expansion into categorical CNFs of width at most \(b\), with absolute coefficient mass bounded by a nonnegative \(W\) satisfying \(\mathbb EW\le(1-\theta_q)^{-1}\). Consequently its correlation with any real test function is at most \(W\) times the maximum absolute correlation with those narrow CNFs, simultaneously for all test functions. No polynomial-degree or finite-field conclusion is included.

Proof: narrow tests. A compatible path prescribes one pattern on \(U_j\). On its cylinder each \(c_{U_h}\) is a fixed positive integer \(c_h\), and \(a_{U_h}=c_h+w_h\), where \(w_h\) counts violations of an indexed family of remaining width-at-most-\(b\) clauses. Uniformly permute that family together with \(c_h\) distinct markers, and conjoin the clauses before the first marker. At a point with \(w_h\) violations, the expectation of this Boolean test is \(c_h/(c_h+w_h)\): the earliest among the markers and violated clauses must be a marker. Independent choices for all \(h\), conjoined with \(K\) and the cylinder, realize the path term as \(\omega(P)\mathbb E J_P\), where \(\omega(P)=\prod_h1/c_h\). Equality to one alphabet value is expressed by \(q-1\) unit inequalities, so each realized \(J_P\) still has width at most \(b\). Thus \(W=1+\sum_{P\ { m compatible}}\omega(P)\) bounds total absolute mass.

Proof: reverse mass. Draw a uniform full assignment \(y\) independently of \(T\), then restrict outside \(T\). A compatible path cylinder has conditional probability \(q^{-|U_j|}\); multiplying by \(q^{|U_j|}\) changes its compatibility count into a sum over paths violated at \(y\). Put \(R_h=T\setminus U_h\), \(D_h=U_h\setminus U_{h-1}\), and let \(V_R\) be violated clauses whose scopes avoid \(R\). Then \(i_h\in V_{R_h}\), \(D_h\subseteq E_{i_h}\), \(|D_h|>b\), \(c_h=|V_{R_h}|\). Terminal \(R_j\) and reverse choices \((i_h,D_h)\) recover \(T\) and the path injectively. If \(\pi(R)=p^{|R|}(1-p)^{d-|R|}\), the weighted probability is \[\pi(R_j)\prod_{h=1}^j\frac{(qp/(1-p))^{|D_h|}}{|V_{R_h}|}.\] Allowing every eligible reverse choice only increases this nonnegative sum. At each state its one-step sum is at most \[\frac1{|V_R|}\sum_{i\in V_R}\sum_{\ell>b}\binom{|E_i|}{\ell}\left(\frac{qp}{1-p}\right)^\ell\le\theta_q,\] with value zero if \(V_R\) is empty. Induction bounds length-\(j\) mass by \(\theta_q^j\). Sum over terminal states, whose \(\pi\)-mass is one, then over \(j\), proving the estimate. Triangle inequality gives the correlation statement. This is the source's full finite argument with its binary cylinder factor replaced explicitly, not a claim about correlated restrictions. \(\square\)

If \(k\ge b+1\), its hypothesis forces \(p<(b+1)/(qk+b+1)\): the \(\ell=b+1\) term and \(\binom{k}{\ell}\ge(k/\ell)^\ell\) imply \(qp/(1-p)<(b+1)/k\). Thus reducing a large original width to a much smaller width uses a small live probability in this sufficient estimate.

External randomness does not supply a field identity

A positive mixture cannot replace the signed expansion exactly in general. If a finite random Boolean function \(J\) satisfies \(\mathbb EJ(x)=H(x)\in\{0,1\}\) for every point, then \(J(x)=H(x)\) almost surely at each point; finiteness gives \(J=H\) almost surely. A single positive OR on \(b+1\) variables is not a width-\(b\) CNF: a clause excluding its all-zero point misses some variable, and is also falsified by a point having only that variable equal to one. Hence, on the positive-probability all-live restriction, it admits no exact convex mixture of the required narrow tests.

Keeping a signed, integer-valued random estimator is different, but its real expectation still does not imply an exact characteristic-two realization. The scalar estimator equal to zero or two with equal probability has expectation one and is always zero modulo two. This is a logical control of that proposed inference, not a claim that the source's particular estimator has this law. A direct modular construction could still be possible; it must provide its own identity and degree/rank control. Clearing even denominators can annihilate the coefficient of the target modulo two, while odd denominators alone give no support-count bound from real coefficient mass.

Independent restrictions destroy local hardness

Proposition (local restriction survival). First, on \(m\) disjoint ternary one-hot blocks, independently leave each of the three bits live with probability \(p\) and otherwise fix it to a fair bit. The probability that every block still has a one-hot completion is exactly \(s(p)^m\), where, with \(a=(1-p)/2\), \[s(p)=(p+a)^3+3a(p+a)^2-a^3.\] Any block without a completion makes a standard old one-hot generator a nonzero constant after restriction.

Second, let a finite simple graph on \(n\) vertices have degrees between one and \(\Delta\). Put edge labels in \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), fix arbitrary vertex charges and oriented incidence equations, and independently leave each edge live with probability \(p\) or assign it a uniform label. The probability that no fully fixed vertex violates its charge is at most \[\left(1-(1-1/q)(1-p)^\Delta\right)^{\lceil n/(\Delta+1)\rceil}.\] A violated fully fixed vertex makes the usual local forbidden-pattern CNF contain an empty clause, hence gives an immediate refutation. This is a local screen, not an exact probability of global satisfiability or residual hardness.

Proof. A partially assigned one-hot block extends precisely if it has at most one fixed one and is not the all-fixed-zero pattern. The zero-fixed-one probability is \((p+a)^3\); the exactly-one-fixed-one probability is \(3a(p+a)^2\); subtract the all-fixed-zero probability \(a^3\). Different blocks are independent. Two fixed ones violate a pairwise exclusion generator by a constant one; all fixed zeros violate the row equation by a constant minus one. These exhaust the failure cases.

A graph of maximum degree \(\Delta\) has an independent vertex set of size at least \(\lceil n/(\Delta+1)\rceil\), by the greedy deletion of a vertex and its neighbors. Incident edge sets of distinct vertices in that set are disjoint, so the corresponding bad events are independent. A vertex of degree \(d_v\ge1\) is fully fixed with probability \((1-p)^{d_v}\). Conditional on that event, its signed label sum is uniform in \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\); one uniform incident label suffices, since its coefficient is \(1\) or \(-1\). Its charge fails with probability \(1-1/q\), independently of the charge. Thus its bad-event probability is at least \((1-1/q)(1-p)^\Delta\). Multiply the good-event probabilities on the independent set. Avoiding every bad vertex is a subset of that event, proving the bound. \(\square\)

The categorical version repairs the bit-domain problem: fixing an entire edge label always preserves its one-hot domain. It does not repair the second problem. For fixed \(\Delta\) and \(p\) bounded away from one, the probability of avoiding even these immediate contradictions is exponentially small in \(n\). This applies to the current ternary charged candidate, regardless of its nonzero total charge. The restriction mass estimate is unconditional. Conditioning on survival gives only the generic estimate \(\mathbb E[W\mid S]\le\mathbb EW/\Pr(S)\); the resulting reciprocal-survival allowance is exponentially large. This does not prove the actual conditional mass is large, only that the unconditioned theorem does not control it at the needed scale.
