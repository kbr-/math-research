From first-step mass to a discrete count

Use the exact categorical clause convention: each nontrivial clause forbids one pattern on a set of coordinates, of width at most an integer \(w\ge0\). Retain coordinates independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\) and fix the others to independent uniform values in an alphabet of size \(q\ge2\). Remove satisfied clauses after restriction, but retain original indices for counts. A false empty clause is allowed. Two remaining clauses have the same clause type if they have identical scopes and forbidden patterns.

A clause \(D\) is a subclause of \(C\) if its scope is contained in that of \(C\) and its forbidden pattern agrees there. This is literal inclusion, not semantic implication. Let \(M_b\) count distinct remaining clause types of width greater than an integer \(b\ge0\) that have no proper remaining subclause.

Theorem (minimal clause restriction). Put \[\Theta=\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{w}\binom w\ell\left(\frac{qp}{1-p}\right)^\ell.\] Then \(\mathbb E M_b\le\Theta\), with no assumption \(\Theta<1\). Consequently, with probability at least \(1-\Theta\), every remaining clause has a subclause of width at most \(b\), and the restricted CNF is equivalent on the full remaining cube to the conjunction of its remaining width-at-most-\(b\) clauses. Negative probability lower bounds are merely vacuous. For \(M\) CNFs under the same law, the failure probability for simultaneous pruning is at most \(\min(1,M\Theta)\).

For a uniform affine support over \(F_q\), with the live set independent of the full affine assignment, the same expectation bound counts only minimal wide types whose violation cylinders meet the conditional affine fiber. With probability at least \(1-\Theta\), the restricted CNF equals its narrow sub-CNF on that fiber. A wide clause whose violation cylinder misses the fiber may be removed semantically there, without having a literal narrow subclause.

Proof: each minimal type costs one. In the source's length-one expansion, an alive initially hard clause \(C_i\) has weight \(1/c_i\), where \(c_i\) counts indexed remaining clauses whose scopes lie in its scope and whose forbidden patterns agree with its violation. These are exactly its indexed subclauses. If \(C\) is a minimal type of multiplicity \(m_C\), every one of its copies therefore has \(c_i=m_C\). Their weights sum to one. Distinct minimal types use disjoint indices, so \(M_b\le W_1\), the first-step mass. In the affine variant retain only cylinders meeting the fiber; the same argument applies to the compatible minimal types.

Proof: first-step expectation. Here is the one-step reverse count without a convergence hypothesis. Fix a full assignment \(y\), let \(V_R\) be the indices of clauses violated at \(y\) with scope disjoint from \(R\), and put \(\alpha=qp/(1-p)\), \(\pi(R)=p^{|R|}(1-p)^{d-|R|}\). A hard first clause has live scope \(D\) of size greater than \(b\). Reverse it to \(R=T\setminus D\); its denominator is exactly \(|V_R|\). Reweighting its violation cylinder multiplies by \(q^{|D|}\), so its probability-weighted contribution is \[\pi(R)\frac{\alpha^{|D|}}{|V_R|}.\] The map from the original live set and clause index to \((R,i,D)\) is injective. Summing all eligible \(i\in V_R\), \(D\subseteq E_i\), gives at most \[\pi(R)\frac1{|V_R|}\sum_{i\in V_R}\sum_{\ell>b}\binom{|E_i|}{\ell}\alpha^\ell\le\pi(R)\Theta,\] with zero contribution when \(V_R\) is empty. Sum over \(R\) and average over \(y\). In the uniform full cube the cylinder reweighting is equality. On an affine support it is the already proved inequality \(q^{|D|}\Pr(\mathcal Z_i\mid T,\rho)\ge1\) for compatible cylinders; the same pointwise-in-\(y\) reverse estimate applies. Thus \(\mathbb EW_1\le\Theta\) in both cases.

Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(M_b\ge1)\le\Theta\). In a finite clause family every clause contains a minimal type. In the full cube, absence of wide minimal types therefore gives a narrow subclause for every clause. On an affine fiber, a compatible wide clause cannot contain a minimal subclause incompatible with that fiber, since violation of the wide clause would violate the subclause. All wide clauses not covered by a narrow subclause are therefore true throughout that fiber. These observations prove the equivalence assertions. Finally union-bound over the \(M\) formulas, with no independence assumption between their pruning events. \(\square\)

The source's full narrow-test mixture requires \(b\ge1\) for its unit cylinders. This first-step counting argument needs only \(b\ge0\), since it does not form that mixture. The weaker discreteness observation also holds: the full signed correction to the initial narrow CNF must have mass at least one wherever the two Boolean functions differ. It gives failure at most \(\Theta/(1-\Theta)\) when \(\Theta<1\). The minimal-clause count improves that to \(\Theta\) and supplies syntactic witnesses in the full-cube case. This is a consequence of the read reverse-path proof, not a claimed independent priority result.

Pay for the source pruning explicitly

If \(D\) is a subclause of \(C\), their violation polynomials satisfy the literal identity \[v_C=v_D\,m_{C\setminus D},\] where the extra factor is the product of the omitted literal-violation indicators. This holds over every coefficient field. For categorical variables use their one-hot indicators; for binary variables use \(x\) or \(1-x\). Its product degree is exactly the original width of \(C\).

Lemma (ideal input pruning). At one fresh level of complete ENS blocks, discard identically zero inputs and let \(d_i=\deg g_i\) be their actual original polynomial degrees. For each block select a nonempty subset \(f_j=g_{s_j}\) of its actual input positions and supply literal identities \[g_i=\sum_j a_{ij}f_j,\qquad \deg a_{ij}+d_{s_j}\le d_i\quad(a_{ij}\ne0).\] For accuracy \(h\), original maximum input degree \(\delta\), product cost \(h(\delta+1)\), and complete companions, zero all nonselected coefficient positions and retain the selected positions as the target coefficients. This transfers original degree-\(B\) PC and ordinary NS consequences of retained targets through degree at most \(B\). It is the existing costed input-reduction axiom-image argument with degree-adapted polynomial coefficients, not permission to use arbitrary ideal membership or reset original source costs.

Proof. The original product becomes exactly the selected product \(P'\), whose cost is \(w'=h(1+\max_jd_{s_j})\le h(\delta+1)=w\). Each source companion has image \[g_iP'=\sum_j a_{ij}(f_jP'),\] with every target-generator multiple of degree at most \(w'+d_i\le w+d_i\), the original companion degree. Selected fresh field equations rename to target field equations, while nonselected ones become zero. All substitutions are constant or variable renamings, so proof-line degrees do not increase. Derive axiom images by the displayed certificates and replay the specialized PC proof. For NS, substitute into each original summand and replace its generator image by that certificate; its original cofactor plus generator degree remains within \(B\). The freshness and actual nonzero input degrees make the original product/companion degree the stated cost; if only loose carried bounds are supplied instead, this conclusion uses the corresponding original costed-certificate ceiling. The constant tuple case is included.

All blocks at the fresh level can be treated simultaneously. Any later inputs must receive the same global substitution; their degrees do not increase. The rule provides no decrease in the already supplied compiler ceiling. For clause-indicator tuples with a narrow divisor for each input, the hypotheses are supplied by the displayed monomial factors, with no base consequence or finite-field reciprocal needed. \(\square\)
