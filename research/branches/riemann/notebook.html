Pair atom spectrum

Lemma (pair atom spectrum). lem:pair-atom-spectrum. In the setting above every \(v_z\) lies in \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\), and for all \(z,w\in\mathbb C\) \[v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w=\Lambda\,\widehat{\varphi^2}(z-w),\qquad v_z^{*}v_w=\Lambda\,\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-\bar z).\] Consequently, for \(z=\gamma-i\delta\) with \(\delta\ne0\), with \[\kappa(\delta)={\int\varphi(u)^2\cosh(2\delta u)\,du\over\int\varphi(u)^2\,du}>1,\] one has \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=N_\varphi\), \(v_z^*v_z=\kappa N_\varphi\), and there are orthonormal real vectors \(e_x,e_y\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\) with \[{1\over N_\varphi}\bigl(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+v_{\bar z}v_{\bar z}^{\mathsf T}\bigr)=(\kappa+1)\,e_xe_x^{\mathsf T}-(\kappa-1)\,e_ye_y^{\mathsf T}.\] For real \(z\) the vector \(v_z\) is real and \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}/N_\varphi\) is a rank-one orthogonal projection.

Proof. Put \(F_z(u)=\varphi(u)e^{-iuz}\in L^2(I)\) and \(\varepsilon_k(u)=\Lambda^{-1/2}e^{-i\alpha_ku}\). The \(\varepsilon_k\) are the standard Fourier basis of \(L^2(I)\) multiplied by the unimodular function \(e^{-i\alpha_0u}\), hence an orthonormal basis for \(\langle f,g\rangle=\int_If\bar g\). Since \(v_z(k)=\int F_z(u)e^{i\alpha_ku}\,du=\Lambda^{1/2}\langle F_z,\varepsilon_k\rangle\), Parseval gives \(v_z\in\ell^2\) and \[v_z^*v_w=\Lambda\sum_k\overline{\langle F_z,\varepsilon_k\rangle}\langle F_w,\varepsilon_k\rangle=\Lambda\langle F_w,F_z\rangle=\Lambda\int\varphi^2e^{-iuw}e^{iu\bar z}\,du=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-\bar z).\] Next, \(\overline{v_{\bar z}(k)}=\int\varphi(u)e^{iuz}e^{-i\alpha_ku}\,du\), and the substitution \(u\mapsto-u\) with \(\varphi\) even turns this into \(v_z(k)\). So \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\) and \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w=v_{\bar z}^*v_w=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-z)=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(z-w)\), the last step because \(\varphi^2\) is even. Now let \(z=\gamma-i\delta\). Then \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(0)=N_\varphi\), and \(z-\bar z=-2i\delta\) gives \(v_z^*v_z=\Lambda\int\varphi^2e^{-2\delta u}\,du=\Lambda\int\varphi^2\cosh(2\delta u)\,du=\kappa N_\varphi\), by evenness again. Since \(\cosh(2\delta u)>1\) for \(u\ne0\) and \(\varphi\ne0\), \(\kappa>1\). Write \(v_z=x+iy\) with real \(x,y\). Then \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=|x|^2-|y|^2+2i\,x\cdot y\) and \(v_z^*v_z=|x|^2+|y|^2\), so \(x\cdot y=0\), \(|x|^2=(\kappa+1)N_\varphi/2\) and \(|y|^2=(\kappa-1)N_\varphi/2>0\). As \(v_{\bar z}=x-iy\), \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+v_{\bar z}v_{\bar z}^{\mathsf T}=2(xx^{\mathsf T}-yy^{\mathsf T})\); take \(e_x=x/|x|\), \(e_y=y/|y|\). For real \(z\), \(v_z=\overline{v_z}\) is real with \(|v_z|^2=N_\varphi\). \(\square\)

The bilinear identity is the import's Poisson identity; what is added is its Hermitian companion at complex arguments and the resulting spectrum. Every hypothesis on \(\varphi\) is used: support in \(I\) for the basis expansion; realness in the Parseval step (\(\varphi^2\) rather than \(|\varphi|^2\)) and, with evenness, for \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\); evenness also for the bilinear identity, which without it reads \(\Lambda\int\varphi(u)\varphi(-u)e^{-iu(z-w)}\,du\); and \(\varphi\ne0\) for \(\kappa>1\).

Solitary amplification

Proposition (solitary amplification). prop:solitary-amplification. Let \(Z\) be a configuration with \(p\) conjugate pairs \(\{z_j,\bar z_j\}\) off the real axis, \(z_j=\gamma_j-i\delta_j\), \(\delta_j>0\), with \(\kappa_j=\kappa(\delta_j)\) and vectors \(e_{x_j},e_{y_j}\) from the lemma.

  1. Trace. \(\operatorname{tr}G_Z=\sum_{z\in Z}m_z\), whatever the positions of the points.
  2. Negative index. \(G_Z\) has at most \(p\) negative eigenvalues.
  3. One pair against the others. For each \(j_0\), \[\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\ge e_{x_{j_0}}^{\mathsf T}G_Ze_{x_{j_0}}\ge m_{j_0}(\kappa_{j_0}+1)-\sum_{j\ne j_0}m_j(\kappa_j-1)\,(e_{x_{j_0}}\cdot e_{y_j})^2.\]
  4. Solitary pair. If \(p=1\), then \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\ge m_1(\kappa_1+1)\).
  5. Truncated grid. Let \(\Pi\) be the coordinate projection onto any set of grid indices, \(p=1\), \(\kappa_1\ge2\), and suppose \(\|(1-\Pi)v_{z_1}\|^2\le\epsilon^2\|v_{z_1}\|^2\) with \(\epsilon\le1/4\). Then \(\|\Pi G_Z\Pi\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\lambda_{\max}(\Pi G_Z\Pi)\ge\tfrac45m_1(\kappa_1+1)\).
  6. Growth. If \(\varphi^2\ge c>0\) almost everywhere on \(s\le|u|\le s'\) with \(0\le s<s'\), then \(\kappa(\delta)\ge c\,(s'-s)\,e^{2|\delta|s}/\|\varphi\|_2^2\).

Proof. (1) Each \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}\) has rank one and trace \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=N_\varphi\) by the lemma. (2) and (3) Group the sum by real points and pairs. The lemma gives \[G_Z=\sum_{z\in Z\cap\mathbb R}{m_z\over N_\varphi}v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+\sum_{j=1}^pm_j\bigl[(\kappa_j+1)e_{x_j}e_{x_j}^{\mathsf T}-(\kappa_j-1)e_{y_j}e_{y_j}^{\mathsf T}\bigr],\] a real symmetric finite-rank operator. It is a positive semidefinite operator minus the positive semidefinite operator \(\sum_jm_j(\kappa_j-1)e_{y_j}e_{y_j}^{\mathsf T}\) of rank at most \(p\), so it is nonnegative on a subspace of codimension at most \(p\), which gives (2). Evaluating the quadratic form at the unit vector \(e_{x_{j_0}}\), dropping the nonnegative terms other than the \(j_0\) one and using \(e_{x_{j_0}}\cdot e_{y_{j_0}}=0\) gives (3). (4) is (3) with an empty sum, and \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) is the sum of the squared eigenvalues. (5) Write \(e_x,e_y,\kappa,m\) for the pair. Second-moment identity

Lemma (second-moment identity). lem:gram-second-moment-identity. For every configuration \(Z\), with \(S_Z(s)=\sum_{z\in Z}m_ze^{-isz}\), \[\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2={1\over\|\varphi\|_2^4}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}\omega(s)\,|S_Z(s)|^2\,ds,\qquad S_Z(s)=\sum_{z\in Z\cap\mathbb R}m_ze^{-is\gamma_z}+2\sum_{j=1}^nm_je^{-is\gamma_j}\cosh(\delta_js).\] The same holds for \(B\) with the second sum alone and for \(P\) with the first alone.

Proof. \(G_Z\) is real symmetric of finite rank, so \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\operatorname{tr}(G_Z^2)=\sum_{z,w\in Z}m_zm_w\,g(z-w)^2\). Since \(g^2=\widehat\omega/\|\varphi\|_2^4\), \(g(z-w)^2=\|\varphi\|_2^{-4}\int\omega(s)e^{-isz}e^{isw}\,ds\), and summing gives \(\|\varphi\|_2^{-4}\int\omega(s)S_Z(s)S_Z(-s)\,ds\). For real \(s\), \(\overline{S_Z(s)}=\sum_zm_ze^{is\bar z}=\sum_zm_ze^{isz}=S_Z(-s)\), because \(Z\) and \(m\) are invariant under conjugation. So the integrand is \(\omega|S_Z|^2\). Pairing \(z_j\) with \(\bar z_j\) gives \(e^{-is\gamma_j}(e^{-s\delta_j}+e^{s\delta_j})\). The argument uses only conjugation invariance, so it applies to the sub-configurations defining \(B\) and \(P\). \(\square\)

Reading. With \(z_\rho=(\rho-1/2)/i\) and \(x=e^{s}\), \(e^{-isz_\rho}=x^{-(\rho-1/2)}\), and conjugation invariance makes \(|S_Z|\) even in \(s\). So the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the Gram form at bandwidth \(\Lambda\) is a weighted mean square, over \(|\log x|\le\Lambda\), of the sum \(\sum_\rho m_\rho x^{\rho-1/2}\) over the zeros in the configuration (up to \(s\to-s\)). An off-line pair enters as \(2m\,e^{-is\gamma}\cosh(\delta s)\), of size \(e^{\delta|s|}\). The link of this zero sum to primes through the explicit formula is in the import; it is not re-derived here, and no statement about its size for \(x\) beyond \(T\) is made.

Real configurations

Lemma (real configuration bound). lem:real-configuration-bound. Flat window. Let all points of \(Z\) be real. Cut the line into cells of length \(2\pi/\Lambda\) starting at the smallest point; suppose each cell holds at most \(M_{\mathrm{loc}}\) points counted with multiplicity and the points occupy \(K\) consecutive cells. Then \[\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\le M_{\mathrm{loc}}\Bigl(3+{2\over\pi}(1+\ln K)\Bigr),\qquad\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\sum_{z\in Z}m_z.\] The second inequality holds for every window.

Proof. List the points with repetition as \(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_r\), \(r=\sum m_z\). Each \(v_{\gamma_i}/\sqrt{N_\varphi}\) is a real unit vector, so \(G_Z=EE^{\mathsf T}\) for the matrix \(E\) with these columns, and its nonzero eigenvalues are those of \(E^{\mathsf T}E=(g(\gamma_i-\gamma_k))_{i,k}\), a real symmetric positive semidefinite matrix. Its largest eigenvalue is at most the largest absolute row sum. Fix \(i\). The at most \(3M_{\mathrm{loc}}\) points in its own cell and the two neighbouring cells contribute at most \(1\) each, as \(|g|\le g(0)=1\) on the real line. A point in a cell at index distance \(k\ge2\) has \(|\gamma_i-\gamma|\ge(k-1)2\pi/\Lambda\), and \(|\operatorname{sinc}(t\Lambda/2)|\le2/(\Lambda|t|)\le1/(\pi(k-1))\). Summing over both sides, \(2M_{\mathrm{loc}}\sum_{k=2}^{K-1}1/(\pi(k-1))\le(2M_{\mathrm{loc}}/\pi)(1+\ln K)\). For the second inequality, the eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\ge0\) satisfy \(\sum\lambda_i^2\le\lambda_{\max}\sum\lambda_i\) and \(\sum\lambda_i=\operatorname{tr}G_Z=\sum m_z\) by the trace statement of solitary amplification. \(\square\)

Many-pair norm bound

Proposition (many-pair norm bound). prop:many-pair-norm-bound. In the setting above, with \(n\ge1\) off-axis pairs, let \(h_{jl}=g(z_j-\bar z_l)\), so \(h_{jj}=\kappa_j\) and \(h_{lj}=\overline{h_{jl}}\), and \[R_{jl}={h_{jl}^2\over\kappa_j\kappa_l},\qquad\rho=\lambda_{\min}(R).\]

  1. Reduction to the off-axis part. \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}-\|P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\), and \(\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\ge\lambda_{\max}(B)\ge\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}/(2n)^{3/2}\).
  2. Gram of exponentials. \(R\) is Hermitian positive semidefinite with unit diagonal, so \(0\le\rho\le1\); it is the Gram matrix, in \(L^2(\omega\,ds)\) on \([-\Lambda,\Lambda]\), of the normalized exponentials \(e^{-isz_j}\).
  3. Norm identity. \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=2\sum_{j,l}m_jm_lh_{jl}^2+2\Re\sum_{j,l}m_jm_l\,g(z_j-z_l)^2\), and the first sum equals \(c^{\mathsf T}Rc\) for the real vector \(c=(m_j\kappa_j)_j\).
  4. Lower bound. \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge2\rho\sum_jm_j^2\kappa_j^2-\sum_{j,l}m_jm_l\bigl(1+\kappa(\delta_j-\delta_l)\bigr)\).
  5. Separated pairs, flat window. Suppose \(\delta_j\Lambda\ge1\) and \(\delta_j\le\Delta\) for all \(j\), and \(|\gamma_j-\gamma_l|\ge\tau\) for \(j\ne l\), with \(\tau\ge8\Delta\). Then \(|R_{jl}|\le\tfrac74\bigl(1+(\gamma_j-\gamma_l)^2/(\delta_j+\delta_l)^2\bigr)^{-1}\) for \(j\ne l\) and \(\rho\ge3/5\). If moreover \(\kappa_*=\max_j\kappa_j\ge5M^2+1\) with \(M=\sum_jm_j\), then \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\kappa_*\).

Proof. (1) The first inequality is the triangle inequality for \(G_Z=P+B\). Since \(P\succeq0\), \(\lambda_{\max}(P+B)\ge\lambda_{\max}(B)\). \(B\) is real symmetric of rank at most \(2n\) with \(\operatorname{tr}B=2\sum_jm_j>0\) by the trace statement. If its operator norm is attained at a positive eigenvalue, \(\lambda_{\max}(B)=\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Otherwise it is attained at an eigenvalue \(-\mu<0\); as the eigenvalues sum to a positive number, the positive ones sum to more than \(\mu\), and there are fewer than \(2n\) of them, so \(\lambda_{\max}(B)\ge\mu/(2n)\). In both cases \(\lambda_{\max}(B)\ge\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}/(2n)\ge\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}/(2n)^{3/2}\), the last step because \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le\operatorname{rank}(B)\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\). (2) With \(a_j=v_{z_j}/|v_{z_j}|\) and the inner product \(\langle x,y\rangle=y^*x\) on \(\ell^2\otimes\ell^2\), \(\langle a_j\otimes a_j,a_l\otimes a_l\rangle=(a_l^*a_j)^2=(v_{z_l}^*v_{z_j})^2/(|v_{z_j}|^2|v_{z_l}|^2)=h_{jl}^2/(\kappa_j\kappa_l)\), using \(v_{z_l}^*v_{z_j}=N_\varphi g(z_j-\bar z_l)\) and \(|v_{z_j}|^2=\kappa_jN_\varphi\). A Gram matrix is Hermitian positive semidefinite, and the diagonal is \(1\). Also \(h_{jl}^2=\|\varphi\|_2^{-4}\widehat\omega(z_j-\bar z_l)=\|\varphi\|_2^{-4}\int\omega(s)e^{-isz_j}\overline{e^{-isz_l}}\,ds\), which is the stated Gram matrix after normalization. (3) Expand \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)=N_\varphi^{-2}\sum m_zm_w(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w)^2\) over \(z,w\in\{z_j\}\cup\{\bar z_j\}\). The pairs \((z_j,z_l)\) give \(g(z_j-z_l)^2\) and the pairs \((\bar z_j,\bar z_l)\) the conjugates. For \((\bar z_j,z_l)\), \(v_{\bar z_j}^{\mathsf T}v_{z_l}=v_{z_j}^*v_{z_l}=N_\varphi h_{lj}\), and for \((z_j,\bar z_l)\) the conjugate, \(N_\varphi h_{jl}\); summing over \(j,l\) both give \(\sum m_jm_lh_{jl}^2\). That sum is \(\sum_{j,l}(m_j\kappa_j)(m_l\kappa_l)R_{jl}\). (4) By (2), \(c^{\mathsf T}Rc\ge\rho|c|^2\). For the other sum, \(|g(z_j-z_l)|\le\|\varphi\|_2^{-2}\int\varphi^2e^{-u(\delta_j-\delta_l)}\,du=\kappa((\delta_j-\delta_l)/2)\) by evenness, and by Cauchy–Schwarz \(\kappa(d/2)^2\le\|\varphi\|_2^{-2}\int\varphi^2\cosh^2(du)\,du=(1+\kappa(d))/2\); so \(2\Re\sum\ge-\sum m_jm_l(1+\kappa(\delta_j-\delta_l))\). (5) Here \(h_{jl}=\operatorname{sinc}((t-is)\Lambda/2)\) with \(t=\gamma_j-\gamma_l\), \(s=\delta_j+\delta_l\). From \(|\sin(x+iy)|^2=\sin^2x+\sinh^2y\le\cosh^2y\), with \(b_j=\delta_j\Lambda\), \[|R_{jl}|\le{\cosh^2((b_j+b_l)/2)\over\sinh b_j\sinh b_l}\cdot{4b_jb_l\over t^2\Lambda^2+(b_j+b_l)^2}.\] The first factor equals \(\tfrac12\bigl(1/(\sinh b_j\sinh b_l)+\coth b_j\coth b_l+1\bigr)\), which decreases in \(b_j,b_l\) and at \(b_j=b_l=1\) is \(1.724<7/4\). Setting

By lem:gram-second-moment-identity with \(\omega(s)=\Lambda-|s|\), \(\|\varphi\|_2^4=\Lambda^2\), and evenness of the integrand, \[\|B_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2={2\over\Lambda^2}\int_0^\Lambda(\Lambda-s)\,|\Sigma(s)|^2\,ds,\qquad\Sigma(s)=2\sum_jm_je^{-is\gamma_j}\cosh(\delta_js).\] Write \(\|f\|_\Lambda^2=\int_0^\Lambda(\Lambda-s)|f(s)|^2ds\), so \(\Lambda\|B_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt2\,\|\Sigma\|_\Lambda\), and \(\kappa_\Lambda(\delta)=\sinh(\delta\Lambda)/(\delta\Lambda)\). Subset-sum resonance

Proposition (subset-sum resonance). prop:subset-sum-resonance. Let \(k\ge1\), \(\Lambda_0>0\), \(\delta>0\), \(b=\delta\Lambda_0\), and let \(q_1,\dots,q_k\) be odd integers whose \(2^k\) subset sums are distinct. Take the \(n=2^k\) points \(\gamma_I-i\delta\) with \(\gamma_I=\sum_{i\in I}q_i\pi/\Lambda_0\), \(I\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}\), all of multiplicity one. Then \[\|B_{\Lambda_0}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le{e^{2b}\over2b^2}\,(2k+1)!\prod_{i=1}^k\Bigl({q_i\pi\over2b}\Bigr)^2+3n^2.\] For \(q_i=3^{i-1}\) and \(b\ge1\) the right side divided by \(2n\kappa_{\Lambda_0}(\delta)^2\) is at most \((3/2n)(2k+1)!\prod_i(q_i\pi/2b)^2+9nb^2e^{-2b}\), which is \(O_k(b^{-2k})\). Hence there are no constants \(c>0\) and \(A\) such that \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge c\,b^{-A}\sum_jm_j^2\kappa_j^2\) holds, uniformly in \(n\), for all configurations of simple points at a single bandwidth.

Proof. With \(\alpha_i=q_i\pi/\Lambda_0\), \(D(s)=\sum_Ie^{-is\gamma_I}=\prod_i(1+e^{-is\alpha_i})\) and \(\Sigma(s)=2\cosh(\delta s)D(s)\). Since \(4\cosh^2(\delta s)=e^{2\delta s}+2+e^{-2\delta s}\) and \(|D|\le n\), the part with \(2+e^{-2\delta s}\le3\) contributes at most \((2/\Lambda_0^2)\cdot3n^2\cdot\Lambda_0^2/2=3n^2\). For the rest put \(s=\Lambda_0-u\). As \(q_i\) is odd, \(e^{-i\Lambda_0\alpha_i}=-1\), so \(|1+e^{-i(\Lambda_0-u)\alpha_i}|=|1-e^{iu\alpha_i}|=2|\sin(u\alpha_i/2)|\le u|\alpha_i|\), and \(|D(\Lambda_0-u)|^2\le u^{2k}\prod_i\alpha_i^2\). Therefore \[{2\over\Lambda_0^2}\int_0^{\Lambda_0}u\,e^{2\delta(\Lambda_0-u)}|D(\Lambda_0-u)|^2\,du\le{2e^{2b}\over\Lambda_0^2}\prod_i\alpha_i^2\int_0^\infty u^{2k+1}e^{-2\delta u}\,du={2e^{2b}\over\Lambda_0^2}{(2k+1)!\over(2\delta)^{2k+2}}\prod_i\alpha_i^2,\] which is the stated main term because \(|\alpha_i|/(2\delta)=|q_i|\pi/(2b)\) and \(\Lambda_0^2(2\delta)^2=4b^2\). For the ratio, \(\kappa^2=\sinh^2b/b^2\ge e^{2b}/(6b^2)\) for \(b\ge1\) (as \((1-e^{-2})^2/4>1/6\)). The last sentence follows by taking \(2k>A\) and \(b\to\infty\). \(\square\)

The case \(k=1\), \(q_1=1\) is the configuration of prop:two-pair-cancellation, whose exact leading ratio \(3\pi^2/(4b^2)\) equals the present bound's leading ratio \(3!\,(\pi/2b)^2/2\), computed with \(\kappa^2\sim e^{2b}/(4b^2)\). The implied constant grows quickly with \(k\): for \(q_i=3^{i-1}\) it is about \((2k+1)!\,3^{k(k-1)}(\pi/2)^{2k}/n\), so the decay sets in only for \(b\) well above \(3^k\); in the control below the ratio for \(k=3\) at \(b=24\) is larger than for \(k=2\). For a fixed \(n\) the proposition excludes only exponents \(A<2\log_2n\).

Structural reason. An off-line pair's contribution to the second moment is concentrated in a window of length about \(1/\delta\) at the end \(|s|=\Lambda\) of the range, because of the weight \(e^{2\delta s}\). There the phases \(e^{-is\gamma_j}\) of points within height \(\delta\) of each other are nearly frozen, so heights chosen as subset sums of odd multiples of \(\pi/\Lambda_0\) make the sum vanish to order \(k\) exactly at that end. The same reason applies to every lower bound at one fixed bandwidth that is linear or quadratic in the points' exponentials: the solitary bound, the bound through \(\rho\), and any single test vector. It does not apply when the bandwidth may move by more than the phases' common period, which is what the theorem below uses, nor to the continuation criterion, which tests at complex \(s\) over all scales.

Resonance-free bandwidth

Theorem (resonance-free bandwidth). thm:resonance-free-bandwidth. Let the heights \(\gamma_j\) be pairwise distinct, \(g=\min_{j\ne l}|\gamma_j-\gamma_l|\) (no condition if \(n=1\)), \(\delta_*=\max_j\delta_j\), attained at an index \(j_*\) with multiplicity \(m_*\). Let \(\Lambda_1\ge1/\delta_*\) and \(H\ge4M^2/(gm_*^2)\) (\(H=0\) allowed if \(n=1\)). Then there is \(\Lambda\in[\Lambda_1,\Lambda_1+H]\) with \[\|B_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ \ge\ {\sqrt2\over4}\,m_*\,{e^{\delta_*\Lambda}\over\delta_*\Lambda}-M\ \ge\ {\sqrt2\over2}\,m_*\,\kappa_\Lambda(\delta_*)-M.\] Consequently \(\|G_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\tfrac{\sqrt2}2m_*\kappa_\Lambda(\delta_*)-M-\|G^{\mathrm{on}}_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) at that bandwidth.

Proof. Split \(2\cosh(\delta_js)=e^{\delta_js}+e^{-\delta_js}\): \(\Sigma(s)=e^{\delta_*s}F(s)+R(s)\) with \[F(s)=\sum_ja_j(s)e^{-is\gamma_j},\quad a_j(s)=m_je^{-(\delta_*-\delta_j)s},\qquad R(s)=\sum_jm_je^{-is\gamma_j}e^{-\delta_js}.\] For \(s\ge0\): \(|R(s)|\le M\); each \(a_j\) is positive and nonincreasing with \(a_j(s)\le m_j\); and \(a_{j_*}=m_*\) is constant. Mean of \(|F|^2\). Let \(x_0\ge0\), \(H>0\). Expanding, \(\int_{x_0}^{x_0+H}|F|^2=\sum_j\int a_j^2+\sum_{j\ne l}\int a_ja_le^{-ix(\gamma_j-\gamma_l)}dx\). The diagonal is at least \(Hm_*^2\). For \(j\ne l\), \(f=a_ja_l\) is positive, smooth and nonincreasing, and integration by parts gives \(|\int_{x_0}^{x_0+H}f(x)e^{-i\nu x}dx|\le(f(x_0)+f(x_0+H)+\int|f'|)/|\nu|=2f(x_0)/|\nu|\le2m_jm_l/g\). So \[{1\over H}\int_{x_0}^{x_0+H}|F(x)|^2\,dx\ \ge\ m_*^2-{2M^2\over Hg}\ \ge\ {m_*^2\over2}\qquad(H\ge4M^2/(gm_*^2)).\] For \(n=1\), \(|F|^2=m_*^2\) identically. Average over bandwidths. Put \(Q(\Lambda)=\int_0^\Lambda u\,e^{-2\delta_*u}|F(\Lambda-u)|^2du\), so that \(\|e^{\delta_*s}F\|_\Lambda^2=e^{2\delta_*\Lambda}Q(\Lambda)\) by the substitution \(u=\Lambda-s\). For \(0\le u\le\Lambda_1\) and \(\Lambda\) ranging over \([\Lambda_1,\Lambda_1+H]\), the point \(\Lambda-u\) ranges over an interval of length \(H\) starting at \(x_0=\Lambda_1-u\ge0\). Hence, discarding \(u>\Lambda_1\), \[{1\over H}\int_{\Lambda_1}^{\Lambda_1+H}Q(\Lambda)\,d\Lambda\ \ge\ {m_*^2\over2}\int_0^{\Lambda_1}u\,e^{-2\delta_*u}\,du={m_*^2\over8\delta_*^2}\bigl(1-(1+2\delta_*\Lambda_1)e^{-2\delta_*\Lambda_1}\bigr)\ \ge\ {m_*^2\over16\delta_*^2},\] the last step because \((1+x)e^{-x}\le3e^{-2}<1/2\) for \(x=2\delta_*\Lambda_1\ge2\). So some \(\Lambda\) in the interval has \(Q(\Lambda)\ge m_*^2/(16\delta_*^2)\) (for \(n=1\) and \(H=0\) this holds at \(\Lambda=\Lambda_1\) directly). Conclusion. At that \(\Lambda\), by the triangle inequality in the weighted norm and \(\|R\|_\Lambda^2\le M^2\Lambda^2/2\), \[{\Lambda\over\sqrt2}\|B_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|\Sigma\|_\Lambda\ge e^{\delta_*\Lambda}\sqrt{Q(\Lambda)}-\|R\|_\Lambda\ge{m_*e^{\delta_*\Lambda}\over4\delta_*}-{M\Lambda\over\sqrt2}.\] Multiplying by \(\sqrt2/\Lambda\) gives the first inequality, and \(e^{x}/x\ge2\sinh x/x\) the second. The statement for \(G_\Lambda\) is the triangle inequality. \(\square\)

What is and is not used. Distinct heights and the least gap enter only through the off-diagonal oscillatory integrals; two off-axis points at the same height and different \(\delta\) are excluded by the hypothesis and not treated, and \(H\to\infty\) as \(g\to0\). Nothing is assumed about the sizes of the other \(\delta_j\): pairs with \(\delta_j\) close to \(\delta_*\) are handled by monotonicity of the amplitudes, which the worst-case \(\rho\) of the many-pair bound could not do. The hypothesis \(\Lambda_1\ge1/\delta_*\) serves only the constant. The benchmark is the single largest pair: against \((\sum_jm_j^2\kappa_j^2)^{1/2}\) a factor up to \(\sqrt n\) or more is not recovered by this theorem.

What it gives conditionally (a computation, not a theorem about \(\zeta\)). Fix a configuration at distinct heights and \(a\ge0\), and suppose \(\|G_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le Ce^{a\Lambda}\) for all \(\Lambda\) in an interval \([\Lambda_1,\Lambda_1+H]\) as in the theorem. Let \(K\) bound \(\|G^{\mathrm{on}}_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) over the whole interval (the real-configuration lemma bounds it at each bandwidth, with cells whose length depends on the bandwidth). Then \(e^{\delta_*\Lambda}\le2\sqrt2\,\delta_*\Lambda\,(Ce^{a\Lambda}+M+K)/m_*\) at one \(\Lambda\ge\Lambda_1\), so for bounded \(\delta_*\), \(\delta_*\le a+O(\log(\Lambda_1(C+M+K))/\Lambda_1)\). In the other direction the Setting gives \(\|B_\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le M\sqrt{2\kappa_\Lambda(\delta_*)^2+2}\) at every bandwidth. So in the model the growth exponent in \(\Lambda\) of the second moment over such a range equals the largest \(\delta\) up to the stated error, for configurations at distinct heights. The range is the limitation: \(H=4M^2/(gm_*^2)\) depends on the unknown configuration, and with \(M\) the total off-line multiplicity up to height \(T\) it may be a power of \(T\) against \(\Lambda\) of order \(\log T\). A statement confined to bandwidths within a bounded factor of \(\log T\) is not obtained, and by the reviewer's examples below it is not to be expected in this form.
