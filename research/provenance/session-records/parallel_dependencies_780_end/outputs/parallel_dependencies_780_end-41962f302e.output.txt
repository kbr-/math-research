
###parity-tree-prover
1. The prover: what cannot help
Notation: \(W\) a uniform affine subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^v\) of dimension \(k\), \(W^\perp\) the space of linear forms vanishing on its direction space (uniform of dimension \(a=v-k\)), \(R_p\) the span of the \(\ell\) forms \(b_{i,t}+b_{i',t}\) of a pair \(p=(i,i')\). A tree solves the collision search on \(W\) if every leaf subspace lies in the collision flat of some pair.
Lemma L (every prover must touch many rows). Let \(r\ell+\log_2\binom{n+1}r+t\le k\). Except with probability \(2^{-t}\) over \(W\), the following holds for every parity decision tree that solves the collision search on \(W\) and every assignment, to each query node, of a form in the label bits that agrees with the query on \(W\): on some path the assigned forms jointly touch more than \(r-2\) rows. In particular a tree whose queries touch at most \(\omega\) rows each has depth more than \((r-2)/\omega\).
Proof. A fixed nonzero linear form annihilates the direction space of \(W\) with probability \((2^a-1)/(2^v-1)<2^{-k}\), and at most \(\binom{n+1}r2^{r\ell}\) nonzero forms are supported on \(r\) rows or fewer. So except with probability \(2^{-t}\) no nonzero form supported on at most \(r\) rows is constant on \(W\); equivalently, for every set \(S'\) of at most \(r\) rows the projection of \(W\) to the label bits of \(S'\) is onto. This is the event that every set of \(r\) rows has jointly free labels on \(W\), stated without proof in the earlier entry. Note \(r\le k/\ell\le n+1\). Assume the event, fix a tree and an assignment, and suppose that on every path the assigned forms jointly touch at most \(r-2\le n-1\) rows. A delayer walks down the tree and keeps the set \(S\) of rows touched so far and a point \(x\) of the whole bit space that satisfies the answers given so far and is injective on \(S\). At a node with assigned form \(\tilde q\) it adds the rows of \(\tilde q\) to \(S\), changes \(x\) on the new rows to distinct labels not used on the old rows (possible because \(|S|\le n\), and harmless because the earlier forms do not involve the new rows), and answers \(\tilde q(x)\). Let the walk end at a leaf \(U\) with pair \(p\). If a row of \(p\) lies outside \(S\), change \(x\) there so that the two labels of \(p\) differ; otherwise they differ already, because \(x\) is injective on \(S\). As \(|S\cup p|\le r\), some \(w\in W\) agrees with \(x\) on the rows of \(S\cup p\). The assigned forms agree with the queries on \(W\) and are supported on \(S\), so \(w\) gives the delayer's answers and lies in \(U\); but \(w\) does not lie in the collision flat of \(p\). So the tree does not solve the search. (The same argument at an inner node shows that every answer of the delayer is realised on the current subspace of \(W\), so the walk never enters an empty branch.) \(\square\)
The proof uses the uniformity of \(W\) only for the probability of the free-rows event; the statement holds for every affine subspace on which every \(r\) rows are jointly free, and it uses of the board only that there are \(n\) labels.
For \(k=0.3v\) the condition reads \(r\le0.3(n+1)-(\log_2\binom{n+1}r+t)/\ell\), which allows \(r\) about \((0.3-0.88/\ell)n\), at least \(0.3n-n/\ell\): vacuous for \(\ell\le4\) (at \(\ell=4\) the condition allows \(r=2\) only), about two rows at \(\ell=5\), and a fraction of \(n\) that tends to \(0.3\) as \(\ell\) grows. So on a generic subspace of codimension \(0.7v\) every tree whose queries touch at most \(\omega\) rows each has depth more than \((r-2)/\omega\): ordinary decision trees on the bits need depth of order \(n\). This is the standard adversary argument, run on \(W\) through the free rows; it is a bound at rank of order \(v\), but only for the weakest kind of query. A prover that does better must use queries that touch many rows in every representation; all but a \(2^{-t}\) fraction of the forms on \(W\) are of this kind (counting sentence of the review). I found no strategy that uses such queries (two wide queries whose sum is a pair form give one narrow form for two queries, and are available on the whole space as well), and no proof that none exists. The attempt to build a prover of depth \(o(n)\) ends here.


###parity-tree-delayer
2. A density delayer
Lemma D (density delayer). Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of points of an affine subspace \(W\). If a parity decision tree of depth \(d\) solves the collision search on \(W\), there are a pair \(p\) and an affine subspace \(U\) of \(W\) inside the collision flat of \(p\), of codimension \(s\le d\) in \(W\), with \(|U\cap G|\ge2^{-s}|G|\); equivalently the density of \(G\) in \(U\) is at least its density in \(W\).
Proof. The delayer answers every query that is not constant on the current subspace by the side containing at least half of the points of \(G\) in it. After \(s\) such queries the subspace \(U\) has codimension \(s\) and \(|U\cap G|\ge2^{-s}|G|>0\). The path ends at a leaf, which lies in a collision flat. \(\square\)
Use on the whole space. Let \(W\) be the whole bit space and \(G=G^{[j]}\) be the points whose \(n+1\) labels take exactly \(n+1-j\) values (\(j=1\): one colliding pair), and \(\bar c_j\) the average number of colliding pairs of a point of \(G\). All pairs are equivalent, so \(|G\cap F_p|=\bar c_j|G|/\binom{n+1}2\), and the density of \(G\) inside a collision flat is smaller than in the whole space by the factor \(\beta_j=(n+1)/(2\bar c_j)\). Inside the flat of \(p\), with the two rows of \(p\) merged, \(G\cap F_p\) is the set \(S_{j-1}\) of labelings of \(n\) rows by \(n\) labels with image size \(n-j+1\). So on the full space a tree of depth \(d\) exists only if some affine subspace of codimension at most \(d-\ell\) of \(\mathbb F_2^{n\ell}\) carries \(S_{j-1}\) at density at least \(\beta_j\) times its average, a density boost \(\beta_j\). The depth is thus at least \(\ell\) plus the least codimension at which near-injective labelings can be concentrated by the factor \(\beta_j\) on an affine subspace. For an affine subspace \(H\) with annihilator \(A\) (a space of characters, \(|A|=2^s\)) the boost is \(\sum_{\chi\in A}\pm\hat S(\chi)/\hat S(0)\), at most \(1\) plus the sum of the \(2^s-1\) largest values of \(|\hat S(\chi)|/\hat S(0)\), \(\chi\ne0\) (the spectral bound of the table below), hence at most \(1+(2^s-1)\max_{\chi\ne0}|\hat S(\chi)|/\hat S(0)\). On a generic \(W\) the factor \(\beta_j\) holds only on average over the pairs, and the identification with \(S_{j-1}\) is not available; Lemma D itself holds for every \(W\) and every \(G\). The record already gives a depth bound on the whole space by another route: by Lemma T the depth is at least the bit degree, which is at least \(n/2+1\) (recorded); the interest of Lemma D is its use on \(W\), where the bit degree is unknown.


###parity-tree-spectra
3. Spectra of near-injective labelings
Here \(S_j\) is a set of labelings of \(n\) rows, the rows of the board with the two rows of \(p\) merged. Two closed forms. For bijections (\(S_0\)) the character with the same nonzero functional \(z\) on every row has coefficient exactly 1: its value on a bijection is \(\prod_x(-1)^{\langle z,x\rangle}\) over all labels, which is 1 because \(n/2\) is even for \(\ell\ge2\). All bijections lie in the subspace where the sum of the \(n\) labels is zero, of codimension \(\ell\): boost \(n\), above \(\beta_1=(n+1)/2\). On the board this is the subspace of the collision flat of \(p\) on which the labels of all rows except one row of \(p\) sum to zero; it contains every point whose only collision is \(p\). With \(G^{[1]}\) the delayer of Lemma D therefore guarantees only depth \(2\ell\). For \(S_1\) the same characters have coefficient \(-1/(n-1)\): the labels are all labels with one label \(x^\circ\) missing and one label \(x^*\) doubled, the sum of all labels is \(x^\circ+x^*\), and by the symmetry of \(S_1\) under permutations of the labels this is uniform over the nonzero vectors, so the coefficient is \(2\frac{n/2-1}{n-1}-1\).
Exact computation. The new tool near_injective_spectrum.py computes the Walsh transforms of the indicators of \(S_0,S_1,S_2\) on \(\mathbb F_2^{n\ell}\) for \(n=4\) and \(n=8\) (\(2^{24}\) points), lists the largest coefficients, and searches the 24 characters of largest coefficient for the best boost at codimension up to four (a lower bound on the maximal boost; the spectral bound above is an upper bound). Nonvacuity, checked before the run: the question is whether a boost \(\beta_j\) is reachable at codimension below \(\log_2\) of what the trivial bound \(2^s\) allows; on eight holes \(\beta_2=2.0625\) and \(\beta_3=1.243\) (from \(\bar c_2=1008/462\), \(\bar c_3=9576/2646\)), so codimensions two to four decide something.

On four holes the largest coefficients are 1 for \(S_0\) and \(1/3\) for \(S_1\), as the closed forms say. Consequences. On eight holes no affine subspace of codimension at most three boosts \(S_1\) to \(2.0625\) (spectral bound 2.00), so by Lemma D every parity decision tree for the full bit PHP on eight holes (nine rows) has depth at least \(\ell+4=7\), against 5 from Lemma T and the recorded degree bound; the halving tree has depth 17. With \(S_2\) the bound is 6, and \(j=3\) cannot give more than 7, because the boost 1.2531 found at codimension four exceeds \(\beta_3\). If the largest coefficient of \(S_1\) is \(1/(n-1)\) for every \(n\), as on four and eight holes (not proved), the weaker form of the spectral bound gives depth at least \(\ell+\log_2\bigl(1+(n-1)(\beta_2-1)\bigr)\), about \(3\ell-2\): logarithmic, below the \(n/2+1\) that the record already gives on the whole space. The spectral bound wastes almost everything: the best boosts found grow by a factor of about 1.13 per codimension, not 2.


###parity-concentration-sequential
1. A sequential bound for subspaces defined on few rows
Labels are the points of \(\mathbb F_2^\ell\), \(n=2^\ell\), and there are \(\rho\le n\) rows. For a set \(S\) of labelings and an affine subspace \(H\) of codimension \(s\) the boost is \(\Pr_{x\in S}[x\in H]/2^{-s}\). Let \(A\) be the space of linear forms that are constant on \(H\) (dimension \(s\)) and \(T\) the set of rows touched by \(A\), \(\tau=|T|\). Fix an ordering of \(T\) as positions \(1,\dots,\tau\), let \(A_i\) be the forms of \(A\) supported on positions at most \(i\) and \(c_i=\dim A_i-\dim A_{i-1}\), so \(\sum_ic_i=s\); call \(i\) a pivot position if \(c_i>0\).
Lemma N (sequential boost bound). (a) For \(S\) the injective labelings of \(\rho\le n\) rows, \(\mathrm{boost}(H)\le\prod_{i\ \mathrm{pivot}}\frac n{n-i+1}\le\frac{n^\tau(n-\tau)!}{n!}\) for every ordering of \(T\), with equality on the birthday subspace of the previous entry. (b) For \(S=S_1\), the labelings of \(n\) rows with exactly one colliding pair, \(\mathrm{boost}(H)\le\prod_{i\ \mathrm{pivot}}\frac{n+3}{n-i+1}\) for every ordering of \(T\) (the proof gives \(n+2\)).
Proof. A form of \(A_i\) whose component on position \(i\) vanishes lies in \(A_{i-1}\), so \(A_i/A_{i-1}\) injects into the functionals of the label of position \(i\). Hence for \(x\in H\) and given \(x_1,\dots,x_{i-1}\) the label \(x_i\) lies in a coset \(K_i(x_{<i})\) of codimension \(c_i\), with \(n2^{-c_i}\) labels, and \(H\) is the set of \(x\) with \(x_i\in K_i(x_{<i})\) for all \(i\). Reveal the rows of \(T\) in order; \(\Pr[x\in H]\) is the product over \(i\) of the conditional probabilities of \(x_i\in K_i\) given that the earlier conditions hold, each of which is at most the largest value of \(\Pr[x_i\in K_i\mid x_{<i}]\). (a) Given \(x_{<i}\), the label \(x_i\) is uniform on the \(n-i+1\) unused labels, so the probability is at most \(n2^{-c_i}/(n-i+1)\); non-pivot positions contribute at most 1. On the birthday subspace (distinct \(t\)-bit prefixes on \(m\) rows) every position of \(T\) is a pivot and \(K_i\) never contains a used label, so equality holds throughout. (b) The number of ways to complete an injective labeling of the first \(i-1\) rows to an element of \(S_1\) is \(f(i)=(n-i+1)!\sum_{k=i}^n(k-1)\), by the recursion \(f(i)=(i-1)(n-i+1)!+(n-i+1)f(i+1)\), \(f(n+1)=0\) (for \(i=1\) read \(q_1=0\); either row \(i\) repeats one of the \(i-1\) used labels and the rest is injective into the \(n-i+1\) unused ones, or it takes an unused label); in particular \(|S_1|=n!\,n(n-1)/2\). So before the collision \(x_i\) equals a given used label with probability \((n-i+1)!/f(i)\), in total \(q_i=2(i-1)/((n-i+1)(n+i-2))\), and is uniform on the unused labels otherwise; after the collision it is uniform on the \(n-i+2\) unused labels. In both cases \(\Pr[x_i\in K_i\mid x_{<i}]\le\frac{n2^{-c_i}}{n-i+1}+q_i\min\bigl(1,\frac{n2^{-c_i}}{i-1}\bigr)\le2^{-c_i}\Bigl(\frac n{n-i+1}+\frac{2n}{(n-i+1)(n+i-2)}\Bigr)\le2^{-c_i}\frac{n+2}{n-i+1}\), since the second term occurs only for \(i\ge2\). \(\square\)
Finite check. The new tool sequential_boost_check.py draws random affine subspaces whose forms live on at most \(\tau\) rows, computes the exact boosts of bijections and of \(S_1\) by enumeration, and compares them with the bound minimised over all orderings. On four holes (2000 subspaces, \(\tau\le4\)) and eight holes (400 subspaces, \(\tau\le5\), codimension up to eight) there is no violation. For bijections the bound is attained, also nontrivially (571 and 120 of the subspaces touch at least two rows and have boost above one, and 282 and 43 of these attain the bound exactly), so the test has power for part (a) and for the pivot count: an error there would show as a ratio above one. For the constant of part (b) it has none: the largest ratios for \(S_1\), 0.57 and 0.73, come from one-row subspaces (boost 1 against \((n+3)/n\)), with two or more rows they are 0.48 and 0.70, and in no sample does the boost of \(S_1\) exceed even the bound with constant \(n\). Part (b) rests on its proof; the reviewer checked the conditional law of \(S_1\) by brute force on four holes (\(f(i)=144,36,10,3\), \(q_i=0,1/6,2/5,1\)). Outputs: results directory.
What the lemma gives and what it does not. The bound depends on the rows touched, not on the codimension: with \(\tau\) rows the boost of \(S_1\) is at most \((1+3/n)^\tau n^\tau(n-\tau)!/n!\), about \(\exp(\tau^2/2n)\), so the birthday subspace is optimal among subspaces defined on few rows, and a boost \(\beta_2\approx n/4\) needs \(\tau\) at least about \(\sqrt{2n\ln(n/4)}\) rows. For forms of large row width the bound says nothing, and for bijections it cannot: the sum-zero subspace has boost \(n\) at codimension \(\ell\), every ordering has a pivot at the last position with ratio \(n\), and (a) is attained there as well. In the application of Lemma D the subspace lives inside a collision flat on the \(n\) merged entities, \(T\) counts entities, and a query that touches a row of the pair touches the merged entity. There the lemma gives depth at least \(\ell\) plus about \(\sqrt{2n\ln(n/4)}/\omega\) on the whole space for queries of row width \(\omega\), which is weaker than the injective-point adversary of Lemma L (order \(n/\omega\)). So Lemma N adds no depth bound; it shows that Question A is entirely a question about forms of large row width, the same place where the prover side stopped.


###parity-concentration-wide
2. Forms of large row width: what was tried
Absolute values lose the signs. The boost is a signed sum of normalised coefficients over the characters of \(A\), and the spectral bound of the previous entry replaces it by the sum of absolute values. Take \(H\) the subspace that fixes the labels of \(m\) rows to distinct values (\(s=m\ell\)). For bijections the character that applies one functional \(z\ne0\) to two rows has coefficient exactly \(-1/(n-1)\) (the sum of two distinct uniform labels is uniform on the nonzero vectors), and \(A\) contains \((n-1)\binom m2\) of them: absolute sum \(\binom m2\), signed sum \(\binom m2/(n-1)\) (for each pair, \(\sum_{z\ne0}-(-1)^{\langle z,a_i+a_{i'}\rangle}/(n-1)=1/(n-1)\)), which is the first-order term of the true \(\ln\) boost \(m^2/2n\). Likewise for \(S_1\), where the two-row coefficient is \(1/\binom n2-(1-1/\binom n2)/(n-1)\), about \(-(1-3/n)/n\) (previous entry), and the absolute sum is \(\binom m2(1-O(1/n))\). So no argument through absolute values can show a boost below \(1+\binom m2\) at codimension \(m\ell\), that is, it cannot answer Question A beyond codimension about \(\ell\sqrt{n/2}\); counting also the characters on \(j\) disjoint pairs (about \((m^2/2)^j(n-1)^j/j!\) of them with coefficients about \(n^{-j}\); heuristic, not proved) the absolute sum is about \(\exp(m^2/2)\) and the method stops near \(1.2\,\ell^{3/2}\), barely above the \(2\ell-2\) of the spectra.
A closed form for one functional on \(w\) rows. For bijections the character that applies one functional \(z\ne0\) to \(w\) rows has coefficient \((-1)^{w/2}\binom{n/2}{w/2}/\binom nw\) for even \(w\) and zero for odd \(w\): the labels of the \(w\) rows form a uniform \(w\)-subset of the labels, half of which satisfy \(\langle z,x\rangle=1\), and \(\sum_j(-1)^j\binom{n/2}j\binom{n/2}{w-j}\) is the coefficient of \(x^w\) in \((1-x^2)^{n/2}\). It gives \(-1/7\) at \(w=2\) and \(w=6\) on eight holes, as in the computed spectrum, and about \(2^{-n/2}\) at \(w=n/2\): single characters of middle width are negligible, and only a subspace with very many of them, with aligned signs, could matter.
A mixed programme and its ceiling (heuristic, no claim). Split \(A\) into the part \(A_0\) generated by characters of row width below \(w_0=4s/\ell\) and the rest. The boost of \(H\) is the boost of the larger subspace \(H_0\) defined by \(A_0\) plus a signed sum over \(A\setminus A_0\), all of whose characters have width at least \(w_0\); if coefficients of width \(w\) are at most \((Cw/n)^{w/2}\) (true for the closed form above, not proved for \(S_1\) in general), the \(2^s\) characters of the rest contribute little as long as \(s\) is below about \(\ell\,n^{1/2-\delta}\). The subspace \(H_0\) touches at most \(w_0s=4s^2/\ell\) rows and has at most \(s\) pivots, so by Lemma N its boost is at most about \(\exp(4s^3/(\ell n))\), below \(\beta_2\) only for \(s\) up to about \((\ell^2n)^{1/3}\). The programme would therefore end at depth of order \((\ell^2n)^{1/3}\), below the birthday ceiling and below the working degree.
The transfer, reformulated. The account in the previous entry counted the \(2^{sv}\) subspaces of codimension \(s\); the better count is over characters. Let \(W=c+W_0\) with \(W_0\) a uniform subspace of dimension \(k\) and \(c\) uniform, \(G\) a point set and \(f(\varphi)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{x\in G}\varphi(x)\). Expanding the indicator of \(W\) gives, for every character \(\chi\), \(\sum_{x\in G\cap W}\chi(x)=2^{-a}|G|\sum_{\psi\in W_0^\perp}\psi(c)f(\chi\psi)\): the coefficient of \(G\cap W\) at the class of \(\chi\) is \(f(\chi)\) plus the noise \(N(\chi)=\sum_{\psi\ne0}\psi(c)f(\chi\psi)\), normalised by \(1+N(1)\). The average over \(c\) gives \(\mathbb E N(\chi)=0\) and \(\mathbb E N(\chi)^2=\sum_{\psi\ne0}\Pr[\psi\in W_0^\perp]f(\chi\psi)^2<2^{-k}2^v/|G|\) by Parseval, about \(2^{-k+1.44n}\) for \(G^{[2]}\). This controls one character, not all \(2^v\) of them; a simultaneous bound needs moments of order above \(v/(k/2-0.72n)\), in which the average over \(c\) leaves the tuples of characters with trivial product, and the characters with large coefficients (small row width, and their products with the uniform characters) have to be separated as main terms, which is where the free-rows event of Lemma L would enter. I did not carry this out: its outcome would be that the coefficients of \(G\cap W\) are those of \(G\) up to a small error, after which the transfer inherits exactly the problem of the whole space, signed sums over characters of large row width.


###parity-concentration-decision
3. Decision
The review's rule was: if the two entries yield neither a prover nor a delayer, the value method is parked as a whole. The outcome of the two entries: no prover beyond the trivial ones, and none of bounded row width (Lemma L); a delayer for queries of bounded row width only (Lemma L, depth of order \(n/\omega\)); for unrestricted parity queries a criterion (Lemma D) whose best possible outcome with the set \(G^{[2]}\) is the birthday ceiling \(O(\ell\sqrt{n\log n})\) (for larger \(j\) the needed boost is smaller), no higher than the working degree under the qualifier of the route check, with a logarithmic bound proved, the case of few rows settled (Lemma N), and the case of large row width open on the whole space and on \(W\). Neither strategy resulted for the question as posed. The parity-tree line is parked here, with Question A, the mixed programme and the reformulated transfer as its parked items, and with it the value method (Claim G): its hardness input, the PHP on a generic subspace of linear codimension, has, after the entries since the claim was stated, a computable form (Lemma R), an untestable conjecture (Conjecture H1), and bounds only at rank up to \(n/4\) (Corollary SL, forms of any width) or, at linear codimension, only against provers whose queries have bounded row width (Lemma L). The common obstacle on every side (detectors, provers, delayers, spectra) is rank of order \(v\) in forms that touch a constant fraction of the rows: many wide forms at once, not one wide form, since a single wide equation is harmless by Corollary SL. The next entry is the review that reconsiders the form of the general step, starting from the simplest recorded unexcluded class, one OR over parities of PHP variables at middle rank (Section 2 of the review).

###form-review-type
1. The forms on record are of one type
Thesis. Every form of the general step that has produced a polynomial-calculus exclusion at a degree not tied to the accuracy of the blocks is of one type: a design of an enlarged old base \(\mathcal F\cup E\) composed with old images of the blocks modulo \(E\). The forms differ only in \(E\): a weight \(w\) (over \(\mathbb F_2\), for \(\mathcal F\) containing the Boolean equations and \(\deg w\le B\), \(w\notin\mathcal C_{B+\deg w}(\mathcal F)\) implies that \(\mathcal F\cup\{1+w\}\) has no refutation of degree \(B\), which implies \(w\notin\mathcal C_B(\mathcal F)\); so the one-affine-level theorem, the learning lemma and the relative interfaces are of this type with \(E=\{1+w\}\)), matching restrictions (the literal line, Conjecture L), linear value axioms and term values (Claim G, the reduction). Each needs a hardness statement \(H(E)\) about the old base alone, and the proof techniques on record for \(H(E)\) are four: counting a common weight, restrictions that preserve the base, the top-monomial detectors (rank at most \(n/4\), Corollary SL), and the case split (additive loss). Lemma K (constant-row clamp). Work over \(\mathbb F_p\). Let a source over an old base \(\mathcal F\) have complete first-level blocks \(b\le M\) with old inputs \(g_{b,1},\dots,g_{b,r_b}\) and accuracies \(h_b\), \(P_b=\prod_{u\le h_b}(1-\sum_jr_{b,u,j}g_{b,j})\). Choose constants \(c_{b,u,j}\) and put \(\lambda_{b,u}=\sum_jc_{b,u,j}g_{b,j}\), \(Q_b=\prod_{u\le h_b}(1-\lambda_{b,u})\). (i) If \(\mathcal F\cup\{g_{b,i}Q_b:b,i\}\) has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree \(D\), then \(\mathcal F\) with the blocks has none. (ii) Over \(\mathbb F_2\): if the source has, above the first level, only pure readers in the sense of the recorded reduction, and \(\mathcal F\cup\{Q_b:b\le M\}\) has no refutation of degree \(D\), then the source has none of degree \(D\).
Proof. Substitute \(r_{b,u,j}=c_{b,u,j}\); this is the degree-zero case of the substitution rule. The product \(P_b\) maps to \(Q_b\), each companion \(g_{b,i}P_b\) to \(g_{b,i}Q_b\), each coefficient field equation to \(c^p-c=0\), and old axioms to themselves; no degree changes. This proves (i). For (ii), modulo the axioms \(Q_b\) every first-level product is congruent to 0, so every input of a reader is congruent to a constant, and the two cases of the recorded reduction give the reader a constant image with constant coefficient rows (an input congruent to 1 is selected by the first row, image congruent to 0; if all inputs are congruent to 0 the zero rows give the image 1), and the same one level up. All images use constants, so the factor \(\max(1,w-1)\) of that reduction does not occur. \(\square\)
Scope, as the reviewer pointed out. Part (i) uses nothing about \(\mathcal F\); (ii) and the remarks (b), (c) below are written for \(\mathbb F_2\) (for odd \(p\) the reader cases select an input congruent to \(c\ne0\) with \(c^{-1}\), and flat indicators need powers \(p-1\); not written out). The lemma is not in the record in this form; the nearest records are the constant first-row specialization at the end of the learning lemma, the zeroing of surplus rows in the relative old-target theorem, and the recorded reduction with its nonconstant telescoping images. (a) Part (ii) needs the stronger axioms \(Q_b\); with \(g_{b,i}Q_b\) alone the product is not constant, the axioms only exclude the flat of \(\lambda\) minus the zero set \(Z_b\) of the block. (b) Since \(Z_b=Z_bQ_b\) modulo Booleanity, hardness of \(\mathcal F\cup\{Q_b\}\) at \(B+h\) implies hardness of \(\mathcal F\cup\{Z_b\}\) at \(B\): the hypothesis of (ii) is stronger than the zero-value hypothesis of the recorded reduction, and what it buys is the removal of the factor \(w-1\) at every rank above \(h\). It is Question H at rank \(h\) with total density \(M2^{-h}\). (c) For a block of rank at most \(h\), taking the \(\lambda_u\) a basis makes the companions Boolean consequences; this is the recorded packing. So for first-level blocks the accuracy is the width of the avoidance axiom the block needs, whatever its rank, and the choice of the \(\lambda\) is ours.

Remark (few blocks are conservative in the stable range; a corollary of two records). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(N\ge2B-1\), and adjoin to \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\) fresh complete blocks \(1,\dots,M\) whose inputs are old polynomials of degree at most \(\delta\), of any rank and accuracy. If an old polynomial \(t\) of degree \(k\) has a polynomial-calculus derivation of degree \(d\) from the enlarged system and \(d+M\max(\delta,k)\le B\), then \(t\in\mathcal I_k(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun})\). Proof: eliminate the blocks one at a time by the transfer lemma, whose base may contain the other blocks, at the cost \(\max\{d+\delta,d+k,2k\}\) each; the result is a derivation from \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\) within \(B\), and \(\mathcal C_B\cap\mathcal P_{\le k}=\mathcal I_k\) brings \(t\) down to its own degree. \(\square\) So the difficulty of the simplest class lies only in the number of blocks: whatever their ranks, \(M\) blocks give no refutation and no old consequence of degree \(k\) while \(d+M\max(\delta,k)\le(N+1)/2\).


###form-review-falsification
3. Falsification attempts
(1) Do term values weaken the wall on the twisted approximate-majority source? In the test of Claim G the value axioms were the \(0.7v\) linear equations \(u_i=0\), \(i\in I\), and that entry records that every term then lies in their ideal, so hardness with those equations gives the zero-value hypothesis \(E'=\{Z_c=0\text{ for every term }c\}\). Conversely \(E'\) is a weaker sufficient hypothesis, so the sentence there that the claim escapes "exactly if" the PHP stays hard on a generic subspace of codimension \(0.7v\) holds for that \(E\) only. Does \(E'\) avoid linear codimension? The points on which every (positive) term vanishes are the union of the coordinate subspaces \(\{u_S=0\}\) over the hitting sets \(S\) of the terms. For the recorded parameters (\(v\) DNFs of \(\lceil4v(3/2)^t\rceil\) terms, \(t=\lceil3\log_2v\rceil\) uniform coordinates each) a set of density \(\sigma\) misses a given term with probability \((1-\sigma)^t\); the expected number of missed terms is at least \(4v^{2}(1.5(1-\sigma))^t\), which exceeds \(v\) by a power of \(v\) exactly when \(\sigma<1-2^{-1/3}/1.5=0.4709\) (at \(\sigma=0.47\) it is at least \(3.2\,v^{1.007}\)), and a union bound over the sets of size \(\lfloor0.47v\rfloor\) suffices because supersets of hitting sets are hitting sets (failure probability at most \(0.09^v\), reviewer's arithmetic). So with high probability every hitting set has more than \(0.47v\) elements, a sharpening of the \(v/3\) that the approximate-majority property gives by itself. Every point allowed by \(E'\) lies on a coordinate subspace of codimension above \(0.47v\), a generic subspace after the twist. Random sets are hitting sets from density about \(0.58\) (equivalently, a complement fixed beforehand may have density up to about \(0.42\); reviewer). The only recorded route to hardness with \(E'\) is one such subspace, so term values move the instance of Conjecture H1 from dimension \(0.3v\) to at most about \(0.53v\): the wall stays. The source has many narrow terms of total density about \(4v^{0.75}\gg1\); this is the opposite regime to (HW).
(2) Is (HW) false for structured blocks? For some choices of the \(\lambda\), yes: if every block is a row clause \(\bigvee_jx_{ij}\) and every \(Q_b\) keeps the same \(h\) holes, the axioms \(g_{b,i}Q_b\) give \(x_{ij}=0\) outside the kept holes at degree about \(h+1\), and what is left is a PHP with \(h\) holes, refutable in degree about \(h/2+1\) (the upper bound is recalled from the literature and matches the recorded closures on six and eight holes; not retrieved). This example has \(M2^{-h}<1\), so the density condition is meaningful only for generic dense blocks, and (HW) can only hold for suitable choices of the \(\lambda\). For row clauses the suitable choice is trivial (\(\lambda_1\) the row sum, which is 1 modulo the row axiom); for other literal blocks it would have to make the kept cells expanding, where published bounds for graph PHP apply (imported context recorded in the literature section, not checked against this base). For exponentially many literal blocks the recorded families refute the PHP. I found no refutation of (HW) for polynomially many generic blocks with \(M2^{-h}<1\).
(3) Is (HW) within reach for generic blocks? On the design side the count is favourable: a design must annihilate the multiples of the \(Q_b\) of degree up to \(B\), about \(M\) times the quotient dimension at degree \(B-h\) against the quotient dimension at degree \(B\), and the recorded closures behave exactly by this count until a saturation jump; the jump is a polynomial-calculus effect outside the reach of a Nullstellensatz design, so a design-side proof could at best cover the counting regime. A proof needs that the only relations among these multiples modulo the matching marginals are those forced by the base axioms. Statements of this kind for dense random systems are, as far as I remember the literature on semi-regular sequences over \(\mathbb F_2\), conjectures and not theorems (from memory, not retrieved); what makes the present case different is that the base has an explicit design theory (fillings by the stable filtration, row trades) in which the statement becomes linear algebra on zero-marginal arrays. This is a reason to try a bounded step, not evidence that it will succeed.
