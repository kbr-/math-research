The exact branch and escape identities

Lemma (Boolean branch escape). Let \(q\) be a binary old polynomial of degree at most \(\delta\), where \(\delta\ge1\) is the retained positive source-cost convention (constant inputs are allowed), and let \(K\ge0\) satisfy \(K+2\delta\le D\). Put \(U=Q_K\), \(W=Q_{K+\delta}\), \(h=\dim U\). Let \(E:U\to W\) be inclusion and define \[ A[f]=[qf],\qquad B[f]=[(1-q)f],\qquad P=EE^*,\quad C=E^*A,\quad T=(I-P)A. \] Adjoints use the just-proved nondegenerate binary forms. Then \[ E^*E=I,\quad A+B=E,\quad A^*B=0,\quad A^*A=C=C^*,\quad B^*B=I+C,\quad T^*T=C+C^2. \tag{1} \] Moreover \(\operatorname{im}A\cap\operatorname{im}B=0\). Writing \(e=\operatorname{rank}T\) and \(\kappa_A=\operatorname{rank}A-\operatorname{rank}(A^*A)\), and similarly for \(B,T\), gives \[ \operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B=h+e,\qquad \kappa_A+\kappa_B=e-\operatorname{rank}(T^*T)=\kappa_T \le e\le \dim W-h. \tag{2} \] Thus this branch-radical cost is exactly the radical of its scalar degree-escape image, not automatically that of the existing stacked actual-error map.

Proof. Multiplication is well-defined by degree-bounded absorption of \(J\). Every binary polynomial is Boolean modulo the old variable axioms, and \(q^2-q\) has a certificate through degree \(2\delta\). For completeness, in characteristic two cross terms in a square vanish; each squared monomial reduces to that monomial by replacing squared variables one at a time using \(x^2-x\), never exceeding its original degree \(2\deg m\). Linearity gives the stated certificate. The global evaluation sum also has the pointwise identity \(q^2=q\).

Inclusion preserves the forms, so \(E^*E=I\). The identity \(A+B=E\) holds in characteristic two. For any \(f,g\in U\), \(\langle Af,Bg\rangle=\mu(q(1-q)fg)=0\) and \(\langle Af,Ag\rangle=\mu(qfg)=\langle Ef,Ag\rangle=\langle Af,Eg\rangle\). These prove the first Gram identities; \(B=E+A\) then gives \(B^*B=I+C\). Since \(P\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(E(U)\), \[ A=EC+T,\quad E^*T=0,\quad B=E(I+C)+T,\quad A^*A=C^2+T^*T=C, \] which proves (1).

If \(Af=Bg\), then \(qf+(1-q)g\in J\) at degree at most \(K+\delta\). Multiply by \(q\), paying at most \(K+2\delta\), and use the displayed Boolean certificate times \(f,g\). It follows that \(qf\in J\); hence the common image is zero. This uses the pseudoideal and the original degree window, not just a zero scalar moment.

Now \(\operatorname{im}A+\operatorname{im}B=E(U)+\operatorname{im}A\); quotienting this sum by \(E(U)\) leaves precisely the image of \(T\). Directness therefore gives the first identity in (2). For any operator \(C\) in characteristic two, \[ \ker(C^2+C)=\ker C\oplus\ker(I+C). \] Indeed a vector in the left side decomposes as \((I+C)x+Cx\) in the two right kernels; their intersection is zero. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(C^2+C)=\operatorname{rank}C+\operatorname{rank}(I+C)-h\). Subtract this equality from the first identity in (2), using (1). The quantity \(e-\operatorname{rank}(T^*T)\) is the dimension of the radical of \(\operatorname{im}T\), by rank-nullity for its restricted pairing. Finally \(T\) takes values in \(E(U)^\perp\), whose dimension is \(\dim W-h\). This proves (2). \(\square\)

Radicals do not prevent exact splitting here

Lemma (orthogonal branch extension). In a finite-dimensional nondegenerate symmetric bilinear space over any field, let \(s\ge1\) and let \(W_1,\ldots,W_s\) be pairwise orthogonal subspaces whose sum is direct. There is an orthogonal decomposition of the ambient space into nondegenerate subspaces \(H_i\), with \(W_i\subseteq H_i\). Zero subspaces are allowed. In particular, in the setting above there is a selfadjoint idempotent \(R\) on the existing \(W\) such that \[ RE=A,\qquad (I-R)E=B. \tag{3} \] No new ambient variables or external dual register are introduced.

Proof of the general statement. Split \(W_i=F_i\oplus N_i\), where \(N_i\) is its radical. Every complement \(F_i\) has a nondegenerate restricted form: a vector orthogonal to \(F_i\) is also orthogonal to \(N_i\), hence lies in \(N_i\), and the intersection is zero. The \(F_i\) are mutually orthogonal, and \(F=\bigoplus_iF_i\) is nondegenerate. Inside \(F^\perp\), \(N=\bigoplus_iN_i\) is totally isotropic. Choose a basis \(n_a\) of \(N\), grouped by \(i\). Ambient nondegeneracy supplies vectors \(s_b\in F^\perp\) satisfying \(\langle n_a,s_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}\): the pairing onto \(N^*\) is surjective. On the span of these vectors and \(N\), the Gram matrix is \[ \begin{pmatrix}0&I\\ I&M\end{pmatrix}, \] which is nonsingular regardless of the symmetric matrix \(M\).

Replace \(s_b\) by \(s_b+\sum_a L_{ab}n_a\). This replaces \(M\) by \(M+L+L^{\mathsf T}\). For each unordered pair of indices in different groups, choose one of the two corresponding entries of \(L\) to cancel that entry of \(M\), and set the other to zero. No diagonal entry needs alteration, so this works also in characteristic two. The resulting paired spaces \(N_i+\operatorname{span}\{s_b:b\text{ in group }i\}\) are nondegenerate and mutually orthogonal. Add \(F_i\) to each, and assign the orthogonal complement of their total to any one group. The result is the desired decomposition (for \(s\ge1\)).

For (3), take \(W_1=\operatorname{im}A\), \(W_2=\operatorname{im}B\). Their orthogonality and directness were proved above. Let \(R\) project onto \(H_1\) along \(H_2\). Since \(E=A+B\), it fixes \(A\) and kills \(B\), proving (3). The proof uses room already present in the ambient nondegenerate space; it does not charge an artificial rank loss for the branch radicals. \(\square\)

Controls and the remaining quantitative question

The previous identical isotropic image control does not contradict (3): its two images have nonzero intersection. Conversely, let \(e_0\) be a nonbridge edge and \(q=x_{e_0,0}+x_{e_0,1}\), with \(K=0,\delta=1\). Every nontrivial single-edge character is noncut. Hence \(\mu(q)=0\), while \(\mu(1-q)=1\), and both quotient classes are nonzero. We have \(h=1\), ranks of \(A,B\) equal to one, \(e=1\), \(\kappa_A=1,\kappa_B=0\), and \(T=q\) is isotropic. Equations (2) hold, and (3) still splits the two images exactly. This is a mechanism control, not a hard ENS block: choosing \(q\) itself erases \(q(1-q)\) through degree two. Constant \(q\) gives the opposite control, \(e=0\) and the trivial projection.

The near-half separator import concerns literal separator coordinates and private variable groups; it cannot be substituted for the argument above for arbitrary nonlinear \(q\). Its proper-cover example also warns that local pieces do not establish closure of their sum. Here directness and the projection extension are proved in one common quotient. They still do not assert that independently extended projections commute on the whole ambient space, have low-degree polynomial implementations there, or respect all mixed ENS constraints. The older orthogonal-unit counterexample explicitly rules out that latter automatic inference.

At \(e=0\), \(C^2=C\) and \(A=EC\), but demanding \(e=0\) would impose an unnecessary invariant-space condition. For \(e>0\), (3) already provides exact auxiliary projections. The actual partial-image bound measures stacked weighted input products modulo their current image. Nothing in (1)–(3) bounds that escape by the scalar \(e\) relative to the surviving weight. Nor does it give the small overlaps needed for a full matrix smoothing estimate after several different branch choices.
