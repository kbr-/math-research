The denominator actually used

The named Restriction bound and its complete proof in OpenAI, Beyond the Square-Root Exponent for Depth-Three Boolean Circuits, were reread from the pinned local TeX; exact version and file digest are in source provenance. This is a reading of that lemma, not a certification of the headline circuit theorem or a Lean replay.

For a CNF with indexed violation indicators \(v_i\), the proof begins with the integer count \(q=\sum_i v_i\). Along a compatible path it uses the count \(a_{U_h}\) of violated clauses having at most the target width of live coordinates outside \(U_h\). On the path's cylinder, \[a_{U_h}=c_h+w_h,\qquad c_h\ge h,\] where \(c_h\) is a positive constant counting already determined violations, and \(w_h\) counts an indexed family of narrow residual clauses. The marker mixture has pointwise expectation \(c_h/(c_h+w_h)\), while the path weight contains \(\prod_h1/c_h\). Thus the path fraction still contains \(1/(c_h+w_h)\). In backward counting, the constant count is the number of eligible violated clause indices and cancels that branching count over the reals. Neither positivity nor this division is an operation in characteristic two.

The existing marker-valuation lemma bounds the even denominators of grouped mixture coefficients. The present question is different: how much polynomial degree is needed to identify the assignment-dependent divisibility strata of the count? A selector for a stratum is not a realization of the rational reciprocal.

Violation digit degree

Theorem. Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_M\in\mathbb F_2[x]\) have degree at most \(w\ge0\). On Boolean assignments regard their values as the integers zero and one. Fix nonnegative integers \(c,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_M\), and define the integer-valued count \[V(x)=c+\sum_i\lambda_i F_i(x).\] For every \(s\ge1\), put \(Q=2^s\). There are explicit binary polynomials \(B_j\), \(0\le j<s\), for the binary digits of \(V\), with \(\deg B_j\le2^jw\). Consequently \[E_s=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(1+B_j),\qquad \deg E_s\le R_s:=w(2^s-1),\] is the indicator of \(2^s\mid V\) on the Boolean cube, including \(V=0\). For positive counts, \(B_j\prod_{t<j}(1+B_t)\) indicates exact valuation \(\nu_2(V)=j\), with degree at most \(w(2^{j+1}-1)\).

Construction and proof. Form the polynomial in an auxiliary indeterminate \(t\), over \(\mathbb F_2[x]\), \[A(t,x)=(1+t)^c\prod_{i=1}^M\left(1+F_i(x)\big((1+t)^{\lambda_i}-1\big)\right),\qquad B_j=[t^{2^j}]A.\] At a Boolean point this is \((1+t)^{V(x)}\). If \(V=\sum_j\epsilon_j2^j\), characteristic two gives \((1+t)^V=\prod_{j:\epsilon_j=1}(1+t^{2^j})\). Its coefficient at \(t^{2^j}\) is \(\epsilon_j\). Every nonconstant choice from a factor indexed by \(i\) contributes at least one power of \(t\), so a term contributing to that coefficient uses at most \(2^j\) of the \(F_i\)'s. This proves the degree bound, independently of the integer weights and the number of clauses. The displayed products of digits give the stated selectors and degree sums. \(\square\)

Paid certificate consequence. In the unshifted unweighted case \(c=0,\lambda_i=1\), let \(W\) be a binary polynomial of degree at most \(b\) which is zero at every Boolean point where \(V=0\) or where \(V\) is a positive multiple of \(2^s\). Then \(W\) has an ordinary NS certificate from \(F_i\) and old Boolean equations through \(b+R_s\). In particular, if a degree-\(D\) old Boolean PC pseudoideal contains all the \(F_i\), \(b+R_s\le D\), and \(W\notin J_D\), such a support restriction on \(W\) is impossible.

Certificate proof. First regard the construction as a polynomial in formal placeholders \(Y_i\), each later replaced by \(F_i\). The polynomial \(1+E_s(Y)\) has zero constant term and total placeholder degree at most \(2^s-1\), so it is a sum of multiples of the \(Y_i\), with every term of substituted degree at most \(R_s\). Thus \(W(1+E_s(F))\) has a certificate from the original \(F_i\)'s through \(b+R_s\). By the support assumption, \(WE_s(F)\) is Boolean-zero and has a Boolean-axiom certificate through its degree, at most \(b+R_s\): division by \(x_i^2-x_i\) does not increase degree and leaves a multilinear remainder, uniquely determined by its Boolean values. Add the two certificates to obtain \(W\). For an unsatisfiable CNF, \(V=0\) never occurs; if every positive count has valuation below \(s\), take \(W=1\) to obtain a refutation through \(R_s\). This is a sufficient upper bound, not an exact PC-degree characterization.

The digit construction uses the same elementary-symmetric/Frobenius mechanism as the recorded integer-imbalance certificate. It must not be confused with finite-precision replay: that theorem lifts the lowest bit to a residue ring with a degree cost linear in the precision. Here higher bits of an integer count are themselves computed over \(\mathbb F_2\), costing \(2^j\) in the bound.

Nonvacuity and sharp degree. Take \(M=2^s-1\), unweighted count, and disjoint width-\(w\) violation monomials \(F_i\), with \(w\ge1\). Since \(0\le V<2^s\), the multilinear representative of \(E_s\) is the CNF satisfaction polynomial \(\prod_i(1+F_i)\), of degree \(wM=R_s\). Thus the bound is sharp for full-cube representation. For the certificate consequence, the single false clause \(F_1=1\), \(s=1\), \(W=1\) gives a degree-zero certificate. These are controls, not proposed hard families.

Degenerate cases. With no \(F_i\), the digits are constants from \(c\); with \(c=0\), \(E_s=1\). Zero integer weights are allowed. Constant generators are covered by \(w=0\). The valuation selector is used only for positive counts; divisibility of zero is kept in \(E_s\) explicitly. The certificate consequence needs the unshifted unweighted case: a positive offset alone is not an old axiom and supplies no refutation.

Path normalizer width gap

Proposition. For every target width \(b_0\ge1\) and integer \(s\ge1\), the source's forward path-fraction construction has a duplicate-free, irredundant width-\((b_0+1)\) CNF and a compatible path for which \(c_1=1\), \(w_1\) is a count of width-one clauses on \(2^s-1\) free variables, and the Boolean indicator of \(2^s\mid a_{U_1}\), where \(a_{U_1}=c_1+w_1\), has exact degree \(2^s-1\) on that path cylinder. Thus clause width does not bound this assignment-dependent count valuation or selector degree independently of clause count. The backward recurrence denominator \(c_{U_1}=|V_{R_1}|\) is one in this example; no large backward denominator is asserted.

Proof. Put \(m=2^s-1\). Use shared variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_{b_0}\) and private variables \(t_0,\ldots,t_m\), with clauses \[C_i=z_1\lor\cdots\lor z_{b_0}\lor t_i\quad(0\le i\le m).\] Each clause is essential: set all shared variables to zero, its own private variable to zero and every other private variable to one. This violates only that clause. There are no duplicates or literal-subsumed clauses.

Take all variables live and the empty outside restriction. Every clause is initially hard for target width \(b_0\), so the source's initial easy CNF \(K\) is one. Select \(C_0\) as a one-step path. Its violating cylinder fixes all the \(z_j\) and \(t_0\) to zero. Only \(C_0\)'s scope is then fully determined, so \(c_1=1\); every other clause reduces to the unit clause \(t_i\). Hence \[a_{U_1}=c_1+w_1=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-t_i).\] It ranges from one to \(2^s\). Divisibility by \(2^s\) holds precisely when every remaining \(t_i=0\), whose unique multilinear indicator is \(\prod_{i=1}^m(1+t_i)\), of degree \(m\). The corresponding rational path term on the cylinder is exactly \(1/(c_1+w_1)\), so this is the source's denominator, not an invented count. \(\square\)

Scope and representation controls. Taking, for example, restriction probability \(p=1/4\) makes the source lemma's \(\theta=(2/3)^{b_0+1}<1\). The all-live event has positive, possibly tiny probability. This example does not refute the source's expectation bound or prove that a hard restriction encounters this path. Irredundancy rules out fixing it just by deleting duplicate or subsumed clauses, but arbitrary new representations and aggregate cancellation remain possible. The claim concerns the full-cube degree of a selector on one source cylinder, not the degree of every possible certificate in a hard quotient. It gives no lower bound on the precision of an optimally grouped aggregate.

Nonvacuity. \(b_0=s=1\) gives the two clauses \((z\lor t_0)\), \((z\lor t_1)\), the source's first even denominator. All larger \(s\) retain the same fixed target width and grow the number of essential clauses. The CNF is satisfiable; this is a path-normalizer control.

Degenerate cases. The construction requires \(b_0\ge1\), matching the source lemma's allowance of unit clauses, and \(s\ge1\). When the original width is at most the target width there is no such path, consistent with the source's \(W=1\) case. On the constructed cylinder the denominator is always positive; zero denominators are not used.

Compare the degree/error tradeoff before transferring it

For a fixed CNF, independently choose integer weights \(\lambda_i\) uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,2^s\}\), set \(c=0\), and use \(E_s\) as an approximant to satisfaction. On a satisfying point it is one. On any fixed violating point, at least one included weight is uniform modulo \(2^s\), independently of the others; their sum is uniform modulo \(2^s\). The false-positive probability is therefore \(2^{-s}\), with degree guaranteed only by \(w(2^s-1)\). The preceding disjoint-clause example, with the possible choice of all weights one, attains that ceiling.

Ordinary binary rows choose independent bits \(c_{ji}\) and use \[A_s=\prod_{j=1}^s\left(1+\sum_i c_{ji}F_i\right).\] They have the same one-sided, pointwise error probability \(2^{-s}\), but degree at most \(sw\) for every choice. Thus the digit method improves no worst-case degree/error guarantee at this interface; \(2^s-1\ge s\), strictly for \(s\ge2\). This compares guaranteed degrees, not the actual degree of every random draw. The randomized integer weighting changes the source's normalization and is not asserted to preserve its clause-count-independent mass bound.

Neither pointwise probability bound is a rank bound in the charged quotient. In particular, using an independent error budget of order \(1/M\) for \(M\) tests would require \(2^s\) of order \(M\), costing degree of order \(wM\) in the digit guarantee rather than \(w\log M\) in the row guarantee. The former cannot furnish the desired sublinear old-degree bound at arbitrary polynomial proof-size exponents by that sufficient policy. A different correlated or aggregate-rank argument is not ruled out. Original ENS replay and cofactor costs are not reset by this comparison of output polynomials.
