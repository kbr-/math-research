21 September 2026 — Standard equation clauses compile at constant degree into power-selector inputs

Status: working proof of a syntactic Res(Lin) transfer; no PHP lower bound. Its one-level inputs are degree-\(p-1\) selectors, generally not affine. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Missing implication tested. Can arbitrary-width standard equation clauses be handled without exponential residue splitting, and with a degree bound independent of DAG height? We allow polynomial selector inputs temporarily but retain their exact form and degree; a successful translation must expose, rather than hide, the remaining exclusion obligation.

Statement and source format

Fix a prime \(p\), put \(\delta=p-1\), and let \(v\) old variables be Boolean. Let a standard syntactic \(\mathrm{Res}(\mathrm{lin}_{\mathbb F_p})\) refutation have \(S\) lines, with input CNF clauses of width at most \(w\). Include Boolean axioms among the initials and set \(w_*=\max(w,2)\). The rules are the resolution, literal weakening, simplification and contraction audited in the preceding entry; arbitrary semantic inference is not assumed.

For an affine equation \(L=0\), set \(t_L=1-L^\delta\). There is a complete one-level ENS family with at most \(S\) blocks, each at accuracy one and having at most \(\binom{v+\delta}{\delta}\) inputs, each input literally some \(t_L\) from its source line. With coefficient equations \(r^p-r\), this family and the ordinary CNF polynomial axioms have an ordinary PC refutation through

\[D=\max\{4p-1,\ p+w_*\delta\}.\]

No proof-height factor occurs. The initial polynomial for \(\bigvee_i(L_i=0)\) is \(F_A=\prod_iL_i\); for Boolean CNF literals these are affine coordinate forms, up to nonzero signs. For fixed \(p\), both the registry inventory and the coefficient-variable count are polynomial in \(S,v\), even if the original intermediate clauses have enormous width. This is an existence statement about a PC refutation and registry, not a bound on monomial-expanded PC length.

Compression and complete blocks

In each line choose a basis subset of its selectors in the vector space of ordinary polynomials of degree at most \(\delta\). Every omitted selector is a constant linear combination of this subset. The bound follows from the displayed ambient dimension. No Boolean quotient is needed for this compression. Empty spans have an empty basis. For the chosen basis \(B_C\), introduce fresh coefficients and set

\[P_C=1-\sum_{j\in B_C}r_{C,j}t_j,\qquad E_{C,j}=t_jP_C.\]

The complete block contains every companion \(E_{C,j}\) and every coefficient field equation. Thus \(\deg P_C\le p\), \(\deg E_{C,j}\le2p-1\). Any original selector in line \(C\) times \(P_C\) is a constant combination of these companions at the same ceiling. No derived line \(P_C\) is added as an axiom. For an empty basis \(P_C=1\), no coefficient variables or companions are required.

Boolean vanishing certificates used below

A polynomial in old Boolean variables that vanishes on the whole Boolean cube has an ordinary Boolean-ideal certificate through its own total degree: reduce each power using \(x_i^2-x_i\) without increasing degree; the remaining multilinear polynomial is zero by evaluation on the cube. This argument works over every field. It will be used only for an explicit local selector identity, not for any PHP consequence.

Initial values and one-premise rules

For an initial clause \(A\), its original selectors satisfy

\[1-\prod_{i=1}^{a}(1-t_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}t_i\prod_{j<i}(1-t_j),\qquad \prod_i(1-t_i)=F_A^\delta.\]

Multiply by \(P_A\). Each summand on the right is obtained from \(t_iP_A\), a constant combination of complete companions, by multiplying by the preceding factors. The term \(F_A^\delta P_A\) follows from the initial axiom \(F_A\). All terms have degree at most \(p+a\delta\); for Boolean axioms, \(F_A=x_i(x_i-1)\) is already an old Boolean equation. Hence the initial \(P_A\) is derived within \(D\).

For weakening from \(A\) to \(T\), every basis selector of \(A\) lies in the selector span of \(T\). The identity \(P_T=P_AP_T+\sum_jr_{A,j}t_jP_T\) derives \(P_T\) from the completed line \(P_A\) and the \(T\)-companions through \(\max(D,2p)\). Simplification deletes only \(a=0\) with \(a\ne0\), whose selector is identically zero, so the same argument applies. Contraction leaves the span unchanged.

Resolution and exact degree ledger

Consider premises \(A=C\vee(f=0)\), \(B=E\vee(g=0)\), and conclusion \(T=C\vee E\vee(h=0)\), where \(h=\alpha f+\beta g\). In the basis subset of \(A\), all selectors except possibly \(t_f\) come from \(C\); let \(r_f\) be its coefficient if selected, or zero otherwise. Likewise define \(r_g\). A selector that also occurs in the context can still be designated as pivot; the remaining ones are context selectors. Using the completed premise values and the \(T\)-companions derives

\[R_A=P_T-r_ft_fP_T=0,\qquad R_B=P_T-r_gt_gP_T=0\]

through \(\max(D,2p)\). For example \(R_A=P_AP_T+\sum_{j\text{ context}}r_{A,j}t_jP_T\). Each context selector is in the selector span of \(T\).

At every old Boolean assignment, \(t_ft_g(1-t_h)=0\): if both first selectors are one then \(f=g=0\), hence \(h=0\); otherwise their product is zero. Let \(I=t_ft_g(1-t_h)\). Its degree is at most \(3\delta\), so it has the Boolean certificate just proved. Also \(t_hP_T\) is a constant combination of \(T\)-companions. Consequently

\[Q=t_ft_gP_T=t_ft_g(t_hP_T)+IP_T=0\]

has a PC derivation through \(\max(D,4p-3)\). The last local step is the ordinary polynomial identity

\[P_T=R_A+r_ft_fR_B+r_fr_gQ.\]

The middle product has degree at most \(3p\). Constructing the last term by multiplying the completed \(Q\) line by its two coefficient variables costs at most \(4p-1\) even if its derivation previously reached \(4p-3\). Thus the entire inference fits \(D\). Multiplication by a polynomial means its expansion into monomials, each implemented by variable multiplications followed by field-linear combinations. Crucially, multiply the completed premise lines rather than their whole derivations; the degree does not accumulate along the DAG.

Induct through the original topological line order, in one fixed registry. The empty final clause has \(P_\varnothing=1\), so this yields the asserted PC refutation. Coefficient field equations remain in the source although this forward simulation does not need them. The empty-initial-clause case already has axiom one and is immediate.

A maximal-rank tuple with no multilinear restriction kernel

Their underlying affine forms have rank \(s\), and the \(g_i\) are linearly independent ordinary polynomials. Their full-field common-zero set is \((\mathbb F_p^\times)^s\). Restriction to this set is injective on the whole space of multilinear polynomials, hence on every graph weight space spanned by squarefree matching monomials.

Proof. Choose distinct nonzero \(a,b\in\mathbb F_p\). A multilinear polynomial vanishing on \(\{a,b\}^s\) is zero. Induct on \(s\): write it as \(A(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{s-1})+Y_sB(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{s-1})\). Evaluating at \(a,b\) and subtracting gives \((a-b)B=0\) on the smaller grid, so the induction hypothesis gives \(B=0\) and then \(A=0\). The base case is constant polynomials. Since the two-point grid lies in the common-zero set, injectivity follows. In particular no nonzero graph weight can lie in the ordinary ideal generated by these selectors: membership would force vanishing on that set. Linear independence of the inputs follows by comparing the distinct monomials \(Y_i^{p-1}\). This proves the assertion.

The failure already occurs for \(p=3\) and a single accuracy-one block. An arbitrary equation clause listing the coordinate equations supplies precisely this kind of selector tuple, so the compiler does not prohibit it. Thus the full-field injectivity and the Boolean-domain collapse are compatible. The normalization must preserve Boolean relations rather than demand literal ideal membership in the selector ideal alone.

This control is not a proof that every selector tuple has a large affine part after Boolean reduction. The existing two-bit control gives \(1-(x+y)^{p-1}=(1-x)(1-y)\) on Boolean inputs for odd \(p\), whose quadratic term does not vanish. Coordinate selectors are therefore a falsification control, not a general solution.
