Controls for local choice and gluing

Proposition (multicast field floor). Fix \(t\ge2\) relays. A source holds two symbols over \(\mathbb F_q\), sends one scalar linear combination to each relay, and every pair of relays feeds a receiver that must recover both symbols. Such a scalar linear code exists exactly when \(t\le q+1\), although every receiver has an individual cut of size two.

Proof. Let \(v_i\in\mathbb F_q^2\) be the coefficient vector sent to relay \(i\). Every pair must be linearly independent, so all \(v_i\) are nonzero and represent distinct one-dimensional subspaces. There are \((q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1\) such subspaces. Conversely select representatives from any \(t\) of them; every receiver inverts its two-by-two coefficient matrix. The network has two edge-disjoint source-to-receiver paths and only two incoming receiver edges, proving the cut statement. \(\square\)

Thus four relays fail over \(\mathbb F_2\) but succeed over \(\mathbb F_4\). This does not refute network coding over a sufficiently large field or vector coding. It rejects transferring scalar solvability to binary complete source coefficients for free. For a surviving weight, a constant source coefficient must satisfy its weighted field equation; a nonbinary constant does not. Polynomial maps are a different, retained possibility, as the preceding source control demonstrates.

Proposition (cyclic join gap). For every \(r\ge3\), binary variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\) with pair constraints \(z_i+z_{i+1}=c_i\) (indices cyclic) and \(\sum_i c_i=1\) have nonempty local relations and equal projections on every shared coordinate, but no global satisfying tuple.

Proof. Each constraint relation has two tuples and projects onto both binary values at either coordinate. Two different edges of a simple cycle share at most one variable, proving the projection assertion. Summing all equations gives zero on the left and one on the right, ruling out a global tuple. \(\square\)

This is a classical constraint control, not a claim that these are actual source equations. A directed dependency DAG does not imply a join tree for its scopes: for example, one may list the scopes \(\{z_1,z_2\},\{z_2,z_3\},\{z_1,z_3\}\) in an acyclic computation order while their constraint hypergraph is cyclic. The recorded unsupported compiler-forest hypothesis cannot be supplied by the word “DAG.” Tree decompositions or faithful paid separators remain different questions.
