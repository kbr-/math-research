Affine pair division

Statement. In \(\mathbb F_2[z,y_1,\ldots,y_v]\), \(v\ge0\), let \[g=P_0+P_1,\qquad P_i=\prod_j\ell_{i,j},\] where every \(\ell_{i,j}\) is a supplied affine polynomial with binary coefficients, empty products are one, and zero factors are allowed. Let \(S\ge2\) count the gates and wires of these explicitly written affine forms and products, including repeated factors. For any integer \(k\ge0\), suppose \(B^k\mid g\), \(B=z^2+z\). The formal polynomial \(g/B^k\) has a division-free unbounded-fan-in binary arithmetic circuit of size \(O((S+1)^4)\) and depth at most twenty, independent of \(k\). This is a circuit-existence construction from the supplied factor lists and the divisibility premise, not a factorization theorem for arbitrary small circuits or a succinct-power model.

Preprocessing. If both products contain a zero factor, return the zero quotient immediately. If exactly one product contains a zero factor, the remaining product is nonzero; a nonzero binary affine factor has only scalar unit one. Remove exactly \(k\) occurrences of \(z\) and of \(z+1\) from that product; divisibility guarantees they occur, by unique factorization. If both summands are nonzero, cancel their common full affine factors by their multiplicities, obtaining \[P_0=P A,\quad P_1=P C,\quad \gcd(A,C)=1.\] All these objects remain products of the supplied affine factors. If \(A=C=1\), then \(g=0\) and the zero quotient is correct. Otherwise at least one product is nonconstant. Write \(q=2^s\) for the largest power of two dividing every nonconstant affine-factor multiplicity in both \(A\) and \(C\). It is finite and at most the number of supplied factors. Divide the multiplicities by \(q\) to write \(A=U^q,C=V^q\). Thus \(A+C=(U+V)^q\), with every repeated power paid by ordinary wires.

Why the reduced root order is zero or \(q\). It suffices first to examine \(z=0\). If just one of \(A(0),C(0)\) is zero, their sum has order zero. They cannot both be zero, since that would give the common factor \(z\). If both are nonzero but unequal, the order is again zero. Suppose therefore \(A(0)=C(0)\ne0\). Every nonconstant binary affine factor has the form \(u(y)\) or \(z+u(y)\), with \(u\) affine. A factor \(z\) is excluded in this case. Nonconstant \(u\)'s are irreducible in \(\mathbb F_2[y]\), so the two specialization products have exactly the same multiplicity of each such \(u\). Coprimality of \(A,C\) forces, for each \(u\), all those occurrences on one side to be \(u\) and all on the other side to be \(z+u\). If one side used both forms, the other side would share one of them. Factors \(z+1\) may occur on only one side; constant-one factors are ignored.

After division of all multiplicities by \(q\), the same description holds for \(U,V\), with at least one odd multiplicity. Their specialization products are equal: Frobenius is injective on the polynomial domain. In its fraction field the logarithmic derivative difference at zero is \[\frac{\partial_z U(0)}{U(0)}+\frac{\partial_z V(0)}{V(0)} =e+\sum_u\frac{m_u\bmod2}{u(y)},\] where \(e\) is the parity of the remaining \(z+1\) multiplicity. This rational function is nonzero. If a nonconstant \(u\) has odd multiplicity, multiply by \(u\) and reduce at its prime: its own residue is one, while all other terms have residue zero in the localization, since the distinct affine \(u\)'s are nonassociate. If no such \(u\) is odd, maximality of \(q\) forces \(e=1\). Thus \(\partial_z(U+V)(0)\ne0\), so \(U+V\) has root order exactly one, and \(A+C\) has root order exactly \(q\). Replacing \(z\) by \(z+1\) is a binary affine automorphism preserving full-factor multiplicities and coprimality. The identical argument gives root order zero or that same \(q\) at one. No rational logarithmic derivative is an output circuit gate; it is only the proof of this cancellation rule.

Divide only the primitive roots. Write \(P=D z^{r_0}(z+1)^{r_1}\), with neither root dividing \(D\). Set \(\epsilon_a=1\) if \(k>r_a\), and zero otherwise, for \(a=0,1\). Divisibility of \(g\) and the just-proved root orders give \[\epsilon_a=1\Longrightarrow U+V\text{ vanishes at }a, \qquad r_a-k+\epsilon_a q\ge0.\] Use at most one divided difference at zero and at most one at one to form the exact polynomial \[T=\frac{U+V}{z^{\epsilon_0}(z+1)^{\epsilon_1}}.\] Each operation is the already proved fixed-root circuit construction; removing the other root does not destroy the required vanishing, since the roots are distinct. Then the complete quotient is \[\frac{g}{B^k}=D\,z^{r_0-k+\epsilon_0q}(z+1)^{r_1-k+\epsilon_1q}T^q.\] All exponents are nonnegative. This formula includes the case where the common factor alone already supplies the divisor, and does not require root cancellation to occur at both points.

Circuit ledger. \(U+V\) has affine-input/product/sum depth at most three and size \(O(S+1)\). One fixed-root divided difference has quadratic size and depth at most twice the old depth plus one; two give size \(O((S+1)^4)\) and depth at most fifteen. No iteration indexed by \(k\) occurs. Form \(T^q\) with \(q\) ordinary wires into one product, and multiply by the remaining affine factors and root powers, whose total descriptions are \(O(S+1)\). Each exponent is at most \(r_a+q\le2S\); the Frobenius repetition has \(q\le S\). One or two additional product layers stay below depth twenty and preserve the fourth-power size bound. If \(g\ne0\), degree already forces \(2k\le\deg g\le S\); an arbitrarily large declared \(k\) in the zero case still gives the zero circuit. All constants remain zero and one. This proves the statement.

Nonvacuity. For every \(q=2^s\ge1\) and every \(v\ge1\), put \(R=\prod_{i=1}^v(z+y_i)\) and \[P_0=z^{2q}R,\quad P_1=z^qR,\quad g=B^qR.\] These are explicitly two products of binary affine factors, with a nonconstant pivot-dependent quotient \(R\). The factor and wire count is \(O(q+v)\); taking \(q\) growing with \(v\) gives unbounded multiplicities and variables at polynomial description size. This also is an actual invariant-probe dividend. Let \(F\) consist of \(z,1+z\) and all old Boolean generators, including the indexed \(B\), and let \(L=F\cdot Z\), \(H=R L^q\). The class is nonempty, with the unit certificate on the first two inputs. The probe has common polynomial value \(R\), uses \(q\) ordinary repeated wires, and its reciprocal evaluation is \(R\). The recorded denominator construction gives \(B^qR\), which the Frobenius identity writes as the supplied pair above at polynomial size and constant added depth. This is an easy inconsistent existence control, not a candidate hard family or a new restriction on the Frege goal.

Degenerate cases. Zero factors, identical summands and empty products were handled in preprocessing. At \(k=0\) the original sum itself is the quotient. With no other variables the same proof uses only the possible constant specialization \(u=1\). A single nonzero product admits direct factor removal. Without divisibility no formal quotient is asserted. Binary affineness is essential to the contact argument: higher-degree factors such as \(z^3+y\) and \(y\) can have primitive contact order three, and extension-field slopes need not satisfy the residue pairing used here. No general field or nonlinear-factor extension is claimed.

The existing obstruction and the next boundary

The field qualification can be tested explicitly. In \(\mathbb F_4\), with \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), the coprime products \((z+y)(\omega z+y)(\omega^2z+y)\) and \(y^3\) sum to \(z^3\). Expanding cancels the two middle coefficients because \(1+\omega+\omega^2=0\). Their full-factor multiplicities have common Frobenius power one, but the contact order is three. This concerns the contact rule outside its binary-coefficient hypothesis; it is not a claim that the quotient theorem is false over every extension.

The recorded probe \(H=L+(L+1)Z_b^n\prod_i(1+zy_i)\) has common value one and final dividend \(B^{n+1}\). Its high intermediate Taylor coefficient is hard, but after reciprocal substitution the \(L+1\) numerator is exactly zero. Removing that zero branch leaves a pure affine product, so the desired quotient is one without extracting the intermediate coefficient. This applies the existing control rather than rerunning it. The present theorem requests no generic growing Taylor operation on an arbitrary gate.

The two-summand contact rule cannot simply be retained for three summands. Let \(E=(z+y)(z+y+1)\), \(C=y(y+1)\), so \(E+C=B\). For every \(q=2^s\), \[E^{3q}+C^{3q}+(ECB)^q=B^{3q}.\] The cube identity \(a^3+b^3+ab(a+b)=(a+b)^3\), followed by Frobenius, proves it. All three terms are products of binary affine factors, their common gcd is one, and the largest common Frobenius power in their factor multiplicities is \(q\), while both root orders of their sum are \(3q\). Thus the pair's contact classification fails for this presentation. The quotient is still the constant one; regrouping via the displayed identity makes division cheap. This is a control against importing the pair proof into triples, not a lower bound or an optimized-division obstruction.

Primitive triple contact

Statement and proof. In \(\mathbb F_2[z,y]\), let \(E=(z+y)(z+y+1)\), \(C=y(y+1)\), \(B=z(z+1)\). For every \(M=2^t\), \(t\ge1\), \[g=E^{M+1}+C^{M+1}+BC^M=B^M E.\] Indeed \(E+C=B\), and Frobenius gives \(E^M=C^M+B^M\). Hence \[E^{M+1}+C^{M+1}+BC^M =E(C^M+B^M)+C^{M+1}+BC^M =B^M E+C^M(E+C+B)=B^M E.\] The three summands are products of binary affine factors. Their common gcd is one, since \(E,C\) are coprime. Their largest common Frobenius power in full-factor multiplicities is one: \(M+1\) is odd, and the third term has one copy each of \(z,z+1\). Nevertheless both root orders of \(g\) are exactly \(M\), since \(E(0,y)=E(1,y)=C(y)\ne0\). This all-parameter family refutes a uniform bound on primitive triple contact, and therefore the proposed tactic of global common-power deflation followed by a fixed number of elementary root divisions. It does not refute cheap division: \(g/B^M=E\) is explicit.

Structural reason. The third product can cancel a shared coefficient block across two other products even when the whole three-term expression has neither a common factor nor a common Frobenius power. In this family, regroup \(C^{M+1}+BC^M=C^M(C+B)=C^M E\), then factor \(E\) and use \(E^M+C^M=B^M\). This is a polynomial-size constant-depth regrouping with original \(M\) power wires. A larger sum needs an operation handling such partial cancellations; increasing the number of primitive-root divisions does not provide it. The example leaves other regroupings and direct quotient algorithms available.

Nonvacuity. The factor description is \(O(M+1)\) and \(M\to\infty\). Add unused old variables to obtain unbounded variable counts. With the same nonempty unit input class used above, the full-class invariant probe \(H=E L^M\) has common value \(E\), and its reciprocal dividend is \(B^M E\). The displayed triple is another polynomial-size constant-depth representation of that actual final dividend; no new divisor or field is introduced. Taking the number of old variables comparable to \(M\) keeps the entire description polynomial in the easy source encoding. This is a falsification control for the division tactic, not a hard formula. Degenerate cases. At \(t=0\) root order is one and no unbounded-contact conclusion follows; the statement uses \(t\ge1\). The formal polynomial \(C\) is nonzero even though its Boolean values vanish, so no formal root order is replaced by cube values.
