Transpose linear obstruction

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(m>n\ge1\), with independent original variables \(A:m\times n\), \(B:n\times m\), \(E=AB\), \(F_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+E_{ij}\), \(b_x=x^2+x\), and

\[P=\prod_{i,j}(1+F_{ij})=\prod_iE_{ii}\prod_{i\ne j}(1+E_{ij}).\]

Let \(\tau\) exchange \(A_{i\ell}\) and \(B_{\ell i}\), exchange their Boolean placeholders, and send the rank placeholder \(Y_{ij}\) to \(Y_{ji}\). This compatible involution sends \(E\) to \(E^{\mathsf T}\) and fixes \(P\). There is no formally \(\tau\)-invariant linear Boolean correction \(Q(X,Z)\) satisfying \(Q(X,0)=0\), \(Q(X,b)=P\). There is also no formally \(\tau\)-invariant Hilbert-like full certificate for the exact generator tuple \((F,b)\). These are existence obstructions to the added symmetry and linearity, without any circuit-size hypothesis.

Boolean-correction proof. Write \(Q=\sum_{i,\ell}(c_{i\ell}Z_{A_{i\ell}}+d_{i\ell}Z_{B_{\ell i}})\). Invariance implies \(d_{i\ell}=\tau c_{i\ell}\). Let \(\rho\) identify \(B_{\ell i}=A_{i\ell}\) and \(Z_{B_{\ell i}}=Z_{A_{i\ell}}\). Since \(\rho\tau=\rho\), each coefficient pair cancels, so \(\rho Q=0\) identically, and \(\rho Q(X,b)=0\). But \(\rho P\ne0\) formally. To verify that nonzeroness at every allowed parameter, set all but the first column of \(A\) to zero and write its first column as independent \(t_1,\ldots,t_m\). Then

\[\rho P\big|_{A_{i\ell}=0,\ \ell>1}=\left(\prod_i t_i\prod_{i\lt j}(1+t_it_j)\right)^2\ne0.\]

Each factor is a nonzero polynomial and the polynomial ring is a domain. This contradicts \(Q(X,b)=P\). No Boolean evaluation of \(P\), square-root extraction or determinant complexity is used.

Full-certificate proof. Write a Hilbert-like certificate as \(C=\sum_{ij}U_{ij}(X)Y_{ij}+\sum_x c_x(X)Z_x\). At the Boolean old point where every row of \(A\) and every column of \(B\) equals the first standard basis vector, \(E\) is the all-one matrix. This point is fixed by \(\tau\); every \(b_x\) and every diagonal \(F_{ii}\) is zero, and every off-diagonal \(F_{ij}\) is one. Invariance gives \(U_{ji}=\tau U_{ij}\), hence \(U_{ji}(a)=U_{ij}(a)\). Off-diagonal pairs cancel in characteristic two and all remaining generator values vanish. Thus \(C(a,F(a),b(a))=0\), contradicting the formal certificate identity one. The point does not satisfy all rank equations; it tests the certificate identity on an old input, which that identity must cover.

Degenerate cases. At \(n=1,m=2\), the fixed-locus polynomial is \(t_1^2t_2^2(1+t_1t_2)^2\), still nonzero formally, although zero on Boolean points. \(m\le n\) is excluded from the rank contradiction; no statement about a refutation of a satisfiable system is inferred. \(n=0\) is excluded: its diagonal equations are unit generators with immediate invariant linear certificates, so this exclusion matters. All row/column exchanges have two elements; there is no unpaired original Boolean placeholder. Additional unit inputs or other auxiliary generators are outside the exact full tuple and could remove the obstruction.

Nonvacuity. At every \(m=2n\), \(n\ge1\), the original family has \(4n^2\) old variables and a polynomial input encoding independent of the proposed proof-size exponent. The asymmetric complete formula of lem:tail-cofactor-correction gives a finite correction, and product telescoping gives a full Hilbert-like certificate. The proposition therefore excludes their invariant linear replacement, not certificate existence. Its fixed-locus and fixed-point witnesses above apply at these unbounded counts.

Orbit product certificate

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let a finite group \(G\) permute old variables and the complete generator/placeholder tuple compatibly. For any supplied general IPS certificate \(C(X,V)\), with \(C(X,0)=0\), \(C(X,f(X))=1\), define

\[N_G(C)=1+\prod_{g\in G}(1+gC).\]

Then \(N_G(C)\) is an invariant general IPS certificate. No odd-order hypothesis is needed. If \(C\) has gate/wire size \(S\), depth \(d\), formal total degree at most \(D\), and placeholder degree at most \(r\), the construction has size \(O(|G|(S+1))\), depth at most \(d+3\), total degree at most \(|G|D\), and placeholder degree at most \(|G|r\). Circuits use unbounded-fanin sums/products, binary constants and ordinary input permutations. For the involution \(\tau\), the expanded form

\[N_{\{1,\tau\}}(C)=C+\tau C+C\tau C\]

has size \(O(S+1)\), depth at most \(d+2\), and both degree ceilings at most twice the originals. An original Hilbert-like certificate yields placeholder degree at most two, not an invariant Hilbert-like certificate.

Proof. Compatibility ensures every \(gC\) still vanishes at zero placeholders and evaluates to one at the full generator tuple. The product of their complements is one at zero placeholders and zero on that tuple; adding one gives the required identities. The group permutes the factors under left multiplication, and multiplication is commutative, proving formal invariance. Copy the supplied circuit once for each group element and permute its leaves; add a complement gate per copy, one product gate and one final addition. This gives the displayed size/depth costs. Multiplication sums degree ceilings, so both degree bounds follow without assuming that cancellations reduce costs. For two factors, expand in characteristic two and use one product and one sum layer. This uses no determinant gate, field enlargement or division by group order. It cannot turn an unavailable small certificate into an available one. A growing group still pays its full orbit size. The degree ledger is for formal output polynomials; it gives no PC-degree guarantee or cancellation-based internal-degree reset.

Degenerate cases. The trivial group returns \(C\). A unit generator fixed by the action with certificate \(V_0\) is sent to \(1+(1+V_0)^{|G|}\), still valid; for the order-two group it is \(V_0^2\), so linearity need not survive even this case. With a satisfiable generator tuple no supplied certificate exists and the premise is absent; no satisfiable instance is refuted. Repeated generators and nonfaithful group actions keep validity and the explicit orbit cost. An empty finite group is not a group. \(D=r=0\) is incompatible with the two certificate identities over a nonzero field.

Nonvacuity. The supplied asymmetric rank certificate at every \(m>n\ge1\), described above, and the compatible transpose involution satisfy the hypotheses. At the fixed Boolean old point of the proposition, both transformed certificate values are one; the nonlinear product term is one, giving the output one instead of the zero trace. These witnesses exist at unbounded variable counts without a small-circuit assumption.

Actual pairing and its remaining cost

For the full tail correction \(Q\), direct linear tracing gives \((Q+\tau Q)(X,b)=P+P=0\). Multiplication gives \((Q\tau Q)(X,b)=P^2\), so it changes the residual and does not by itself correct \(P\). A concrete full invariant general certificate is nonetheless

\[C_{\rm pair}(X,Y,Z)=1+\left(\prod_{i,j}(1+Y_{ij})\right)^2+Q(X,Z)\tau Q(X,Z).\]

It is invariant, vanishes at all zero placeholders, and evaluates to \(1+P^2+P^2=1\). This is a direct alternative to first assembling a Hilbert-like certificate and then taking its orbit product. If the shared tail-minor circuits have size \(H\), depth \(e\), the previous ledger gives size \(O(H+m^4+m^2n+1)\), depth at most \(e+10\) for this full paired certificate. Its formal output degree is at most \(4m^2-2\): the linear Boolean correction has joint degree at most \(2m^2-1\), its paired product at most twice that, and the input-placeholder product squared has degree \(2m^2\). Boolean-placeholder degree is at most two; input-placeholder degree can grow to \(2m^2\). These are output costs, not PC transfer or internal minor degree bounds. The construction keeps every original generator and placeholder and requires no new variables.

Neither form reduces \(H\); it uses two transformed copies of the same complete correction. Applying lem:prime-field-residual-normalization afterward gives a linear Boolean correction with polynomial size/bounded-depth overhead but cannot promise \(\tau\)-invariance, since the proposition excludes that conclusion. The old odd-order average preserves Hilbert-like form because its trace of one is one; the order-two trace is zero. Nonlinear repair trades away linearity while retaining the original binary family, full formal identities, polynomial input budget and constant-factor/constant-depth transformation. Direct determinant restrictions still concern individual registers, and no symmetric target is adopted as an unproved necessary condition for general IPS hardness.
