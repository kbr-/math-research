The direct square-completion projection

No gate, wire, depth or actual joint degree increases; simplifying the projected circuit is optional. All original rank variables and generator labels are retained.

From rank refutations to counter refutations

Fix \(i,j\). Substitute \[\widetilde A=\begin{pmatrix}U\\ e_i^{\mathsf T}E\end{pmatrix},\qquad \widetilde B=\begin{pmatrix}V&Ee_j\end{pmatrix}.\] Direct matrix multiplication gives \[UE=HU,\quad EV=VH,\quad E^2=E+VHU.\] Consequently the full rectangular generator matrix becomes \[\widetilde A\widetilde B+I_{n+1}=\begin{pmatrix}H&HUe_j\\e_i^{\mathsf T}VH&G_{ij}+(VHU)_{ij}\end{pmatrix}.\tag{1}\] All identities are formal; no matrix inverse or satisfying-point assumption is used.

Map its matrix placeholders, in that same block order, to \[Y_{ab},\qquad\sum_kY_{ak}U_{kj},\qquad\sum_kV_{ik}Y_{kb},\qquad W+\sum_{a,b}V_{ia}Y_{ab}U_{bj}.\tag{2}\] Each is linear in the complete target placeholder list. Original entries of \(U,V\) use their own Boolean placeholders. For the added entries use \[K_{ab}=\sum_k\bigl(U_{kb}^2Z^V_{ak}+V_{ak}Z^U_{kb}\bigr),\qquad K_{ab}(U,V,b)=E_{ab}^2+E_{ab}.\tag{3}\] The last equality follows termwise from \((vu)^2+vu=u^2(v^2+v)+v(u^2+u)\), with cross terms cancelling in characteristic two. It covers every added-row/column Boolean field, including overlaps between them.

Under the target generator specialization, maps (2) give exactly (1), and the field maps give every original Boolean generator at its substituted old entry. Therefore grafting these maps into any supplied rank certificate gives its formal specialization one. With all target placeholders zero, every source placeholder map is zero, giving the other certificate identity. Placeholder linearity is preserved.

The old maps have degree at most two; every placeholder map has joint degree at most three, so the actual joint degree is at most \(3D\). Only the needed row/column of \(E\) and their field witnesses are computed, alongside the \(O(n^2)\) terms in (2). Their joint gate/wire size is \(O(n^2)\), maximum depth at most three. Sharing them and retaining every original circuit wire gives \(O(S+n^2)\) and the conservative \(d+4\) bound. This is one global coefficient-field-preserving substitution, not a reset of input degrees.

From counter refutations to inverse consequences

Let \(C_{ij}\) be any supplied Hilbert-like counter certificate. Its polynomial uniquely has the form \[C_{ij}=C^0_{ij}(U,V,Y,Z)+c_{ij}(U,V)W.\] Compute \(C^0\) by setting \(W=0\), and \(c\) by setting \(Y=Z=0,W=1\), using two copies of the circuit without a formal coefficient algorithm. At the full counter tuple, \[C^0_{ij}(H,b)+c_{ij}(1+E_{ij})=1.\] Multiplication by \(E_{ij}\), followed by (3), gives the complete consequence certificate \[D_{ij}=E_{ij}C^0_{ij}+c_{ij}K_{ij},\qquad D_{ij}(H,b)=E_{ij},\qquad D_{ij}(0,0)=0.\tag{4}\] It is linear in all retained \(H\) and Boolean placeholders. No counter generator is silently omitted: its coefficient \(c\) is precisely what multiplies the paid Frobenius witness.

Since \(\deg C^0\le D\), \(\deg c\le D-1\), \(\deg E_{ij}\le2\), and \(\deg K_{ij}\le3\) jointly, (4) has joint degree at most \(D+2\). Computing all \(E,K\) jointly costs \(O(n^3)\) wires and depth at most three; the two copies per counter use \(O(n^2S)\) size. This gives joint depth at most \(\max(d,3)+2\) for all consequences.
