Exact setup and the global evaluation sum

Let \(G\) be a connected simple graph on \(n=2^m-1\) vertices, with maximum degree \(\Delta\), and edge expansion \(|\delta S|\ge\varepsilon|S|\) for \(0<|S|\le n/2\). Let \(c\in F_3^V\) have total \(\sigma\ne0\). Keep the existing window \[\max(4,2\Delta)\le D<\varepsilon n/8.\] Choose \(k=2^r\), \(1\le r<m\), with \(2D/\varepsilon<k\), and put \(\lambda=\sigma/k\in F_3\). The choice \(k=2^{m-1}\) always meets this additional inequality. Work over \(F_4=F_2(\omega)\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), in the recorded Fourier and one-hot encodings. Write \(\nabla t\) for the edge potential difference and \(\operatorname{div}y\) for the vertex incidence map, so \(\langle\nabla t,y\rangle=\langle t,\operatorname{div}y\rangle\).

For each \(k\)-subset \(S\subseteq V\), let \[\mathcal A_S=\{y\in F_3^E:\operatorname{div}y=c-\lambda\mathbf1_S\}.\] Its right side has total zero, so this is a nonempty affine space with \(3^{|E|-n+1}\) points. Different \(S\)'s give disjoint spaces because \(\lambda\ne0\). Define a linear functional on all old polynomials by \[\mu(f)=\sum_{|S|=k}\sum_{y\in\mathcal A_S}f(y),\tag{1}\] where the sum is in \(F_4\), Fourier variables evaluate as \(U_e=\omega^{y_e},V_e=\omega^{-y_e}\), and one-hot variables as their \(0/1\) indicators. This is not a real expectation. Its finite support consists of assignments with exactly \(k\) wrong vertex charges, each discrepancy \(\lambda\).

All character moments in the required window

Theorem (defect slice design). The functional (1) is normalized and binary-valued on binary one-hot polynomials. For every edge character \(m_\alpha\) of support at most \(2D\), \[\mu(m_\alpha)= \begin{cases} 0,&\alpha\notin\operatorname{im}\nabla,\\ \omega^{c\cdot t},&\alpha=\nabla t,\quad |\operatorname{supp}t|<n/2, \end{cases}\tag{2}\] where the small-support potential in the second line exists uniquely. Restricted to old degree at most \(D\), \(\mu\) is the constant-class coordinate of the existing character reduction \(R_D\). In particular it annihilates \(J_D=\ker R_D\), and on the binary one-hot pullback it annihilates the existing \(J_{2,D}\). This is a representation of that old functional, not equality of its scalar kernel with \(J_D\).

Proof: normalization and characters of a flow fiber. The number \(\binom nk\) is odd by the binary-slice lemma. Each flow space has odd size, so \(\mu(1)=1\). Binary one-hot evaluations are binary, giving the stated coefficient descent directly. For a fixed \(S\), translate its affine space by its cycle-space kernel. A nontrivial character on that kernel has zero sum: translation by a vector with character value \(\omega\) or \(\omega^2\) multiplies the sum by that value different from one, forcing it to vanish. Orthogonality of incidence image and kernel, by finite-dimensional linear algebra over \(F_3\), says that this occurs precisely when \(\alpha\notin\operatorname{im}\nabla\). If \(\alpha=\nabla t\), its value is constant on the fiber, and \[\sum_{y\in\mathcal A_S}\omega^{\alpha\cdot y} =\omega^{t\cdot c-\lambda\sum_{v\in S}t_v},\] since its odd cardinality is one in \(F_4\).

Proof: small representatives. Suppose \(|\operatorname{supp}\alpha|\le2D\) and \(\alpha=\nabla t\). If all three level sets of \(t\) had size at most \(n/2\), expansion applied to them would give \[2|\operatorname{supp}\alpha|=\sum_{a\in F_3}|\delta(t^{-1}(a))|\ge\varepsilon n,\] contrary to \(4D<\varepsilon n\). Thus one level set has more than half the vertices. Subtract its value from \(t\), leaving a potential supported on fewer than \(n/2\) vertices. All boundary edges of that support lie in \(\operatorname{supp}\alpha\), so \[|\operatorname{supp}t|\le2D/\varepsilon<k.\] Connectedness makes potentials unique modulo constants, and two potentials of support less than \(n/2\) cannot differ by a nonzero constant, proving uniqueness.

Proof: sum over defects. On the defect indicators \(z_v=\mathbf1_{v\in S}\), the character factor is represented over \(F_4\) by \[\omega^{-\lambda\sum_vt_vz_v} =\prod_{v\in\operatorname{supp}t}\bigl(1+(\omega^{-\lambda t_v}-1)z_v\bigr). \] Its Boolean degree is below \(k\), so the binary-slice lemma makes its sum over all \(k\)-subsets equal to its value one at zero. This proves (2).

For a character of degree at most \(D\), a noncut lies outside the constant cut-code class. A cut with its small potential is identified by the old relation with \(\omega^{c\cdot t}\) times one. Thus (2) agrees with the constant-class coordinate of \(R_D\). Local Fourier reduction does not increase degree and agrees with evaluation; the conclusion follows for every polynomial through \(D\), including the affine one-hot pullback. All ordinary old generator multiples through that ceiling are annihilated because the recorded pseudoideal contains them and the degree-\(D\) PC closure. No new cofactor or original-source degree is reset. \(\square\)

The full moment matrix recovers the quotient

Corollary (charged slice pairing). The bilinear form \(B(f,g)=\mu(fg)\), for old polynomials of degree at most \(D\), has radical exactly \(J_D\) in Fourier coordinates. It induces a nondegenerate pairing on \(Q_D=V_D/J_D\). On binary one-hot coordinates it descends to a nondegenerate \(F_2\)-pairing on \(V_D/J_{2,D}\). Products in this assertion can have degree \(2D\); their moments come from the global sum (1), not from an assertion that the original pseudoideal is defined or proper at twice its ceiling.

Proof. Index a matrix by all locally reduced characters of support at most \(D\), and set \[M_{\alpha,\beta}=\mu(m_\alpha m_{-\beta}).\] By (2), an entry is zero unless \(\alpha-\beta\) is a cut. In a fixed cut-code class, choose a representative \(\alpha_0\) among the indices. Write \(\alpha-\alpha_0=\nabla t_\alpha\) with the small potential. The phase defining the old quotient coordinate is \(a_\alpha=\omega^{c\cdot t_\alpha}\). The difference \(t_\alpha-t_\beta\) has support less than \(n/2\), since each support is at most \(2D/\varepsilon\) and \(4D/\varepsilon<n/2\). It is therefore the unique small potential for \(\alpha-\beta\). Consequently \[M_{\alpha,\beta}=a_\alpha a_\beta^{-1}\] on that class. Every class block is an outer product of nonzero vectors and has rank one. Its left kernel is exactly the phase-adjusted zero-sum relation defining \(R_D\). Thus the full matrix's left kernel is \(J_D\) after local reduction, and its rank is \(\dim Q_D\). Inversion \(\beta\mapsto-\beta\) permutes the whole character index set, so the ordinary product matrix \(\mu(m_\alpha m_\beta)\) has the same left kernel. It is symmetric, hence its right kernel is also \(J_D\), proving well-definedness and nondegeneracy on the quotient.

The affine Fourier/one-hot maps induce the recorded filtered isomorphism. For binary one-hot representatives the entries \(\mu(fg)\) are binary. A binary matrix has the same rank over \(F_2\) and \(F_4\); together with the existing descent of \(J_D\), this proves the binary assertion. It does not imply nondegeneracy on every subspace or on every kernel of an error map. The general finite-family pairing theorem still has different hypotheses and conclusions. \(\square\)

This is a canonical charged-space instance, unlike the older generic pairing on a matching quotient, which may need a larger coefficient field. It gives an exact way to test an old quotient class through moment pairings. It supplies neither free low-degree adjoints nor multiplication closure beyond the recorded ceiling.
