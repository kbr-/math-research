The subfield-offset ceiling

Corollary (subfield offset ceiling). Let \(E\) be a finite field of characteristic two and \(K=\mathbb F_{2^k}\subsetneq E\), with \(k\ge1\). Let \(\beta\in E\setminus K\) and let \(g\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) be multilinear of degree at most \(r\), where \(r\ge1\). The system consisting of \(g-\beta=0\) and all Boolean equations \(x_i^2-x_i=0\) has an ordinary PC refutation over \(E\) through degree \(2r\). No bound on \(k\), the number of monomials, or \([E:K]\) is required.

Proof. Write \(g=\sum_S a_Sx_S\), including a possible constant term, and define coefficientwise Frobenius conjugates \[ f_j=\sum_S a_S^{2^j}x_S-\beta^{2^j},\qquad 0\le j\le k. \] Each has degree at most \(r\), and \(f_0\) is an input. From a derived final line \(f_j\), multiply separately by its monomials and take their scalar combination to derive \(f_j^2\). These ordinary PC steps have degree at most \(2r\); the earlier derivation of \(f_j\) is reused without multiplying its old lines. In characteristic two, \[ f_j^2-f_{j+1}=\sum_S a_S^{2^{j+1}}(x_S^2-x_S). \] For \(S=\{i_1,\ldots,i_t\}\), the literal identity \[ x_S^2-x_S=\sum_{h=1}^t(x_{i_h}^2-x_{i_h})\prod_{a<h}x_{i_a}^2\prod_{a>h}x_{i_a} \] has every summand of degree at most \(2t\le2r\). Thus the Boolean axioms derive the displayed difference within the same ceiling, and subtraction derives \(f_{j+1}\). Repeating does not add degree costs. Since all \(a_S\in K\), subtracting \(f_0\) from \(f_k\) gives the constant \(\beta-\beta^{2^k}\). It is nonzero: the roots of \(T^{2^k}-T\) in \(E\) are exactly the \(2^k\) elements of \(K\), by the polynomial root bound. Scalar division yields one. Constant \(g\) already gives a nonzero constant input; this also satisfies the bound. \(\square\)

This is the existing bounded Frobenius argument with multiplier one. Alternatively expand the coefficients in an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis of their span together with \(\beta\); the companion lemma recovers the constant coordinate. Its hypotheses allow an extension-valued input system, so the argument does not require the input itself to be defined over \(\mathbb F_2\). The proof above supplies the complete specialization and its ordinary-degree ledger. Finiteness of \(E\) is retained to match that lemma and the tested source, although the direct argument needs only the finite subfield; strictness follows from the outside-target assumption.

Source comparison and exact setting

Ben-Sasson–Impagliazzo's journal Theorem 5.1 bounds Gaussian width for bounded-width mod-2 systems; Corollary 5.4 gives a quarter-variable PC upper bound in odd proof characteristic. Its proof orders a minimally inconsistent family at random. The earlier Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi Theorem 6 instead provides the general Laurent-relation lower-bound interface. The source proof’s displayed small-subsystem and sampling bookkeeping is not used: in general the subsystem has at most \(v+1\) rows, and sampling a family of \(m\) rows to have mean size \(j\) would require probability \(j/m\).

Let \(q\) be prime, \(F\) a field with \(\operatorname{char}F\ne q\), and \(a_i\in\mathbb F_q^v\), \(b_i\in\mathbb F_q\). The modular equations \(a_i\cdot x=b_i\) are assumed inconsistent over \(\mathbb F_q^v\) itself. Each row has support at most \(w\), where \(w\ge1\). The polynomial input over \(F\) consists of all Boolean equations and, for each row, the falsity indicator of each Boolean assignment to its support violating the modular equation. The modular coefficients are evaluated in \(\mathbb F_q\), not reduced into the proof field. This is a literal CNF encoding with at most \(\sum_i2^{|\operatorname{supp}a_i|}\) row clauses.

The degree bound

Theorem (random modular ceiling). In the setting above, select an inconsistent subsystem of \(m\le v+1\) rows. Put \[ C=\min\left\{v,\left\lceil (1-1/q)v+w\sqrt{2m\log(4m+2)}\right\rceil\right\}. \] There is an ordinary PC refutation of the Boolean encoding over \(F\) of degree at most \[ \left\lceil C/2\right\rceil+w+1. \] In particular, for fixed \(q,w\) the degree is at most \((1/2-1/(2q))v+O_w(\sqrt{v\log v})\). A contradictory zero row instead gives the constant axiom immediately.

Small subsystem. Choose a basis of the row space of the coefficient matrix. The corresponding equations are consistent since those rows are independent. Since the full system is not, one additional equation is incompatible with them. This yields at most \(v+1\) rows and scalars \(\lambda_i\in\mathbb F_q\) with \[ \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i a_i=0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i b_i\ne0. \] Refuting this subsystem refutes the original input. No assumption \(m\le v\) is needed.

Random path. Draw independent uniform \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\in\mathbb F_q\). Beginning at the zero combination, set row coefficients successively to \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\). Then set them successively to \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\). This is a path of \(2m\) single-row changes from zero to an inconsistency witness. Its intermediate coefficient vectors are \[ (\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_j,0,\ldots,0),\qquad (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j,\rho_{j+1},\ldots,\rho_m). \] At a first-stage position, a coordinate of the resulting row sum is either zero or uniform in \(\mathbb F_q\). At a second-stage position it is uniform if some remaining random row has a nonzero coefficient there; otherwise it is zero, because the final row sum is zero. Primality of \(q\) makes every nonzero coefficient invertible. Thus at each of the \(2m+1\) states, the expected support is at most \((1-1/q)v\).

Changing one \(\rho_i\) alters the support size by at most \(w\). Exposing the independent coefficients gives a Doob martingale whose increments have absolute value at most \(w\). The bounded-difference estimate therefore gives \[ \Pr[W-\mathbb EW\ge t]\le \exp[-t^2/(2mw^2)]. \] For clarity, this estimate follows by applying the conditional bound \(\mathbb E e^{\theta Z}\le e^{\theta^2w^2/2}\) to each mean-zero increment \(Z\in[-w,w]\), multiplying these bounds, using Markov's inequality and minimizing over \(\theta=t/(mw^2)\). The conditional bound follows from convexity between the two endpoints and \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\). With \(t=w\sqrt{2m\log(4m+2)}\), a union bound over the states has failure probability at most one half. Hence some path has support at most \(C\) at every state. The cap by \(v\) is automatic. Reusing rows in this path is allowed in a proof.

Character realization. Adjoin a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\) in a finite algebraic extension \(E/F\). Define \[ u_j(t)=1+(\zeta^t-1)x_j,\qquad X(a)=\prod_j u_j(a_j). \] The literal identity \[ u_j(s)u_j(t)-u_j(s+t)=(\zeta^s-1)(\zeta^t-1)(x_j^2-x_j) \] gives multiplication and inverse reduction through degree two. For every scalar \(t\in\mathbb F_q\), \(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}\) is a scalar linear combination of the row's forbidden Boolean indicators through degree at most \(w\): multilinear interpolation assigns zero coefficient to every allowed tuple. This also covers \(t=0\), whose polynomial is zero.

For a current row sum \(a\) and charge \(b\), retain a derived relation \[ A-\zeta^bB, \] where \(A,B\) are character products on disjoint supports, their exponent difference is \(a\), and each support has size at most \(\lceil|\operatorname{supp}a|/2\rceil\). The zero starting relation is available by subtraction. When the path changes row \(i\) by scalar \(t\), derive \[ (A-\zeta^bB)X(ta_i)+\zeta^bB(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}) =AX(ta_i)-\zeta^{b+tb_i}B. \] Multiplication by an affine factor is simulated by variable multiplication and a scalar combination; multiplication by a product uses those steps in sequence. Both displayed summands fit degree \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w\). Repeated factors on a variable reduce by the literal identity above without increasing the current degree.

When both sides have a factor on the same variable, multiply the entire relation by the inverse of the second-side factor, and reduce. This removes that second-side factor and keeps on the first side only the exponent difference, possibly zero. Each operation increases temporary degree by at most one and neither reduced support increases. After removing common supports, their union is the support of the new row sum. If one side has at least two more factors, multiply by the inverse of a factor on that larger side and reduce; this transfers that factor, inverted, to the smaller side. The larger support does not increase. These operations balance the new relation while keeping all temporary degrees at most \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w+1\). Every Boolean correction is a multiple of the explicit degree-two identity by the other factors at the same ceiling. Thus these are PC inferences, not divisions or unbounded semantic consequences.

At the endpoint both character supports are empty and the charge is nonzero, giving \(1-\zeta^{\sum_i\lambda_i b_i}\ne0\). Scale to obtain one. Finally choose an \(F\)-basis of \(E\) containing one and take coefficient components of every proof line. Extension-scalar operations become \(F\)-linear combinations of components, and variable multiplication commutes with taking components. Since the original indicator and Boolean axioms lie in \(F[x]\), this yields the claimed refutation over \(F\) at the same degree. This descent does not assert a useful expanded proof-size bound. \(\square\)

Encoding and both degree directions

At a vertex \(a\), the compact encoding uses Boolean variables \(u_a,v_a\), the domain axiom \(u_av_a=0\), and three point indicators \[\chi_{a,0}=(1-u_a)(1-v_a),\quad\chi_{a,1}=u_a(1-v_a),\quad\chi_{a,2}=(1-u_a)v_a.\] For every edge \(ab\) and colour \(c\in\{0,1,2\}\), include \(\chi_{a,c}\chi_{b,c}=0\). These are precisely the polynomial translations of the proposed clauses, up to harmless nonzero signs. The CNF has \(2N\) occurring variables (each occurs in its domain clause), \(N+3|E|\) clauses and width at most four.

Write \[L_{a,0}=1-u_a-v_a,\qquad L_{a,1}=u_a,\qquad L_{a,2}=v_a.\] Their sum is literally one, and \(\chi_{a,c}-L_{a,c}=\epsilon_cu_av_a\), where \((\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)=(1,-1,-1)\). Thus \[\chi_{a,c}\chi_{b,c}-L_{a,c}L_{b,c} =\epsilon_cu_av_a\chi_{b,c}+\epsilon_cL_{a,c}u_bv_b.\tag{1}\] Each right-hand term is derivable from a domain axiom through degree at most four. Consequently all quadratic collision lines \(L_{a,c}L_{b,c}=0\) have ordinary PC derivations through degree four. Multiplication by any affine polynomial is implemented by separate variable multiplications and linear combination; it adds at most one degree to the current line. Earlier derivations are reused, not multiplied wholesale.

The standard polynomial system \(\operatorname{Col}(G,3)\) uses variables \(y_{a,c}\), row sums \(\sum_cy_{a,c}-1\), pair exclusions \(y_{a,c}y_{a,d}\) for \(c\ne d\), edge collisions \(y_{a,c}y_{b,c}\), and Booleanity. This is the system in Conneryd–de Rezende–Nordström–Pang–Risse, equations (2.2). Their Theorem 1.1 supplies linear lower bounds in the stated random-graph regimes over every field. Their Boolean reductions are free by convention; ours are charged. We use their encoding definition, not their lower-bound proof as a premise.

Lemma (compact colouring transfer). Over any field, an ordinary degree-\(D\) PC refutation of either of these two systems gives one of the other through degree \(\max\{D,4\}\).

Proof. In the standard-to-compact direction substitute \(y_{a,c}\mapsto L_{a,c}\). Row sums become zero. Put \(B_u=u^2-u\), \(B_v=v^2-v\), and \(Q=uv\) at one vertex. The remaining local images are \[L_0^2-L_0=B_u+B_v+2Q,\quad L_1^2-L_1=B_u,\quad L_2^2-L_2=B_v, \quad L_0L_1=-B_u-Q,\quad L_0L_2=-B_v-Q,\quad L_1L_2=Q.\] They follow through degree two in every characteristic. Edge images follow from (1) through four.

In the compact-to-standard direction substitute \(u_a\mapsto y_{a,1}\), \(v_a\mapsto y_{a,2}\). Booleanity and the compact domain axiom are standard axioms. Put \(R_a=\sum_cy_{a,c}-1\), \(Q_a=y_{a,1}y_{a,2}\), and write \(\widehat\chi\) for the substituted point indicators. Then \[\widehat\chi_{a,0}-y_{a,0}=-R_a+Q_a,\qquad \widehat\chi_{a,1}-y_{a,1}=\widehat\chi_{a,2}-y_{a,2}=-Q_a.\] For each edge, the identity \[\widehat\chi_{a,c}\widehat\chi_{b,c}-y_{a,c}y_{b,c} =(\widehat\chi_{a,c}-y_{a,c})\widehat\chi_{b,c} +y_{a,c}(\widehat\chi_{b,c}-y_{b,c})\] uses only row and pair axioms with products of degree at most four. The substituted edge clause therefore follows through four. In both directions replay every source line. An affine substitution never increases polynomial degree; a nonzero source multiplication has predecessor degree at most \(D-1\), so its affine-variable replay fits \(D\). Zero lines can be discarded. Insert the local axiom derivations once and reuse their final lines. This proves the maximum, without an additive cost per vertex. \(\square\)

This is the same affine replay mechanism already audited in the ternary Fourier transfer and the historical polynomial substitution lemma. The current local domain and clauses are different, and their certificates are given above. No new general substitution principle is claimed. The source's linear lower bound is compatible with the upper bound below; a positive unspecified linear coefficient is not our near-half target.

An independent set makes the remaining branching cheap

Theorem (compact colouring ceiling). Let \(G\) be a non-three-colourable simple graph on \(N\) vertices. Let \(H\) be any non-three-colourable subgraph with an independent set \(I\), and put \(S=V(H)\setminus I\). Its compact CNF has an ordinary PC refutation over every field through \[\max\{4,|S|+1\}.\tag{2}\] In particular, choose an inclusion-minimal such \(H\) on \(h\ge4\) vertices. Then the original formula has a refutation through \[h-\left\lceil\frac{h-1}{3}\right\rceil+1 \ \le\ \frac{2N}{3}+\frac43 =\frac v3+\frac43.\tag{3}\] The displayed integer bound is at least four for \(h\ge4\).

Proof. For every map \(\alpha:S\to\{0,1,2\}\), define the branch polynomial \[P_\alpha=\prod_{a\in S}L_{a,\alpha(a)}.\] It has degree at most \(|S|\), and their sum is literally \(\prod_{a\in S}\sum_c L_{a,c}=1\). We derive every \(P_\alpha=0\).

If \(\alpha\) gives the same colour \(c\) to two adjacent vertices \(a,b\in S\), multiply the already derived collision line \(L_{a,c}L_{b,c}\) by the branch factors at all other vertices of \(S\). This derives \(P_\alpha\) through degree \(|S|\), after the degree-four setup.

Otherwise \(\alpha\) is a proper colouring of \(H[S]\). Some vertex \(t\in I\) must have neighbours of all three colours in \(S\): if every vertex of \(I\) had an available colour, choosing one independently at each would extend \(\alpha\) to \(H\), since \(I\) is independent. This would contradict the hypothesis. For each \(c\), choose a neighbour \(a_c\in S\) with \(\alpha(a_c)=c\). The three neighbours are distinct. Multiply \(L_{t,c}L_{a_c,c}\) by all branch factors except the one at \(a_c\), obtaining \(L_{t,c}P_\alpha\) through degree at most \(|S|+1\). Sum the three lines. Their sum is exactly \(P_\alpha\), without Boolean reduction. Sum all branch lines to obtain one. This proves (2); it makes no polynomial proof-size assertion.

For the uniform bound, minimality makes \(H-z\) three-colourable for any \(z\in V(H)\). A largest colour class in such a colouring is independent in \(H\) and has at least \(\lceil(h-1)/3\rceil\) vertices. Use it as \(I\). Formula axioms of \(H\) are a subset of those of \(G\), so the same derivation refutes the original formula without assigning or charging any outside variables. Since \(h\le N\), (3) follows. \(\square\)

The proof is ordinary PC throughout. It does not use Booleanity after the local collision setup (which itself uses only the domain clauses), and the subgraph in (2) need not be induced; Booleanity is retained as part of the declared CNF encoding and is used in the two-way transfer. Its low ceiling uses the derived affine collision lines and their final-line reuse; multiplying their full degree-four derivations by all branch factors would give an unnecessary additive overhead. The affine partition idea has a prior analogue in whole-column conditioning; the independent-set extension and critical-subgraph argument here supply the graph-specific bound.

What the exclusion teaches

The user requested: “When you find an upper bound that rules out a candidate family, use this for learning: how it might apply to other cabdidates, whether they can be strengthened, or perhaps another stronger candidate could be proposed to replace the ruled out one.” Increasing expansion, density, or graph degree cannot rescue this three-colour encoding: (3) assumes none of their upper bounds. The structural issue is that three allowed states on two bits have affine indicators, while a critical graph supplies a large independent set. Both features are needed for this proof.

For the other queued candidates, Boolean states also have affine indicators, but the independent-extension argument does not transfer automatically. In a triple not-all-equal hypergraph, a colour class may contain two vertices of a triple while containing no full triple; assigning the third leaves a joint constraint on those two, so “independent” in that weak hypergraph sense does not permit separate extensions. A set meeting every constraint in at most one unassigned variable would permit a related conditioning test, but no sufficiently large such set has been proved for the proposed random family. The same missing structural guarantee applies to the majority generator and random three-CNF candidates. Their queued tests should include this possibility; no exclusion of those families follows here.

A proposed replacement is compact four-colourability: use all four states of two bits, omit the domain clause, and forbid equal endpoint states for each edge. On a graph without isolated vertices this has \(2N\) actual variables and \(4|E|\) width-four clauses. Seek sparse vertex-critical non-four-colourable graphs, using the critical core itself as the instance so that \(h=N\); this is a candidate selection requirement, not a hardness theorem. The four point indicators cannot all be affine: already the indicator of \(11\) is zero at \(00,10,01\) and one at \(11\), whereas every affine function satisfies \(f(11)=f(10)+f(01)-f(00)\). Thus the degree-one state-branching shortcut is unavailable over any field. The direct degree-two indicator version of (2) costs at most \(2(|S|+1)\), and the critical-subgraph argument only guarantees an independent set of size \(\lceil(h-1)/4\rceil\). The resulting bound is \(2(h-\lceil(h-1)/4\rceil+1)\), which is above the target \(N\) when \(h=N\), so this particular exclusion does not decide the replacement in that normalization. If the ambient graph has a much smaller critical core, this bound can instead fall below \(N\); merely requiring a growing core would not prevent that failure. Other low-degree representations or upper bounds may still rule it out; that is the queued test.
