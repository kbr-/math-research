Hamming ball unit rows
Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(\Gamma\) be an unsatisfiable Boolean old system in \(v\) variables, all generators of degree at most \(u\ge2\), containing the old Boolean equations. Fix \(1\le r\le v\), original accuracy \(h\ge r\), and an original source ceiling \(B\ge\max(u,2h^2+3h)\). For each of any finite number of parents, choose an invertible affine Boolean coordinate system \(y_a=A_ax+b_a\). Its input list consists of all genuine bottom products on the tuples \((1-y_{a,i})_{i\in T}\), \(|T|=r\). All bottom and parent blocks have accuracy \(h\), with complete companions and fields. Bottoms may be shared when their actual defining tuples agree; no raw coefficient readers or parent dependencies are allowed.
Theorem. There is one coherent binary polynomial assignment to all fresh coefficients, of maximum old degree \(T_0=u-1\), whose bottom companions are Boolean-zero and whose parent products belong to the old ideal through degree \(u+r-1\). All parent companions have old certificates through \(u+2r-1\), and fields through \(2T_0\). Thus if the old system has no PC refutation through \(T_0B\), this complete source has no degree-\(B\) PC or NS refutation. The map uses one active row per parent and never increases its original accuracy. The number of parents does not enter the degree bound. No polynomial-size representation of the coefficient maps is claimed.
Small-ball interpolation. In one coordinate system \(y\), let \(S_r=\{\mathbf1_U:|U|<r\}\). The squarefree monomials \(y_U\), \(|U|<r\), form a basis of functions on \(S_r\): their evaluation matrix indexed by subsets of size below \(r\), ordered by size, is the inclusion matrix \(\mathbf1[U\subseteq V]\), triangular with diagonal one. Equivalently the indicator of \(\mathbf1_U\) on this ball has the degree-at-most-\(r-1\) representative \[e_U(y)=y_U\sum_{\substack{V\subseteq[v]\setminus U\\ |V|\le r-1-|U|}}y_V.\tag{1}\] At a ball point containing \(U\), the sum is \((1+1)^{|W\setminus U|}\); the upper cutoff includes every subset of \(W\setminus U\). It is one exactly when \(W=U\). Points not containing \(U\) give zero.
Express the old generators in \(y\) using the affine inverse; their degrees stay at most \(u\). Every ball point violates some generator. Assign each point to one such generator \(G_j\), and sum its \(e_U\)'s to get \(q_j\) of degree at most \(r-1\). Then \[F=1+\sum_jq_jG_j\] vanishes on \(S_r\) and has degree at most \(u+r-1\). Boolean reduction does not increase degree. Its multilinear remainder has no monomial of degree below \(r\): successively evaluate it at points of increasing support below \(r\), or use the triangular matrix above. Assign to each remaining monomial one \(r\)-subset of its support. Factoring those monomials gives \[F=\sum_{|T|=r}c_Ty_T+R_{\rm bool},\qquad \deg c_T\le u-1,\tag{2}\] where \(R_{\rm bool}\) has an ordinary Boolean certificate through \(u+r-1\). Thus \[1+\sum_{|T|=r}c_Ty_T\in(\Gamma) \quad\hbox{through degree }u+r-1.\tag{3}\] Transport back to \(x\). Affine substitution preserves all these degrees; each \(y_i^2+y_i\) is a binary constant combination of the old \(x_j^2+x_j\), so its certificate is retained. This constructs actual bounded cofactors, not merely vanishing at field points.
Source maps and replay. Assign each bottom its literal \(r\) rows, then zero-pad. Its product is exactly \(y_T\), and its companions are Boolean-zero through \(r+1\). In a parent's first row assign the coefficient of that actual product to \(c_T(x)\), and set all later rows to zero. The product becomes \(1+\sum_Tc_Ty_T\), which has the old certificate (3). Multiply it by each mapped input \(y_T\) to certify every parent companion through \(u+2r-1\). Every coefficient image is a binary polynomial, so its field equation has a Boolean certificate through \(2(u-1)\). These constructions work independently for the different parent coordinate systems and use the same old ideal; no separately chosen surviving weights have to be reconciled.
All certificates fit \(T_0B\). Indeed \(B\ge2r+1\) and \[(u-1)(2r+1)-(u+2r-1)=2r(u-2)\ge0.\] Old generators fit as well, and \(B\ge2\) covers the field images. The global substitution has degree at most \(T_0\); a nonzero predecessor of an original variable-multiplication line has degree at most \(B-1\), so expanding multiplication by its image fits \(T_0B\). A refutation would give an old refutation there. Ordinary NS has its same-degree PC replay. Original bottom and parent ceilings remain \(2h+1\) and \(2h^2+3h\). The weight is one throughout.
Growing-family consequence. Take the previous power-growing family with \(\Delta=\lceil n^\alpha\rceil\), \(0<\alpha<1/2\), old width \(u=O(\Delta)\), and old proper cutoff \(D_0=\Theta(n\Delta)\). Use \(r=\lceil\log n\rceil\), \(h=\lceil n^\alpha\log n\rceil\), \(B=\lceil h^2\log n\rceil\). Then \[T_0B=O(n^{3\alpha}(\log n)^3)=o(n^{1+\alpha})=o(D_0).\] For any fixed \(A\), polynomially many parents in the original \(N\), with all their \(r\)-subset bottoms and arbitrary predetermined affine bases, fit a polynomial source inventory: each basis and parent adds \(N^{o(1)}\) description length. The family is fixed independently of \(A\). This removes the reviewer's proposed many-basis control by polynomial coefficient maps at its original accuracy. The constant-row-only version is not decided, but that restriction is not necessary for the lower-bound route. No hard old restriction is needed for this positive construction.
Ownership and scope. The small-ball interpolation is the same elementary inclusion-matrix mechanism used in the existing Hamming-ball records. The near-half notebook's full ideal relaxation ceiling already gives the PC bound from an interpolation-degree hypothesis. Here the explicit monomial division supplies original-axiom cofactors of degree \(u-1\), allowing an actual simultaneous ENS coefficient map. Neither a generic PC derivation nor a small zero-set cardinality alone supplies those cofactors. The low-degree map remains a supplied-source result, not coverage of arbitrary compiled inputs.
Nonvacuity. On the growing family take \(\alpha=1/4\) and any polynomial inventory of invertible affine bases. Each bottom has rank \(r\le h\), and the displayed original ceiling lies in the old hard range. Distinct bases can overlap globally; the construction does not require support disjointness or a common input across parents.
Degenerate cases. For \(r=1\), the ball is the origin and all interpolation cofactors \(q_j\) are constants; (2) still gives degree \(u-1\) coefficients. For \(r=v\), the ball omits only the all-one point and the same finite argument holds, although the hardness/replay premise may then fail. An empty parent inventory is old hardness alone. If an old generator is already one, the old-hardness premise fails. Without an invertible affine coordinate map the interpolation proof must be rechecked on its actual image; no such generalization is claimed.
