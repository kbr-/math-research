Residual orbit norm

Statement. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with old variables \(X=(x_1,\ldots,x_v)\), Boolean tuple \(b_j=x_j^2+x_j\), and matching placeholders \(Z_j\). Let a finite group \(G\) of order \(k\) permute the old variables and their placeholders compatibly, and let \(R(X)\) be invariant. Suppose \(Q(X,0)=0\), \(Q(X,b)=R\). For \(k\ge2\), set

\[D_R(X,Z)=R(X+Z)+R(X),\quad H_k(R)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}R^j,\] \[Q_G=\prod_{g\in G}gQ+D_RH_k(R).\]

Then \(Q_G\) is invariant, \(Q_G(X,0)=0\), and \(Q_G(X,b)=R\). The trivial group can use \(Q_G=Q\). For supplied binary division-free unbounded-fanin circuits of gate/wire sizes \(S,S_R\) and depths \(d,d_R\), the construction has size \(O(k(S+1)+S_R+k^2+v)\), depth at most \(\max(d+2,d_R+5)\). If \(Q\) has formal joint degree at most \(D\), placeholder degree at most \(r\), and \(R\) has degree at most \(e\), then joint degree is at most \(\max(kD,(k-1)e)\) and placeholder degree at most \(\max(kr,e)\). These are circuit/output bounds, not a PC-degree transfer.

Proof. Binary coefficients give \(R(X+b)=R(X^2)=R(X)^2\), hence \(D_R(X,b)=R^2+R\). Ordinary finite geometric telescoping gives \((R^2+R)H_k(R)=R^k+R\). Every transformed correction has value \(R\), so the product has value \(R^k\), which cancels that first term. Zero-placeholder specialization is zero in both summands. Group multiplication permutes the product factors; \(D_R\) and \(H_k(R)\) are invariant because \(R\) is and \(X+Z\) commutes with the action.

Copy the supplied correction once per group element. Compute \(R(X)\) and \(R(X+Z)\) with shared circuits and one added leaf-sum layer. Each power in \(H_k\) uses an ordinary product with its displayed number of wires; the total is \(O(k^2)\), with no succinct exponent gate. One product and one sum finish the construction, giving the conservative depth bound. Multiplication sums the stated degree ceilings. The second term has old/joint degree at most \((k-1)e\) but placeholder degree at most \(e\), since \(H_k(R)\) is placeholder-free. This proves all bounds.

Scope. This is the target-\(R\) correction analogue of lem:orbit-product-certificate, not an extension of odd-order linear averaging. It permits nonlinear placeholders and retains the orbit cost. For a cyclic row group of order polynomial in the input length, any small bounded-depth correction therefore has an invariant general correction with polynomial size and constant added depth. This supports cyclic symmetry as a costed search restriction for general correction circuits; it does not preserve Hilbert-like form, the matched simulation image or a PC cutoff. A full symmetric row group still pays its factorial order.

Cyclic placeholder degree

Statement. If \(M=2^a>N\ge1\), every Boolean correction \(Q(A,Z)\) satisfying \(Q(A,0)=0\), \(Q(A,b)=R(A)\) and invariant under the single cyclic permutation of all \(M\) rows has total Boolean-placeholder degree at least \(M\). The symmetric matching correction attains \(M\), so the bound is sharp. No circuit-size hypothesis is used.

Proof. Identify all old rows with one common formal row \(x\), but retain the separate placeholder rows. The resulting polynomial is still cyclically invariant, with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_2[x]\) fixed pointwise by that row action. Thus its placeholder monomials have equal coefficients on each orbit. Suppose its original placeholder degree is below \(M\), and then identify all placeholder rows with one common row \(z\). Its nonconstant placeholder monomials fall into orbits of the cyclic group of order \(M\); every orbit size is a power of two. A fixed monomial must have the same exponent pattern in every row and therefore positive total degree at least \(M\). Thus all monomials of positive degree below \(M\) have even orbit size, and each orbit cancels after the row identification. The zero-degree part vanishes because \(Q(A,0)=0\). Consequently the whole fixed-row specialization is zero.

Now set \(z=x^2+x\). The correction identity instead gives

\[\left(\sum_\ell x_\ell\right)^M\left(1+\sum_\ell x_\ell^2\right)^{\binom M2}\ne0.\]

It is nonzero in the formal polynomial domain; restricting to the first column gives \(x^M(1+x^2)^{\binom M2}\). This contradiction proves the lower bound. The preceding complete formula gives the upper bound. The fixed-row locus is formal, not a Boolean point where this residual would vanish.

What the bound does not say. An unbounded-fanin product of \(M\) placeholders already has constant depth and polynomial size. Placeholder degree \(M\) therefore proves no circuit-size lower bound. It applies to this invariant residual-correction model, not arbitrary full certificates with input placeholders. Indeed the same matching identity gives the invariant full certificate \(1+T(Y_{ij},1+Y_i)\) using no Boolean placeholders at all; it has the same unpaid explicit matching size. Residual normalization can produce asymmetric linear Boolean corrections, and the new orbit norm can then recover cyclic symmetry while losing linearity. The model distinctions are essential.
