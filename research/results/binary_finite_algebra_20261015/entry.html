Finite algebra evaluation

Statement. Let \(A\ne0\) be a fixed finite-dimensional associative unital algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\), of dimension \(r\ge1\), with a fixed binary coordinate basis. All modules below are unital left modules. The following are equivalent: (i) every simple \(A\)-module has dimension one over \(\mathbb F_2\); (ii) the coordinate functions of the ordered product of \(n\) arbitrary \(A\)-valued inputs have polynomial-size, fixed-depth \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\) circuits as \(n\to\infty\); (iii) coordinate evaluation of every size-\(L\), fixed-depth-\(h\) arithmetic formula over \(\mathbb F_2\), with ordered unbounded products and \(A\)-valued inputs, has size polynomial in \(L\) and depth bounded independently of \(L\). The circuit gates are unbounded AND, OR, NOT and MOD2; size counts gates and wires. In (ii), all \(rn\) input coordinates are free Boolean wires and all \(r\) output coordinates are computed. Constants and exponents may depend on fixed \(A\); no uniform-in-\(r\) assertion is made.

Under (i), a faithful fixed binary linear map \(\lambda:A\hookrightarrow\operatorname{UT}_r(\mathbb F_2)\) exists. For supplied leaf-coordinate circuits of total size \(G\) and depth \(a\), the formula evaluator can be chosen with size \[ O_A\!\left((L+r+2)^{r+4}+G\right) \] and depth at most \(a+2h+3\). If (i) fails, some odd prime \(p\), fixed by \(A\), has a constant-depth, linear-size reduction from residue zero modulo \(p\) to the product-coordinate problem. The reduction does not require a chosen lift in \(A\) to be a unit.

Image consequence. Assume (i), fix \(A\), and retain all hypotheses, generator labels and compiler conventions of cor:triangular-image-probe-values. Evaluate old variables and every clause, Boolean and commutator placeholder at \(A\)-valued functions of Boolean parameters, with total coordinate-entry circuit size \(G\) and depth \(a\). For \(E\) specified schemes and an outer \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\) probe of size \(U\), depth \(u_0\), suppose the same probe/schemes have equal Boolean values on the original and the order-\(r\) transformed image. With \(R=S+r+1\), that value has size \[ O_A\!\left(ER^{O(r)}+G+U\right) \] and depth at most \(a+u_0+34\), independently of the original non-input proof structure. The used input list, \(S\), fixed algebra/basis and compiler are construction data; the initial-segment polynomial is not assumed a complete certificate. This is a Boolean function conclusion, not formal extraction, exact resource-class closure or a theorem about special evaluations in the negative case.

Composition flag and the positive case

Every simple \(A\)-module is a quotient of the left regular module \(A\): any nonzero vector generates it. It is therefore finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb F_2\). The regular module has a composition series, for example by repeatedly choosing a maximal proper invariant subspace and inducting on dimension. Under (i), each simple quotient in that series is a binary line. Thus it is a full invariant flag of the \(r\)-dimensional binary vector space \(A\).

Choose a basis adapted to this flag. Left multiplication \(L_b:x\mapsto bx\) preserves every flag member, so its matrix is upper triangular for every \(b\in A\). The map \(\lambda(b)=L_b\) is a unital algebra homomorphism, binary linear and faithful: \(L_b(1)=b\). Its entries are fixed binary linear combinations of the supplied coordinates of \(b\). Coordinates of a product are recovered from its multiplication matrix by applying it to the fixed coordinate vector of \(1\), then undoing the basis change.

The complete triangular-path calculation in the previous proof evaluates all entries of a size-\(L\), depth-\(h\) formula at \(r\)-dimensional upper triangular inputs within \((L+r+2)^{r+4}\) gates/wires and twice the formula depth, plus its supplied leaf circuits. The fixed input and output linear maps add constant size per coordinate occurrence and at most two parity layers. Sharing supplied entry circuits gives the displayed \(O_A(\cdot)+G\) form and conservative depth allowance. This proves (i) implies (iii); (iii) implies (ii) by the single ordered product formula of size \(O(n)\), depth one. All basis choices depend on \(A\) alone, not on \(n\) or an alleged proof exponent.

A quotient with an odd-order unit

Suppose (i) fails and choose a simple module \(V\) with \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}V>1\). By Schur's argument, its endomorphism ring is a finite division ring: a nonzero module endomorphism has zero kernel and full image, hence is invertible. Let \(K=\operatorname{End}_A(V)^{\mathrm{op}}\), so \(V\) is a right \(K\)-space via its module endomorphisms. The density theorem, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.5, gives a unital binary-algebra surjection \[ \rho:A\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{End}_K(V)\cong\operatorname{Mat}_d(K), \qquad d=\dim_KV . \] This is the source's finite-dimensional density conclusion with the right-module/opposite-ring convention made explicit. Since \(V\) is finite over \(\mathbb F_2\), it is finite over \(K\). No algebraic-closure hypothesis or classification of finite division rings is used.

If \(d\ge2\), use the block diagonal unit with binary block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and identity on the remaining coordinates. It is nonidentity and its cube is the identity, so it has prime order three even if \(K\) is noncommutative. If \(d=1\), then \(K\ne\mathbb F_2\), because otherwise \(V\) would be a binary line. Write \(|K|=2^s\), \(s\ge2\). Its unit group has nontrivial odd size \(2^s-1\), and contains an element of odd prime order.

For completeness, the group fact needs no further algebra classification. If a prime \(p\) divides a finite group's order, the set of \(p\)-tuples with product one has cardinality \(|G|^{p-1}\), divisible by \(p\). Cyclic rotation preserves this set, even in a noncommutative group, and its orbits have size one or \(p\). Fixed tuples are \((g,\ldots,g)\) with \(g^p=1\). Their number is divisible by \(p\); since the identity tuple occurs, there is a nonidentity solution, of order \(p\). Apply this to an odd prime divisor of \(2^s-1\). Hence in either case a fixed quotient \(B\) of \(A\) has a fixed unit \(g\) of odd prime order \(p\).

The binary circuit obstruction

Choose any lift \(b\in A\) with \(\rho(b)=g\). For Boolean input bits \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), the coordinates of \[ b_i=(1-x_i)1+x_i b \] are affine binary functions. The quotient product is \[ \rho(b_1\cdots b_n)=g^{\sum_i x_i}. \] Thus it equals \(1\) precisely at residue zero modulo \(p\). The map \(\rho\) is fixed binary linear and equality to the fixed coordinate vector of \(1\) is a fixed conjunction of coordinate tests. If all product coordinates in \(A\) had polynomial-size fixed-depth parity circuits, these input maps, the fixed quotient map and equality test would give such circuits for that residue predicate. The lift \(b\) need not be invertible; the proof uses only its quotient image.

Smolensky, Algebraic Methods in the Theory of Lower Bounds for Boolean Circuit Complexity, Theorem 2, rules out polynomial-size fixed-depth circuits over AND, OR, NOT and MOD2 for modulus not a power of two, in particular every fixed odd prime. The primary scanned statement and its displayed proof were read at printed page 81 and checked visually; the gate definitions and abstract were checked as well. A final NOT reconciles the paper's nonzero-residue convention with our zero-residue detector. No OCR-dependent quantitative exponent is imported and no new proof of the underlying published circuit bound is claimed. This contradicts (ii) and proves the equivalence and negative reduction.

What the exact prefix does

Controls and limits

Nonvacuity. For any fixed \(r\ge2\), \(A=\mathbb F_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^r)\) satisfies (i): on a simple module the kernel of the central nilpotent \(\epsilon\) is nonzero and invariant, hence everything; the module is then simple over \(\mathbb F_2\). Ordered products have unbounded input length with fixed \(A\). A genuinely noncommutative positive example is the binary upper triangular two-by-two algebra. Its strictly upper triangular ideal has square zero, annihilates every simple module, and its diagonal quotient is \(\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2\), whose simple modules are binary lines. The negative cases include \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb F_2)\), both yielding order three. \(\mathbb F_8\) supplies an order-seven quotient control, showing why a MOD3-only source check would not suffice for the general theorem. These are checks of both sides of the all-algebra statement, not proposed hard formula families.

For the image consequence, contradictory nonempty units give a normalized proof at unbounded presentations with every original generator label retained. On any positive-case algebra, specialize placeholders to their actual generator values and take a coordinate functional sending \(1_A\) to one. Both images then give one. Arbitrary other costed placeholder schemes are covered by the theorem. This is nonempty invariance, not a hard target.

Degenerate cases. Empty products give \(1_A\), and a zero factor gives zero. The zero algebra and non-unital algebras are excluded; the faithful regular and density arguments use \(1\ne0\). A negative-case algebra still has easy restricted products, such as inputs all equal to one. A changing algebra or dimension is outside the fixed-\(A\) circuit classification. Noncommutativity alone is not the obstruction: the triangular positive example is noncommutative, whereas \(\mathbb F_4\) is commutative and negative. Nonbinary source constants, hidden hard entry functions and unproved two-image resource closure are not licensed.

Structural reason. A full binary composition flag makes every product coordinate a sum over boundedly many strict transitions. In its absence, a simple-module quotient exposes an odd-order residue computation using only affine input coordinates. This separates generic coordinate cost from the desired special-source operation. If a proposed evaluation ranges only in a smaller generated subalgebra, that actual subalgebra and restriction need their own analysis; the negative case does not prohibit a cheap source-sensitive evaluator. Conversely an efficient pointwise probe invariant under the specified transformation in a positive algebra has the proved cheap value. There is no assertion that every useful invariant has either this form or this invariance.
