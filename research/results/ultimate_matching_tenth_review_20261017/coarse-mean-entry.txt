Theorem (coarse-mean flow obstruction)
Statement. Fix an integer \(r\ge0\) and \(0<\theta<\min(1/1542,\kappa/6160)\). Let \(\Phi\) be a level-by-level field, local at level \(j_1-r\) (for every level-\((j_1-r)\) square \(T'\) and every \(j\ge j_1\), \(\Phi_j\) on \(T'\) is a function of \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\) and the points in \(T'\); at \(r=0\) this is the locality of the Setting), such that for every \(j_1\le j<J\) and every level-\((j-r)\) square \(P\), \[\int_P\Phi_{j,k}\,dx=\sum_{S\subseteq P,\ S\text{ of level }j}\frac{\rho_j}2\,\epsilon_S\,E_k(S),\] with signs \(\epsilon_S\in\{-1,1\}\) that are \(\mathcal F_j\)-measurable; the steps \(j<j_1\) are unrestricted beyond measurability. Then for every \(L>0\), \(\alpha\in(2,\infty]\) and \(M\), with probability tending to one, \(I_k\ge M\). At \(r=0\) this is thm:square-mean-flow-obstruction; for every \(r\) the condition holds when \(\Phi_j=\Phi^0_j+\Psi_j\) with \(\Phi^0_j\) a step meeting that theorem's mean condition on the level-\(j\) squares (the dyadic step among them) and \(\Psi_j\) any \(\mathcal F_{j+1}\)-measurable correction, local at level \(j_1-r\), with \(\int_P\Psi_{j,k}=0\) on every level-\((j-r)\) square, for instance one divergence-free on each level-\((j-r)\) square with zero normal flux on its boundary (piecewise \(C^1\) with continuous normal components), whatever it does to the means of the finer squares.
Proof. It is the proof of thm:square-mean-flow-obstruction run on squares \(r\) levels coarser; each step is written out where it changes. The push of a coarse mean. For a level-\((j-r)\) square \(P\), \(j\ge j_1\), let \(\xi_P=|P|^{-1}\int_P\Phi_{j,k}=X_P/(2\cdot4^r\rho_j)\) with \(X_P=\sum_{S\subseteq P}\epsilon_SE_k(S)=\sum_{z\in P}\epsilon_{S(z)}(1\{z\text{ in the lower half of }S(z)\}-\frac12)\), \(S(z)\) the level-\(j\) square of \(z\), as \(|P|=4^r\rho_j^2\). Given \(\mathcal F_j\) the counts \(n_S\) are fixed and the points of each \(S\) are i.i.d. uniform in \(S\), independent across the \(S\); each term is \(\pm\frac12\) with probability \(\frac12\) each, whatever \(\epsilon_{S(z)}\), so \(X_P\) is a sum of \(n_P\) i.i.d. terms of mean \(0\), variance \(\frac14\) and size \(\frac12\), standard deviation \(s=\sqrt{n_P}/2\). If \(P\) is good, \(n_P\ge N|P|/2\) and \(s\ge2^r\rho_j\sqrt{N/8}\). With \(\sigma_P\) an \(\mathcal F_j\)-measurable sign, lem:block-lower-tail at \(x=1\) on the terms times \(\sigma_P\) (it needs \(n_P\ge256\), true for large \(N\) since \(n_P\ge\kappa\log N\,4^{J-j+r}/2\)) gives \(P(\sigma_PX_P\ge s\mid\mathcal F_j)\ge p_0\), so \(P(\sigma_P\xi_P\ge h_r\mid\mathcal F_j)\ge p_0\) with \(h_r=2^{-r}\sqrt{N/32}\le s/(2\cdot4^r\rho_j)\). The walk and the chain. For a level-\((j-r)\) square \(P\) let \(U_j(P)=|P|^{-1}\int_PR_j\), \(R_j=\sum_{i<j}\Phi_{i,k}\), \(\mathcal F_j\)-measurable; for each of its four children \(P'\) (level \(j-r+1\), equal areas) put \(\delta_{P'}=U_{j+1}(P')-U_j(P)-\xi_P\), \(\mathcal F_{j+1}\)-measurable, with \(\sum_{P'}\delta_{P'}=0\) since the mean of \(R_{j+1}=R_j+\Phi_{j,k}\) over \(P\) is the average of its means over the children. With \(\sigma_P=\operatorname{sign}U_j(P)\) (\(+1\) at \(0\)) and \(P'_*\) the first child with \(\sigma_P\delta_{P'}\ge0\), \(|U_{j+1}(P'_*)|\ge|U_j(P)|+\sigma_P\xi_P\). The chain starts at the good level-\((j_1-r)\) squares all of whose ancestors are good, at step \(j=j_1\), and runs through steps \(j_1\le j<J\), keeping \(P'_*\) when \(\sigma_P\xi_P\ge h_r\) and \(P'_*\) is good; its squares at step \(j\) have level \(j-r\ge j_1-r\), so each lies inside one level-\((j_1-r)\) square \(T'\), and by induction \(|U_j(P)|\ge(j-j_1)h_r\). With \(m'=m\) steps, the area \(W\) of the chain's last squares (level \(J-r\)) has, as in the theorem, \(\mathbb E[W\mid\mathcal F_{j_1}]\ge(p_0/4)^{m'}W_{j_1}-Y\), \(Y\) the expected area of bad squares, \(\mathbb EY\le2N^{-\kappa/8}\) by the exceptional counts; \(\mu=(p_0/4)^{m'}\ge N^{-\theta\log_2(4/p_0)}\), so \(\mathbb E[W\mid\mathcal F_{j_1}]\ge\mu/4\) with probability tending to one when \(\theta\log_2(4/p_0)<\kappa/8\). Many squares. \(W=\sum_{T'}W(T')\) over the level-\((j_1-r)\) squares, each \(W(T')\) a function of \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\) and the points of \(T'\) (the chain inside \(T'\) uses \(\Phi_i\) on \(T'\), local at level \(j_1-r\), and \(\Phi_i\), \(i<j_1\), \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\)-measurable), conditionally independent given \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\) (the point sets of distinct level-\((j_1-r)\) squares are independent given their counts), with variance at most \(\sum_{T'}|T'|^2/4=4^{r-j_1}/4\); Chebyshev gives \(P(W<\mu/8\mid\mathcal F_{j_1})\le16\cdot4^rK\log N\,N^{2\theta-1+2\theta\log_2(4/p_0)}\to0\) for fixed \(r\). Cost. On a last square \(Q\) (level \(J-r\)), \(U_J(Q)\) is the mean of \(\Phi_k\) over \(Q\) and \(|U_J(Q)|\ge m'h_r\); by Jensen for the convex \(z\mapsto\psi_\alpha(4|z|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))\), \(I_k\ge W\psi_\alpha(u_N)\), \(u_N=4mh_r/(L\sqrt{N\log N})=2^{-r}m/(\sqrt2L\sqrt{\log N})\ge\frac{2^{-r}\theta}{\sqrt2L}\sqrt{\log N}\), as \(m\ge\theta\log_2N-1\ge\theta\ln N\) for large \(N\) (logarithms natural where not marked; thm:square-mean-flow-obstruction's proof writes \(m\ge\frac12\theta\log N\) for the same constant, a harmless slip not copied here). For finite \(\alpha>2\), \(u_N^\alpha\) grows like \((\log N)^{\alpha/2}\), faster than \(\theta\log_2(4/p_0)\log N\), so \(I_k\to\infty\); for \(\alpha=\infty\), \(u_N\ge2\) for large \(N\) and \(I_k\ge N^{1-\theta\log_2(4/p_0)}/8\to\infty\). The conditions on \(\theta\) are the theorem's (\(\log_2(4/p_0)=770\)); the factor \(4^r\) is a constant. The particular case: \(\int_P\Phi^0_{j,k}=\sum_{S\subseteq P}\int_S\Phi^0_{j,k}\) gives the sum, and a correction divergence-free on \(P\) with zero normal flux has \(\int_P\Psi_{j,k}=\int_{\partial P}x_k\Psi_j\cdot n-\int_Px_k\operatorname{div}\Psi_j=0\). \(\square\)
Corollary (lagged flow obstruction)
Statement. The lagged restoring flow of ex:lagged-restoring-tails, in its continuous form (the dyadic flow, and after the step of each level \(j\ge1\) the correction \(a\,(\text{main},\text{other})\) on each pair of sibling level-\(j\) squares inside their level-\(j-1\) parent, main \(=\pm2\,\mathrm{tent}\) on the two children in the exchanged coordinate and other \(=4C(y)S(x)\) in the other coordinate, then the same with the coordinates exchanged), with any amplitudes \(a\) that are \(\mathcal F_{j+1}\)-measurable and, for \(j\ge j_1\), functions of \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\) and the points in the level-\((j_1-1)\) square containing the pair (the script's damped Newton descent on the pair's quartic cost among them), has \(I_k\to\infty\) in probability for both \(k\), every \(L>0\) and every \(\alpha\in(2,\infty]\).
Proof. The theorem with \(r=1\). Count the correction after level \(j\) in step \(j\): it reads the counts of level \(j+1\) (its script's docstring: "After the step of level j >= 1 (which uses the counts of level j+1)"), so the step is \(\mathcal F_{j+1}\)-measurable; it is local at level \(j_1-1\): at step \(j\ge j_1\) the correction lives on a parent of level \(j-1\ge j_1-1\), inside one level-\((j_1-1)\) square \(T'\), and the descent reads the running field on its pair only, which on \(T'\) is a function of \(\mathcal F_{j_1}\) (the steps before \(j_1\)) and the points of \(T'\) (the steps from \(j_1\) on, by induction); at \(j=j_1\) the pair's two level-\(j_1\) squares are distinct, which is why the theorem's locality is taken at level \(j_1-r\) and not \(j_1\). Its mean on each level-\((j-1)\) parent \(P\) vanishes in both coordinates, for every amplitude: quoted from lagged_test.py's unit_shapes, "main = 2.0 * np.concatenate([np.ones(m), -np.ones(m)])[:, None] * T[None, :]" is \(+2T\) on one child and \(-2T\) on the other, summing to zero over the pair, and "other = 4.0 * c[:, None] * S[None, :]" with "S = np.where(u < 0.5, -2.0, 2.0)" sums to zero over each row of a child, \(S\) taking \(-2\) and \(+2\) on equal halves (the child side \(m=G\gg j\) is a power of two at least \(4\) for \(j<J\)); in the continuous form the same integrals vanish, \(\int(\pm2\,\mathrm{tent})=0\) over the pair and \(\int_0^1S=0\). The coordinate-\(2\) exchange is the same with the axes exchanged. The dyadic step meets thm:square-mean-flow-obstruction's mean condition on the level-\(j\) squares (that theorem's statement), so step \(j\) meets the theorem's condition at \(r=1\). \(\square\)
