Question. On near-half-family and the family statement, through its thin alternative: what ceiling does conditioning on the states of pairs give for an arbitrary unsatisfiable system with pair groups, and which such systems does it put below \(0.77N\)?

New mathematics. lem:pair-conditioning-ceiling: an unsatisfiable system on \(N\) pairs with axioms of degree at most \(d\), each in the variables of at most \(w\) pairs, is refuted over \(\mathbb F_2\) in degree at most \(\max\{d,w+1,\tau\}\), \(\tau\) the elimination depth of the graph that joins two pairs sharing an axiom; and \(\tau\le N(1-2/(\Delta+1))+\log_2N+1\) by an induced forest, \(\Delta\le r(w-1)\) the graph's maximum degree, when each pair is in at most \(r\) axioms.

General statement. lem:pair-conditioning-ceiling, for every system of its form.

The lemma

Setting. Boolean variables \(u_i,v_i\), \(i\in[N]\), over \(\mathbb F_2\), with the Boolean axioms and the pair axioms \(u_iv_i\). Further axioms \(f\), each a polynomial of degree at most \(d\) in the variables of a set \(T_f\) of at most \(w\) pairs. The system is unsatisfiable. The states of a pair are \(0=(0,0)\), \(1=(1,0)\), \(2=(0,1)\), with indicators \(\chi_{i,0}=1+u_i+v_i\), \(\chi_{i,1}=u_i\), \(\chi_{i,2}=v_i\): affine, summing to \(1\), and modulo the pair and Boolean axioms \(\chi_{i,c}\,p(u_i,v_i)\equiv\chi_{i,c}\,p(c)\) for every polynomial \(p\), by \(u\chi_0=u+u^2+uv\), \(v\chi_1=uv\), \(u\chi_1=u^2\) and their mirror images. The pair graph joins two pairs when some \(T_f\) contains both. For a graph \(G\) let \(\tau(G)\) be \(0\) if \(G\) is empty, the largest \(\tau\) of a component if \(G\) is disconnected, and \(1+\min_x\tau(G-x)\) if \(G\) is connected and not empty.

Lemma (pair conditioning ceiling). (1) For every set \(I\) of pairs, with \(s=N-|I|\) and \(\tau=\tau(G[I])\) for the pair graph induced on \(I\), \(1\in C_D\) for \(D=\max\{d,w+1,s+\tau\}\). Since removing a vertex lowers \(\tau\) by at most one, \(s+\tau(G[I])\ge\tau(G)\), with equality at \(I=[N]\): the statement is \(1\in C_D\) for \(D=\max\{d,w+1,\tau(G)\}\), and the sets \(I\) serve to bound \(\tau(G)\) from above. (2) If the pair graph has maximum degree \(\Delta\ge1\), which is at most \(r(w-1)\) when every pair lies in at most \(r\) sets \(T_f\), there is \(I\) with \(|I|\ge2N/(\Delta+1)\) inducing a forest, and \(\tau\le\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor+1\) for it; so \(1\in C_D\) for \(D=\max\{d,w+1,\lfloor N(1-2/(\Delta+1))\rfloor+\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor+1\}\).

Proof. (1) Reduce each \(f\), within degree \(d\), modulo the pair and Boolean axioms to a multilinear polynomial with at most one variable from each pair of \(T_f\); its degree is then at most \(|T_f|\le w\). For a set \(R\) of pairs and an assignment \(\rho\) of states to \(R\) let \(\chi_\rho=\prod_{i\in R}\chi_{i,\rho_i}\). Base. If an axiom \(f\) with \(T_f\subseteq R\) is false under \(\rho\), then \(\chi_\rho\in C_{\max\{d,w+1,|R|\}}\): multiply the reduced \(f\) by the indicators of the pairs of \(T_f\) one at a time, reducing after each; after \(k\) of them the polynomial is \(k\) indicators times \(f\) with \(k\) pairs substituted, of degree at most \(k+(|T_f|-k)\le w\), passing through degree at most \(w+1\); at the end it is the product of the \(|T_f|\) indicators, the value of \(f\) being \(1\); then multiply by the other indicators of \(R\), reaching degree \(|R|\). Induction. Claim: if \(R\supseteq[N]\setminus I\), \(\rho\) assigns \(R\), and \(H\) is a union of components of \(G[I\setminus R]\) such that the axioms \(f\) with \(T_f\subseteq R\cup V(H)\), restricted by \(\rho\), are unsatisfiable, then \(\chi_\rho\in C_{\max\{d,w+1,|R|+\tau(H)\}}\). By induction on the number of vertices of \(H\). The pairs of one \(T_f\) outside \(R\) are pairwise joined, so they lie in one component; hence the restricted axioms split over the components of \(H\) into systems on disjoint variables, together with those inside \(R\), and one of these is unsatisfiable. If it is one inside \(R\), an axiom is false under \(\rho\) and the base applies. Otherwise a component \(C\) of \(H\) has an unsatisfiable system; choose \(x\in C\) with \(\tau(C-x)=\tau(C)-1\). For each state \(c\), \(\rho'=\rho\cup\{x\mapsto c\}\) on \(R\cup\{x\}\) with \(H'=C-x\), a union of components of \(G[I\setminus(R\cup\{x\})]\), satisfies the hypothesis, the system of \(C\) with \(x\) fixed being unsatisfiable; so \(\chi_{\rho'}\in C_{\max\{d,w+1,|R|+1+\tau(C)-1\}}\), and \(\chi_\rho=\sum_c\chi_{\rho'}\) identically. Since \(\tau(C)\le\tau(H)\) the claim follows. Apply it with \(R=[N]\setminus I\), \(H=G[I]\) and every \(\rho\), the whole system being unsatisfiable under each; then \(1=\sum_\rho\chi_\rho\).

(2) Order the pairs uniformly at random and let \(I\) be the pairs with at most one neighbour earlier in the order. Each pair of \(I\) has at most one earlier neighbour in \(I\), so \(G[I]\) has no cycle, the latest vertex of a cycle having two earlier neighbours on it. A pair of degree \(\delta\) is in \(I\) with probability \(2/(\delta+1)\), being first or second among itself and its neighbours; so the expected size is at least \(2N/(\Delta+1)\) and some order attains it. For a tree on \(m\) vertices \(\tau\le\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor+1\): remove a vertex whose removal leaves components of at most \(m/2\) vertices, and induct. The pair graph has degree at most \(r(w-1)\). \(\square\)

The recorded colouring theorem is the case of edge axioms with \(I\) an independent set, \(\tau=1\), where part (1) gives its bound \(\max\{4,s+1\}\); its larger \(I\), a third of the vertices of a critical subgraph, is special to colourings. The induced forest by a random order is a known argument, which I reproduce and did not read in a source. For a positive form \(F^{\pm}\) the lemma gives, through lem:dual-rail-degree-collapse, the same ceiling for a formula of width \(w\) in which a variable occurs at most \(r\) times.

What it leaves of the bridge

Conditioning puts a pair system below \(0.77N\) exactly when the elimination depth of its pair graph is below it. The forest bound guarantees that, for large \(N\), when \(2/(\Delta+1)>0.23\), that is \(\Delta\le7\): for instance axioms on two pairs with each pair in at most seven, or on three pairs with each pair in at most three. With \(r=1\) the statement is vacuous, disjoint axioms being unsatisfiable only if one is. The forest is one upper bound on the depth; the reviewer recalls, unread, results on fragmenting graphs of bounded degree that might extend the guarantee to \(\Delta\) of about twelve, and recalls, also unread, that graphs of bounded degree with elimination depth above \(0.77N\) exist, random regular graphs of large degree. A system with pair groups that could be proper at \(0.77N\) therefore has a pair graph of elimination depth at least about \(0.77N\), uses three values to a pair, is not binomial, by the quarter ceiling as far as its recorded statement for block binomial systems goes, and is not a three-colouring. For such a system the record has no ceiling below \(N+1\), and its lower-bound methods give at most about a quarter of the variables, \(N/2\), as said in the last entry. So the bridge's last question is: a degree bound of \(0.77N\), about \(0.39\) of the variables, for a narrow system with pair groups whose pair graph has elimination depth above that. That is an instance of the standing obstacle of the route, a lower bound above the range of expansion for narrow axioms, on a frame of pairs.

For the check on shattering, which named pair groups with a parity contradiction: a system with pair groups shatters at most \(N\) variables by its frame, so its shattered sets are within about \(2K\) as soon as \(K\) is about \(N/2\); a binomial parity contradiction on pairs is refuted by about \(N/2\) plus lower-order terms, by prop:block-binomial-quarter-ceiling, so it meets the check's condition only at the edge of its ceiling, and one that is not binomial is the system described above. That check is a queue item of its own, not a sub-idea of the bridge, and stays queued with this reported against its named attempt; its own next entry decides what becomes of it.

Degenerate cases. \(I\) empty: \(\tau=0\) and the bound is \(\max\{d,w+1,N\}\), the frame's ceiling up to one. \(I\) everything with axioms on one pair each: \(s=0\), \(\tau=1\), bound \(\max\{d,2\}\). An axiom that is the constant \(1\): the base with \(R\) empty, degree zero. A satisfiable system: the claim's hypothesis fails for some \(\rho\), as it should. A pair in no axiom: an isolated vertex, in \(I\), harmless.

Nonvacuity. Three-colouring of the complete graph on four vertices in the pair encoding: \(N=4\), \(d=4\), \(w=2\); \(I\) one vertex gives \(s=3\), \(\tau=1\), bound \(4\), the recorded value; \(I\) two adjacent vertices gives \(s=2\), \(\tau=2\), bound \(4\) again.
