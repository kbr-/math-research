Make the local cancellation constructive

The source's non-zerodivisor proof starts with supplied polynomial cofactors \[ \ell g=\sum_{i=1}^m q_i f_i,\qquad \ell\notin\operatorname{radsp}(f_1,\ldots,f_m). \] Here each \(f_i\) is a multiplication term and the radical span means the span of all its linear factors, not the radical ideal. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), choose an invertible binary linear coordinate change \(\tau\) taking \(\ell\) to t and the radical span into coordinates different from t. Then \(f_i'=\tau(f_i)\) is independent of t and \[ t g'=\sum_i q_i'f_i'. \] Instead of requesting every t coefficient as in the source's membership proof, use the recorded polynomial first divided difference \[ \mathcal D_0 a=\frac{a(t)-a(0)}t. \] Linearity and t-independence of the \(f_i'\) give exactly \[ g'=\sum_i(\mathcal D_0q_i')f_i'. \] Thus a supplied cofactor circuit can be transformed without a generic coefficient table. The full existing product-telescoping proof was reread: one divided-difference pass has quadratic gate/wire cost and depth at most twice the incoming depth plus one. Apply it jointly to the supplied \(q_i'\). Charge the written linear forms when entering and leaving \(\tau\); no extension constants or division gates are required. This is an application of the existing operation, not a new independent lemma.

This repair pays one cancellation. For an unbounded list of cancelled factors, iterating that implementation repeats the size/depth recurrence; it supplies no polynomial-size fixed-depth bound uniform in that list. This is a limitation of the bound obtained, not a lower bound on the best implementation. Cancelling factors of a pure root-power generator with the stronger source Lemma 6.4 reduces its exponent, but still starts with arbitrary membership cofactors. When the target generator is just \(z^k\), that equivalence is \(zg\in(z^k)\) iff \(g\in(z^{k-1})\). It neither constructs the initially unknown quotient nor bounds its circuit from the dividend circuit alone.

Try the full ideal reconstruction, with polynomial z retained

The source's ideal CRT takes three supplied memberships, say \[ h=i_1+au=i_2+bf=i_3+cg,\qquad i_j\in I, \] with its precise radical-span separation among the multiplication terms u,f,g. Its proof first cancels every factor of f from the membership \(bf\in(I,u)\), obtains a membership \(h\in(I,uf)\), and then cancels every factor of g from \(cg\in(I,uf)\). The resulting membership in \((I,ufg)\) is valid. Replacing each cancellation by the explicit divided difference above does not control the number of factor cancellations or guarantee small circuits for the starting \(i_j,a,b,c\). Thus the full proof can transfer already supplied cofactors with iterated costs, but this attempt supplies no fixed-depth bound for the desired starting cofactors.

For the root divisor, take \(I=(z^k)\) in the polynomial ring rather than \(E(z)[x]\). The span of its factors is the line spanned by z. Pure root factors are inside that line, so the outside-span cancellation hypothesis cannot simply be applied to them. Factors outside it can be cancelled from existing memberships as above, with their original multiplicities paid. At the other root use the binary affine change \(z\mapsto z+1\); homogeneous source statements require corresponding homogenization and retain its variables/degrees. Nothing in this change produces a growing quotient circuit for free. Working over \(E(z)\) instead would make the desired ideal the whole ring, losing the polynomial divisibility premise.

The direct path theorem cannot start at the desired dividend \(C\in(z^k)\), because it requires \(C\notin I\). I also tried a boundary ideal \((z^{k+1})\) when the exact root order is k: the theorem can then detect a nonzero remainder by a path, but the resulting congruence is modulo that ideal plus path-node generators. Those additional generators need not be divisible by \(z^k\), and their coefficients are not supplied with circuit bounds. Dividing the terms of such a congruence separately is unjustified. The alternative \(I=0\) provides a nonzero path for a nonzero C but carries still less information about its root multiplicity. These adaptations preserve the valid source theorem and leave the constructive division step open; no failure of all possible path-based algorithms is claimed.

Test the coefficient-heavy implementation against a known obstruction

Requesting all coefficients of the intermediate cofactors would be a stronger, invalid generic requirement. The following is the recorded elementary-symmetric obstruction applied inside the cancellation setting. Let \(q=2^a\ge2\), \(r=2q-1\), \(k\ge1\), and \[ R(t,y)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t+y_j). \] Take the proper multiplication-term ideal \(I=(x^k)\), \(\ell=t\), \(g=x^kR\), and supply its one cofactor \(q_1=tR\). Then \[ t g=q_1x^k,\qquad [t^q]q_1=[t^{q-1}]R=e_q(y). \] The input identity, supplied cofactor and final cofactor R all have binary affine-product descriptions of size \(O(k+r)\) and fixed depth. On the Boolean y cube, \(e_q\) is majority on r inputs by cor:binary-component-depth-gap's full proof. Thus a generic polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth binary routine returning every requested growing-order intermediate coefficient is unavailable even on these cofactors. This is exactly that recorded majority mechanism, not a new Boolean lower bound. The existing first divided difference returns R cheaply here. Consequently this control rejects a coefficient-heavy implementation, not final cofactor construction or the general triple quotient question.
