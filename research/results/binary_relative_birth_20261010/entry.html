A complete affine source with conflicting leading presentations

Fix \(r\ge2\), \(n=2r\), \(R=n\), and \(D\ge n+3\). Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with only old Boolean equations through \(D\), weight one, and the degree-compatible graded-lexicographic order \[ x_1>x_2>\cdots>x_{2r}. \] Set \[ A_1=x_1,\quad B_1=x_2,\qquad A_i=x_1+x_{2i-1},\quad B_i=x_2+x_{2i}\quad(2\le i\le r). \] For each \(i\), introduce a fresh complete accuracy-one ENS block on the actual affine tuple \((A_i,B_i)\), including its two coefficient Boolean equations. Choose the constant row \((1,1)\). Its product is \(P_i=1+A_i+B_i\), and its two companions reduce to the same error: \[ A_iP_i\equiv A_iB_i,\qquad B_iP_i\equiv A_iB_i. \] The corrections are \(A_i^2+A_i\) and \(B_i^2+B_i\), Boolean certificates through degree two. The original block product and companion costs remain two and three. No empty row, unit input, or nonbinary coefficient is used.

Let \(\mathcal K_i\subseteq B_{\le n}\) be the actual kernel of multiplication by \(A_iB_i\), and let \(\mathcal K=\bigcap_i\mathcal K_i\). Form the ordinary squarefree leading ideals \(I_i\) and \(I_{\cap}\) from these spaces as in the relative-complex lemma. The old weight kernel is zero in this Boolean-only model.

Proposition (initial kernel intersection gap). For every \(i\), \[ I_i=(t_1,t_2),\qquad\text{but}\qquad I_{\cap}\subsetneq\bigcap_i I_i=(t_1,t_2). \tag{1} \] The actual common kernel has degree-counting polynomial \[ \sum_{k=0}^{2r}\bigl(\dim(\mathcal K\cap B_{\le k}) -\dim(\mathcal K\cap B_{\le k-1})\bigr)z^k=(2z+z^2)^r, \tag{2} \] where the negative-cutoff space is zero. Thus its first nonzero degree is \(r\), with dimension \(2^r\) there, while the naive intersection of individual leading ideals already contains \(t_1,t_2\) in degree one.

Proof of the individual leading ideals. Multiplication by \(A_iB_i\) vanishes on \(f\) precisely when \(f\) vanishes on the affine flat \(A_i=B_i=1\). Its Boolean ideal is generated by \(1+A_i,1+B_i\). For \(i=1\), eliminate \(x_1,x_2\) as constants. For \(i\ge2\), these same monic linear relations eliminate them as \(1+x_{2i-1},1+x_{2i}\), involving only lower-ordered variables. This substitution does not increase degree and decreases leading order. The remaining \(2r-2\) Boolean coordinates are free on that flat; their squarefree monomials are an independent quotient basis. Hence the only forbidden squarefree leading supports are those containing \(x_1\) or \(x_2\), proving \(I_i=(t_1,t_2)\). This is an exact common monomial order, not independently optimized orders for different blocks.

Proof of the joint degree count. The linear change \[ u_i=A_i,\quad v_i=B_i \] is invertible over \(\mathbb F_2\): recover \(x_1=u_1,x_2=v_1\), and \(x_{2i-1}=u_i+u_1,x_{2i}=v_i+v_1\) for \(i\ge2\). It and its inverse preserve the Boolean algebra and degree filtration. Now set \(y_i=1+u_i,z_i=1+v_i\). The condition \(u_iv_i f=0\) is restriction-zero at \(y_i=z_i=0\); in the squarefree basis it requires every monomial of \(f\) to meet the pair \(\{y_i,z_i\}\). For all \(i\), each pair contributes either \(y_i\), \(z_i\), or \(y_i z_i\). These monomials form a degree-compatible basis of the common kernel, proving (2), exactly the existing conjunction-cover mechanism in these simultaneous coordinates.

Every relation in \(\mathcal K\) belongs to every \(\mathcal K_i\), so their leading ideals give \(I_{\cap}\subseteq\bigcap I_i\). Equation (2) says no nonzero relation has degree below \(r\); a degree-compatible order therefore gives no leading monomial below \(r\). Since \(r\ge2\), \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) cannot belong to \(I_{\cap}\), proving strictness. A degree-\(r\) witness is \(\prod_i(1+A_i)\). All identities and multiplier comparisons fit the stated \(D\). \(\square\)

Test the allowed repairs before calling it an obstruction

There is a simultaneous affine-coordinate repair here: in the \(y_i,z_i\) coordinates just constructed, the individual leading ideals are \((y_i,z_i)\), and their intersection is their product. It is the exact joint kernel model, with the degree-\(r\) generator count retained. Thus the counterexample does not prove that no common coordinate model exists. It refutes discarding relation coefficients and composing the original local leading ideals as if initial formation commuted with intersection.

There is also an easier source normalization, already supplied by the historical exact-packing lemma. Instead of constants \((1,1)\), set the block coefficients to the old affine polynomials \[ (\beta_{i,1},\beta_{i,2})=(1+B_i,1). \] Then its product is \((1+A_i)(1+B_i)\), and both companions vanish by Booleanity through degree three. The nonconstant coefficient field equation is \(B_i^2+B_i\), certified through two; the other is zero. These are one coherent degree-one substitution for all blocks, with no new weight. It preserves original proof degrees rather than paying one factor per block. The exact-packing proof was reread; this is its rank-two, accuracy-one instance, not a new normalization theorem.

The Boolean base is satisfiable and the example does not assert a hard-quotient lower bound. Even within this model, the existence of the affine repair prevents interpreting the degree-\(r\) fixed-constant-prefix kernel as an obstruction to legal polynomial maps. A valid main-source theorem must consider those maps and their global maximum degree.
