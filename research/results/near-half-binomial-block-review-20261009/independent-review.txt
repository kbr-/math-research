Reviewer: one fresh-context medium-effort reviewer (Claude Code medium-reviewer agent).

A. Level selection instead of the binomial ratio: choose the level K per proof by pigeonhole (c monotone,
1 <= c(K) <= C(n,<=K); among K0, K0+d, ..., D at most log2 C(n,<=D)/log2 t steps have ratio below 1/t), with
contraction 1 - 1/(2t), t = 2^(O(d log n)). If tolerated, the family requirement drops to a proper closure at
polynomial or linear D. Suspicion stated: it would turn known linear F_2 degree bounds into the goal; expect a
hidden budget (rounds consuming degree, or K fixed before the proof). Test: redo the accounting.
B. Thin closure as near-half of an intrinsic dimension: by Kruskal-Katona a level with at most C(X,j)
monomials bounds all higher levels; thin and proper at D > K with X about 2K says the closure is proper
beyond X/2. Test: compute X_j on stored examples; try to prove D <= X_K/2 + O(w) in general, which would end
the thin endpoint.
C. Evidence against the approach: ceilings at most n/3 for every structured family; methods stop near n/4;
static count needs covering sum 1 + O(sw/n) while near-partitions fall in degree c*w; restriction loses at
most 1 per dimension, so near-half is hereditary on subspaces of small codimension. Would spend one cycle on
a general ceiling (1/2 - c) n for polynomially many rows.
More attainable endpoint: thin proper closure (logically weaker; free of the affine normalization), but by B
the same phenomenon in a different quotient.
