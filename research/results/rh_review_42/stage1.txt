STAGE 1 REPLY (medium reviewer, fresh context; recalled statements are marked by it as recalled and unread).

Is the fixed-theta statement attackable? No, not by anything known to it: a quasi-Riemann hypothesis in disguise.
- By item 2, the bound at one fixed theta gives delta <= c/theta for every off-line pair, a zero-free half-plane
  Re s > 1/2 + c/theta. No half-plane Re s > sigma_0 with sigma_0 < 1 is known (recalled, unread).
- By the explicit formula, the zero sum to height T at x up to T^theta is the error for primes in intervals
  (x, x + x/T]; the statement is square-root cancellation, up to T^eta, in the Selberg variance integral at
  interval length h = x^(1-1/theta) (recalled, unread).
- Known there: asymptotics from zero-density estimates (Huxley; Guth-Maynard), never a power saving; any power
  saving in psi(x) - x is equivalent to a zero-free strip (recalled, unread).
- Mean-value theorems give (T + N) sum |a_n|^2, and with N = T^theta > T the diagonal is the T^theta of item 2.
Correctness point on item 2: "nontrivial only for theta > 1" holds only if 2A <= (1+eta)/2; with the unconditional
count A about 1 the bound delta <= 2A/theta beats 1/2 only for theta > 4A. State which A is used.
A materially different architecture: none found that is not a restatement. Family averaging (item 4) is the only
non-circular mechanism listed, its diagonal controlled by the family size and not by T; the tests passed so far
could not have detected an error in the mean-square step; base no decision on it until audited; by the extraction
lemma it cannot reach 1/2. Turan with a localized count gives only conditional one-height exclusions.
Reason none exists within the Gram model: the model sees zeros only through the prime side at scales where the
prime information is equivalent to the zero-free region sought.
