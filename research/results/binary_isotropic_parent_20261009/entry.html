Alternating source completion

Lemma (alternating source completion). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), fix a common retained polynomial ring, a dictionary \(Z=(z_1,\ldots,z_k)^\mathsf T\) of actual polynomials, and finitely many parent tuples \(g_a=A_aZ\), where \(A_a\) is binary \(m\times k\). Tuples of shorter length may first be padded by zeros; the dictionary may include one for affine offsets. Each parent has its original positive accuracy \(h_a\), private coefficients, all companions and their Boolean fields. No new dictionary variables or axioms are introduced. Set \[ U=\begin{pmatrix}0_m\\Z\end{pmatrix},\quad V=0_{m+k},\quad M_a=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_a\\A_a^\mathsf T&0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1} \] Then \(M_a\) is binary alternating and \[ V+M_aU=(g_a,0_k)^\mathsf T,\qquad U^\mathsf TV=U^\mathsf TM_aU=0 \tag{2} \] as literal ordinary polynomial identities. Replacing each tuple by (2), with fresh coefficients for the zero positions, preserves ordinary PC and NS old-target proof degrees in both directions. It preserves existence of a coherent old-only degree-\(T\) coefficient map and an old weight of degree \(b\) satisfying the same paid weighted-generator conditions, and preserves every companion multiplication image after zero coordinates are discarded. All earlier source values and the degrees of existing products, companions and fields are retained; padding adds degree-two fields.

Proof: exact source identity. For one row with coefficients \((r,s)\), the new row value is \(r^\mathsf Tg_a+s^\mathsf T0=r^\mathsf Tg_a\). Thus the whole product is literally the old product, independent of every new \(s\). Existing companions are identical, new companions are zero, and only the independent equations \(s^2-s\) are added. The old proof is already a new proof. Conversely set all new \(s\) to zero in any new proof: its new axioms become zero, every old axiom stays unchanged, and substitution never raises polynomial degree. This works for NS cofactors and primitive PC inference alike. It also preserves downstream uses of the same whole product, if present, because that polynomial has not changed.

Extend any old-only coefficient map by assigning \(s=0\), or discard its values on \(s\) in the reverse direction. Weighted nonzero companions and old coefficient fields are identical; the extra fields vanish in the extension. There is no new map-degree, multiplier-degree or original replay charge. At any common quotient/domain window the tuple error map merely has zero extra coordinates, so its rank and kernel are unchanged. Equality for the full map including coefficient-field errors holds under the canonical extension \(s=0\). Arbitrary padded maps may have extra nonzero field errors; discarding those preserves satisfaction but does not assert equality of total error ranks. Equation (2) follows by block multiplication; no field extension or implicit quotient relation is used. \(\square\)

Nonzero coordinates do not remove the representation. Suppose \(k\ge1\) and every retained dictionary polynomial \(z_j\) is nonzero; identically zero dictionary positions may be deleted first. Let \(L\) be the binary \(m\times k\) matrix whose every row is \(e_1^\mathsf T\), and put \[ S=\begin{pmatrix}I_m&L\\0&I_k\end{pmatrix},\quad U'=SU=(z_1,\ldots,z_1,Z)^\mathsf T,\quad M'_a=S^{-\mathsf T}M_aS^{-1},\quad V'=0. \] Here \(S^{-1}=S\). Every coordinate of \(U'\) is nonzero, \(M'_a\) remains alternating by congruence, and \[ M'_aU'=S^{-\mathsf T}(g_a,0)^\mathsf T=(g_a,L^\mathsf Tg_a)^\mathsf T. \] The last coordinates are redundant constant combinations of the actual inputs, not new independent functions. A row coefficient vector changes by \(r'=Sr\); its row value and whole product stay identical. Boolean fields transform literally by \((\sum_iS_{ji}r_i)^2-\sum_iS_{ji}r_i=\sum_iS_{ji}(r_i^2-r_i)\). Companion vectors transform by the inverse-transpose matrix, so both generator families are constant combinations of each other.

Optional shear costs. Put \(E_* =\max_a\{2,h_a(\delta_a+1)+\delta_a\}\), with \(\delta_a\) the maximum actual input degree of a nonzero tuple, and ignore zero companions. At \(B\ge E_*\), the two PC transports fit \(B\): the generator-combination preludes fit \(E_*\), after which their final lines are reused. For ordinary NS, retain instead the safe reverse ceiling \(B+\max_a\delta_a\); do not infer same-degree cofactor transport from the PC prelude. Indeed every nonzero degree-\(\mu_i\) companion has original degree \(h_a(\delta_a+1)+\mu_i\), since coefficients are fresh. Replacing it by a constant combination of other companions costs at most \(\delta_a-\mu_i\le\delta_a\) extra after multiplying its original cofactor. Fields transform through degree two. The forward direction can reuse the unchanged first \(m\) companions. The exact zero-padding equivalence above incurs neither prelude nor excess. Thus nonzero coordinates of \(U'\) do not make the class intrinsically simpler, but an additional independence hypothesis would still need source justification.

What the reduction settles

One can take \(Z\) to be the actual reused earlier whole products and old affine coordinates, with one for offsets. Thus every family of the previously difficult arbitrary parent mixtures fits (1) simultaneously. More generally any finite polynomial input family has a common dictionary consisting of its listed inputs; this is only notation, not permission to add free variables for them. The padded arity is \(m+k\), the number of added fields is \(\sum_a h_ak\), and the construction is polynomial in the explicit input inventory. All fields remain in the source. It supplies neither a small-rank dictionary nor an efficient removal algorithm.

In particular, the branch \(wU^\mathsf TV\in J_D\) already contains this whole family for every \(w,J_D\), since its left side is identically zero. A general theorem solving that branch would solve the original parent-mixture problem. This does not refute such a theorem; it prevents the convenient alternating representation from being mistaken for additional structure. Nor does it contradict the preceding paired case, which supplied a nonzero surviving product. The weaker common-weight endpoint is preserved exactly, so this conclusion does not depend on demanding perfect fixed-weight prefixes.
