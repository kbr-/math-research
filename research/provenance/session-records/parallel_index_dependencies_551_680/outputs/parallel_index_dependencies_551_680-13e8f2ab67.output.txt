lean-finite-augmented-chain-api  Statement and conventions 
   For a finite vertex type \(V\), let \(K\) be a downward-closed family of finite subsets containing the empty set. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), write \(C(K,k)\) for coefficient functions on all subsets of \(V\) supported on \(k\)-element faces of \(K\). The cell count \(k\) corresponds to reduced simplicial degree \(k-1\). The ambient boundary is \((\partial c)(T)=\sum_{v\notin T}c(T\cup\{v\})\). It is linear, maps \(C(K,k+1)\) into \(C(K,k)\), and vanishes on \(C(K,0)\). In particular, on a vertex chain its empty-face coordinate is the sum of the vertex coefficients, including when \(V\) is empty. 
    Proof.  Each boundary coordinate is a finite sum of coordinate projections, hence preserves addition and scalar multiplication. If \(T\) is not a face, every superset \(T\cup \{v\}\) is not a face by downward closure. If \(|T|\ne k\) and \(v\) is outside \(T\), then \(|T\cup \{v\}|=|T|+1\ne k+1\). In either case every summand vanishes for a supported input. For a chain supported at cell count zero, every insertion outside \(T\) has positive cardinality and therefore contributes zero. These arguments establish all stated endpoint cases without treating the empty face as a vertex. 
   Restriction to the \(k\)-element faces and extension by zero are inverse linear maps: on a face they leave the coefficient unchanged; off that set the support condition forces zero. Thus \(C(K,k)\) agrees exactly with the usual finite coefficient space on \(k\)-faces. Taking \(K\) to be sets of cells with injective row and column projections gives the chessboard complex: injectivity is inherited by subsets, and both projections are vacuously injective on the empty set. Reordering the three face conditions identifies its exact-face coefficient functions with the existing ChessboardChain interface.

### 636 third-party:augmented-chain-naturality
lean-augmented-chain-naturality  Statements and complete human-readable proofs 
   Use the supported augmented coefficient spaces from  H01 . Let \(e:V\longrightarrow W\) be an injective map of finite vertex types. For an ambient coefficient function \(c\), define \(e_*c(T)=c(e^{-1}T)\) when every vertex of \(T\) belongs to the image of \(e\), and zero otherwise. This map is linear, injective, and commutes with the augmented boundary. If \(e\) sends every face of \(K\) to a face of \(L\), it restricts to a linear chain map \(C(K,k)\longrightarrow C(L,k)\) in every cell count \(k\). For an inclusion of face families on the same vertex type, the chain map is simply the unchanged coefficient function. A bijective relabeling has the inverse induced by the inverse vertex map. 
    Proof of naturality.  If \(T\) contains a vertex outside the image of \(e\), then every \(T\cup \{w\}\) does too, so both \(\partial (e_*c)(T)\) and \(e_*(\partial c)(T)\) are zero. Otherwise restrict the boundary sum to \(w\) in the image of \(e\): all other terms are zero by definition. Write \(w=e(v)\), uniquely. The condition \(w\notin T\) becomes \(v\notin e^{-1}T\), and the preimage of \(T\cup \{e(v)\}\) is \(e^{-1}T\cup \{v\}\). Thus the remaining sum is exactly \((\partial c)(e^{-1}T)\). This proves naturality including augmentation. Linearity follows coordinatewise from the definition. Also \(e_*c(e(S))=c(S)\), proving injectivity. If a potentially nonzero target coefficient is at \(T\), then \(T=e(e^{-1}T)\); its preimage must be a face of \(K\) of cell count \(k\), so the target is a face of \(L\) of the same count. This proves preservation of support. The inclusion identity and its commutation with the boundary are immediate. For a bijection, the induced map evaluates at the inverse image of \(T\); applying the inverse bijection restores every coefficient, proving the two-sided inverse. 
    Chessboard transpose.  The cell swap \((i,j)\mapsto (j,i)\) exchanges the two injective-projection conditions defining a matching. Thus it sends every face of the \(s\times N\) chessboard complex to a face of the \(N\times s\) complex. Apply the preceding chain-map construction; applying the swap twice is the identity. Consequently the resulting transpose map preserves cell count, commutes with the boundary, and is invertible. Generic injective maps and face-preservation witnesses likewise permit deleted rows or column complements to be embedded and relabeled without changing incidence.

Indeed a nonzero insertion term has \(S=T\cup \{v\}\), with \(v\) outside \(T\) and \(S\) a face; conversely \(|S|=|T|+1\) and \(T\subseteq S\) give a unique such vertex \(v\). Reindexing this bijection proves equality of the two finite sums. Applying the checked chessboard face equivalence therefore identifies the supported differential with the original  chessboardBoundary . At cell count zero the original outgoing boundary and the supported outgoing boundary are both zero. Hence for every \(k\), the boundary of the supported chain corresponding to \(c\) is exactly the supported chain corresponding to its original boundary. In particular, the original boundary vanishes if and only if the ambient supported boundary vanishes. This last implication uses injectivity of the chain-space equivalence, not merely equality of dimensions.

### 637 third-party:chessboard-parameter-arithmetic
chessboard-parameter-arithmetic  Claim: chessboard parameter arithmetic 
   For natural numbers \(a,b\), let \(\nu(a,b)=\min\{a,b,\lfloor(a+b+1)/3\rfloor\}\). This parameter is symmetric, satisfies \(\nu\le a,b\) and \(3\nu\le a+b+1\), and is positive when both sides are positive. A zero side gives \(\nu=0\); a one-row board with positive column count has \(\nu=1\). For \(2\le a\le b\), 
   \[
    \nu(a,b)\le b-1.
  \] 
   If also \(2\le t&lt;b\), put \(\mu=\nu(a-1,b-t)\). Then 
   \[
    \nu(a,b)+1\le\mu+t,
    \qquad (\nu(a,b)-2)-t+1\le\mu-2,
  \] 
   where the second formula is an integer inequality. For \(1\le a\le b\) and every natural \(t\), both \(\min(a-1,b-t)\) and \(\min(b-t,a-1)\) are strictly below \(\min(a,b)\), with natural truncated subtraction for board sizes. Finally \(2s-1\le N\) implies \(\nu(s,N)=s\); the Lean version uses natural subtraction and also holds for \(s=0\). 
    Full arithmetic proof.  Symmetry follows by exchanging the first two minimum entries and commuting addition. The three bounds are immediate from the minimum and the defining floor inequality. If \(a,b\ge1\), then all three entries are at least one; if one side is zero their minimum is zero. When \(a=1\) and \(b\ge1\), the minimum is therefore exactly one. 
   For the ordered bound, if \(a&lt;b\) then \(\nu\le a\le b-1\). If \(a=b\ge2\), then \(2b+1&lt;3b\), so \(\lfloor(2b+1)/3\rfloor\le b-1\). For the intersection inequality let \(r=\nu-t+1\), regarded as an integer. If \(r\le0\), the nonnegativity of \(\mu\) suffices. Otherwise, \(\nu\le a\) and \(t\ge2\) give \(a-1\ge r\), while \(\nu\le b-1\) gives \(b-t\ge r\). The numerator of the third minimum entry is exactly \(a+b-t\), because \(a\ge2\) and \(t&lt;b\). Moreover 
   \[
    a+b-t\ge3\nu-t-1\ge3\nu-3t+3=3r,
  \] 
   the second inequality being equivalent to \(2t\ge4\). Hence its floor after division by three is at least \(r\), and so is their minimum \(\mu\). Rearranging gives the additive natural inequality and the stated integer-degree inequality. Either orientation of the intersection has smaller side at most \(a-1&lt;a=\min(a,b)\), proving the induction decrease. For the stable range, \(s=0\) is immediate. For \(s\ge1\), the assumption gives \(N\ge s\) and \(s+N+1\ge3s\); thus all entries of the minimum defining \(\nu(s,N)\) are at least \(s\), and its first entry equals \(s\). This proves every parameter assertion. 
    Reduced-degree boundaries.  If \(k\) counts cells, its reduced simplicial degree is the integer \(k-1\). For natural \(k,n\), the interval condition \(-1\le k-1\le n-2\) is equivalent to \(k&lt;n\), simply by adding one and using integrality. For an integer \(q&lt;-1\), no natural \(k\) satisfies \(k-1\le q\), since \(k-1\ge-1\). In particular \(n=0\) requests no degree, and \(n=1\) requests only \(k=0\), the augmentation coordinate. These are arithmetic facts, not assertions that an empty-face-only complex is acyclic. The restriction \(t&lt;b\) in the intersection theorem is the nonempty-board case; \(t=b\) is deliberately excluded from the inductive homology application.

Its thirteen theorem declarations cover the complete statement above. The  full verification report  preserves their explicit types and axiom reports alongside the existing claim audit; the  pre-proof scope map  and  reproduction record  describe dependencies and setup. No assumption of \(H\) or custom axiom is used.

Reviewing the signed expression before coding avoided silently replacing \(\nu-t+1\) by truncated natural subtraction followed by one. ### 638 lem:augmented-cone-contraction
lean-augmented-cone-contraction  Statement and full proof 
   Let \(K\) be a downward-closed family of finite subsets of a finite vertex set \(V\), containing the empty face. Then every supported \(k\)-cell cycle \(c\), for every \(k\ge 0\), has a supported \((k+1)\)-cell filler. This includes the empty-face augmentation \(k=0\). A complex containing only the empty face has no cone vertex and is not covered by the hypothesis. 
    Construction.  On every ambient coefficient function define \(h(c)(S)=c(S\setminus \{v\})\) if \(v\in S\), and zero otherwise. We prove the full identity \(\partial h(c)+h(\partial c)=c\), without a cycle or support assumption. If \(v\notin S\), the only potentially nonzero insertion term of \(\partial h(c)(S)\) inserts \(v\), and gives \(c(S)\); \(h(\partial c)(S)=0\). If \(v\in S\), the insertion terms of \(\partial c(S\setminus \{v\})\) split into the \(v\) term \(c(S)\), and terms inserting a vertex \(w\) outside \(S\). Each latter term is exactly \(h(c)(S\cup \{w\})\), so \(\partial c(S\setminus \{v\})=c(S)+\partial h(c)(S)\). These repeated terms cancel over \(\mathbb F_2\), proving the identity at \(S\).

Thus \(S=(S\setminus \{v\})\cup \{v\}\) is a face by the cone hypothesis and has \(k+1\) cells. Hence \(h\) preserves the required shifted support. For \(\partial c=0\) the contraction identity gives \(\partial h(c)=c\). This proves exactness in every cell count, and in particular for a full simplex with a chosen vertex. ### 639 third-party:chessboard-star-cover
lean-chessboard-star-cover  Statement and proof 
 For a downward-closed finite complex \(K\) containing the empty face and a vertex \(v\), the closed star consists of faces \(S\) for which \(S\cup \{v\}\) is a face of \(K\). It is a subcomplex: \(S\subseteq S\cup \{v\}\), and adjoining \(v\) preserves inclusions. It is a cone with apex \(v\), because adjoining \(v\) twice gives the same face. These facts include the empty face. 
 On an \((a+1)\)-by-\(b\) chessboard with \(a+1\le b\), let \(L_j\) be the closed star of \((0,j)\). Every nonattacking matching \(M\) is in a star. If \(M\) contains \((0,j)\), then adjoining that cell changes nothing. Otherwise its row projection avoids 0. Injectivity of row projection gives \(|M|\le a\), while column injectivity gives exactly \(|M|\) occupied columns. Since \(a\lt b\), choose an unused column \(j\). Adjoining \((0,j)\) creates neither a repeated row nor a repeated column, so \(M\) belongs to \(L_j\). Thus the first-row stars cover the whole complex. This also proves the one-row case; the later nerve description requires at least two rows. 
 The shared cover interface records intersections by requiring membership in every selected complex. Its nerve contains an index face precisely when it is empty or the corresponding intersection has a vertex. An intersection with only the empty face is therefore excluded as a nonempty nerve face. The empty index face is retained separately. Covering asserts that every face of \(K\) belongs to some member; subcomplex inclusion remains a separate hypothesis, preventing an implicit strengthening of the later cover lemma.

### 642 lem:finite-cover-double-complex
lean-finite-cover-double-complex  Statements and full human-readable proof 
   Let \(K\) and \(L_i\) be finite augmented complexes on a finite vertex type \(V\), with finite cover-index type \(I\). Use the  shared intersection, nerve, and cover definitions . A double coefficient \(d(J,S)\) has bidegree \((a,b)\) when it is zero unless \(|J|=a\), \(|S|=b\), \(S\in K\), \(S\in L_i\) for all \(i\in J\), and \(J\) belongs to the nerve. Nerve membership means \(J\) is empty or its intersection has a vertex. Thus the \(a=0\) edge consists of \(K\) chains, while the \(b=0\) edge consists of nerve chains. These augmented coordinates retain precisely the nonempty-intersection convention needed later; an empty-face-only intersection contributes no nonempty nerve face. 
   Define \(H\) by the insertion boundary in \(J\) and \(V\) by the insertion boundary in \(S\). Both are linear; \(H\) lowers \(a\) by one and \(V\) lowers \(b\) by one, with zero outgoing maps when their count is zero. Each squares to zero by  H02 . They commute: at \((J,S)\), both HVd and VHd sum \(d(J\cup \{i\},S\cup \{v\})\) over \(i\notin J\) and \(v\notin S\); interchange the two finite sums. Hence \((H+V)^2=H^2+HV+VH+V^2=0\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). This is a finite coefficient model of the total differential; the later direct chain chase needs no spectral-sequence convergence theorem. 
    Support proof.  Removing a cover index preserves common-face membership and nerve membership by downward closure of the nerve. Removing a vertex preserves membership in \(K\) and every \(L_i\), while \(J\)'s nerve membership remains unchanged. Each insertion term therefore can be nonzero only in the claimed lower bidegree. At count zero an insertion would have positive count, so every term vanishes. Support is closed under addition coordinatewise. These facts justify the differential identities on supported spaces, rather than merely on ambient functions.

Since \(b\gt 0\), choose \(v\in S\); downward closure places \(\{v\}\) in every such \(L_i\). Thus \(J\) really belongs to the vertex-nonempty nerve. Consequently \(u\) has bidegree \((a+1,b)\), proving exactness of each positive-vertex-degree augmented horizontal row.

### 643 third-party:chessboard-star-intersections
lean-chessboard-star-intersections  Exact intersection statement and proof 
 Let \(J\) contain at least two distinct columns of an \((a+1)\)-by-\(b\) board. The intersection of their first-row closed stars consists exactly of matchings avoiding row 0 and every column of \(J\). Choose distinct \(j,k\) in \(J\). A cell \(x\) in row 0 in a common face would equal \((0,j)\), by row injectivity in its extension by \((0,j)\), and also equal \((0,k)\), a contradiction. If \(x\) uses column \(j\) in \(J\), column injectivity in the extension by \((0,j)\) forces \(x=(0,j)\), contradicting the absence of row 0. Membership in one star implies the original face is a matching. Conversely, a matching avoiding that row and those columns can be extended by each \((0,j)\) without a row or column collision, so belongs to every selected star. 
 Enumerate the complement of \(J\) by a bijection with \(b-|J|\) columns. Map the smaller board cell \((r,c)\) to \((r+1,e(c))\), where \(e\) is this enumeration. Both coordinates are injective, so matchings map to matchings. Every common face pulls back by predecessor in its nonzero row and inverse complement enumeration. This gives an exact face correspondence with the a-by-\((b-|J|)\) chessboard, including \(a=0\) or \(|J|=b\). If \(|J|=b\), no cells remain and the common complex has exactly the empty face, not a vertex.

### 646 third-party:homological-cover-lemma
lean-homological-cover-lemma  Exact statement and source correspondence 
   Let \(K\) be a finite augmented complex, and let \((L_i)_{i\in I}\) be finitely many subcomplexes covering \(K\). Let \(N\) be their vertex-nonempty nerve. Fix \(n\ge 0\). Suppose  AcyclicThrough N n , and for every nonempty \(J\in N\) and every \(r\) with \(|J|+r\le n\), every \(r\)-cell cycle in the intersection \(L_j\) admits an \((r+1)\)-cell filler there. Then  AcyclicThrough K n . With \(n=q+2\) this is the paper's homological cover lemma: an intersection of \(t\) members is \((q-t+1)\)-acyclic, and \(q\)-acyclicity of the nerve implies \(q\)-acyclicity of \(K\). Indeed an \(r\)-cell chain has reduced degree \(r-1\), so \(r-1\le q-t+1\) is exactly \(t+r\le n\). Empty-face-only intersections are absent from \(N\) and are not assumed exact. At \(t=n\), only augmentation exactness \(r=0\) is required, not connectivity. 


### 647 third-party:chessboard-homological-bound
lean-chessboard-homological-bound  Claim and conventions 
   For natural \(a,b\ge1\), put \(\nu(a,b)=\min\{a,b,\lfloor(a+b+1)/3\rfloor\}\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), for every \(0\le k&lt;\nu(a,b)\), each augmented \(k\)-cell cycle of \(\Delta_{a,b}\) is the boundary of a \((k+1)\)-cell chain. Equivalently, the reduced homology vanishes in degrees \(-1\) through \(\nu(a,b)-2\). Cell count zero is the empty-face augmentation coordinate. In Lean this is  AcyclicThrough (chessboardComplex a b) (chessboardNu a b) , with the two explicit positive-size hypotheses. 
    Augmentation base case.  In any finite complex with a vertex \(v\), a chain supported on zero-cell faces vanishes away from the empty set. Its cone at \(v\) is therefore supported only on the singleton \(\{v\}\), which is a face. The  cone identity  gives \(\partial \operatorname{cone}(v,c)+\operatorname{cone}(v,\partial c)=c\). For a cycle the second term is zero, so this cone fills \(c\). This argument does not require the entire complex to be a cone. A one-row board with at least one column has a vertex and \(\nu(1,b)=1\), so this proves its complete required range. 
    Transpose transport.  Swapping the two coordinates preserves both matching conditions and induces inverse chain maps, commuting with the augmented boundary by  H03 . Pull a cycle to the transposed board, fill it there, and push the filler back. Support and cell count are preserved, and boundary naturality and the inverse identity give the original cycle. Thus exactness transfers in either direction; \(\nu\) is symmetric. 
    Inductive assembly.  Use strong induction on \(\min(a,b)\). It suffices to handle the ordered board \(a\le b\), since the preceding transpose transport treats the other orientation without invoking induction at the same measure. The one-row case was proved above. For \(a\ge2\), take the  first-row closed stars . They are subcomplexes and cover the board. Their  nerve  is the boundary of the simplex on \(b\) columns. Since \(\nu(a,b)\le b-1\), H05/H10 supply exactness of this nerve in every cell count below \(\nu(a,b)\). 
   It remains to check the exact hypotheses of the  homological cover lemma . Let \(J\) be a nonempty nerve face, with \(t=|J|\), and let \(r\ge0\) satisfy \(t+r\le\nu(a,b)\). If \(t=1\), the intersection is a single star and the cone theorem fills every \(r\)-cycle. If \(t\ge2\), nerve membership gives \(t&lt;b\); the full star family has no common vertex. The  intersection equivalence  identifies the augmented chains with those of \(\Delta_{a-1,b-t}\), preserving the differential and transporting actual fillings. Both dimensions are positive and its smaller dimension is at most \(a-1&lt;\min(a,b)\), so induction applies. 
   Write \(\mu=\nu(a-1,b-t)\). The  checked arithmetic inequality  \(\nu(a,b)+1\le\mu+t\), together with \(t+r\le\nu(a,b)\), gives \(r&lt;\mu\). The induction hypothesis therefore fills exactly the required intersection cycles. Every hypothesis of the cover lemma is satisfied; it gives exactness of the whole board for every \(k&lt;\nu(a,b)\), completing the induction. The verified cover lemma includes the augmentation-only case, so this proof needs a separate base argument only for one-row boards; boards with \(\nu=1\) and at least two rows are covered by the same argument. This is an equivalent organization of the appendix proof, using its recorded finite-chain alternative for the cover step.
