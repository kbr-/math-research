Binary polynomial multiplicity degree

Determine, or bound tightly, the minimum degree \(D(n,k)\) of a polynomial \(P\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with Hasse multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) and at most \(k-1\) at the origin, below the threshold \(k\ge2^{n-2}\) of the published question: is the multiplicity bound \(2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) attained there, and if not, what is the true value?

Origin: starting research record.

Starting point

The thread starts from the binary multiplicity note (audit:binary-multiplicity-cover-question, preprint draft in publications/binary-polynomial-multiplicity/): the multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel lemma of Dvir, Kopparty, Saraf and Sudan gives \(D(n,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) for all positive \(n,k\), answering the bound asked in Question 4.3 of Bishnoi, Boyadzhiyska, Das and Mészáros (CPC 2023). In correspondence with an author of that paper (24 September 2026, paraphrased), the question's threshold was explained as the range where hyperplane covers attain this value, and two directions were suggested: decide whether the algebraic bound is tight for smaller \(k\), where extremal polynomials need not be products of linear forms, or improve it; and consult the open questions of Sauermann and Wigderson, who study the algebraic problem.

Where we stand

The goal is proved and verified in Lean. \(D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\) for \(k<2^{n-1}\), the co-author's range (formula theorem). With BBDM's hyperplane covers, \(D(n,k)=2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) for \(k\ge2^{n-1}\). So \(D\) is known for all \(n,k\ge1\), and the Schwartz–Zippel bound is attained exactly when \(k\ge2^{n-2}\) (complete formula; an earlier contrary claim was corrected).

  • Lower half: the multiplicity bound at \(n=m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\) plus the one-dimension step.
  • Upper half: Menezes' Catalan truncation \(y_1^\ell g_{k-\ell}\) (arXiv 2609.19009), with a telescoping proof in every dimension (per-order construction).
  • Prior art: Menezes proves the per-order value for \(n\ge k-1\). Our part is \(m(k)\le n<k-1\), the elementary lower bound and the characteristic-two proof (prior art).

Per-order values: solved in every dimension. The least degree with origin order exactly \(\ell\) is

\[ \delta(n,k,\ell)=n+2\ell+\sum_{j<n}\bigl\lfloor(k-\ell-1)/2^j\bigr\rfloor \]

for all \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\) (theorem). For \(n\ge m(k)\) it equals \(n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)\), Menezes' formula below his range. Its minimum over \(\ell\) is \(D(n,k)\).

  • The upper bound is \(y_1^\ell F^{2q}g_{r+1}\), where \(k-\ell-1=q2^n+r\) and \(F\) is the hyperplane product (construction).
  • The lower bound reads the degree off \(y\)-coefficients of the jet (lemma). Low degree forces multi-additive forms to vanish on the span \(V\) of \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\), and reduction modulo the subspace polynomial of \(V\) isolates the jet's order-\(\ell\) part.
  • The same law holds in base \(q\) on every full grid \(\mathbb F_q^n\), for every prime power \(q\) (\(q\)-ary theorem).
  • These results are not formalized in Lean. Whether they go into the preprint is the user's decision.

Working mathematical context

Conventions. Degree is total degree of the formal polynomial. \(\operatorname{mult}_a(P)\) is the least \(|\alpha|\) with a nonzero coefficient of \(z^\alpha\) in \(P(a+z)\); equivalently all Hasse derivatives of order below it vanish at \(a\). The origin condition \(\operatorname{mult}_0(P)\le k-1\) excludes \(P=0\) and is not linear, so minimum degree is a comparison of two linear spaces rather than one solve. Never reduce modulo the field equations.

The origin-order bounds: high-origin cover theorem and punctured first-jet lemma (\(q=2\), \(k=2\): degree \(\ge n+1\) with nonzero origin). What Sauermann–Wigderson settle over \(\mathbb F_2\)

Read from arXiv 2010.00077v2 (Theorems 1.3–1.5 and Section 4.2). Over \(\mathbb R\), for \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge2k-3\): origin order at most \(k-2\) forces degree at least \(n+2k-3\), and origin order exactly \(k-1\) forces \(n+2k-2\), both attained. Theorem 1.5 (origin order \(k-1\)) holds over every field and every \(n\ge1\). Their proof of the other bounds uses characteristic zero only through the nonvanishing of the Catalan numbers; in characteristic two \(C_2=2\) vanishes, so their Theorem 1.3 holds for \(k=2,3\) and fails first at \(k=4\), where their explicit \(\mathbb F_2\) polynomial \(\prod_\ell(x_\ell+1)\cdot Q\) has degree \(n+4\) and value one at the origin. They pose no problem about \(\mathbb F_2\) beyond this discussion; their concluding remarks concern real hyperplane covers and fixed \(n\) with large \(k\). So over \(\mathbb F_2\) the large-\(n\) values are \(D(n,2)=n+1\) and \(D(n,3)=n+3\), far above the note's bound, and the open range is \(k\ge4\), in particular \(k>(n+3)/2\), where their theorem does not apply at all.

Exact values

Method. At the origin the Hasse conditions only say that \(P\) has no monomials of degree below its order. Hence a polynomial of degree at most \(d\) with multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point and origin order exactly \(\ell\) exists if and only if \(r(\ell,d)<r(\ell+1,d)+N_\ell\), where \(r(\ell,d)\) is the \(\mathbb F_2\)-rank of the nonzero-point conditions restricted to monomials of degree in \([\ell,d]\) and \(N_\ell\) is the number of degree-\(\ell\) monomials. The condition for point \(a\) and \(|\beta|<k\) at monomial \(x^\alpha\) is \(\prod_i\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}a_i^{\alpha_i-\beta_i}\bmod2\), evaluated by Lucas' theorem. The kernel research/tools/bmd_min_degree.cpp computes the rank profiles by bitset elimination with columns in ascending degree and extracts witnesses by one Gauss–Jordan pass in descending degree: in reduced form a free degree-\(\ell\) column gives a kernel vector vanishing on all lower degrees, hence a witness of origin order exactly \(\ell\).

Validation. A brute-force enumeration (research/tools/bmd_reference.py) agrees on \((n,k)=(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\), including per-order minima. The kernel reproduces every known value: \(D(n,1)=n\) (Alon–Füredi), \(D(n,2)=n+1\) and \(D(n,3)=n+3\) for \(n\ge3\), origin order \(k-1\) at exactly \(n+2k-2\), and \(D(3,4)=7\), \(D(4,4)=8\). Every witness is re-checked by direct evaluation of its Hasse coefficients with exact binomials, and for \(n\le5\) a direct solve also confirms nonexistence one degree below each minimum. The faster three-elimination mode agrees with the per-order mode on all 21 cases where both ran.

The conjectured formula

With \(m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\), the least dimension at which \(k\) reaches the published threshold, every computed value satisfies

\[ D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor=2k+\bigl(n-m(k)\bigr)\qquad(1\le k<2^{n-1}). \]

For \(2^{n-2}\le k<2^{n-1}\) the right side is \(2k\), the multiplicity bound, which the question's authors report is attained there by hyperplane covers (the exact covering theorem was not re-read this cycle; the computed threshold cases \((3,2),(3,3),(4,4),(5,8)\) agree). Below the threshold it says that each dimension beyond \(m(k)\) costs exactly one degree. For large \(n\) it reads \(n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\), recovering \(n+1,n+3,n+4\) for \(k=2,3,4\), and it lies below the real answer \(n+2k-3\) exactly when \(k\ge4\). The formula was stated after the \(n\le5\) and \(n=6,k\le4\) data and then tested with the degree cap set to the prediction: the new cases \((6,5),(6,6),(7,4)\) gave 12, 14, 11 as predicted, while \((7,2),(7,3)\) were already fixed by Sauermann–Wigderson.

A reduction for the upper half. \(\prod_i(1+x_i)\) has multiplicity exactly \(|a|\) at each point \(a\). So if a single polynomial \(Q_k\), independent of \(n\), has degree \(2k-m(k)\), order below \(k\) at the origin and multiplicity at least \(k-|a|\) at every point of weight \(1\le|a|<k\), then \(\prod_i(1+x_i)Q_k\) gives \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-m(k)\) for every \(n\). This holds for \(k=2\) with \(Q=1+\sum_ix_i\) and for \(k=4\) by Sauermann–Wigderson. The \(D(5,5)\) data show \(Q_k\) must be allowed a positive origin order.

24 September 2026 — The upper half reduces to powers of two and holds for every k up to 15

Working proofs of a general reduction and of the upper bound for k≤15 from finite certificates; powers of two from 16 open. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The conjectured formula needs an upper-bound construction of degree \(n+2k-m(k)\), \(m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\), for all \(n,k\). The previous entry reduced this to an \(n\)-independent factor \(Q_k\) with \(P=\prod_i(1+x_i)Q_k\). This cycle looks for such \(Q_k\), proves what they give for all \(n\), and stops at a proved bound or a precise reduction.

Facts used. (A) For nonzero \(P,Q\), \(\operatorname{mult}_a(PQ)=\operatorname{mult}_a P+\operatorname{mult}_a Q\): the lowest homogeneous parts of \(P(a+z)\) and \(Q(a+z)\) multiply to a nonzero form. (B) \(F_n=\prod_i(1+x_i)\) has \(\operatorname{mult}_a F_n=|a|\), since \(1+a_i+z_i\) is \(z_i\) or \(1+z_i\). (C) \(g=x_1^2+x_1\) satisfies \(g(a+z)=z_1+z_1^2\), so \(\operatorname{mult}_ag=1\) at every point.

The power-of-two reduction

Working lemma. Let \(j\ge0\), \(n\ge1\) and \(Q\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with (i) \(\deg Q\le2^{j+1}-j-2\), (ii) \(\operatorname{mult}_0Q=t<2^j\), (iii) \(\operatorname{mult}_aQ\ge2^j-|a|\) whenever \(1\le|a|<2^j\). For \(0\le r<2^j\) and \(k=2^j+r\), the polynomial \(P=F_nQg^r\) has degree at most \(n+2k-m(k)\), origin order \(t+r<k\) and multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point. Hence \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. Here \(m(k)=j+2\) and \(\deg P\le n+2^{j+1}-j-2+2r=n+2k-m(k)\). By (A)–(C), \(\operatorname{mult}_0P=t+r<2^j+r\). For \(1\le|a|<2^j\), \(\operatorname{mult}_aP\ge|a|+(2^j-|a|)+r=k\); for \(|a|\ge2^j\), \(\operatorname{mult}_aP\ge|a|+r\ge k\). The factor \(g^r\) is what makes the computed origin orders equal \(k-2^{\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor}\).

Certificates valid in every dimension

Working lemma. For a finite set \(L\) of partitions let \(Q^{(n)}=\sum_{\lambda\in L}m_\lambda^{(n)}\), the monomial symmetric polynomials in \(n\) variables (zero when \(\lambda\) has more than \(n\) parts). For \(w\ge1\), a vector \(b\in\mathbb N^w\) and a multiset \(c\) of positive integers, let \(N(\lambda;b,c)\) be the number of maps from the \(w+\#c\) positions to values, with equal parts of \(\lambda\) not distinguished, that use each nonzero part of \(\lambda\) exactly once, put at a "one" position with value \(b_i\) either nothing (only if \(b_i=0\)) or a part \(p\) of which \(b_i\) is a bit-submask, and put at a "zero" position with value \(c_l\) exactly the part \(c_l\). Then for every \(n\), every point \(a\) of weight \(w\) and every \(\beta\) that restricts to \((b,c)\) on the coordinates with \(a_i=1\) and on those with \(a_i=0,\beta_i>0\), the coefficient of \(z^\beta\) in \(Q^{(n)}(a+z)\) is \(\sum_{\lambda\in L}N(\lambda;b,c)\bmod2\). So if these sums are even for all \(1\le w<K\) and all \(\Sigma b+\Sigma c<K-w\), then \(\operatorname{mult}_aQ^{(n)}\ge K-|a|\) for every \(n\) and every \(1\le|a|<K\).

Proof. The coefficient of \(z^{\beta_i}\) in \((a_i+z_i)^{\alpha_i}\) is \(\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\), odd exactly when \(\beta_i\) is a bit-submask of \(\alpha_i\) (Lucas), if \(a_i=1\), and \([\alpha_i=\beta_i]\) if \(a_i=0\). So the coefficient in \(m_\lambda^{(n)}(a+z)\) is the parity of the number of distinct exponent vectors \(\alpha\) in the orbit of \(\lambda\) with every factor odd. Such \(\alpha\) vanish at zero positions with \(\beta_i=0\), equal \(\beta_i\) at the other zero positions, and at one positions are \(0\) (only when \(\beta_i=0\)) or a nonzero part satisfying the submask condition. These \(\alpha\) correspond exactly to the maps counted by \(N\), and the zero parts fill the remaining coordinates uniquely, so the count does not involve \(n\). If \(N>0\) then \(\lambda\) has at most \(w+\#c\le n\) nonzero parts; if \(\lambda\) has more than \(n\) parts both sides are zero. A pair \((b,c)\) with \(w+\#c>n\) does not occur in dimension \(n\).

The upper bound for every \(k\le15\)

Working theorem (finite certificates). For every \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le15\), \(D(n,k)\le n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. A symmetric search (research/tools/bmd_symmetric_q.py, the conditions of the certificate lemma over all partitions of size at most the degree bound) found sets \(L_0,\ldots,L_3\) for \(K=1,2,4,8\): \(Q_1=1\), \(Q_2=1+e_1\), \(Q_4\) equal to the Sauermann–Wigderson characteristic-two factor \(1+\sum_i(x_i^3+x_i^2+x_i)+\sum_{i\ne j}(x_i^3+x_i^2)x_j+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j+\sum_{i<j<l}x_ix_jx_l\), and \(Q_8\), a sum of 72 monomial symmetric functions of degree at most 11 (listed in research/results/binary-multiplicity-degree/c2-symmetric-q/symmetric-q-k1-11.json). research/tools/bmd_check_certificates.py verifies directly that each \(L_j\) contains the empty partition (so \(Q^{(n)}(0)=1\) in every dimension), has sizes at most \(2^{j+1}-j-2\), and satisfies all 216 parity conditions (certificates-check.json). Symmetric \(Q_k\) of the target degree exist for every \(k\le11\).

24 September 2026 — A discrete derivative proves the whole lower half; the formula holds for every k up to 15

Working proofs of the lower bound for all k and of the exact value for k≤15; the upper half beyond 15 remains the power-of-two factor conjecture. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The lower half of the conjectured formula, \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-m(k)\), had no progress. It would follow from a one-dimension step \(D(n-1,k)\le D(n,k)-1\) and the multiplicity bound at the threshold dimension \(m(k)\). This cycle proves that step, tested first against every adjacent pair of the exact table (16 pairs, all differing by exactly one).

Admissible polynomials exist. With \(h=\prod_{y\ne0}(1+y(x))\) over the nonzero linear forms \(y\), \(h(0)=1\) and \(h\) has multiplicity at least \(2^{n-1}\) at every nonzero point (the \(2^{n-1}\) forms with \(y(a)=1\) each vanish there), so \(h^k\) is admissible and \(D(n,k)\) is finite.

The one-dimension step

Working lemma. For \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le k<2^n\), \(D(n-1,k)\le D(n,k)-1\).

Proof. Let \(P\) attain \(D(n,k)\), with degree \(d\), origin order \(\ell\le k-1\) and lowest homogeneous component \(L\ne0\) at the origin. At most \(\ell\le2^n-2\) distinct linear forms divide \(L\), fewer than the \(2^n-1\) nonzero forms, so some nonzero form \(y\) does not divide \(L\). An invertible linear substitution \(A\) with \(y\circ A=x_n\) preserves degree, permutes the nonzero points and preserves multiplicities (\(\operatorname{mult}_a(P\circ A)=\operatorname{mult}_{Aa}P\), since an invertible linear substitution in \(z\) keeps the lowest component nonzero); so assume \(x_n\nmid L\). Write \(P=\sum_{t\ge0}x_n^tP_t(x')\) and set

\[ S(x')=P(x',0)+P(x',1)=\sum_{t\ge1}P_t(x'). \]

(a) \(\deg S\le d-1\), since \(\deg P_t\le d-t\). (b) For \(a'\ne0\) both \((a',0)\) and \((a',1)\) are nonzero; setting \(z_n=0\) in \(P((a',c)+z)\) sends each monomial to itself or to zero, so \(P(x',c)\) has multiplicity at least \(k\) at \(a'\), and so has \(S\). (c) \(P(z',1)\) is the restriction at the nonzero point \((0',1)\), so it has no terms of degree below \(k\). \(P(z',0)\) has lowest component \(L(z',0)\), nonzero of degree \(\ell\) because \(L=x_nL'+L(x',0)\) and \(x_n\nmid L\). The lower bound

Working theorem. For every \(k\ge1\) and \(n\ge m(k)\), \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\).

Proof. With \(m=m(k)\), \(2^{m-2}\le k<2^{m-1}\), so the multiplicity bound gives \(D(m,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{m-1}\rfloor=2k\). For \(m<n'\le n\), \(k<2^{m-1}<2^{n'}\), so the step gives \(D(n',k)\ge D(n'-1,k)+1\); summing, \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-m\).

24 September 2026 — Prior art: Menezes (2026) proves the formula for n ≥ k−1, and the one-dimension step extends it to the whole range

Imported statement plus conditional theorem: the formula for every k and n ≥ m(k), assuming Menezes' upper bound; the large-n formula is a rediscovery. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 (claude-opus-5-5), default reasoning).

Question and stopping point. The route review named prior art as the highest risk to the thread's value. The check is a bounded literature search for three things: the characteristic-two value \(n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\), the one-dimension step and the power-of-two reduction. It stops at the exact matches and at what they leave open.

The imported result

Two web searches were run: one for vanishing to high order on the hypercube in positive characteristic, following Sauermann–Wigderson, and one for a punctured multiplicity Nullstellensatz in characteristic two. They found Dean Menezes, Characteristic drops for high-order vanishing on the hypercube, arXiv 2609.19009 (submitted 14 August 2026; unrefereed). Its order of vanishing is defined through Hasse derivatives, the coefficients of \(P(a+z)\), which is exactly our multiplicity.

Theorem 1.1 (as stated in the arXiv HTML). Let \(F\) be any field, \(k\ge2\), \(n\ge k-1\) and \(0\le\ell\le k-1\). Let \(\delta_F(n,k,\ell)\) be the least degree of a polynomial of order at least \(k\) at every nonzero vertex of \(\{0,1\}^n\) and order exactly \(\ell\) at the origin. Then

\[ \delta_F(n,k,\ell)=n+2k-2-\rho_F(k-\ell), \]

where \(\rho_F(s)\) is the least \(r\) such that \(s-1=a_1+\dots+a_r\) with every Catalan number \(C_{a_i-1}\) nonzero in \(F\).

Corollary 1.3 (characteristic two). \(\delta(n,k,\ell)=n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)\), where \(s_2\) is the binary digit sum.

The upper bound comes from an explicit basis construction valid for \(n\ge k-1\). For \(\ell=k-1\) the paper also gives a single polynomial that works for every \(n\ge1\). Section 8 remarks that for small \(n\) the hypothesis \(n\ge k-1\) is substantial, because the basis elements cancel. Only the statements were read, not the proofs, so this is recorded as an unrefereed import.

Consequence for this thread. Over \(0\le t\le k-1\) the digit sum \(s_2(t)\) is at most \(\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\), with equality at \(t=2^{\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor}-1\). Minimizing Corollary 1.3 over \(\ell\) therefore gives

\[ D(n,k)=n+2k-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\quad\text{for }n\ge k-1. \]

In that range our conjectured formula is a rediscovery. The corollary also gives exactly the minimizing orders we observed in the exact table: \(\{1\}\) for \(k=5\), \(\{0,2\}\) for \(k=6\) and \(\{0,1,3\}\) for \(k=7\).

The range \(n\ge k-1\) misses most of the co-author's question, whose range is \(k<2^{n-1}\), that is \(n\ge m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\). For example, \(k=2^{n-2}\) lies in the question's range, but there \(n=\log_2k+2\) is far below \(k-1\).

The full range from the one-dimension step

Conditional theorem (assuming the upper bound of Menezes' Corollary 1.3 at \(n=k-1\)). For every \(k\ge1\) and \(n\ge m(k)\),

\[ D(n,k)=2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor. \]

Proof. For \(k=1\), \(D(n,1)=n\) by Alon–Füredi and \(\prod_i(1+x_i)\). Let \(k\ge2\). The lower bound gives \(D(n,k)-n\ge2k-m(k)\).

  • If \(n\ge k-1\), the consequence above gives equality.
  • If \(m(k)\le n<k-1\), then for every \(n<n'\le k-1\) we have \(k<2^{m(k)-1}\le2^{n'}\). So the one-dimension step applies at each \(n'\), and chaining it gives \(D(n,k)-n\le D(k-1,k)-(k-1)=2k-m(k)\).

Only the upper bound at \(n=k-1\) is imported; the lower bound and the step are this thread's working results. The theorem is unconditional for \(k\le31\) by our certificates.

The per-order step

Working lemma. For \(n'\ge2\), \(k<2^{n'}\) and \(0\le\ell\le k-1\), if \(\delta(n',k,\ell)\) is finite then \(\delta(n'-1,k,\ell)\le\delta(n',k,\ell)-1\).

Proof. Repeat the proof of the one-dimension step with \(P\) attaining \(\delta(n',k,\ell)\) in place of \(D(n',k)\). That proof uses only \(\ell\le k-1<2^{n'}-1\): distinct linear forms over \(\mathbb F_2\) are pairwise coprime, so at most \(\ell\) of them divide the lowest component, and some nonzero form does not. Part (c) shows that the origin order of \(S\) is exactly \(\ell\): \(P(z',0)\) has lowest part of degree \(\ell\), while \(P(z',1)\) has order at least \(k>\ell\). Part (a) holds for formal polynomials: \(\deg S\le\deg P-1\). Minimizing over \(\ell\) gives back the recorded step.

A per-order upper bound below the published range

Conditional corollary (same import). For \(k\ge2\), \(\max(1,\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor)\le n<k-1\) and \(0\le\ell\le k-1\),

\[ \delta(n,k,\ell)\le n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1). \]

Proof. Corollary 1.3 makes \(\delta(k-1,k,\ell)\) finite. For \(n<n'\le k-1\) we have \(n'\ge\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+1\), hence \(k<2^{n'}\), and \(n'\ge2\). Descending induction with the per-order step keeps every \(\delta(n',k,\ell)\) finite and gives the bound.

The corollary is vacuous for \(k\le5\), where \(k-1\le m(k)\). The upper half of the conditional theorem holds by the same chain for \(n\ge\max(1,m(k)-2)\); only the lower bound needs \(n\ge m(k)\). A matching per-order lower bound below \(n=k-1\) is proved here only for the orders that attain \(D\).

The proof, as formalized

The formal proof is theirs, with one substitution. They apply the Hasse derivative \(P^{(i,0)}\) and restrict it to a line; the formal proof takes a coefficient slice instead, which is the same polynomial and avoids multivariate Hasse derivatives. The complete argument follows.

Induction. The induction runs over finite types, empty or of the form \(\mathrm{Option}\,\sigma\), up to equivalence.

  • Equivalence step. Renaming variables along an equivalence \(e\) preserves total degree. It also preserves multiplicity: \(\operatorname{mult}_a(\operatorname{rename}_eP)=\operatorname{mult}_{a\circ e}P\), because nonzero coefficients of the renamed shift come from coefficients of the same degree. It permutes the points of \(S^\sigma\).
  • No variables. A nonzero constant has multiplicity 0, so the scaled sum is 0.
  • Option step. Write \(P=\sum_{j\le t}P_j(x')\,x_0^j\) with \(P_t\ne0\), where \(x_0\) is the new variable (Mathlib's optionEquivLeft). Then \(\deg P_t+t\le\deg P\).

The line bound. Fix \(a'\in S^\sigma\) and choose a monomial \(z'^\beta\) of \(P_t(a'+z')\) of least degree, so \(|\beta|=\operatorname{mult}_{a'}P_t\). Let \(g\in F[y]\) be the coefficient of \(z'^\beta\) in \(P(a'+z',y)\). Its \(y^j\)-coefficient is the \(z'^\beta\)-coefficient of \(P_j(a'+z')\). So \(g\ne0\), its \(y^t\)-coefficient is nonzero, and \(\deg g=t\).

For \(c\in F\) put \(r=\operatorname{mult}_c g\), which equals the trailing degree of \(g(c+w)\). Shifting \(x_0\) by \(c\) commutes with taking the \(z'^\beta\)-coefficient: this is the lemma sliceMap_taylor, checked on monomials using \(\binom{n}{r}c^{n-r}\). Hence the coefficient of \(z'^\beta w^r\) in \(P((a',c)+(z',w))\) is the \(w^r\)-coefficient of \(g(c+w)\), which is nonzero. Therefore \(\operatorname{mult}_{(a',c)}P\le|\beta|+\operatorname{mult}_c g\).

Summing over \(c\in S\), and using that the root multiplicities of \(g\) in \(S\) sum to at most \(\deg g=t\):

\[ \sum_{c\in S}\operatorname{mult}_{(a',c)}P\le|S|\operatorname{mult}_{a'}P_t+t. \]

Summing over \(a'\). With the induction hypothesis \(|S|\sum_{a'}\operatorname{mult}_{a'}P_t\le\deg P_t\,|S|^{|\sigma|}\) and \(|S^\sigma|=|S|^{|\sigma|}\):

\[ |S|\sum_{a}\operatorname{mult}_aP\le|S|\deg P_t|S|^{|\sigma|}+t|S|^{|\sigma|+1}\le\deg P\,|S|^{|\sigma|+1}. \]

Unscaled form. Every multiplicity is finite because \(P\ne0\). If \(S=\emptyset\), the sum is empty because \(\sigma\) is nonempty. Otherwise divide by \(|S|\).

Substitution facts (M08). Let \(x_i\mapsto\sum_jA_{ij}x_j\) be a linear substitution.

  • It maps a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(d\) to one of degree \(d\) (Mathlib's IsHomogeneous.aeval). Hence it commutes with taking homogeneous components, and it does not raise total degree.

  • The origin order is the least \(j\) with a nonzero component of degree \(j\). So an invertible substitution preserves it.

Choosing coordinates. Let \(\ell\) be the origin order and \(L\) the degree-\(\ell\) component, which is nonzero with \(\ell\le k-1<2^n-1\).

  • If \(x_{\text{none}}\mid L\circ A\), then substituting back with \(A^{-1}\) would give \(\lambda_v\mid L\). So \(x_{\text{none}}\nmid L\circ A\).

The core (step_core). Let \(Q'\) be \(Q\) viewed as a polynomial in \(x_{\text{none}}\), and write \(r_c(Q)=Q'(c)\). Put \(S=r_0(Q)+r_1(Q)\).

25 September 2026 — The upper half of the all-dimension formula holds in every dimension

Working proof: δ(n,k,ℓ) ≤ Φ(n,k,ℓ) for all n ≥ 1 and 0 ≤ ℓ < k. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. The all-dimension conjecture \(\delta(n,k,\ell)=\Phi(n,k,\ell)\), with \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)=n+2\ell+\sum_{j<n}\lfloor(k-\ell-1)/2^j\rfloor\), had no construction attaining \(\Phi\) below the base dimension. This cycle looks for the missing construction, guided by minimal-degree witnesses from the jet kernel.

Outcome. Products of the truncated product with BBDM's hyperplane product attain \(\Phi\) in every dimension. So only the lower bound of the conjecture remains open.

The construction

Working theorem (thm:per-order-all-dimension-upper-bound). Let \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\). Put \(c=k-\ell\) and write \(c-1=q2^n+r\) with \(0\le r<2^n\). Define

\[ P=y_1^{\,\ell}\,F^{2q}\,g_{r+1},\qquad F=\prod_{u\in\mathbb F_2^n\setminus\{0\}}(1+u\cdot x),\qquad y_1=x_1^2+x_1, \]

where \(g_s\) is the truncated product. Then \(P\) has multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point and exactly \(\ell\) at the origin, and \(\deg P\le\Phi(n,k,\ell)\). Hence \(\delta(n,k,\ell)\le\Phi(n,k,\ell)\).

Proof. The facts used are recorded:

  • Fix \(a\ne0\) and \(i\) with \(a_i=1\). Flipping \(u_i\) is an involution that changes \(u\cdot a\) by one, so exactly \(2^{n-1}\) vectors \(u\) satisfy \(u\cdot a=1\). All of them are nonzero, so they index factors of \(F\) that vanish at \(a\). Hence \(\operatorname{mult}_aF\ge2^{n-1}\). Also \(F(0)=1\) and \(\deg F\le2^n-1\). These are steps of the hyperplane cover proof, whose Lean proof (hyperplane_cover_upper_bound, with card_dot_eq_one) establishes them for the same \(F\).
  • \(g_s\) has multiplicity at least \(s\) at every nonzero point, \(g_s(0)=1\), and \(\deg g_s\le n+2(s-1)-s_2(s-1)\) (truncated product; Lean mult_g_ge, eval_zero_g, totalDegree_g_le).
  • \(y_1^\ell\) has multiplicity at least \(\ell\) everywhere and exactly \(\ell\) at the origin (Lean one_le_mult_y, mult_zero_y_pow).

Multiplicities of a product are at least the sum. At the origin, \(F^{2q}g_{r+1}\) has constant term \(1\), so it is a unit in the local ring, and multiplying by it keeps the origin order \(\ell\) of \(y_1^\ell\). At \(a\ne0\) the total is at least \(\ell+2q\cdot2^{n-1}+(r+1)=\ell+(q2^n+r+1)=\ell+c=k\). At the origin it is \(\ell+0+0=\ell\). The degree is at most

\[ 2\ell+2q(2^n-1)+n+2r-s_2(r)=n+2\ell+2(c-1)-2q-s_2(r). \]

In the proof of the all-dimension minimum lemma, Legendre's formula gave \(\sum_{j<n}\lfloor(c-1)/2^j\rfloor=2(c-1)-2q-s_2(r)\), so this bound is \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)\). \(\square\)

For \(c-1<2^n\) we have \(q=0\), and \(P\) is the per-order construction. With the minimum lemma, the theorem reproves the upper half of the complete formula, since \(D(n,k)\le\min_\ell\Phi(n,k,\ell)\).

The witnesses at \((2,3)\) and \((2,4)\) are the truncated product itself, which led to the construction above.

25 September 2026 — A hyperplane dichotomy proves the order-zero value in dimensions up to three

Working proof: δ(n,c,0) = n + Σ_{j<n}⌊(c−1)/2^j⌋ for n ≤ 3 and every c; the argument first fails at (4,9). Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. The all-dimension conjecture reduces to the lower bound \(\delta\ge\Phi\), since the upper bound is proved. The per-order step (lem:per-order-dimension-step: \(\delta(n-1,k,\ell)\le\delta(n,k,\ell)-1\) for \(n\ge2\), \(k<2^n\)) gains one degree per dimension, while \(\Phi(n,c,0)-\Phi(n-1,c,0)=1+\lfloor(c-1)/2^{n-1}\rfloor\). So the step alone fails once \(c-1\ge2^{n-1}\). This cycle looks for a second mechanism that pays for the blocks of \(2^{n-1}\) units of multiplicity, which the construction pays with the hyperplane product \(F\).

Outcome. Restricting to the \(2^n-1\) affine hyperplanes that avoid the origin gives a dichotomy. Either the degree is at least \(2c\), or \(F\) divides \(P\). Combined with the step, this proves \(\delta(n,c,0)=\Phi(n,c,0)\) for every \(c\) when \(n\le3\). The combined recursion first falls short at \((n,c)=(4,9)\), where the true value is \(19=\Phi\).

The dichotomy

Working lemma (lem:hyperplane-restriction-dichotomy). Let \(n,k\ge1\), and let \(P\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) have multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point and order \(\ell\) at the origin. The value of \(\ell\) plays no role except that it is carried over to \(P'\). Then either \(\deg P\ge2k\), or \(P=F\,P'\) with \(F=\prod_{u\ne0}(1+u\cdot x)\), where \(P'\) has multiplicity at least \(k-2^{n-1}\) at every nonzero point, exactly \(\ell\) at the origin, and \(\deg P'=\deg P-(2^n-1)\). In particular, taking \(k=c\) and \(\ell=0\), with \(\delta(n,c,0)=0\) for \(c\le0\),

\[ \delta(n,c,0)\ge\min\bigl(2c,\;2^n-1+\delta(n,c-2^{n-1},0)\bigr). \]

Proof.

  • For \(n=1\): \(H_1=\{1\}\), and \(P\) vanishes there, so \(1+x=F\) divides \(P\). Now let \(n\ge2\). Fix \(u\ne0\) and an affine bijection \(\varphi:\mathbb F_2^{n-1}\to H_u=\{u\cdot x=1\}\). Composition with an affine map does not lower Hasse multiplicity, so \(P\circ\varphi\) has multiplicity at least \(k\) at every point of \(\mathbb F_2^{n-1}\): all points of \(H_u\) are nonzero. By part (c) of the origin-jet lemma, \(\bigcap_z\mathfrak m_z^k=(y)^k\), so \(P\circ\varphi\in(y)^k\).
  • By part (a), a nonzero element of \((y)^k\) has only terms \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) with \(|e|\ge k\), hence degree at least \(2k\). So either \(\deg P\ge\deg(P\circ\varphi)\ge2k\), or \(P\circ\varphi=0\). In the latter case \(P\) lies in the kernel \((1+u\cdot x)\) of \(\varphi^*\).
  • If \(\deg P<2k\), all \(2^n-1\) pairwise non-associate irreducibles \(1+u\cdot x\) divide \(P\), so \(F\mid P\) and \(\deg P'=\deg P-\deg F=\deg P-(2^n-1)\).
  • Hasse multiplicity is additive under products, because lowest homogeneous parts multiply in a domain. At \(a\ne0\) exactly \(2^{n-1}\) factors of \(F\) vanish, each simply, so \(\operatorname{mult}_aF=2^{n-1}\) and \(\operatorname{mult}_aP'\ge k-2^{n-1}\). At the origin \(F(0)=1\), so \(P'\) keeps the order \(\ell\). For \(\ell=0\) and \(c\le2^{n-1}\), the bound for \(P'\) is \(\deg P'\ge0\). \(\square\)

The order-zero value in dimensions up to three

Two exact identities of \(\Phi(n,c,0)=n+\sum_{j<n}\lfloor(c-1)/2^j\rfloor\) match the two mechanisms:

  • (S) \(\Phi(n,c,0)=\Phi(n-1,c,0)+1\) when \(c-1<2^{n-1}\), since the \(j=n-1\) term vanishes;
  • (D) \(\Phi(n,c,0)=2^n-1+\Phi(n,c-2^{n-1},0)\) when \(c>2^{n-1}\), since subtracting \(2^{n-1}\) lowers the \(j\)-th term by exactly \(2^{n-1-j}\).

Moreover \(\Phi(n,c,0)\le2c\) exactly when \(2q+s_2(r)\ge n-2\), where \(c-1=q2^n+r\) with \(0\le r<2^n\). This follows from the Legendre form \(\Phi=n+2(c-1)-2q-s_2(r)\) of the minimum lemma.

Working theorem (thm:order-zero-value-small-dimensions). For \(1\le n\le3\) and every \(c\ge1\), \(\delta(n,c,0)=\Phi(n,c,0)\):

  • \(\delta(1,c,0)=c\);
  • \(\delta(2,c,0)=c+1+\lfloor(c-1)/2\rfloor\);
  • \(\delta(3,c,0)=c+2+\lfloor(c-1)/2\rfloor+\lfloor(c-1)/4\rfloor\).

Proof. The upper bound is the all-dimension upper bound. For the lower bound, induct on \(n\), and within \(n\) on \(c\).

  • \(n=1\). \((1+x)^c\) divides \(P\), so \(\deg P\ge c\).
  • Small \(c\). For \(n=2,3\) and \(c-1<2^{n-1}\), we have \(c\le2^{n-1}<2^n\), and \(\delta(n,c,0)\) is finite by the upper bound. So the per-order step applies with \(\ell=0\) and gives \(\delta(n,c,0)\ge\delta(n-1,c,0)+1\ge\Phi(n-1,c,0)+1=\Phi(n,c,0)\) by (S).
  • Large \(c\). Otherwise \(c>2^{n-1}\) and the dichotomy gives \(\delta(n,c,0)\ge\min(2c,\Phi(n,c,0))\) by induction and (D). It remains to check \(\Phi(n,c,0)\le2c\). For \(n=2\) this is \(1+\lfloor(c-1)/2\rfloor\le c\). For \(n=3\) and \(c\ge5\) it follows from \(\lfloor(c-1)/2\rfloor+\lfloor(c-1)/4\rfloor\le3(c-1)/4\le c-2\). \(\square\)

Where the two mechanisms stop

Let \(R(n,c)\) be the recursion \(R(n,c)=\max\bigl(R(n-1,c)+1,\ \min(2c,\,2^n-1+R(n,c-2^{n-1}))\bigr)\), with the step term used only when \(c-1<2^{n-1}\), \(R(1,c)=c\) and \(R(n,c)=0\) for \(c\le0\). It is a proved lower bound for \(\delta(n,c,0)\). Shortfalls start where \(c-1\ge2^{n-1}\) and \(2q+s_2(r)<n-2\). There the step does not apply and the dichotomy only gives \(2c<\Phi\). They are then inherited through both rules: \((4,17)\) inherits one from \((4,9)\) through (D), and \((5,9)\) through (S). An arithmetic comparison over \(n\le7\), \(c\le64\) (research/tools/bmd_dichotomy_bound.py, output in research/results/bmd-r4-hyperplane-dichotomy/) finds \(R=\Phi\) for all \(n\le3\), and \(R\le\Phi\) everywhere. The first shortfall is \((4,9)\): \(R=18\), while the exact value is 19. For \(n=4\) the shortfalls are \(c\equiv1\pmod8\), \(c\ge9\). In the gap, a polynomial of degree between \(2c\) and \(\Phi-1\) has a nonzero restriction to some \(H_u\). That restriction lies in \((y)^c\) of \(H_u\), which alone is compatible with degree \(2c\). The missing ingredient has to use the origin and the hyperplane restrictions at the same time.

General statement. lem:hyperplane-restriction-dichotomy holds for all \(n,k,\ell\). thm:order-zero-value-small-dimensions proves the order-zero case of conj:binary-multiplicity-per-order-all-dimensions for \(n\le3\).

Dependencies re-read: the origin-jet lemma (a) and (c), the per-order step and its hypotheses \(n\ge2\), \(k<2^n\), the minimum lemma's Legendre form, and the all-dimension upper bound. The recursion's comparison with \(\Phi\) was computed by script. A fresh-context reviewer checked the dichotomy (affine restriction, \((y)^k\) on the hyperplane, divisibility, \(\operatorname{mult}_aF=2^{n-1}\)), the identities (S) and (D), the criterion \(\Phi\le2c\), and the induction for \(n\le3\), including the bases. It found no mathematical error. It asked for the case \(n=1\), the unused hypothesis \(\ell<k\), a link to the step, and a precise description of where the recursion falls short; all are made.

Assessment. For the first time the lower bound \(\delta\ge\Phi\) is proved on an infinite family below the base dimension, and the missing mechanism is now located. The missing implication is the set where \(R<\Phi\), which starts at \((4,9)\) and is inherited upward. It needs an argument that improves "degree \(\ge2c\) on a hyperplane" using the other hyperplanes and the origin. Possible routes: apply the dichotomy to \(P'\) after dividing only the hyperplanes on which \(P\) vanishes, with Schwartz–Zippel on \(P'|_{H}\); or bring the origin in through the step on \(P\) restricted to linear hyperplanes. The line keeps good prospects; the case \((4,9)\) is small enough to study exactly. 25 September 2026 — The per-order formula is proved: additive polynomials on the span of the y_i

Working proof: δ(n,k,ℓ) = n+2k−2−s₂(k−ℓ−1) whenever k−ℓ ≤ 2^n, hence for all n ≥ m(k). Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. The first case of \(\delta\ge\Phi\) not covered by the step and the hyperplane dichotomy is \((n,c,\ell)=(4,9,0)\). This cycle looks for the mechanism that decides it. It starts from a closed form for the coefficients of the polynomial attached to an origin jet.

Outcome. The degree of \(P(v)\) is read off from \(y\)-coefficients of \(w=v\circ x\), where \(x(Y)=\sum_{t\ge0}Y^{2^t}\) is additive. The lower bound then follows from a root count, applied to multi-additive forms one variable at a time: a nonzero additive polynomial of degree below \(2^n\) cannot vanish on the \(2^n\)-element \(\mathbb F_2\)-span of \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\). This proves the per-order conjecture for every \(k\), and the all-dimension conjecture for \(n\le4\).

The degree in terms of \(y\)-coefficients

Let \(x(Y)=\sum_{t\ge0}Y^{2^t}\in\mathbb F_2[[Y]]\), the root of \(X^2+X=Y\) with \(x(0)=0\). Substituting \(x_i=x(y_i)\) is an automorphism of \(\mathbb F_2[[y]]\) that preserves the order.

Working lemma (lem:y-coefficient-degree-formula). Let \(v\) be an origin jet (\(|\beta|<k\)), let \(P(v)\) be as in the origin-jet lemma, and put \(w(y)=v(x(y_1),\ldots,x(y_n))\). Then the coefficient of \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) (\(|e|<k\)) in \(P(v)\) is

\[ [y^e]\Bigl(w\prod_{i:\varepsilon_i=0}\bigl(1+x(y_i)\bigr)\Bigr), \]

and therefore

\[ \deg P(v)=\max\Bigl\{n-|S|+2|e|:\ S\subseteq[n],\ |e|<k,\ [y^e]\bigl(w\,u^S\bigr)\ne0\Bigr\},\qquad u^S=\prod_{i\in S}\bigl(1+x(y_i)\bigr). \]

Proof.

  • One variable. Let \(f\in\mathbb F_2[x]\) vanish to order at least \(k\) at \(x=1\), and write \(f=\sum_j(p_j+q_jx)y^j\). In \(\mathbb F_2[[y]]\), the substitutions \(x\mapsto x(y)\) and \(x\mapsto1+x(y)\) both send \(x^2+x\) to \(y\). The second has \(f(1+x(y))\equiv0\) modulo \(y^k\), since \(x(y)\) has order 1. So modulo \(y^k\):
    • \(\sum_j q_jy^j\equiv f(x(y))+f(1+x(y))\equiv f(x(y))\), because \((p+qx)+(p+q+qx)=q\);
    • \(\sum_jp_jy^j\equiv\sum_jq_j(1+x(y))y^j\equiv(1+x(y))f(x(y))\), from \(f(1+x(y))\equiv0\).
  • Several variables. \(P(v)=T\bigl(\sum_\beta v_\beta\prod_ig_{\beta_i}(x_i)\bigr)\), and \(g_b(x)=x^b(1+x^{2^K})\) vanishes to order \(2^K\ge k\) at \(1\). The coefficient of \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) factors over the variables: in variable \(i\) it is \([y_i^{e_i}]\bigl((1+x(y_i))^{1-\varepsilon_i}g_{\beta_i}(x(y_i))\bigr)\). Since \(x(y)^{2^K}\) has order \(2^K\ge k>e_i\), we may replace \(g_b(x(y))\) by \(x(y)^b\). Summing over \(\beta\) gives the displayed coefficient.
  • The degree. The degree formula is part (a) of the origin-jet lemma, with \(S=\{i:\varepsilon_i=0\}\). \(\square\)

As a control, the formula was compared with the kernel's degree of \(P(v)\) for 240 random jets (\(n\le3\), \(k\le8\)), with zero mismatches (research/results/bmd-r5-additive-roots/ycoef-check.txt).

The per-order value when \(k-\ell\le2^n\)

Working theorem (thm:per-order-value-small-multiplicity). Let \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\) with \(k-\ell\le2^n\). Then

\[ \delta(n,k,\ell)=n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1). \]

In particular this holds for every \(n\ge m(k)=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\), since then \(k<2^{n-1}\). So the per-order conjecture is proved.

Proof. The upper bound is the per-order construction. For the lower bound, put \(c=k-\ell\) and \(h=s_2(c-1)\). The only use of \(c\le2^n\) is the degree bound \(c-1<2^n\) in step 3. Suppose \(\deg P\le n+2k-3-h\) for some admissible \(P\) of origin order \(\ell\). By the origin-jet lemma we may take \(P=P(v)\) with \(\operatorname{ord}v=\ell\). Then \(w\) has order \(\ell\): its degree-\(d\) parts \(w_d\) vanish for \(d<\ell\), and \(w_\ell\ne0\).

  • Step 1: the degree conditions. Take \(|e|=k-1\) and \(|S|\le h\) in the lemma. Then \(n-|S|+2(k-1)>\deg P\), so the degree-\((k-1)\) part of \(w\,u^S\) vanishes. Expanding \(u^S=\sum_{T\subseteq S}x^T\), with \(x^T=\prod_{i\in T}x(y_i)\), and inducting on \(|T|\) gives \((w\,x^T)_{k-1}=0\) for every set \(T\) of at most \(h\) distinct indices.
  • Step 2: the multi-additive forms. For \(0\le j\le h\) let \[ E_j(Y_1,\ldots,Y_j)=\sum_{t_1,\ldots,t_j\ge0}Y_1^{2^{t_1}}\cdots Y_j^{2^{t_j}}\,w_{k-1-\sum_a2^{t_a}} \] (terms with \(k-1-\sum2^{t_a}<\ell\) vanish). Since \(x(y_i)=\sum_ty_i^{2^t}\), we have \((w\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_j})_{k-1}=E_j(y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_j})\). So \(E_j\) vanishes at every tuple of distinct generators. \(E_j\) is symmetric and additive in each variable. Its degree in each variable is at most \(c-1<2^n\), because only exponents with \(\sum2^{t_a}\le k-1-\ell=c-1\) occur.
  • Step 3: induction on \(j\). We show \(E_j=0\) as a polynomial. For \(j=0\) this is \(w_{k-1}=0\), which is step 1 with \(S=\emptyset\). Assume \(E_{j-1}=0\).
    • At a tuple with a repeated generator \(Y_1=Y_2\), the terms with \(t_1\ne t_2\) cancel in pairs. The terms with \(t_1=t_2=t\) carry coefficients \(w_{k-1-2^{t+1}-\ldots}\), which are coefficients of \(E_{j-1}\) and hence zero. So \(E_j\) vanishes on \(\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}^j\).
    • By additivity it vanishes on \(V^j\), where \(V\) is the \(\mathbb F_2\)-span of the \(y_i\): \(2^n\) distinct elements of the domain \(\mathbb F_2[y]\).
    • Viewed as a polynomial in \(Y_1\) over \(\mathbb F_2[y][Y_2,\ldots,Y_j]\), specialized at any point of \(V^{j-1}\), it has degree \(<2^n\) and \(2^n\) roots, so it is zero. Each \(Y_1\)-coefficient therefore vanishes on \(V^{j-1}\), and induction on the number of variables gives \(E_j=0\).
  • Step 4: the contradiction. Write \(c-1=\sum_{a=1}^h2^{t_a}\) in binary. The coefficient of \(\prod_aY_a^{2^{t_a}}\) in \(E_h\) is \(w_{k-1-(c-1)}=w_\ell\), so \(w_\ell=0\), a contradiction. Hence \(\deg P\ge n+2k-2-h\). \(\square\)

For \(k-\ell-1<2^n\) the value equals \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)\) (part (i) of the minimum lemma). So this theorem is the all-dimension conjecture in the range \(k-\ell\le2^n\). It covers the case \((4,9,0)\) and all 136 values of the computer-assisted check. As a consistency check, all 477 exact values with \(k-\ell\le2^n\) among the 81 computed cases agree with it, 306 of them below \(m(k)\) (theorem-vs-data.txt).

Every order in dimensions up to four

Working corollary (cor:per-order-all-dimensions-small-n). For \(1\le n\le4\) and all \(0\le\ell<k\), \(\delta(n,k,\ell)=\Phi(n,k,\ell)\).

Proof. The upper bound is the all-dimension upper bound. For the lower bound, induct on \(c=k-\ell\). If \(c\le2^n\), apply the theorem above. If \(c>2^n\), the first sentence of the dichotomy gives \(\delta(n,k,\ell)\ge\min\bigl(2k,\,2^n-1+\delta(n,k-2^{n-1},\ell)\bigr)\), since \(P'\) keeps order exactly \(\ell\). The right side is defined because \(k-2^{n-1}-\ell=c-2^{n-1}\ge1\). By induction, \(\delta(n,k-2^{n-1},\ell)\ge\Phi(n,k-2^{n-1},\ell)\). Now \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)=2\ell+\Phi(n,c,0)\), and identity (D) of the small-dimension entry at \(c>2^{n-1}\) gives \(2^n-1+\Phi(n,k-2^{n-1},\ell)=\Phi(n,k,\ell)\). Finally, \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)\le2k\) is equivalent to \(2q+s_2(r)\ge n-2\), where \(c-1=q2^n+r\). This holds because \(q\ge1\) and \(n\le4\). \(\square\)

What remains. The all-dimension conjecture is open only for \(n\ge5\) and \(k-\ell>2^n\). 25 September 2026 — The per-order minimum in every dimension: δ(n,k,ℓ) = n + 2ℓ + Σ_{j<n}⌊(k−ℓ−1)/2^j⌋

Question. The additive-roots theorem proves \(\delta\ge\Phi\) when \(k-\ell\le2^n\). There the forms \(E_j\) have degree below \(2^n\), so vanishing on the span \(V\) of \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\) forces them to vanish identically. Outcome. The lower bound holds in every case, so the all-dimension conjecture is a theorem. The proof couples the degree conditions at \(|e|=k-1-t\) for all \(t\). Their forms \(E^{(t)}_j\) satisfy a recursion under repeated arguments, which makes them vanish on \(V^j\). Reducing modulo \(L_V\) then isolates the coefficient \(w_\ell\).

The theorem

Working theorem (thm:per-order-value-all-dimensions). For all \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\),

\[ \delta(n,k,\ell)=\Phi(n,k,\ell)=n+2\ell+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{k-\ell-1}{2^j}\Bigr\rfloor . \]

Setup. The upper bound is the all-dimension upper bound. For the lower bound, write \(m=k-\ell-1=q2^n+r\) with \(0\le r<2^n\), and let \(h=2q+s_2(r)\). By the Legendre form in the minimum lemma, \(\Phi=n+2(k-1)-h\). Also \(m\) is a sum of exactly \(h\) powers of two, each at most \(2^{n-1}\): \(2q\) copies of \(2^{n-1}\) and the binary digits of \(r\). Fix such an ordered decomposition \(m=\sum_{a=1}^h2^{s_a}\), with all \(s_a\le n-1\).

Suppose some admissible \(P\) of origin order \(\ell\) has \(\deg P\le\Phi-1\). By the origin-jet lemma we may take \(P=P(v)\), where \(\operatorname{ord}v=\ell\). Let \(w=v\circ x\), with \(x(Y)=\sum_{t\ge0}Y^{2^t}\); its parts satisfy \(w_d=0\) for \(d<\ell\) and \(w_\ell\ne0\).

  • Step 1: conditions (\(C_t\)). In the \(y\)-coefficient lemma take \(|e|=k-1-t\) with \(t\ge0\). The term \(n-|S|+2(k-1-t)\) is at least \(\Phi\) exactly when \(|S|\le h-2t\). So \((w\,u^S)_{k-1-t}=0\) for every \(S\subseteq[n]\) with \(|S|\le h-2t\). Möbius inversion over \(u^S=\sum_{T\subseteq S}x^T\) gives \((w\,x^T)_{k-1-t}=0\) for every set \(T\) of distinct indices with \(|T|\le h-2t\).
  • Step 2: the forms. For \(t\ge0\) and \(j\ge0\) put \[ E^{(t)}_j(Y_1,\ldots,Y_j)=\sum_{t_1,\ldots,t_j\ge0}Y_1^{2^{t_1}}\cdots Y_j^{2^{t_j}}\;w_{k-1-t-\sum_a2^{t_a}} . \] Each \(E^{(t)}_j\) is symmetric and additive in each variable, and \(E^{(t)}_j(y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_j})=(w\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_j})_{k-1-t}\). Setting the first two arguments equal, the terms with \(t_1\ne t_2\) cancel in pairs. The diagonal terms \(t_1=t_2=s\) give \(Y^{2^{s+1}}\), which are the terms of \(E^{(t)}_{j-1}(Y,\ldots)\) with \(Y\)-exponent \(\ge2\). The \(Y^1\) terms of \(E^{(t)}_{j-1}\) are \(Y\,E^{(t+1)}_{j-2}\). Hence \[ E^{(t)}_j(Y,Y,Z_3,\ldots,Z_j)=E^{(t)}_{j-1}(Y,Z_3,\ldots,Z_j)+Y\,E^{(t+1)}_{j-2}(Z_3,\ldots,Z_j). \]
  • Step 3: vanishing on \(V^j\). We show by induction on \(j\), for all \(t\ge0\) simultaneously, that \(E^{(t)}_j\) vanishes on \(V^j\) whenever \(j\le h-2t\). The case \((t,j)\) uses the case \((t+1,j-2)\).
    • For \(j=0\) this is \(w_{k-1-t}=0\), which is (\(C_t\)) with \(T=\emptyset\).
    • For \(j\ge1\), a tuple of generators with distinct indices is covered by (\(C_t\)). A tuple with a repeated generator can be brought to the form \((Y,Y,\ldots)\) by symmetry. By the recursion it is then covered by \(E^{(t)}_{j-1}\), with \(j-1\le h-2t\), and \(E^{(t+1)}_{j-2}\), with \(j-2\le h-2(t+1)\). Both vanish on the span by induction.
    • So \(E^{(t)}_j\) vanishes at all tuples of generators, and by multi-additivity on \(V^j\).
  • Step 4: reduction modulo \(L_V\). Let \(L_V(Y)=\prod_{a\in V}(Y-a)\). \(V\) has \(2^n\) distinct elements in the domain \(\mathbb F_2[y]\), and \(L_V\) is monic and additive over the domain \(\mathbb F_2[y]\). For \(s\ge0\) let \(\rho_s(Y)\) be the remainder of \(Y^{2^s}\) modulo \(L_V\). It is additive, of degree \(<2^n\), and equal to \(Y^{2^s}\) for \(s<n\). Since \(L_V\) divides \(Y^{2^s}-\rho_s(Y)\), \(\rho_s(a)=a^{2^s}\) for \(a\in V\). Let \(R\) be \(E^{(0)}_h\) with each \(Y_a^{2^{t_a}}\) replaced by \(\rho_{t_a}(Y_a)\).
    • \(R\) agrees with \(E^{(0)}_h\) on \(V^h\), so \(R\) vanishes there.
    • \(R\) is multi-additive of degree \(<2^n\) in each variable. Specialize all but one variable to points of \(V\): the result has \(2^n\) roots and degree \(<2^n\), so it is zero. Repeating variable by variable gives \(R=0\).
  • Step 5: the coefficient of \(M^*=\prod_aY_a^{2^{s_a}}\) in \(R\). A monomial \(\prod_aY_a^{2^{t_a}}\) of \(E^{(0)}_h\) carries \(w_{k-1-\sum2^{t_a}}\), which is zero unless \(\sum2^{t_a}\le k-1-\ell=m\). Reduction never raises the \(Y\)-degree and lowers it strictly whenever some \(t_a\ge n\). \(M^*\) has \(Y\)-degree exactly \(m\). So the only monomial of \(E^{(0)}_h\) that contributes to \(M^*\) is \(M^*\) itself, and it is unreduced because all \(s_a<n\). Its coefficient is \(w_{k-1-m}=w_\ell\). Since \(R=0\), \(w_\ell=0\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

For \(q=0\) the reduction is trivial, since all \(t_a<n\), and step 3 can be replaced by the identical vanishing used in the additive-roots proof. The new ingredients for \(q\ge1\) are the coupling of the conditions (\(C_t\)) through the repeated-argument recursion and the reduction modulo \(L_V\). In the construction \(y_1^\ell F^{2q}g_{r+1}\), each block of \(2^n\) in \(m\) is paid by \(F^2\), and correspondingly each block costs two parts of size \(2^{n-1}\) in \(h\).

25 September 2026 — The per-order minimum on F_q^n for every prime power q

Working proof: δ_q(n,k,ℓ) = n(q−1) + qℓ + (q−1)Σ_{j<n}⌊(k−ℓ−1)/q^j⌋ for every prime power q. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. Does the binary proof transfer to the base-\(q\) law? The ingredients to transfer are the origin jets, the coefficient formula, the root count on the span, and the construction.

Outcome. Everything transfers, so the conjecture holds for every prime power \(q\). The only new points are these:

  • Lagrange interpolation over the \(q\) roots of \(X^q-X=y\);
  • generators repeated up to \(q-1\) times;
  • the vanishing of multinomial coefficients \(q!/\prod m_i!\) modulo \(p\) for the collapse of \(q\) repeats;
  • a subadditivity bound for the construction.

Setup. Fix a prime power \(q=p^a\). Let \(y_i=x_i^q-x_i\), and let \(x(y)=-\sum_{t\ge0}y^{q^t}\) be the root of \(X^q-X=y\) with \(x(0)=0\); it is \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear. The origin-jet lemma holds over \(\mathbb F_q\) with the same proof:

  • the basis is \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) with \(0\le\varepsilon_i<q\), with degree \(\max(|\varepsilon|+q|e|)\), since the top monomials \(x^{\varepsilon+qe}\) are distinct;
  • \((y)^k=\bigcap_a\mathfrak m_a^k\), by the Chinese remainder dimension \(q^n\binom{n+k-1}{n}\);
  • jets are lifted by \(g_b=x^b(1-x^{q-1})^{q^K}\), with \(q^K\ge k\).

Write \(P(v)\) for the admissible polynomial of a jet \(v\), and \(w=v\circ x\).

The coefficient formula

Working lemma (lem:q-ary-coefficient-formula). The coefficient of \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) (\(|e|<k\)) in \(P(v)\) is \([y^e]\bigl(w\prod_i\gamma_{\varepsilon_i}(x(y_i))\bigr)\), where \(\gamma_\varepsilon(x)\) is the coefficient of \(X^\varepsilon\) in \(1-(X-x)^{q-1}\). Explicitly, \(\gamma_\varepsilon(x)=\delta_{\varepsilon,0}-\binom{q-1}{\varepsilon}(-x)^{q-1-\varepsilon}\), and \(\binom{q-1}{\varepsilon}\ne0\) in \(\mathbb F_p\) by Lucas.

Proof. Take one variable, with \(f\) vanishing to order \(\ge k\) at every \(a\in\mathbb F_q^*\), and write \(R(X)=\sum_{j<k}R_j(X)y^j\) with \(\deg R_j<q\).

  • The \(q\) series \(x(y)+a\), \(a\in\mathbb F_q\), all satisfy \(X^q-X=y\). So \(R(x(y)+a)\equiv f(x(y)+a)\) modulo \(y^k\). This vanishes for \(a\ne0\), because \(x(y)\) has order 1.
  • \(R\) has degree \(<q\) in \(X\), so Lagrange interpolation at the nodes \(x(y)+a\) gives \(R(X)\equiv f(x(y))\,\ell_0(X-x(y))\). Here \(\ell_0(Z)=1-Z^{q-1}\), the unique polynomial of degree \(<q\) that is \(1\) at \(0\) and \(0\) on \(\mathbb F_q^*\).
  • Taking coefficients of \(X^\varepsilon\) gives the one-variable case. The multivariate case factors as in the binary lemma, and \(g_b(x(y))\equiv x(y)^b\) coefficientwise. \(\square\)

Put \(d_i=q-1-\varepsilon_i\in[0,q-1]\). For \(0<\varepsilon_i\le q-1\), \(\gamma_{\varepsilon_i}\) is a nonzero multiple of \(x^{d_i}\) (a constant for \(d_i=0\)). For \(\varepsilon_i=0\), that is \(d_i=q-1\), \(\gamma_0=1-x^{q-1}\). The term \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) has degree \(n(q-1)-|d|+q|e|\).

The theorem

Working theorem (thm:q-ary-per-order-value). For every prime power \(q\), \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\), the least degree of \(P\in\mathbb F_q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with Hasse multiplicity \(\ge k\) at every nonzero point of \(\mathbb F_q^n\) and exactly \(\ell\) at the origin is

\[ \delta_q(n,k,\ell)=n(q-1)+q\ell+(q-1)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{k-\ell-1}{q^j}\Bigr\rfloor=n(q-1)+q(k-1)-h, \]

where \(k-\ell-1=Qq^n+r\) with \(0\le r<q^n\), and \(h=qQ+s_q(r)\) (the Legendre form of the previous entry).

Upper bound. Put \(F=\prod_{u\in\mathbb F_q^n\setminus\{0\}}(1-u\cdot x)\), of degree \(q^n-1\), with \(F(0)=1\). At \(a\ne0\) exactly \(q^{n-1}\) factors vanish, each simply, so \(\operatorname{mult}_aF=q^{n-1}\). Put \(G=P(1)\), the polynomial of the jet \(v=1\) truncated at \((y)^{r+1}\). It has multiplicity \(\ge r+1\) at nonzero points and \(G(0)=1\). By the lemma with \(w=1\), a nonzero coefficient at \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) requires, in each variable \(i\), that \(e_i\) be a sum of exactly \(d_i\le q-1\) powers of \(q\), or \(e_i=0\) when \(\varepsilon_i=0\). With at most \(q-1\) parts no base-\(q\) carries occur, so \(s_q(e_i)\le d_i\) in every case. Such a term has degree \(\sum_i(q-1-d_i+qe_i)\le n(q-1)+\sum_i(qe_i-s_q(e_i))\). Digit sums are subadditive and \(qm-s_q(m)\) is increasing, so with \(\sum e_i\le r\) this is at most \(n(q-1)+qr-s_q(r)\). The polynomial \((x_1^q-x_1)^\ell F^{qQ}G\) then has:

  • multiplicity \(\ge\ell+qQ\cdot q^{n-1}+r+1=k\) at nonzero points;
  • order exactly \(\ell\) at the origin, since \(F^{qQ}G\) is a unit there;
  • degree \(\le q\ell+qQ(q^n-1)+n(q-1)+qr-s_q(r)=n(q-1)+q(k-1)-h\).

Lower bound. This follows the binary proof, with these changes. Suppose \(\deg P(v)\le n(q-1)+q(k-1)-h-1\), with \(\operatorname{ord}v=\ell\).

  • Step 1. Take \(|e|=k-1-t\). The degree bound kills the coefficients for all \(d\in[0,q-1]^n\) with \(|d|\le h-qt\). Expanding the factors \(1-x_i^{q-1}\) and inducting on \(|d|\) gives \((w\prod_ix_i^{d_i})_{k-1-t}=0\) for all such \(d\).
  • Step 2. Let \(E^{(t)}_j(Y_1,\ldots,Y_j)=\sum_{t_1,\ldots,t_j}\prod_aY_a^{q^{t_a}}\,w_{k-1-t-\sum_aq^{t_a}}\). It is symmetric and \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear in each variable. Up to the sign \((-1)^j\), \((w\prod x_i^{d_i})_{k-1-t}\) equals \(E^{(t)}_{|d|}\) at the tuple listing each \(y_i\) exactly \(d_i\) times. So \(E^{(t)}_j\) vanishes at generator tuples in which no generator appears \(q\) or more times, for \(j\le h-qt\).
  • Step 3. Put \(q\) equal arguments \(Y\).
    • The ordered exponent tuples \((t_1,\ldots,t_q)\) with a given multiset occur \(q!/\prod m_i!\) times. That count is \(\equiv0\pmod p\) unless the multiset is a single value, by Lucas.
    • Hence \(E^{(t)}_j(Y^{\times q},Z)=E^{(t)}_{j-q+1}(Y,Z)-Y\,E^{(t+1)}_{j-q}(Z)\), with \(j-q+1\le h-qt\) and \(j-q\le h-q(t+1)\).
    • Induction on \(j\), for all \(t\) at once, shows that \(E^{(t)}_j\) vanishes at all generator tuples. By multilinearity it vanishes on \(V^j\), where \(V\) is the \(\mathbb F_q\)-span of the \(y_i\): \(q^n\) distinct elements of \(\mathbb F_q[y]\).
  • Step 4. The subspace polynomial \(L_V(Y)=\prod_{a\in V}(Y-a)\) is a monic \(q\)-polynomial of degree \(q^n\). Replace each \(Y_a^{q^{t_a}}\) in \(E^{(0)}_h\) by its remainder modulo \(L_V\). The result \(R\) has degree \(<q^n\) in each variable and vanishes on \(V^h\), a grid of \(q^n\) distinct points per variable in the domain \(\mathbb F_q[y]\). So \(R=0\).
  • Step 5. Write \(k-1-\ell=\sum_{a=1}^hq^{s_a}\), with \(qQ\) parts \(q^{n-1}\) and \(r\)'s base-\(q\) digits as repeated parts, so all \(s_a<n\). As in the binary proof, only \(M^*=\prod Y_a^{q^{s_a}}\) contributes to its own coefficient in \(R\), which is \(w_\ell\ne0\). This is a contradiction. \(\square\)

How it compares with that thread's cover bounds is left to that thread.

25 September 2026 — The cube over odd characteristic: Menezes confirmed, the binary laws break from n = 3

Finite check and refutation; conjecture for n = 2 over every field. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. Does the per-order law reach Menezes' own setting, the cube \(\{0,1\}^n\) over a field of odd characteristic? Method. research/tools/bmd_jet_orders_q.cpp gained a --cube mode: basis \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\), \(y=x^2-x\), over \(\mathbb F_p\). The exchange law fails

Powers \(F^j\) and \(F^j(1-x_1)(1-x_2)\) give the matching upper bound. This will be written with a proof next.

25 September 2026 — The square over every field, and the exact value of the polynomial relaxation for covers

Working proofs: the square over every field at order zero; the exact value of the cover relaxation. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. This entry takes two steps from the last one. First, the \(\ell=0\) case of the dimension-two conjecture over every field. Second, what the \(q\)-ary theorem says about the odd-field thread's covering problem. That thread showed that multiplicity conditions alone cannot prove its target \(n(q-1)+q(k-1)-1\) for \(f_q(n,k,1)\), \(3\le k\le q\), via a polynomial \(Q_k\) of degree \(\le n(q-1)+(q-1)(k-1)\) (higher-jet obstruction).

The square over every field

Working theorem (thm:cube-square-order-zero-any-field). Let \(F\) be any field and \(c\ge1\). The least degree of \(P\in F[x_1,x_2]\) with \(P(0)\ne0\) and Hasse multiplicity \(\ge c\) at \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\) and \((1,1)\) is \(T(c)=2c-\lceil(c-1)/2\rceil\).

Proof.

  • Upper bound. Let \(G=(1-x_1)(1-x_2)(1-x_1-x_2)\), of degree 3. Each nonzero point lies on exactly two of the three lines, so \(G\) has multiplicity 2 there, and \(G(0)=1\). For \(c=2j\) take \(G^j\), of degree \(3j=T(2j)\). For \(c=2j+1\) take \(G^j(1-x_1)(1-x_2)\), of degree \(3j+2=T(2j+1)\). Its multiplicities are \(2j+1\), \(2j+1\) and \(2j+2\).
  • Step. Write \(P=\sum_jx_2^jP_j(x_1)\) and \(S(t)=P(t,1)-P(t,0)=\sum_{j\ge1}P_j(t)\), so \(\deg S\le\deg P-1\). \(S(0)=-P(0,0)\ne0\), because \(P(0,1)=0\). Both restrictions vanish to order \(\ge c\) at \(t=1\), so \((t-1)^c\mid S\). Since \(S\ne0\), \(\deg S\ge c\), and \(\deg P\ge c+1\).
  • Dichotomy. Each line \(L\in\{x_1=1,\ x_2=1,\ x_1+x_2=1\}\) avoids the origin and contains two nonzero cube points. Parametrize \(L\) affinely by \(t\). The restriction of \(P\) vanishes to order \(\ge c\) at two values of \(t\), so it is zero or of degree \(\ge2c\). If \(\deg P<2c\), the three pairwise non-associate affine forms all divide \(P\), so \(P=GP'\). Multiplicities add under products, so \(P'\) has multiplicity \(\ge c-2\) at the three points, \(P'(0)=P(0)\ne0\), and \(\deg P'=\deg P-3\).
  • Induction. With \(\delta(c)=0\) for \(c\le0\), \(\delta(c)\ge\max\bigl(c+1,\min(2c,3+\delta(c-2))\bigr)\). Now \(T(1)=2\), \(T(2)=3\), \(T(c)=T(c-2)+3\) for \(c\ge3\), and \(T(c)\le2c\). Induction on \(c\) gives \(\delta(c)\ge T(c)\). \(\square\)

The proof uses no property of the field. It fits the \(\ell=0\) values among the 169 computed values over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\) with \(n=2\) (previous entry). An independent brute force by rank over all monomials gives \(T(c)\) for \(c\le9\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_7\) (research/results/bmd-r10-square-and-cover-limit/sq.py). For \(\ell>0\) the step no longer controls the origin order. In characteristic \(\ne2\), only the swap of coordinates fixes both the origin and the cube. In characteristic 2, \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\) acts on the three nonzero points, and the binary theorem covers every such field. So the rest of the conjecture is open for fields of characteristic \(\ne2\).

The polynomial relaxation of the covering problem

Working corollary (cor:polynomial-relaxation-value-for-covers). Let \(q\) be a prime power and \(n,k\ge1\). The least degree of a polynomial over \(\mathbb F_q\) with multiplicity \(\ge k\) on \(\mathbb F_q^n\setminus\{0\}\) and \(<k\) at the origin is

\[ D_q(n,k)=n(q-1)+q(k-1)-\max_{0\le m\le k-1}\Bigl(q\bigl\lfloor m/q^n\bigr\rfloor+s_q\bigl(m\bmod q^n\bigr)\Bigr). \]
  • \(f_q(n,k,1)\ge D_q(n,k)\), because the product of the affine forms of a cover is such a polynomial.
  • No argument that uses only these value and multiplicity conditions can prove more than \(D_q(n,k)\), because \(D_q(n,k)\) is attained.
  • For \(k\le q^n\) the maximum is \(\max_{m<k}s_q(m)\).
  • For \(k\le q\) the value is \(D_q(n,k)=n(q-1)+(q-1)(k-1)\). So the thread's \(Q_k\) is optimal, and its deficit \(k-2\) from the covering target is exactly the deficit of the relaxation.

Proof. Minimize the \(q\)-ary theorem over \(\ell\), using the form \(n(q-1)+q(k-1)-h(k-\ell-1)\). For \(k\le q\), \(m\le k-1<q\le q^n\) gives \(h(m)=s_q(m)=m\), with maximum \(k-1\). \(\square\)

This extends the thread's obstruction to every \(k\), including \(k>q\): the relaxation's gap to the target \(n(q-1)+q(k-1)-1\) is \(\max_mh(m)-1\). For odd \(q\) and \(k\ge3\) the gap \(\max_mh(m)-1\) is at least 1, so a covering proof must use affine factorization, as that thread concluded; the size of the gap is now exact. For \(q=2\) the gap can vanish, for example at \(n\ge2\), \(k=3\).

General statement. thm:cube-square-order-zero-any-field (every field and \(c\)) and cor:polynomial-relaxation-value-for-covers (every prime power \(q\), \(n\), \(k\)). The first proves the \(\ell=0\) case of conj:cube-square-per-order-any-field.

It found no mathematical error. It asked for scope corrections, all made: characteristic 2 versus odd characteristic, the \(\ell=0\) data, odd \(q\) for the gap, the process note, and a shorter status line.

25 September 2026 — The square over every field, every origin order

Working proof: δ_F(2,k,ℓ) = 2k − ⌈(k−ℓ−1)/2⌉ for every field F. Produced by Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5, default reasoning).

Question. The previous entry proved the \(\ell=0\) case of the dimension-two conjecture. It left \(\ell>0\) open in characteristic \(\ne2\), because the step does not keep the origin order.

Outcome. The step is not needed. The line dichotomy alone reduces every case to origin order \(\ell\ge k-2\), and the two base cases follow from the origin-jet lemma.

The theorem

Working theorem (thm:cube-square-per-order-any-field). For every field \(F\) and \(0\le\ell<k\), the least degree of \(P\in F[x_1,x_2]\) with Hasse multiplicity \(\ge k\) at \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\) and \((1,1)\), and exactly \(\ell\) at the origin, is

\[ \delta_F(2,k,\ell)=2k-\Bigl\lceil\frac{k-\ell-1}{2}\Bigr\rceil . \]

Facts over any field. Let \(y_i=x_i^2-x_i\). The following hold over every field, with the same one-line reasons as in the binary origin-jet lemma:

  1. \(F[x_1,x_2]\) has the basis \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^2\), and \(\deg=\max(|\varepsilon|+2|e|)\): \(y_i\) is monic, so the top monomial of \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) is \(x^{\varepsilon+2e}\).
  2. \((y)^j\) is spanned by the basis elements with \(|e|\ge j\), because \(x_i^2=y_i+x_i\), so multiplying by \(x_i\) never lowers \(e\).
  3. \((y)^j=\bigcap_{a\in\{0,1\}^2}\mathfrak m_a^j\): the inclusion holds, the four ideals are comaximal, and both quotients have dimension \(4\binom{j+1}{2}\).
  4. Hasse multiplicity \(\ge j\) at \(a\) means membership in \(\mathfrak m_a^j\).

In particular a nonzero element of \((y)^j\) has degree \(\ge2j\).

Proof. Put \(m=k-\ell-1\) and \(\tau(k,\ell)=2k-\lceil m/2\rceil\), and let \(G=(1-x_1)(1-x_2)(1-x_1-x_2)\).

  • Upper bound. \(y_1\) vanishes simply at all four cube points and has order 1 at the origin. So \(y_1^\ell\) times the polynomial of the order-zero theorem for \(c=k-\ell\) has multiplicity \(\ge k\) off the origin and order exactly \(\ell\) at it. Its degree is \(2\ell+2c-\lceil(c-1)/2\rceil=\tau(k,\ell)\).
  • Dichotomy. This is the line argument of the order-zero theorem, which never used the origin. If \(\deg P<2k\), then \(P=GP'\), where \(P'\) has multiplicity \(\ge k-2\) at the three points. \(G\) is a unit at the origin, so \(P'\) has order exactly \(\ell\) there, and \(\deg P'=\deg P-3\).
  • Induction for \(m\ge2\). Here \(k-2>\ell\), so \(P'\) is an instance with parameters \((k-2,\ell)\). By induction on \(m\), \(\deg P\ge\min(2k,3+\tau(k-2,\ell))=\min(2k,\tau(k,\ell))=\tau(k,\ell)\), since \(\tau(k,\ell)-\tau(k-2,\ell)=3\) and \(\tau\le2k\).
  • Base \(m=1\) (\(k=\ell+2\), target \(2\ell+3\)). Either \(\deg P\ge2k>2\ell+3\), or \(P=GP'\). Then \(P'\) has multiplicity \(\ge\ell\) at all four cube points, so \(P'\in(y)^\ell\) by (c) and (d). By (a) and (b), \(\deg P'\ge2\ell\), so \(\deg P\ge2\ell+3\).
  • Base \(m=0\) (\(k=\ell+1\), target \(2\ell+2=2k\)). Either \(\deg P\ge2k\), or \(P=GP'\), where \(P'\) has multiplicity \(\ge\ell-1\) everywhere and order exactly \(\ell\) at the origin. If \(\ell=0\), \(\deg P\ge3\). If \(\ell\ge1\), then \(P'\in(y)^{\ell-1}\) by (c) and (d). If \(\deg P'\le2\ell-2\), then by (a) and (b) all its terms \(x^\varepsilon y^e\) have \(|e|=\ell-1\) and \(\varepsilon=0\), so \(P'=H(y_1,y_2)\) with \(H\ne0\) homogeneous of degree \(\ell-1\). Its lowest form at the origin is \(H(-x_1,-x_2)\ne0\), of degree \(\ell-1\), which contradicts order \(\ell\). So \(\deg P'\ge2\ell-1\) and \(\deg P\ge2\ell+2\). \(\square\)

Over \(\mathbb F_2\) this is the \(n=2\) case of the binary theorem. It fits all 169 computed values over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\) with \(n=2\) (research/results/bmd-r9-odd-cube/summary.txt). So the conjecture holds.

What this says about \(n=3\). The same scheme needs hyperplanes that avoid the origin and meet the cube in a sub-cube, together with a matching product \(G\). In dimension two the three lines play both roles in every characteristic. In dimension three over odd characteristic, the plane \(x_1+x_2+x_3=1\) meets the cube only in the three unit vectors (in characteristic 2 it also contains \((1,1,1)\)), and the multiplicities of the product differ from point to point. This fits the exchange-law failure at \(n=3\).

General statement. thm:cube-square-per-order-any-field, for every field and all \(0\le\ell<k\). It proves conj:cube-square-per-order-any-field.

Verification gate. Dependencies re-read: the order-zero theorem, its line argument, and the facts (a)–(d).
