The subfield-offset ceiling

Corollary (subfield offset ceiling). Let \(E\) be a finite field of characteristic two and \(K=\mathbb F_{2^k}\subsetneq E\), with \(k\ge1\). Let \(\beta\in E\setminus K\) and let \(g\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) be multilinear of degree at most \(r\), where \(r\ge1\). The system consisting of \(g-\beta=0\) and all Boolean equations \(x_i^2-x_i=0\) has an ordinary PC refutation over \(E\) through degree \(2r\). No bound on \(k\), the number of monomials, or \([E:K]\) is required.

Proof. Write \(g=\sum_S a_Sx_S\), including a possible constant term, and define coefficientwise Frobenius conjugates \[ f_j=\sum_S a_S^{2^j}x_S-\beta^{2^j},\qquad 0\le j\le k. \] Each has degree at most \(r\), and \(f_0\) is an input. From a derived final line \(f_j\), multiply separately by its monomials and take their scalar combination to derive \(f_j^2\). These ordinary PC steps have degree at most \(2r\); the earlier derivation of \(f_j\) is reused without multiplying its old lines. In characteristic two, \[ f_j^2-f_{j+1}=\sum_S a_S^{2^{j+1}}(x_S^2-x_S). \] For \(S=\{i_1,\ldots,i_t\}\), the literal identity \[ x_S^2-x_S=\sum_{h=1}^t(x_{i_h}^2-x_{i_h})\prod_{a<h}x_{i_a}^2\prod_{a>h}x_{i_a} \] has every summand of degree at most \(2t\le2r\). Thus the Boolean axioms derive the displayed difference within the same ceiling, and subtraction derives \(f_{j+1}\). Repeating does not add degree costs. Since all \(a_S\in K\), subtracting \(f_0\) from \(f_k\) gives the constant \(\beta-\beta^{2^k}\). It is nonzero: the roots of \(T^{2^k}-T\) in \(E\) are exactly the \(2^k\) elements of \(K\), by the polynomial root bound. Scalar division yields one. Constant \(g\) already gives a nonzero constant input; this also satisfies the bound. \(\square\)

This is the existing bounded Frobenius argument with multiplier one. Alternatively expand the coefficients in an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis of their span together with \(\beta\); the companion lemma recovers the constant coordinate. Its hypotheses allow an extension-valued input system, so the argument does not require the input itself to be defined over \(\mathbb F_2\). The proof above supplies the complete specialization and its ordinary-degree ledger. Finiteness of \(E\) is retained to match that lemma and the tested source, although the direct argument needs only the finite subfield; strictness follows from the outside-target assumption.

Pair the transfers needed in opposite directions, performing the two moves of each pair consecutively. \[d_F\le c_wm+E_w(m),\qquad c_w=\frac12-\frac1{4w-2},\quad E_w(t)=4w\sqrt{(t+1)\log(4t+4)}+3w+2.\tag{2}\]

Small independent sets: the whole quotient is low-degree. Modulo Booleanity and edge products, every monomial reduces without increasing degree to either zero or an independent-set monomial. Large independent sets: condition on the complement. Choose a maximum independent set \(I\) and put \(C=V(G)\setminus I\), of size \(n-\alpha\). A non-vacuous growing-modulus source

Proposition (growing modulus source). Let \(q\) be an odd prime. Fix a bipartite incidence graph with \(m\) row vertices of degree \(k\ge3\) and \(v\) variable vertices of degree three, with no repeated edge, so \(mk=3v\). Sample every supported matrix entry and every component of \(b\) independently and uniformly in \(\mathbb F_q\); unsupported entries of \(A\) are zero. With probability at least \(1-\varepsilon\), where

\[\varepsilon=\frac{m+3v}{q}+\frac{mq}{2^k}+\frac{2^v}{q^m},\tag{1}\]

the system \(Ax=b\) has full row rank and a field solution, has no Boolean solution, every supported coefficient is nonzero, and every row has at least one allowed Boolean tuple. Each row relation is essential on all its \(k\) variables, every variable occurs in exactly three blocks, and the canonical CNF has at most \(m2^k\) clauses of width \(k\), with no auxiliary variables. With an additional failure probability at most \(q/(m2^k)\), the sum of the row allowed-set sizes is at most \(2m2^k/q\).

Proof. For any row set \(T\), incidence counting gives \(k|T|\le3|N(T)|\), hence \(|N(T)|\ge|T|\). Hall's theorem supplies a matching saturating the rows. The corresponding \(m\)-column minor has a nonzero determinant polynomial: its matching monomial is a product of distinct edge-entry indeterminates and cannot cancel with a different permutation's monomial. Its total degree is \(m\). The elementary finite-field polynomial zero bound therefore gives rank-failure probability at most \(m/q\). This bound is the multiplicity-one consequence of the recorded all-field Schwartz–Zippel theorem. Equivalently, induct on variables, split off a highest power \(d\), and combine the leading-coefficient failure bound with the univariate \(d/q\) root bound. A union bound gives probability at most \(3v/q\) of any zero supported entry. Full row rank makes every \(b\) field-consistent.

For a fixed Boolean vector \(x\), independently sampled targets make \(\Pr[Ax=b]=q^{-m}\). Thus the expected number of Boolean solutions is \(2^v/q^m\), giving the third term in (1). For one row, let \(X\) be the number of allowed tuples. Its \(2^k\) tuple indicators have mean \(1/q\) and are pairwise independent: the two linear forms with coefficient vectors \((x,-1)\) and \((y,-1)\) in the random row entries and target are independent whenever \(x\ne y\). Hence \(\mathbb EX=2^k/q\) and \(\operatorname{Var}X\le2^k/q\). Chebyshev gives \(\Pr[X=0]\le q/2^k\); union bound gives the second term. The row counts are independent across rows, even when scopes overlap, so the same variance calculation for their sum gives the final asserted failure probability. No independence between the four bad events is assumed.

If a row has an allowed tuple and all coefficients are nonzero, flipping any coordinate changes its modular value by a nonzero amount, so that coordinate is essential. This also proves the variables are actual in the canonical encoding. Each forbidden tuple contributes its usual width-\(k\) clause. \(\square\)

Unbounded parameters. For integer \(l\to\infty\), take \(v=l2^l\), \(k=4l\), \(m=3\cdot2^{l-2}\), and choose a prime \(v^2<q<2v^2\). Three partitions into blocks of size \(k\) provide an incidence graph with the required degrees. The bounds in (1), including the additional row-count bound, tend to zero: the local-emptiness term is at most \(O(l^2 2^{-l})\), the rank and coefficient terms are \(O(1/v)\), and \(2^v/q^m\le2^{-v/2}\). The encoding length is \(O(m2^k k\log(v+1))=O(v^5)\). Thus this is an honest unbounded source class, not an assertion of minimality, PC hardness, or applicability of the fixed-width transfer.

Exact star projection

Work over a field of characteristic two with the quadratic encoding. A centre \(c\) has distinct neighbours \(i\in T\), with star equations

\[q_i=(1+u_i+u_c)(1+v_i+v_c).\]

Let \(I_T\) be their ideal in the full Boolean algebra on the star. For a four-element neighbour subset \(U\), define

\[\Delta_U=\det\bigl[(1,u_i,v_i,u_iv_i)\bigr]_{i\in U}.\tag{2}\]

Lemma (four-colour star projection). The elimination ideal \(I_T\cap B_T\) in the boundary Boolean algebra is generated by the \(\Delta_U\) for \(U\subseteq T\), \(|U|=4\). Each generator has degree at most four and has an ordinary PC derivation through degree four from the star quadratics, and through degree four from their original canonical CNF. If \(|T|<4\), the elimination ideal is zero. The statement concerns full ideals and these specific degree-four derivations, not equality of every truncated consequence space.

Proof of elimination. A boundary assignment extends to a centre colour exactly when it uses at most three of the four states. The evaluation matrix of the functions \(1,u,v,uv\) on all four Boolean states has determinant one in characteristic two. Therefore \(\Delta_U\) takes value one exactly when those four neighbours use all four distinct colours, and zero otherwise. The common zero set of all these determinants is precisely the extendible boundary set. Every ideal in a Boolean function algebra is the ideal of functions vanishing on its common zeros: point indicators decompose the algebra into a product of copies of the field. Projection of the full star zero set gives the same extendible set, proving the ideal equality.

Proof of the degree ledger. For four neighbours, let \(M\) have rows

\[\bigl((1+u_i)(1+v_i),\ 1+v_i,\ 1+u_i,\ 1\bigr).\]

Then \(M(1,u_c,v_c,u_cv_c)^T=(q_i)_{i\in U}\) literally. Its determinant equals \(\Delta_U\), since its columns are an invertible triangular change and reversal of the basis in (2), with determinant one in characteristic two. The first row of the adjugate identity gives

\[\Delta_U=\sum_{i\in U} \operatorname{adj}(M)_{1i}q_i.\tag{3}\]

Each adjugate coefficient here omits the first column and uses the remaining column degrees \(1,1,0\), so has degree at most two. Every product in (3) has ordinary degree at most four and is obtained by variable multiplications and linear combinations. The quadratic edge axioms themselves follow from the canonical clauses through degree four; reusing their final lines gives the same ceiling. \(\square\)
