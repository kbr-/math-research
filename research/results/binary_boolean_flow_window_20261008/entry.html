Boolean modular encoding

Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph on \(n\ge2\) vertices, \(m\) edges, and maximum degree \(\Delta\ge1\). Orient its edges, with signs \(\sigma_{ve}\in\{1,-1\}\) chosen oppositely at the endpoints. Fix an integer \(q\ge2\), a field \(F\) with characteristic not dividing \(q\), and charges \(c_v\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) whose sum is nonzero. Use one Boolean variable \(x_e\) per edge. At each vertex require \[\sum_{e\ni v}\sigma_{ve}x_e=c_v\pmod q.\] Encode it by the falsity monomial for each forbidden Boolean assignment to its incident edges, together with the Boolean axioms. Width is at most \(\Delta\), and there are at most \(n2^\Delta\) local clauses. Nonzero total charge proves inconsistency. For the binary goal, take \(F=F_2\) and odd \(q\). This is a Boolean-edge system, not the full \(q\)-label edge system.

Adjoin a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\) in a finite algebraic extension \(E/F\), and define affine polynomials \[u_e(a)=1+(\zeta^a-1)x_e\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z). \] Their exact local identity is \[ u_e(a)u_e(b)-u_e(a+b) =(\zeta^a-1)(\zeta^b-1)(x_e^2-x_e). \] In particular, inverse characters are affine and their cancellation has a degree-two Boolean certificate. A product containing repeated factors on an edge can be reduced to one such factor without exceeding its unreduced degree.

Write \(X_v=\prod_{e\ni v}u_e(\sigma_{ve})\). The vertex relation \(X_v-\zeta^{c_v}\) has a degree-at-most-\(\Delta\) derivation from the forbidden-tuple generators. Indeed, its exact multilinear interpolation is \[ X_v-\zeta^{c_v} =\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}^{E(v)}} \left(\zeta^{\sum_e\sigma_{ve}a_e}-\zeta^{c_v}\right)\chi_a. \] All allowed assignments have coefficient zero; the remaining \(\chi_a\) are the given generators. Thus this is literal polynomial derivability, not merely a semantic implication.

Balance each cut relation

Proposition (balanced modular ceiling). If an ordering of the vertices has cutwidth \(C\), the Boolean modular system just defined has an ordinary PC refutation over \(F\) of degree at most \[ B=\max\{2\Delta,\ \lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta+1\}. \] Consequently, with \[ C_* =\left\lceil\frac{mn}{2(n-1)}+\Delta\sqrt{2n\log(2n)}\right\rceil, \] one can take \(C=C_*\). For fixed \(\Delta\) and \(m=\Theta(n)\), this is \(m/4+O_\Delta(\sqrt{n\log n})\). It is an upper bound on PC degree, not on expanded proof size.

Invariant. For each vertex prefix \(S\), derive a binomial \[A_S-\zeta^{c(S)}B_S,\qquad c(S)=\sum_{v\in S}c_v, \] where \(A_S,B_S\) are character products on disjoint subsets of \(\partial S\), their exponent difference is the oriented cut vector, and each has at most \(\lceil|\partial S|/2\rceil\) factors. Character products are written as products of affine polynomials in the ordinary Boolean variables. The empty prefix has the zero relation, obtainable by subtracting a Boolean axiom from itself; alternatively the first step starts directly with its vertex relation.

Prefix step. Given the invariant and the next vertex \(v\), form \[ (A_S-\zeta^{c(S)}B_S)X_v +\zeta^{c(S)}B_S(X_v-\zeta^{c_v}) =A_SX_v-\zeta^{c(S)+c_v}B_S. \] The multiplication and additions are ordinary PC inferences, using at most \(\lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta\) degree. Multiplication by each affine factor is simulated by variable multiplication and a linear combination. Reduce repeated characters on an edge using the displayed Boolean identity.

If both terms still have a factor on the same edge, multiply the entire relation by the inverse of the factor on the second term and reduce locally. This removes that edge from the second term, and either removes it from both or leaves only the exponent difference on the first. This operation raises temporary degree by at most one; neither reduced support increases. Repeat until the supports are disjoint. Their union is precisely the new prefix cut, because the exponent difference is the sum of the vertex incidence vectors in the prefix.

Finally, whenever one term has more factors than the other by at least two, multiply the relation by the inverse of one factor on its larger side and reduce. The factor moves to the smaller side with inverted exponent, so the exponent difference is unchanged and the larger support decreases. Each move again has temporary degree at most the current larger support plus one. The reduced larger support never exceeds its initial bound \(\lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta\). This establishes the invariant while keeping all intermediate ordinary polynomials through \(B\). No formal division is performed.

At the full prefix both products are one. The resulting constant \(1-\zeta^{\sum_vc_v}\) is nonzero, hence yields a refutation over \(E\). Choose an \(F\)-basis of \(E\) containing one and take coefficient components of every line. Scalar multiplication over \(E\) becomes an \(F\)-linear combination of components, and old-variable multiplication commutes with extraction. This produces an ordinary PC refutation over \(F\) at the same degree. Field descent here need not preserve a useful proof-size bound.

Ordering bound. The earlier ordering proof gives \(C_*\): order independent uniform vertex keys. A prefix of size \(k\) has expected cut \(2mk(n-k)/(n(n-1))\le mn/(2(n-1))\); changing one key changes at most a vertex swap and hence at most \(2\Delta\) crossing edges. Bounded differences gives tail at most \(\exp(-u^2/(2n\Delta^2))\). With \(u=\Delta\sqrt{2n\log(2n)}\), the union bound over nontrivial prefixes is below one. Substitute \(C_*\) in the balanced bound. \(\square\)

The recorded ternary encoding also benefits

Corollary (balanced ternary ceiling). For the full ternary Fourier and one-hot encodings of the previous ceiling, the same bound \(\max\{2\Delta,\lceil C_*/2\rceil+\Delta+1\}\) holds, and is \(m/4+O_\Delta(\sqrt{n\log n})\) on the fixed-degree families.

Proof. In the Fourier system use \(u_e(0)=1\), \(u_e(1)=U_e\), \(u_e(2)=V_e\). The local multiplication table is exactly \(U_e^2=V_e\), \(V_e^2=U_e\), \(U_eV_e=1\), with degree-two generators. Therefore every reduction, cancellation and balancing step above has the same degree certificate. The vertex character generators are already present. The full recorded Fourier-to-one-hot proof costs at most the additional local ceiling \(2\Delta\), with the CNF row conversion through degree three; the displayed bound is at least three when the graph has an edge. Descent from \(F_4\) to the binary one-hot axioms preserves degree as before. This strengthens the prior valid bound, rather than correcting a false claim. \(\square\)

What remains for positive flow

The Alekhnovich–Razborov flow family, Definition 4.11, instead requires positive signed integer flow at every vertex. Its published linear-degree guarantee uses the immunity criterion already bounded in the preceding cycle.

These predicates must be evaluated on Boolean assignments as integer inequalities and then encoded by their Boolean falsity polynomials; reducing the coefficient two in their integer presentation modulo two does not encode the predicate. On an odd-valence star of size at least three, allowed signed net flows include both one and three. For any odd modulus \(q>1\), these have distinct character values, since their difference two is not divisible by \(q\). Hence no single vertex relation of the form \(X_v-\zeta^c\) holds on that allowed set. The balanced modular proof cannot simply be relabelled as a flow proof.
