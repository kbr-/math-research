Fixed-weight source splicing

Theorem. Let \(F\) be any field and \(J\subset F[x]_{\le D}\) a degree-\(D\) PC pseudoideal: an \(F\)-linear subspace closed under variable multiplication whenever the polynomial being multiplied has actual degree below \(D\). No Boolean or field equations are needed for this theorem. Fix an old polynomial \(W\) of degree at most \(b\). Consider an acyclic source \[ P_a=\prod_{u=1}^{h_a}\left(1-\sum_i r_{a,u,i}g_{a,i}\right),\qquad g_{a,i}=H_{a,i}(x,(P_c)_{c

Any two successful maps of the same prefix satisfy \(W(\Phi(P_a)-\Psi(P_a))\in J\) for every block of that prefix. If one successful complete map exists, then every successful prefix extends to a successful complete map by keeping that prefix and using the original complete map's coefficient polynomials in all later blocks, rebuilding products coherently. More generally, entire coefficient blocks can be chosen independently from any family of successful complete maps with this same \(W,T\), and the resulting rebuilt map is successful. Block-local restrictions on coefficient polynomials, such as a selected row-echelon gauge for that block, are inherited from the chosen block; arbitrary constraints coupling different blocks need not be preserved. If all chosen maps additionally satisfy weighted fresh-field equations, so does the spliced map. Surviving weight is an application hypothesis, not needed for the comparison itself.

One-tuple identity. Suppose \(G_i\) are old polynomials of degree at most \(T\delta\), and \(P,Q\) are two accuracy-\(h\) products on that same tuple, with coefficient degrees at most \(T\). Set \(s=h(\delta+1)\). If all \(WG_iP\) and \(WG_iQ\) are in \(J\), then \[W(P-Q)=WP(1-Q)-WQ(1-P)\in J.\] For example, expanding \(1-Q\) by successive prefixes gives \(1-Q=\sum_i G_i A_i\), with \[\deg A_i\le T\bigl((h-1)(\delta+1)+1\bigr)=T(s-\delta).\] Thus each multiple of \(WG_iP\), and similarly of \(WG_iQ\), has degree at most \(b+2Ts\). Each use is legal in the fixed pseudoideal. This is the product-comparison step already used by companion-only descent; it uses neither binary coefficients nor trace.

Induction through the source. Assume the earlier product differences for \(\Phi,\Psi\) belong to \(J\). Let \(G_i=\Phi(g_{a,i})\), \(G_i'=\Psi(g_{a,i})\), and build \[ Q=\prod_u\left(1-\sum_i\Psi(r_{a,u,i})G_i\right). \] This uses \(\Psi\)'s raw coefficient polynomials but \(\Phi\)'s input products. A monomial telescoping argument replaces earlier product atoms one at a time. For an expression of structural weight \(q\), replacing an atom of weight \(s_c\) multiplies its known weighted difference, of actual degree at most \(b+Ts_c\), by factors of degree at most \(T(q-s_c)\). All these multiplications fit \(b+Tq\le D\). It follows that \[ W(Q-\Psi(P_a))\in J,\qquad WG_iQ-WG_i'\Psi(P_a)\in J, \] using respectively \(q=s_a\) and \(q=s_a+\delta_{a,i}\le2L\). Hence \(Q\) is successful on the same tuple as \(\Phi(P_a)\). The one-tuple identity gives \(W(\Phi(P_a)-Q)\in J\) through \(b+2Ts_a\). Adding the first displayed difference proves the induction.

This uses PC reuse at actual degree: a previously obtained low-degree difference may have required the full \(D\) to derive, but its subsequent legal multiplications are checked against its actual degree. The argument does not give ordinary NS cofactors through every smaller intermediate ceiling, and it never treats \(J\) as an unrestricted ideal above \(D\).

Prefix replacement. Compare the supplied successful prefix with the corresponding prefix of the existing complete map using the uniqueness just proved. After its last block, keep the complete map's raw coefficient polynomials. Inductively telescope the earlier weighted product differences in each later product and companion. This proves success and preserves the product comparison with the complete map. No further choice is needed in the suffix. For arbitrary whole-block recombination, at block \(a\) compare the already constructed successful prefix with the prefix of the complete map providing that block; the same argument proves the next block successful. Field equations depend only on that block's coefficient polynomial, so their weighted memberships are copied without reconstruction. This proves all assertions. \(\square\)

What this changes, and what it does not

The result applies to the row-echelon cover at the existing combined reserve: \(b+T\max(B,3L)\le D\) already covers \(b+2TL\). At fixed \(W,T\), a successful prefix with a different rank/pivot pattern cannot destroy the existence of a suffix which existed for another successful prefix. One may search for any successful next block, but must actually solve all of that block's companion equations. A local solver's failure to find a map is not a proof that none exists.

This refines the preceding entry's warning that local row choices can change later inputs. They can change those inputs as polynomials; the theorem shows that completed successful blocks have the same weighted quotient classes, at the stated reserve. It does not apply to merely partially satisfied companion lists, to independently chosen weights, to insufficient multiplication room or to raw-coefficient readers. Those are substantive remaining couplings. The source has not been reduced to independent unconstrained linear systems: even one full block can require several jointly chosen rows.

Nonvacuity. Over any field, take \(J=(x)\cap F[x]_{\le20}\), \(W=1,T=1\), a two-row bottom tuple \((x)\), and a two-row parent tuple \((P-1)\). Their original weights are four and ten, so \(L=10\) fits. Every constant coefficient choice is successful: bottom companions are multiples of \(x\), \(P-1\) is a multiple of \(x\), and so are parent companions. Zero rows give bottom product one; a coefficient one gives a different polynomial \(1-x\). The weighted classes agree and arbitrary successful prefixes splice, although literal products differ. This proper consistent quotient, with any number of unused old variables, tests a nonempty growing family; it is not a Frege hard family.

Degenerate cases. Empty sources or tuples are immediate. With \(W\in J\) the equality remains valid by paid multiplication but gives no lower bound. Accuracy one uses the same identity and reserve. The field may have any characteristic; no Frobenius or Boolean reduction entered the proof. A raw-coefficient parent can read changed coefficients even when prior products agree, so it lies outside the grammar.

Two excluded relaxations. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), use the Boolean-only degree-\(D\) pseudoideal with \(D\ge8\). Two accuracy-one blocks with fixed zero coefficient maps and inputs \(x\) and \(1+x\) are separately successful with surviving weights \(1+x\) and \(x\), but no common nonzero degree-at-most-one weight kills both inputs: adding the two weighted memberships kills the weight itself. Thus “some surviving weight per block” cannot replace “this same weight”. For row mixing, the two-row maps \(((1,0),(0,1))\) and \(((0,1),(1,0))\) on inputs \((x,y)\), with \(W=1\), both succeed. Choosing the first row from the first and the second from the second gives two copies of \((1,0)\); at \((x,y)=(0,1)\) its \(y\)-companion is one. The theorem permits whole-block, not individual-row, recombination. These are direct symbolic counterexamples, not a numerical experiment.
