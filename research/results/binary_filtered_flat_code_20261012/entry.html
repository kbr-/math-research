Affine feature batch bound

Work in the binary algebra \(\mathcal A\) of functions on \(m\) three-state edge pairs, defined by \[I_{\rm loc}=(x_e^2-x_e,y_e^2-y_e,x_ey_e:e\in[m]).\] Write \(V_K\) for its ordinary filtered degree space and \(B_m(K)=\sum_{j\le K}2^j\binom mj\). Local normal reduction never increases degree; a polynomial that is zero as a local function has a certificate from these generators through its actual degree.

Fix \(q\ge1\) pairwise disjoint nonempty edge sets \(S_i\), not necessarily covering all edges, and integers \(1\le r\le d\). On \(S_i\), let \[z_i=\prod_{u=1}^{r_i}(1+L_{iu}),\qquad r\le r_i\le d,\] where the affine \(L_{iu}\) have independent homogeneous parts over \(\mathbb F_2\). Thus \(z_i\) indicates an affine zero flat on the full binary coordinates, restricted to the local three-state domain; that intersection may be empty. A tuple of actual polynomials of degrees at most \(\delta\ge1\) has local classes \[g_j=\ell_j+F_j(z_1,\ldots,z_q),\] where each \(\ell_j\) is homogeneous old-linear and each \(F_j\) is an arbitrary Boolean feature function. Constants are in \(F_j\). The supplied decomposition need not be unique, and no degree bound on its written feature expression is assumed.

Theorem. Let \(1\le K<m/2\), \(s,H\ge1\) be integers, and put \[\rho=K/m,\quad \theta=\frac{K}{2(m-K)},\quad a=\frac{1+\theta}{1+2\theta}=1-\rho/2,\quad t=s\lceil\log_2(q+1)\rceil+1.\] There is a constant binary matrix \(C\) with at most \(H+t\) rows, in the original input positions, for which \(P_C=\prod_u(1+(Cg)_u)\) satisfies \[\frac{\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_K\mapsto(P_Cg_jf)_j)}{B_m(K)} \le \epsilon:=(m+1)\Pr\{Z>s\}+(m+1)(q+1)^s(2/\theta)^{sd}a^H, \qquad Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(q,a^r).\tag{1}\] The rank in (1) is in the full local function algebra. The bound may exceed one; it is used only when it is small. If \(s\ge2eqa^r\), its first term is at most \((m+1)2^{-s}\).

Filtered local kernels. In each block algebra \(\mathcal A_i\), choose a basis of \(\ker(f\mapsto z_if)\) adapted to the degree filtration, and extend it to a degree-adapted basis of \(\mathcal A_i\). Call the former basis inactive and the added vectors active. These are vector-space choices, not assertions that both complements are invariant under multiplication. If \(H_i(u)=(1+2u)^{|S_i|}\) is the full degree polynomial and \(A_i(u)\) the active polynomial, then its degree-\(j\) coefficient is the increment of the restriction Hilbert function on \(\{z_i=1\}\).

The common-flat level proof gives \[A_i(u)\ \le_{\rm coeff}\ (1+2u)^{p_0}(1+u)^{p_1},\quad p_0+p_1+p_2=|S_i|,\quad p_1+2p_2=r_i.\tag{2}\] For completeness, take a degree-compatible monomial order and row-echelon the independent affine constraints. Their leading pivot variables, all Boolean squares, and the pair exclusions belong to the initial vanishing ideal of the flat intersected with the local domain. Standard monomials therefore use neither pivots nor both indicators of an untouched pair. Their degree counts give (2); an empty intersection has zero Hilbert function. This is the exact-level argument of the recorded flat-image refinement, not differentiation of a sum of image bounds.

At \(u=\theta\), eliminating one of a pair's variables has ratio \(a\); eliminating both has ratio \((1+2\theta)^{-1}\le a^2\). Hence \[A_i(\theta)/H_i(\theta)\le a^{r_i}\le a^r.\] Tensor the chosen bases, retaining the ordinary basis on unused edges. This is degree-adapted because the local degree filtration is the tensor-product filtration. A component with active index set \(T\) is annihilated by every \(z_i\) for \(i\notin T\). Every function in that component is consequently supported only where those features are zero. No projector on these components is asserted to preserve an old charged quotient.

Under the positive weights obtained by evaluating the dimension polynomials at \(\theta\), activity in distinct blocks is independent and each probability is at most \(a^r\). Thus the degree-at-most-\(K\) part of components with \(|T|>s\) has dimension at most \[\theta^{-K}(1+2\theta)^m\Pr\{Z>s\}\le B_m(K)(m+1)\Pr\{Z>s\}.\tag{3}\] The last step uses that \(K\) is a mode of \(\operatorname{Bin}(m,K/m)\), so its probability is at least \(1/(m+1)\). The standard exponential-moment estimate \(\Pr(Z>s)\le(eqa^r/s)^s\) proves the stated tail bound. If \(s\ge q\), that tail is zero.

Low affine rank. Put \(R=\dim\operatorname{span}\{\ell_j\}\). If \(R<H\), choose \(R\) actual input positions whose linear parts form a basis, and include these inputs as coefficient rows, giving product \(Q\). Subtract their appropriate binary combinations from every original input to obtain legal input combinations \(g'_j\) which are functions only of \(z\). On the support of \(Q\), all chosen inputs vanish, so \(g_j=g'_j\).

There are at most \((q+1)^s\) feature patterns of weight at most \(s\), including zero, by sorted padded support lists. For each such pattern with \(g'(z)\ne0\), \(t\) uniform independent binary rows miss it with probability \(2^{-t}\). Their union probability is at most one half. Choose rows detecting all of them, and express them back in the original input positions. On every component with \(|T|\le s\), the product of these code factors with the whole companion tuple is zero pointwise: on the support of \(Qf\), the feature vector has weight at most \(s\), and either the tuple vanishes or a selected row is one. Multiplying by \(Q\) cannot enlarge that support. Thus (3) bounds the entire rank. The row count is at most \(R+t<H+t\), without any assumption of component invariance under raw affine multiplication.

High affine rank. If \(R\ge H\), choose \(H\) original inputs with independent homogeneous linear parts, and let \(P\) be their complementary product. The full companion map factors through multiplication by \(P\), so its rank is at most that multiplication rank. On the bad components use (3). For a component with \(|T|\le s\), condition only on the affine probes defining its active features. There are \(L_T=\sum_{i\in T}r_i\le sd\) probes and at most \(2^{L_T}\) assignments \(\beta\). Their Boolean selectors \(\chi_\beta\) have degree at most \(L_T\) and sum to one. On the support of \(\chi_\beta f\), all feature values are fixed: outside \(T\) they are zero, and inside they are fixed by the probes. Consequently, as local functions, \[Pf=\sum_\beta P_{T,\beta}\chi_\beta f,\tag{4}\] where \(P_{T,\beta}\) is the indicator of \(H\) independent affine equations \(\ell_j+c_{j,T,\beta}=0\). Their full-cube affine system is consistent; its intersection with the local domain may be empty.

Restriction of degree-at-most-\(K+L_T\) polynomials to that flat has rank bounded by the cumulative local pivot count. Evaluate that count at the same \(\theta\), not at a new cutoff: \[\operatorname{rank}(u\in V_{K+L_T}\mapsto P_{T,\beta}u) \le\theta^{-(K+L_T)}(1+2\theta)^m a^H \le B_m(K)(m+1)\theta^{-sd}a^H.\tag{5}\] There are at most \((q+1)^s\) relevant active sets and at most \(2^{sd}\) selectors for each. Sum these cumulative ranks in (4), and add (3). This gives (1). Every selector and degree shift is counted. Equations (4)–(5) are an analysis in the full local algebra; no conditioning operator or large auxiliary expression is transported into the charged quotient. \(\square\)

Paid quotient transfer. Let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) binary PC pseudoideal containing the local equations, \(\deg w\le b\), and \(H_w=\dim[wV_K]>0\). At \[b+K+(H+t+1)\delta\le D,\tag{6}\] the weighted error rank in \(J_D\) is no larger than the local rank (1). Indeed every local linear relation among the actual error columns is a local-zero polynomial of degree at most \(K+(H+t+1)\delta\). Degree-nonincreasing local reduction certifies it, then multiplication by \(w\) fits (6). The map also factors through \([wV_K]\), by paid absorption. For \(M\) tuples, possibly with different disjoint dictionaries, if the sum of their \(\epsilon B_m(K)\) bounds is below \(H_w\), a common multiplier \(f\in V_K\) survives and kills all their companions. This is the actual weighted denominator; it is not replaced by \(B_m(K)\). Constant coefficients kill all their fresh fields. Original source replay has its separate ceiling.

Local flat source exclusion

Corollary. Use the recorded growing-expansion charged family with \[\Delta=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil,\quad n\Delta/8\le m\le n\Delta,\quad D=\lfloor n\Delta/64\rfloor,\quad K=\lfloor m/1024\rfloor,\quad \log N=\Theta((\log n)^2).\] Set \(h=\lceil(\log n)^5\rceil\), \(B=\lceil(\log n)^{11}\rceil\). Fix \(A>0\). For sufficiently large \(n\), consider complete binary two-level ENS sources with at most \(M\le N^A\) bottom blocks and at most \(M\) parents, common original accuracy \(h\), private own coefficient domains, all companions and all coefficient fields. Bottom input spans are proper old-affine spans of ranks \[\lceil16384\log n\rceil\le r_i\le d:=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil.\tag{7}\] Every parent input is literally old-affine plus a constant binary combination of genuine bottom products. For each parent, the distinct bottom products appearing in its tuple are supported on pairwise disjoint edge sets. The partition may differ by parent; supports may overlap arbitrarily across parents, and shared products have one interpretation. There are no raw earlier-coefficient readers or parent-to-parent inputs. Then the complete source has no degree-\(B\) PC or ordinary NS refutation. One global constant coefficient assignment and a surviving old weight of degree at most \(K\) suffice.

Proof. Since \(h\ge d\) eventually, select a full affine basis as constant rows in every bottom and zero-pad. Its product is the exact affine-flat indicator, and all its companions are Boolean-zero through the paid original ceiling. Normalize each shared bottom once. Specialized parent inputs have degree at most \(d\), with the feature representation required by the theorem.

The growing-family occupancy calculation gives \(\dim[V_K]\ge B_m(K)/2\) eventually, in a proper binary old quotient. Also \(\rho\ge1/2048\), so with \(r=\lceil16384\log n\rceil\), \[a^r\le e^{-\rho r/2}\le n^{-4},\qquad qa^r\le mn^{-4}=o(1)\] for every parent's \(q\le m\). Use the common upper bound \(q=m\), allowing dummy inactive factors in the count; they add no source inputs. Choose \[s=\lceil\log_2(8M(m+1))\rceil,\quad t=s\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+1,\] \[H=\left\lceil\frac{\log(8M(m+1))+s\log(m+1)+sd\log(2/\theta)}{-\log a}\right\rceil.\tag{8}\] For large \(n\), the first term of (1) is at most \(1/(8M)\), and (8) makes its second at most \(1/(8M)\). Thus every parent rank is at most \(B_m(K)/(4M)\). All parent maps together have rank at most \(B_m(K)/4<\dim[V_K]\); select a surviving common \(W\) of degree at most \(K\).

Since \(\log M=O_A((\log n)^2)\), \(\theta\) and \(1-a\) are bounded away from zero by constants and \[s=O_A((\log n)^2),\quad t=O_A((\log n)^3),\quad H=O_A((\log n)^4).\] Hence \(H+t\le h\) eventually; pad all parent rows to their original accuracy. Original bottom product weights are \(2h\), parent input weights at most \(2h\), and original companion ceilings at most \(2h^2+3h\), which are below \(B\) eventually. The old CNF widths and degree-two domain axioms also lie below \(B\). Finally \(K+B\le D\), since \(K\le n\Delta/1024\) and \(B=o(n\Delta)\). In particular (6) with \(w=1,\delta=d\) holds; the written specialized degrees do not replace the original ceiling.

Every bottom companion remains a local zero and every parent companion is killed by \(W\) in \(J_D\); fresh fields are zero. Substitute the one coherent constant map into a hypothetical original degree-\(B\) proof and multiply all lines by \(W\). Old generators, local certificates and each PC multiplication stay through \(K+B\le D\). The final unit would kill \(W\), contradiction. Ordinary NS replays in PC at its original degree. The graph, accuracies and proof ceiling are fixed independently of \(A\); only the sufficiently-large threshold depends on it. \(\square\)

Controls and scope

Nonvacuity. On any sufficiently large member of the same charged family, partition edges into blocks of \(2r\) edges with \(r=\lceil16384\log n\rceil\), leaving leftover edges unused. On each block pair its edges and use the \(r\) independent constraints \(x_{e_{2j-1}}+x_{e_{2j}}=0\). Their common zero set meets the local domain, and the flat is noncoordinate. This realizes unboundedly many features with rank in (7); parent inputs may be \(x_{e_j}+z_j\) for all those features. Different parents can use different partitions and arbitrary old-affine mixtures, reusing shared bottoms coherently. All data have the required polynomial-in-\(N\) description when any polynomially bounded inventory is selected. The theorem does not require all old axioms to lie in the feature algebra, avoiding the inactive-feature obstruction.

Degenerate cases. A feature whose flat misses the local domain has zero active Hilbert polynomial and only helps the bound. Rank-zero and unit bottom spans are outside (7), rather than silently counted as rare features. For a parent using no features, use the same affine-rank split directly: fewer than \(H\) affine directions are killed exactly by a basis (or a unit row), while \(H\) independent directions give rank at most \((m+1)a^H B_m(K)\), within (8)'s bound. Thus no artificial rare feature is needed. An empty parent inventory needs only bottom normalization. A zero tuple has zero companion map. If a tuple contains the unit as a legal row, it has an immediate zero-product choice; (1) need not recognize every such simplification. At \(s\ge q\) the bad tail is zero. The excluded \(K=0\) lacks the positive \(\theta\) used in this count. Intersections and probes throughout are in the local domain, never satisfying points of the inconsistent charged base.

The full-kernel complement is not a new coordinate system preserving all multiplications. Low-rank removal uses semantic support, and the high-rank case explicitly pays probe branches and the degree shift. This avoids the false full-cell basis from the prefix-basis test and the prohibition on summing separate exact-degree error bounds. The outside-affine diversity floor concerns fixed zero-column certificates; the present high-rank argument bounds the image itself and need not make each basis column zero. No finite-field positivity or free projection is used.
