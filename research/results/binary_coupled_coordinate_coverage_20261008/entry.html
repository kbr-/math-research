Imported diagonal partition rank

Imported theorem. For any field, any finite set \(X\), and tensor order \(d\ge2\), the partition rank of a diagonal tensor with \(m\) nonzero diagonal entries is \(m\). Partition rank counts sums of products across arbitrary nontrivial bipartitions of the slots. This is Naslund, The Partition Rank of a Tensor and k-Right Corners, Lemma 11. The definition, statement and induction proof were read in the primary source. The proof contracts one slot, annihilating singleton terms with a large-support functional; ordinary matrix rank starts the induction. The theorem has no characteristic restriction. The paper's separate right-corner application has a characteristic restriction which is not imported here.

Diagonal factor cost

Let \(F\) be any field, \(t\ge1\), \(d=2t\), and \(M\ge1\). Take independent Boolean variables \(x_{i,j}\), \(1\le i\le M\), \(1\le j\le d\). Put \[ z_i=\prod_{j=1}^d x_{i,j},\qquad C=\sum_{i=1}^M c_i z_i,\qquad m=|\{i:c_i\ne0\}|. \] Independent spectator variables are allowed. Let \(R\) have degree below \(d\).

Theorem (diagonal factor cost). If, as functions on the full Boolean cube over \(F\), \[ C+R=\sum_{\ell=1}^s E_\ell F_\ell,\qquad \deg E_\ell,\deg F_\ell\le t, \] then \[ s\binom{d}{t}\ge m. \] The factors need not be Boolean-valued. In particular extending the coefficient field does not bypass this bound. It is a full-cube statement, not a claim modulo arbitrary additional old equations or after weighting.

Proof: retain only squarefree top degree. Fix spectator variables to constants and replace every factor by its multilinear representative; degrees do not increase and the functional equality persists. Every product now has ordinary degree at most \(2t=d\). Boolean reduction of a nonsquarefree monomial of that degree strictly lowers its degree. Therefore equality of functions forces equality of the squarefree degree-\(d\) coefficients of the displayed ordinary polynomials. The lower-degree \(R\) contributes nothing.

Group the variables by their second index \(j\). For \((i_1,\ldots,i_d)\in[M]^d\), extract the coefficient of \[x_{i_1,1}\cdots x_{i_d,d}.\] For \(C\) this tensor is \(c_i\) when every \(i_j=i\), and zero otherwise: it is diagonal with \(m\) nonzero entries.

For one product \(EF\), a contribution to such a degree-\(d\) squarefree monomial must assign exactly \(t\) of its slots to \(E\) and the complementary \(t\) to \(F\). Thus its coefficient tensor is \[ \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[d]\\|S|=t}} e_S((i_j)_{j\in S})\,f_{[d]\setminus S}((i_j)_{j\notin S}), \] where \(e_S\) is the coefficient function of \(E\) on the specified slots, and similarly for \(F\). Each summand has partition rank at most one. The tensor of the whole sum has partition rank at most \(s\binom dt\). The imported diagonal theorem gives its rank \(m\), proving the inequality. No polarization inverse or division by \(d!\) is used, so the argument remains valid in small characteristic. \(\square\)

The joint degree ceiling is essential to this proof. If \(d=3\) but both factor bounds are two, the product \((xy)(xz)\) reduces to \(xyz\); its ordinary degree-four repeated variable contributes to reduced degree three. That case is outside \(d=2t\), and the present coefficient extraction does not establish its analogous bound. The theorem nevertheless covers unbounded even degrees, including the growing-degree family below.

A basis-independent dense reader space

For every sufficiently large \(M\), there is a binary linear code \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^M\) of dimension \(r=\lfloor M/16\rfloor\) whose nonzero words have weight greater than \(M/4\). Here is the needed existence proof. Choose a uniform binary \(r\)-by-\(M\) generator matrix. Each fixed nonzero message has uniform image in \(\mathbb F_2^M\). The number of words of weight at most \(M/4\) is bounded by \[ \sum_{j\le M/4}\binom Mj \le 3^{M/4}(1+1/3)^M. \] Indeed multiply each term by \((1/3)^j\), use \(j\le M/4\), and bound by the full binomial sum. A union bound over fewer than \(2^{M/16}\) nonzero messages gives failure probability below \[ \left(\frac{2^{17/16}}{3^{3/4}}\right)^M<1, \] since its base to the sixteenth power is \(2^{17}/3^{12}<1\). Thus a matrix avoiding all such words exists; it is injective because zero is among the forbidden images. This elementary existence argument is not a new coding-theory construction.

Use a basis of \(\mathcal C\) to define the actual reader tuple \[g_a=\sum_i c_{a,i}z_i\quad(1\le a\le r).\] Every nonzero constant combination, hence every nonzero coordinate in any constant basis, has a coefficient word of weight greater than \(M/4\). This remains true after any characteristic-two scalar extension: expand the finitely many message coefficients in a binary-independent field basis. The resulting word's support is the union of its binary component words' supports; some component is nonzero. Consequently every nonzero direction in the extended reader span still has weight greater than \(M/4\).

The factor-cost theorem therefore forces every such direction's decomposition into products of degree at most \(t=d/2\) to use more than \[ \frac{M}{4\binom d{d/2}} \] terms. Adding an affine offset does not alter its degree-\(d\) coefficient tensor. This is not cured by finding a different basis or enlarging the coefficient field.

Dense reader factor gap

Corollary (dense reader factor gap). Use the preceding code, even \(d\), and parent accuracy \(h\) satisfying \[h<\frac{M}{4\binom d{d/2}}.\] Under the exact bottom interpretation \(P_i=z_i\), no nonzero coordinate in the reader span admits a direct application of the coupled-row theorem with factor degree at most \(d/2\) and its displayed \(s+1\) rows fitting this accuracy. This is a failure of that advertised criterion; further compression of dependent row values or different weighted constructions are not ruled out.

It occurs in a complete two-layer source over the actual charged old family. Choose \(Md\) selected edge indicators on pairwise edge-disjoint cycles, grouped into \(M\) sets of size \(d\), using the recorded cycle-rich charged graph family and its binary restriction. The graph is simple, connected and oriented, has nonzero total charge and expansion \(\varepsilon\); retain \(\max(4,2\Delta)\le D<\varepsilon n/8\). Bottom \(i\) has accuracy \(d\), literal inputs \((1-x_{i,j})_j\), every companion and fresh field. One parent of accuracy \(h\) reads the above basis combinations of the genuine bottom products. Its original degree ceiling is \[ B=h(2d+1)+2d, \] which dominates the bottom companion degree \(2d+1\) and fresh fields. Include \(B\ge\Delta\) for old generators.

Nevertheless, if \(M+B\le D\), this complete source has an explicit surviving weight and a global constant coefficient map. Normalize each bottom exactly by selecting its \(d\) literals once, set every parent coefficient to zero, and take \[ W=\prod_{i=1}^M(1-x_{i,1}). \] Every \(Wz_i\) has a Boolean zero factor and a certificate through \(M+d\). Thus all parent companions vanish after weighting, while bottom companions already vanish and every fresh coefficient field maps to zero. The original proof window still costs \(M+B\), not a reduced parent degree after zero-padding. The selected-edge characters are distinct modulo cuts, so the Fourier expansion of \(W\) has nonzero coefficients on distinct classes; \(\deg W=M\le D\) and \(W\notin J_D\). Weighted replay would put \(W\) in the old pseudoideal if a source refutation through \(B\) existed. This proves the positive cleanup in the same hard quotient.

Growing-degree, nonvacuous window. Let \[d=2\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2\log_2 M}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad N=Md,\qquad h=\lfloor N^\eta\rfloor,\quad0<\eta<1.\] For large \(M\), \(d\ge2\) tends to infinity and \(\binom d{d/2}\le2^d\le\log_2 M\). Hence \(M/(4\binom d{d/2})>h\) eventually. The source inventory and its written coefficient matrix are polynomial in \(N\). The triangle-replacement expander with \(N\) cycles has \(n=3N\) vertices and a proper window \(D=\Theta(N)\) with the recorded fixed constant. Here \[M+B=\frac Nd+O(dN^\eta)=o(N),\qquad (d/2)B=O(d^2N^\eta)=o(N).\] So both the successful weighted map and the intended balanced-map replay would fit the old window; it is the latter's coordinate/row premise that fails. This is not a finite-degree extrapolation. It also does not refute the goal: the displayed source is deliberately easy by the explicit weight. \(\square\)
