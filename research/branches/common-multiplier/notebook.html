Detection becomes annihilation

Statement. Let \(U,V,W,Z\) be finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field, \(C\le V\), \(\mathcal E\subseteq V\), \(\beta:U\times V\to W\) bilinear and \(Q:W\to Z\) linear. Assume (i) \(Q\beta(U,C)=0\); (ii) for each \(e\in\mathcal E\), \(\operatorname{rank}\beta_e<\dim U\), where \(\beta_e(a)=\beta(a,e)\); (iii) \(\ker Q\cap\beta(U,e)=\{0\}\) for each such \(e\); and (iv) for every nonzero \(a\in U\), the map \(c\mapsto\beta(a,c)\) is injective on \(C\). Given \(y=c+e\) with \(c\in C,e\in\mathcal E\), find any nonzero solution of \(Q\beta(a,y)=0\), then solve \(\beta(a,c')=\beta(a,y)\) with \(c'\in C\). These two linear systems recover \(c\) uniquely. If \(\operatorname{rank}\beta_e\le r\), the first solution space has dimension at least \(\dim U-r\).

Proof. By (i), the first system is \(Q\beta_e(a)=0\). Its kernel contains \(\ker\beta_e\), whose dimension is at least \(\dim U-r>0\). Every solution satisfies \(\beta_e(a)\in\ker Q\cap\beta(U,e)\), hence \(\beta_e(a)=0\) by (iii). Consequently \(\beta(a,y)=\beta(a,c)\). Existence of the second solution follows, and uniqueness is (iv). With bases and the bilinear map supplied, ordinary linear algebra constructs both systems. The theorem supplies no efficient method for finding spaces satisfying the hypotheses.

Duality produces multipliers

Statement. Let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional commutative unital \(k\)-algebra with a fixed functional \(\tau:A\to k\) such that \((a,b)\mapsto\tau(ab)\) is nondegenerate. For every ideal \(I\) and \(p\in A\),

\[p\notin I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists f\in A:\ fI=0,\quad\tau(fp)\ne0.\]

If \(A=\bigoplus_{j=0}^s A_j\), \(\tau\) is supported in degree \(s\), and each pairing \(A_j\times A_{s-j}\to k\) is perfect, then for homogeneous \(I\) and homogeneous \(p\in A_d\setminus I\) one may take \(f\in A_{s-d}\). This is standard Frobenius duality, formulated to retain the protected element. It is not an assertion that a prescribed bounded PC space has these properties.

Proof. If \(p\notin I\), choose a linear functional \(\lambda\) vanishing on \(I\) but not on \(p\), by extending a basis of \(I\) through \(p\). Perfectness represents it uniquely as \(\lambda(a)=\tau(fa)\). For \(e\in I\) and every \(a\in A\), \(ae\in I\), whence \(\tau(aef)=0\). Nondegeneracy gives \(ef=0\). Conversely \(p\in I\) would force \(fp=0\). In the graded case choose \(\lambda\) supported in degree \(d\), zero on \(I_d\), and nonzero on \(p\); the perfect degree pairing represents it with \(f\in A_{s-d}\), and homogeneity makes it zero on all of \(I\). The preceding argument applies unchanged.

Geometric content. Perfect multiplication turns a linear separator into an actual multiplicative separator. The truncated polynomial algebra with complementary monomial bases is an explicit source of such a pairing, already used in prop:freeness-propagation. The same algebraic structure is often called a Poincare duality algebra; no theorem about arbitrary topological spaces is needed here. The concrete model \(k[u,v]/(u^2,v^2)\), with \(\tau\) extracting \(uv\), has dual basis pairs \((1,uv),(u,v),(v,u),(uv,1)\). For \(I=(u)\), \(p=v\), choose \(f=u\); then \(fI=0\), \(fp=uv\ne0\). Its one-point reduced geometry loses exactly the nilpotent information that makes the multiplier work. Miles Reid's Chapter 6 notes, Example 6.8, give the complementary-monomial pairing; the proof above uses the explicit pairing hypothesis, not an inferred pairing on the proof-complexity quotient.

Transfer and its limits

Statement and proof: localization. Let \(M\) be a module over a commutative ring \(R\), \(S\) a multiplicative subset, and let finitely many errors \(e_i\) become zero in \(S^{-1}M\), while \(p/1\ne0\). The definition of module localization gives \(s_i\in S\) with \(s_i e_i=0\); then \(s=\prod_i s_i\) kills all errors and \(sp\ne0\), since otherwise \(p/1=0\). In a multiplicative filtration the degree of this construction is at most the sum of the individual degrees. No subadditive improvement follows. The empty family uses \(s=1\). See the definitions of module localization in Stacks, Section 10.9. In the preceding duality example, localizing at \(f=u\) gives the zero ring since \(u^2=0\); thus useful common multipliers need not be localization denominators preserving the signal.

Statement and proof: scalar descent. Fix finite-dimensional \(k\)-spaces \(U,V_i\), linear error maps \(T_i:U\to V_i\) and a forbidden subspace \(L\le U\). A finite field extension \(K/k\) contains a vector in \(\bigcap_i\ker(T_i\otimes K)\setminus(L\otimes K)\) if and only if the base field contains one in \(\bigcap_i\ker T_i\setminus L\). Expand a vector as \(\sum_j\theta_j f_j\) in a field basis. Each \(T_i f_j=0\) by independence of the basis, and at least one \(f_j\notin L\). The reverse direction is scalar extension. Coefficients stay in the same degree-filtered base space, so there is no degree increase. This is linear descent, requiring neither a Galois extension nor norms. It does not descend a simultaneously chosen nonlinear approximation family, and does not replace Pang's Frobenius specialization argument.

Statement and proof: obstructions. In \(A=k[x,y]/(x,y)^2\), take \(I=(x)\) and \(p=y\). Then \(p\notin I\), but \(\operatorname{Ann}(I)=(x,y)\), whose elements all kill \(p\). Indeed \((a+bx+cy)x=ax\), so annihilation forces \(a=0\). Thus linear separation alone does not imply multiplicative separation without duality or another mechanism. In \(A_m=k[t]/(t^{m+1})\), \(m\ge1\), take \(I=(t)\), \(p=1\). Here \(\operatorname{Ann}(I)=kt^m\), so every nonzero annihilator has least representative degree \(m\). All these rings have one-point reduced spectrum, yet this degree is unbounded. Even perfect duality gives no uniform cheapness. Finally on \(t\ge1\) distinct evaluation points, the one-row error data matrix \((1,\ldots,1)\) has rank one, but a nonzero polynomial vanishing at all points has degree at least \(t\), by the root bound. Low rank of the error data is not low rank of its action on multipliers.

Homological reading. For a genuine module and \(I=(e_1,\ldots,e_r)\), the first map of the cochain Koszul complex is \(m\mapsto(e_1m,\ldots,e_rm)\). Its degree-zero cohomology is precisely \(\operatorname{Ann}_M(I)=\operatorname{Hom}_R(R/I,M)\), by the cyclic-map proof. This is an exact organization of the problem, not a bound on the first nonzero degree or a survival theorem. No assertion about higher Koszul homology is needed. Statement. Let \(X/k\) be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve of genus \(g\), \(A\) a divisor, and \(E\) an effective divisor of degree \(e\). If \(\deg A\ge e+g\), then \(L(A-E)\ne0\). Rank retention sampling

Statement. Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) any prime power, and let \(M_1,\ldots,M_s\) be matrices over \(k\). Suppose their generic pencil has rank \(R\), and that \(R\) is known or structurally certified. A uniform independent \(a\in k^s\) satisfies

\[\Pr\!\left[\operatorname{rank}\sum_i a_iM_i\ge R-\lfloor R/q\rfloor\right]\ge\frac1{R+1}.\tag{1}\]

Repeated independent draws, accepting only after an exact rank test, therefore terminate almost surely in at most \(R+1\) expected draws. At \(R=0\), choose the zero vector immediately. This is a bound in matrix-rank tests, not a promise that the input matrices or generic rank are cheaply available.

Proof of the rank expectation. Pang's rank-specialization proof works over every finite field. To make the extension explicit, take a nonzero maximal minor \(H\) of the generic pencil, of total degree at most \(R\). At a point \(a\), constant row operations make at least \(R-\operatorname{rank}M(a)\) rows of that minor vanish, so its multiplicity at \(a\) is at least that deficiency. For a nonzero polynomial of degree \(d\) on \(k^s\), the sum of point multiplicities is at most \(dq^{s-1}\). Here is the induction: write \(H(y,z)=\sum_{j=0}^tH_j(y)z^j\), \(H_t\ne0\); at \(y=b\), a lowest-degree nonzero coefficient of \(H_t(b+y)\), of degree \(\mu\), is the leading coefficient of a degree-\(t\) polynomial in \(z\). Thus each full multiplicity is at most \(\mu\) plus that univariate multiplicity. Summing over \(z\in k\) gives at most \(q\mu+t\); induction on \(H_t\), and \(\deg H_t+t\le d\), finish. The univariate case is the root-multiplicity bound. Averaging the determinant deficiencies gives \(\mathbb E\operatorname{rank}M(a)\ge(1-1/q)R\).

Integer acceptance bound. Write \(R=qv+r\), \(0\le r<q\), and \(b=R-v\). If acceptance has probability \(\theta\), the rank is at most \(b-1\) on failure and at most \(R\) always. Therefore \(\theta\ge(q-r)/(q(v+1))\). This is at least \(1/(R+1)\), since \((q-r)(R+1)-q(v+1)=R(q-r-1)\ge0\). The geometric waiting-time formula proves (1). For \(q=2,R=1,M(a)=a\), acceptance probability is exactly one half, so the bound is attained in a nontrivial case.

Finite-grid repair

Setting. Work over \(k=\mathbb F_q\). \[\Delta=(q-1)dt,\qquad b+K+\Delta+d\le D,\qquad \eta=(1-1/q)\frac{\mathcal N_A(K-d)}{\mathcal N_A(K)},\qquad M(1-\eta)^t<1.\tag{2}\]

Imported combinatorial input. A product of finite chains has the normalized-matching property: for a subset of a rank level, its upper shadow occupies at least the same fraction of the next level. \[c_A(K-d)\ge\frac{\mathcal N_A(K-d)}{\mathcal N_A(K)}c_A(K).\tag{4}\]

In particular (0) holds whenever \(\operatorname{rank}\rho<\dim S/J\), and so whenever \(\sum_ar_a<\dim S/J\) for upper bounds \(r_a\ge\operatorname{rank}\rho_a\). A basis of \(\ker\rho\) and reduction modulo \(J\) find a vector of \(\ker\rho\setminus J\) when (0) holds.

Proof. \(\ker(\rho,q)=\ker\rho\cap J\). Rank–nullity on \(S\) for both maps gives \(\dim\ker\rho-\dim(\ker\rho\cap J)=\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)-\operatorname{rank}\rho\). The left side is positive exactly when \(\ker\rho\not\subseteq J\). For the sufficient forms, \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)\ge\operatorname{rank}q=\dim S/J\), and \(\operatorname{rank}\rho\le\sum_a\operatorname{rank}\rho_a\le\sum_ar_a\).

Use in the composition. In thm:certified-repair-composition take \(J=S\cap J_D\). Its proof uses the summed bound only to obtain some \(w\in\bigcap_a\ker\rho_a\setminus J_D\); the exact-repair construction, the finite-grid stage, the absorption of earlier errors and replay all start from that chosen \(w\), as its full proof states. So the composition holds verbatim with its hypothesis \(\sum_ar_a<H_0\) replaced by any certificate of (0). This changes the selection hypothesis, not its later costs. A certificate of (0) is itself a cost: an independent bound on each \(\rho_a\) does not bound the stacked rank better than the sum, and for Braun's and Pang's adapters no joint bound is recorded. The curve theorem below supplies one in its own model. It is a linear-algebra refinement; by its registered statement, read in this cycle, lem:weight-compatibility-factorization already uses the predicate “common kernel nonzero in the quotient by the forbidden space” for two families.

Overlapping curve recovery

Setting and statement. Use a smooth projective geometrically integral curve \(X/k\) of genus \(g\) over a finite field, distinct rational received points \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\), a further protected rational point \(R\), and divisors \(A,G\) of nonnegative degrees \(a,m\) supported away from all these points. There are \(L\ge1\) received words

\[y^{(j)}=\operatorname{ev}(f_j)+e^{(j)},\qquad f_j\in L(G).\]

Let \(E_j\) be the exact nonzero support of \(e^{(j)}\), \(E=\bigcup_jE_j\), \(u=|E|\), \(t=\max_j|E_j|\). Assume

\[a\ge u+2g,\qquad n>a+m+t.\tag{1}\]

All ranks below use the same domain \(U=L(A)\). Define \(T_j(\lambda)=(\lambda(P_i)e_i^{(j)})_i\), \(H=\operatorname{ev}L(A+G)\), and the quotient map \(Q:k^n\to k^n/H\). Then

\[\operatorname{rank}T_j=\operatorname{rank}(QT_j)=|E_j|,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(T_1,\ldots,T_L)=\operatorname{rank}(QT_1,\ldots,QT_L)=u.\tag{2}\]

The common kernel is \(L(A-\sum_{i\in E}P_i)\), and evaluation at \(R\) on it is surjective. Thus a common locator \(\lambda(R)=1\) exists. It is found without knowing the supports by solving

\[\lambda\in L(A),\quad N_j\in L(A+G),\quad N_j(P_i)=\lambda(P_i)y_i^{(j)}\quad\forall i,j,\qquad\lambda(R)=1.\tag{3}\]

Every solution satisfies \(N_j=\lambda f_j\) identically and kills every actual error. Separate linear systems with this same locator recover all \(f_j\) and their protected values \(f_j(R)=N_j(R)\). With bases and evaluations, including the \(R\)-evaluation row, supplied, the algorithm uses \(O(Ln^3)\) field operations and \(O(n^2+Ln)\) memory. It may use supplied upper bounds \(u_0\ge u,t_0\ge t\) in (1); the actual supports need not be supplied or guessed. Finiteness of \(k\) is inherited from the dependency's statement and is not used by the new steps; the exact ranks need only \(a\ge u+2g-1\), and a nonzero common locator only \(a\ge u+g\); the full (1) is used for normalization at \(R\).

Proof of the rank identities. Since \(a-u-1\ge2g-1\), Riemann–Roch and the negative-degree dual-section criterion give \(H^1(A-\sum_{i\in E}P_i-R)=0\). The evaluation exact sequence makes

\[L(A)\longrightarrow k^E\oplus k,\qquad \lambda\longmapsto((\lambda(P_i))_{i\in E},\lambda(R))\tag{4}\]

surjective. This is the point-evaluation argument in the full proof of cor:curve-locator-budget, now for the whole finite set. Multiplication by an error factors through evaluation on its support, followed by an injective diagonal map because all its values there are nonzero. This proves the individual ranks. The stacked map factors through evaluation on \(E\), followed by an injective map: each union coordinate has a nonzero error value in at least one component, so an element killed in every component has every coordinate zero. Its rank is therefore \(u\), and its kernel is the stated vanishing space. Surjectivity in (4) supplies the normalized locator. In the language of lem:exact-common-kernel-selection with \(S=U\), \(J=\{\lambda:\lambda(R)=0\}\) and \(\rho=(T_j)_j\), (4) says \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)=u+1>u=\operatorname{rank}\rho\), although \(\dim S/J=1\le u\): the sufficient forms of that lemma fail here and only the exact criterion applies.

A nonzero element of \(H\) has at least \(n-a-m>t\) nonzero coordinates, by the divisor-zero proof in thm:marked-curve-recovery. Thus \(Q\) is injective on vectors supported on any \(E_j\); applying it separately to the components preserves each individual kernel and the common kernel. Rank–nullity proves both observable identities in (2). All comparisons use \(U\), not separate quotients chosen for separate errors.

Proof of recovery and cost. Eliminating \(N_j\), a \(\lambda\in L(A)\) extends to a solution of (3) exactly when \(Q\operatorname{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A\lambda=0\) for every \(j\). Since \(\operatorname{ev}(\lambda f_j)\in H\), \(Q\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{ev}f_j)\operatorname{ev}_A=0\), hence \(Q\operatorname{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A=QT_j\): the observable map of row \(j\) is \(QT_j\). A locator from (4) vanishing on \(E\), together with \(N_j=\lambda f_j\), solves (3), so that system is consistent. For any solution, \(N_j-\lambda f_j\) vanishes at the at least \(n-|E_j|>a+m\) correct positions in row \(j\); the same zero bound makes it identically zero. Since \(\lambda(R)=1\), it is nonzero. Recover each \(f_j\) by the second system of thm:marked-curve-recovery; its uniqueness proof applies verbatim to each row.

For the runtime bound, do not eliminate a single dense matrix with \(L\) separate numerator blocks cubically in its full width. First form \(Q\) once by elimination on the supplied evaluation basis of \(H\). In row \(j\) the observable map \(Q\,\mathrm{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A\) has at most \(n\) rows and \(a+1=O(n)\) columns. Form it in \(O(n^3)\) field operations and insert its rows into a maintained echelon system for \(\lambda\), including \(\lambda(R)=1\). Across all rows this costs \(O(Ln^3)\). The consistent affine solution is a locator. For each word solve \(\lambda(P_i)h(P_i)=\lambda(P_i)y_i^{(j)}\) in \(L(G)\), another \(O(n^3)\) operations. Compute \(N_j=\lambda h\), or just its evaluations and protected value, after recovering \(h\). Streaming the constraint rows requires \(O(n^2)\) workspace; retaining the received words uses \(O(Ln)\). Product coordinates, if requested, use the supplied multiplication representation or an additional solve in the injective evaluation basis of \(H\), again cubic per word. No search through supports is used.

Restriction rank overlap

Statement. Let \(R\) be a commutative ring containing a field \(\mathbb K\), \(U\le R\) a finite-dimensional \(\mathbb K\)-subspace, and for an ideal \(I\) put \(r(I)=\dim U-\dim(U\cap I)\), the rank of \(U\to R/I\). Then for ideals \(I\subseteq I'\) and \(I,J\):

\[r(I')\le r(I),\qquad r(I\cap J)\le r(I)+r(J)-r(I+J).\tag{1}\]

For ideals \(I_1,\ldots,I_M\), the stacked map \(U\to\bigoplus_aR/I_a\) has rank \(r(\bigcap_aI_a)\), and

\[r\Bigl(\bigcap_aI_a\Bigr)\le\sum_{a=1}^Mr(I_a)-\sum_{a=2}^Mr(I_a+I_{b(a)})\tag{2}\]

for every choice of indices \(b(a)<a\). If ideals \(K_1,\ldots,K_C\) satisfy: every \(I_a\) contains some \(K_c\), then

\[r\Bigl(\bigcap_aI_a\Bigr)\le r\Bigl(\bigcap_cK_c\Bigr)\le\sum_{c=1}^Cr(K_c).\tag{3}\]

Increments are monotone in the space: for \(U\le P\) finite-dimensional, \(r_U(I)-r_U(I+J)\le r_P(I)-r_P(I+J)\). For affine flats, let \(R=\mathbb K[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\), \(P=R_{\le k}\), and for an affine span \(S\) let \((S)\) be the ideal it generates in \(R\), not the ideal of polynomials vanishing on the flat's points. If \(1\notin S_a+S_b\), \(\dim S_a=r_a\), \(d_a=v-r_a\) and \(c=\dim(S_a+S_b)-r_a\), then

\[r_U((S_a))-r_U((S_a+S_b))\le\binom{d_a+k}{k}-\binom{d_a-c+k}{k}\le c\binom{d_a+k-1}{k-1}=\frac{ck}{d_a+k}\binom{d_a+k}{k}.\tag{4}\]

So with (2), \(r_U(\bigcap_a(S_a))\le r_U((S_1))+\sum_{a\ge2}\min\{r_U((S_a)),\,c_a\binom{d_a+k-1}{k-1}\}\), where \(c_a\) is the increment of \(S_{b(a)}\) over \(S_a\), and the minimum is \(r_U((S_a))\) when \(1\in S_a+S_{b(a)}\).

Proof. Monotonicity holds because \(U\cap I\subseteq U\cap I'\). The stacked kernel is \(U\cap\bigcap_aI_a\). For (1), \((U\cap I)+(U\cap J)\subseteq U\cap(I+J)\) and \((U\cap I)\cap(U\cap J)=U\cap(I\cap J)\), so

\[\dim U\cap(I\cap J)=\dim U\cap I+\dim U\cap J-\dim\bigl((U\cap I)+(U\cap J)\bigr)\ge\dim U\cap I+\dim U\cap J-\dim U\cap(I+J).\]

Subtracting from \(\dim U\) gives (1). For (2), induct on \(M\) with \(I=I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_{a-1}\), \(J=I_a\): (1) gives \(r(I\cap I_a)\le r(I)+r(I_a)-r(I+I_a)\), and \(I+I_a\subseteq I_{b(a)}+I_a\), so monotonicity gives \(r(I+I_a)\ge r(I_{b(a)}+I_a)\). Any \(b(a)<a\) works because \(I_1\cap\cdots\cap I_{a-1}\subseteq I_{b(a)}\). For (3), \(\bigcap_cK_c\subseteq\bigcap_aI_a\) gives the first inequality by monotonicity, and (2) with \(b\) arbitrary, dropping the nonnegative subtracted terms, gives the second. For the increment, \(r_U(I)-r_U(I+J)=\dim\,(U\cap(I+J))/(U\cap I)\), and this quotient maps injectively to \((P\cap(I+J))/(P\cap I)\), because \(U\cap(P\cap I)=U\cap I\). For (4), in affine coordinates adapted to \(S_a\subseteq S_a+S_b\) (the published affine-coordinates lemma, using \(1\notin S_a+S_b\)), \(P\cap(S)\) is spanned by the monomials of degree at most \(k\) divisible by one of the first \(\dim S\) coordinates, so \(r_P((S_a))=\binom{d_a+k}{k}\) and \(r_P((S_a+S_b))=\binom{d_a-c+k}{k}\). Pascal's rule writes the difference as \(\sum_{i=1}^{c}\binom{d_a+k-i}{k-1}\le c\binom{d_a+k-1}{k-1}\).

Scope. (1) is the standard submodularity of \(I\mapsto\dim(U\cap I)\), and the lemma is elementary. For subsets of the Boolean cube and \(U\) the space of all degree-\(d\) functions, (1) together with \(I_U+I_W\subseteq I_{U\cap W}\) and monotonicity implies the “always \(\le0\)” clause of the registered lem:degreewise-gluing; equality there is its gluing condition. Here \(U\) is arbitrary and restrictions are ordinary, through ideals, so the lemma also covers Braun's row-linear space \(\mathcal L_k\) and literal affine restrictions. An upper bound on the joint rank is what lem:exact-common-kernel-selection and Braun's dimension count need; no exactness is used. Form (4) avoids lower bounds on intersection ranks by comparing increments with the full degree-\(k\) space; the theorem below uses only (3).

Shared-span affine exclusion

Setting. Use the publication's Section 5 exactly. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), take \(m>0\), \(\ell\ge2\), \(n=2^\ell\), \(h=3\ell\), \(v=m\ell\) old bits, the old system \(\mathcal Q_{m,\ell}\), and a finite complete old-affine family of accuracy-\(h\) blocks with disjoint fresh coefficients. The input span \(S_a\) of a block is the span of its affine inputs; rank is \(\dim S_a\); the block is proper if \(S_a\) is nonzero and \(1\notin S_a\). \(\mathcal L_k\) is the row-linear space of the publication's Section 4: the span of the monomials of degree at most \(k\) containing at most one bit from each row, of dimension \(\sum_{j\le k}\binom mj\ell^j\ge\binom mk\ell^k\). For \(k\ge1\) it contains the constants, every bit and so every linear form. \(B=k(D+1)\). The ideal of a flat below is the ideal generated in \(\mathbb F_2[x]\) by its affine span, so restriction is ordinary substitution, not evaluation at Boolean points.

Statement. Let \(k\ge1\) and \(C\ge1\). Suppose there are subspaces \(T_1,\ldots,T_C\) of old affine polynomials, each of dimension greater than \(h(k+1)\), such that the input span of every proper block of rank greater than \(h(k+1)\) contains some \(T_c\). If

\[m\ln(4C)\le k^2,\quad k\le m,\quad D\ge2h+1,\quad2B-1\le n,\quad4(k-1)<n,\tag{6}\]

the augmented system has no ordinary-PC refutation through degree \(D\). The number of blocks is not bounded. Taking \(T\) to be the spans of the proper high-rank blocks themselves gives \(C\le M\), so this contains the published finite-parameter affine exclusion theorem. For fixed natural \(a,A,d\) and every sufficiently large \(\ell\), with \(m=n+1\) and \(k=\lceil(\ell+1)\sqrt2^{\,\ell}\rceil\), the \(k\) of the uniform eventual parameter lemma at constants \(a,A+7,d+1\): if every proper block of rank greater than \(3\ell(k+1)\) contains one of at most \(A2^{a\ell}+1\) subspaces of dimension greater than \(3\ell(k+1)\), the family has no PC refutation of degree \(D\le A(\ell+1)^d\).

Proof of the common kernel. If no proper block has rank greater than \(h(k+1)\), take \(f=1\), as in the published kernel lemma. Otherwise discard every \(T_c\) contained in no proper high-rank span; such a \(T_c\) may contain one, and discarding only lowers \(C\). Each remaining \(T_c\) lies inside a proper span, so \(1\notin T_c\), and by the published affine-coordinates lemma it has a nonempty zero flat \(W_c\) of codimension \(\dim T_c\ge r_*=3\ell(k+1)+1\). If \(r_*>v\) no remaining subspace exists, and we are in the first case. Ordinary restriction to \(W_c\) maps \(\mathcal L_k\) into polynomials of degree at most \(k\) in \(v-\dim T_c\le d=v-r_*\) free coordinates, so its rank is at most \(\binom{d+k}{k}\). The published comparison gives \(\binom{d+k}{k}\le e^{-k^2/m}\binom mk\ell^k\). By (3) with \(K_c=(T_c)\), the ideal generated by \(T_c\), or directly, the joint restriction map to the \(W_c\) has rank at most

\[Ce^{-k^2/m}\binom mk\ell^k\le\tfrac14\binom mk\ell^k<\dim\mathcal L_k,\tag{5}\]

using \(e^{-k^2/m}\le1/(4C)\) from (6). So there is a nonzero \(f\in\mathcal L_k\) whose ordinary restriction to every \(W_c\) is the zero polynomial.

Proof that every block is covered. Let a proper block of rank \(r_a>h(k+1)\) have \(T_c\subseteq S_a\). Choose a basis \(F_1,\ldots,F_t\) of \(T_c\) and extend it to a basis \(F_1,\ldots,F_{r_a}\) of \(S_a\). By the affine-coordinates lemma, applied to \(S_a\) with \(1\notin S_a\), these extend to invertible affine coordinates of degree one. In them, restriction to \(W_c\) sets \(F_1,\ldots,F_t\) to zero and restriction to the zero flat \(Z_a\) of \(S_a\) sets \(F_1,\ldots,F_{r_a}\) to zero. A polynomial whose restriction to \(W_c\) is zero has every monomial divisible by some \(F_j\) with \(j\le t\), so its restriction to \(Z_a\) is zero too. Zero restriction does not depend on the chosen coordinates, because two affine parametrizations of one flat differ by an invertible affine change of its free coordinates. The published restriction-ideal lemma then gives the literal cofactors \(f=\sum_ia_{a,i}g_{a,i}\), \(\deg a_{a,i}\le k-1\), for that block.

Proof of exclusion. The published weighted-removal lemma needs only an ordinary \(f\) of degree \(s\le k\) and, for every block of rank greater than \(h(k+1)\), either one in its span or the literal cofactors. Both are now supplied. It gives \(f\in\mathrm{Cons}_{k(D+1)}(\mathcal Q_{m,\ell})\), contrary to the published row-separation theorem under \(2B-1\le n\) and \(4(k-1)<n\). In the published proofs the inventory \(M\) enters only the kernel count (5); the removal, packing, separation and replay steps are read from the publication's Section 5 and do not use it. For the eventual regime, the uniform eventual parameter lemma is a statement about the numbers \(n,k\) and \(A2^{a\ell}+1\) only; the polynomial-inventory corollary's degree raise to \(\max(D,6\ell+1)\) applies verbatim, with the shared-subspace count in place of the block count.

Strict inventory gain at unbounded parameters

Family. Fix \(\ell\ge16\), \(m=n+1\), \(k=\lceil\sqrt{m\ln4}\rceil\), and \(C=1\). Let \(T_1\) be the span of \(r_*=3\ell(k+1)+1\) old bits \(x_1,\ldots,x_{r_*}\). Put \(G=3\ell(n/4+2)+1\) and \(\rho=G-r_*=3\ell(n/4+1-k)\); here \(\rho\ge3\ell\), because \(4(k-1)<n\) and \(4\mid n\) give \(k\le n/4\), and \(v-G=\ell(n/4-5)-1\ge1\) for \(n\ge32\). For each \(\rho\)-dimensional subspace \(\Lambda\) of linear forms in the remaining \(v-r_*\) bits take a block whose inputs are \(x_1,\ldots,x_{r_*}\) and a basis of \(\Lambda\). Its span is \(T_1\oplus\Lambda\), of rank \(G\). The blocks are proper, because their inputs are linear forms, so their spans do not contain one. They are pairwise distinct, because projection to the remaining bits recovers \(\Lambda\). Their number is \(M\ge2^{\rho(v-G)}\), the standard lower bound for the number of \(\rho\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_2^{v-r_*}\). Every block contains \(T_1\).

Joint rank against the sum. In \(\mathcal L_k\), the restriction to each block's flat \(Z_\Lambda\), of dimension \(v-G\ge1\), has rank at least \(v-G+1\), since the restrictions of the constants and of the old bits span all affine functions on \(Z_\Lambda\). So the sum of the individual ranks is at least \(M\ge2^{\rho(v-G)}\). By (3) the joint rank is at most the rank of the restriction to \(W_1\), at most \(\tfrac14\binom mk\ell^k\le(m\ell)^k\) by (5). At \(\ell=16\), \(\log_2\) of the sum is at least \(\rho(v-G)=771984\cdot262063\) and \(\log_2\) of the joint bound at most \(302\cdot\log_2 1048592<302\cdot21=6342\). The kernels are distinct and incomparable. By the affine-coordinates lemma, a linear form has zero restriction to \(Z_\Lambda\) exactly when it lies in \(T_1\oplus\Lambda\). For \(\Lambda\ne\Lambda'\) of equal dimension, a form in \(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda'\) lies in \(\mathcal L_k\), vanishes on \(Z_\Lambda\) and not on \(Z_{\Lambda'}\).

Comparison with the published hypothesis. The published finite-parameter theorem may be applied with any \(k'\) satisfying its conditions. They include \(4(k'-1)<n\), so \(k'\le n/4\), and then \(h(k'+1)\le3\ell(n/4+1)<G\). So at every admissible \(k'\) all \(M\) blocks are proper and of rank above the threshold, and the theorem needs \(m\ln(4M)\le k'^2\le n^2/16\). But \(\ln M\ge\rho(v-G)\ln2\ge3\ell\,(\ell(n/4-5)-1)\ln2\), using \(v-G=\ell(n/4-5)-1\). For \(\ell\ge16\) this exceeds \(n/16\ge n^2/(16m)\). The published inventory condition therefore fails at every admissible \(k'\). The shared-span condition with \(C=1\) needs only \(m\ln4\le k^2\), which holds by the choice of \(k\). This compares stated hypotheses only; it does not show that no other argument excludes this family.
