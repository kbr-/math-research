Diagonal observation principle

Setting. Let \(U,V\) be finite-dimensional \(\mathbb F_p\)-spaces, \(p\) prime, with a specified \(v_0\ne0\) in \(V\). Let \(\beta_i:U^p\to V\), \(1\le i\le m\), be fixed symmetric \(p\)-linear maps. Define their diagonal maps \(d_i(u)=\beta_i(u,\ldots,u)\), and \(C=\bigcap_i\ker d_i\). Assume

\[\beta_i(C,U,\ldots,U)=0\quad\text{for every }i.\tag{DO}\]

Every \(d_i\) is \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear. Indeed expansion of \(\beta_i(u+v,\ldots,u+v)\) groups mixed terms with coefficients \(\binom pj\), zero in characteristic \(p\), and scaling by \(c\in\mathbb F_p\) gives \(c^p=c\). Let \(r\) be the rank of \(u\mapsto(d_i(u))_i\).

Statement. For any finite extension \(L/\mathbb F_p\) with \(|L|>2r+1\), some \(a_i\in L\) and \(\lambda:V_L\to L\), with \(\lambda(v_0)=1\), give a scalar symmetric \(p\)-linear form

\[B(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\lambda\!\left(\sum_i a_i\beta_i(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\right),\qquad \operatorname{rad}B=C_L.\tag{4}\]

For a finite fixed family over the same \(V,v_0\), one \(\lambda\) works for every member, with separate coefficients, when \(|L|>1+2\sum_a r_a\). If a forbidden subspace \(L_0\le C\) is specified and independently established bounds \(\operatorname{rank}d_i\le R_i\) satisfy \(\sum_i R_i<\dim U-\dim L_0\), then \(C\setminus L_0\) is nonempty over the original prime field. In a filtered application the cost of realizing each \(\beta_i\) must be supplied separately; abstract vector-space scalarization licenses no unproved multiplication or degree reset.

Proof. Choose prime-field bases \(u_j\) of \(U\) and \(\lambda_\ell\) of \(V^*\), and independent parameters \(t_i,z_\ell\). Over \(F=\mathbb F_p(t,z)\), use \(B_{t,z}=\sum_{i,\ell}t_i z_\ell\lambda_\ell\beta_i\). For \(u=\sum_j c_j u_j\), symmetry and characteristic \(p\) give

\[B_{t,z}(u,\ldots,u)=\sum_{i,\ell}t_i z_\ell\sum_j c_j^p\lambda_\ell(d_i(u_j)).\]

The monomials \(t_i z_\ell\) are independent over \(F^p\), by clearing denominators and comparing exponent vectors modulo \(p\). Their coefficients are \(p\)-th powers, so a zero diagonal value implies \(d_i(u)=0\) for every \(i\). Conversely (DO), extended by scalars, makes \(C_F\) lie in the radical. The generic radical is therefore exactly \(C_F\), and the first-factor flattening has rank \(r\). Its entries are bilinear in the two parameter groups. A nonzero maximal minor has degree at most \(2r\). Multiply it by the nonzero linear polynomial \(\sum z_\ell\lambda_\ell(v_0)\), specialize over a field larger than that degree, and normalize \(\lambda\). The elementary nonzero-polynomial grid bound proves existence; the radical inclusion from (DO) holds at every specialization, so a nonzero minor gives equality. Products of the minors prove the finite-family assertion. When \(r=0\), use the empty minor one. Finally \(r\le\sum R_i\), and rank-nullity gives \(\dim C>\dim L_0\); all these are prime-field spaces. This proves the selection statement without selecting a field-valued source assignment.

Why the structural hypothesis matters. It says that diagonal vanishing of the vector observations already kills their mixed products; it is verified from the native algebra in the applications, not assumed from a scalar moment. It is not automatic for symmetric forms. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(U=\mathbb F_2^3\), \(V=\mathbb F_2\), \(v_0=1\), and \(\beta((a,b,c),(a',b',c'))=aa'+bc'+cb'\). Its diagonal map is \((a,b,c)\mapsto a\), so \(C\) is a plane, but \(\beta\) is nonsingular. A nonzero scalar multiple has zero radical, and the zero multiple has radical all of \(U\); neither has radical \(C\), even after scalar extension. This is the already recorded perfect-field moment matrix used as a control on (DO). The generic diagonal argument is used only over the imperfect parameter field. Finite specialization preserves radical rank, not the stronger diagonal-zero criterion.

Relation to the preceding theorem. In the paid-PC setting take \(V=V_D/J\), \(v_0=[1]\), and \(\beta_i(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=[wE_i\prod_j u_j]\). At \(b+e+pK\le D\), field reduction gives \(d_i(u)=[wE_i u]\) on base-field \(U\), and paid absorption proves (DO). Thus the common-moment-radical theorem, with its exact normalization and finite-family bound, is recovered. The abstract statement isolates that proof mechanism; it supplies no new automatic rank bound.

Ordinary restriction moments

Statement. Let \(U\le\mathbb F_p[x]_{\le k}\) be finite-dimensional and let \(\rho_a\) be finitely many affine substitutions into polynomial rings \(\mathbb F_p[y_{a,1},\ldots,y_{a,d_a}]\). There is a scalar symmetric \(p\)-linear coefficient-moment form on \(U\), over a finite extension of cardinality greater than \(2r+1\), whose radical is exactly \(\bigcap_a\ker(\rho_a|U)\) extended by scalars, where \(r\) is the joint ordinary restriction rank. It uses restriction products of degree at most \(pk\). In the binary published affine-family setting below, this retains Braun's common-kernel dimension bound and the original old-PC ceiling \(k(D+1)\).

Proof of the restriction representation. Set \(V=\bigoplus_a\mathbb F_p[y_a]_{\le pk}\), and let \(\beta_a(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\) have only its \(a\)-th component nonzero, equal to \(\prod_j\rho_a(u_j)\). The diagonal observation is \(\rho_a(u)^p\). Frobenius is injective on the ordinary polynomial ring: a nonzero polynomial has a nonzero \(p\)-th power, since its distinct monomials retain distinct exponent vectors and nonzero coefficient powers. Hence \(\ker d_a=\ker\rho_a\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and their joint ranks agree. If \(u\) restricts to zero in every target, all mixed products containing it vanish, proving (DO). Take \(v_0=(1)_a\) when the family is nonempty, and apply the principle. The scalar functional is a tuple of polynomial-coefficient functionals on the restriction targets; it is not asserted to annihilate the old pigeonhole axioms. Empty families need no form construction: every \(u\in U\) is in the common kernel.

\[H=\dim U=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom mj\ell^j,\qquad q=\binom mk\ell^k>0.\]

Ordinary versus finite-point observations

For two bits \(x,y\) in distinct rows, the row-linear polynomial \(f=xy+x\) has ordinary restriction \(t^2+t\ne0\) on the flat \(x=y=t\), but vanishes at every binary point of that flat. Thus replacing coefficient moments by binary point measures changes the kernel. This persists among high-rank blocks: for \(k\ge2\) and \(m\ell\ge3\ell(k+1)+2\), use the equation \(x+y=0\) and pin \(3\ell(k+1)\) other coordinates to zero. The resulting proper flat has rank \(3\ell(k+1)+1\), retains a free \(t\), and has the same discrepancy. The large-board regime just displayed has room for these pins. Literal affine-ideal membership would force the ordinary restriction to be zero, so this example has no such literal representation.

This is not a claim that pointwise affine vanishing can never support removal. Their weighted companion identity pays for that Boolean residual and reaches the same hybrid \(k(D+1)\) ceiling. Those full proofs and their listed later relative-learning/fresh-block variants were read and their distinct fields, PC/NS scopes and costs compared.
