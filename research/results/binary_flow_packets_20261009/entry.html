Arbitrary defect coefficients on bounded flow modes

Let \(\mathcal A_S=\{y:\operatorname{div}y=c-\lambda\mathbf1_S\}\) be the compatible corrected-flow fibers from the preceding cycles. Consider any finite functional \[ \mu(f)=\sum_{S,\beta}a_\beta(S) \sum_{y\in\mathcal A_S}m_\beta(y)f(y), \qquad |\beta|\le b, \tag{4} \] with completely arbitrary \(a_\beta(S)\in\mathbb F_4\). No bound on their defect-polynomial degree, number of subsets or coefficient magnitude is imposed. This is genuinely nonuniform within a fiber when nontrivial modes occur. Assume \(\mu(1)=1\) and \(\mu(C_D)=0\).

If \(\beta-\beta_0=\nabla t\), their ratio on \(\mathcal A_S\) is the scalar \(\omega^{c\cdot t-\lambda\sum_{v\in S}t_v}\), which can be absorbed into \(a_{\beta_0}(S)\). Retain one representative from each original coset, still of support at most \(b\). For the zero coset use representative zero. Missing modes may be added with zero coefficients. This grouping is a pointwise statement about the flow fibers, not a free polynomial-degree reduction in a source proof.

Lemma (flow packet representation). Under (2)–(4), set \[ c_\beta=\mu(m_{-\beta}),\qquad W=\sum_{\beta\ {\rm represented}}c_\beta m_\beta. \tag{5} \] Then \(\deg W\le b\), \(\Lambda(W)=1\), and \[ \mu(f)=\Lambda(Wf)\qquad(\deg f\le L). \tag{6} \] Thus \(W\notin J_D\). If \(\mu\) is binary-valued on binary one-hot polynomials through degree \(b\), \(W\) may be taken to be a binary one-hot polynomial of degree at most \(b\). No small expanded representation of the arbitrary coefficient functions is asserted.

Proof. On each flow fiber, the sum of a character is zero unless it is a cut. Therefore \(\mu(m_\alpha)\) is zero unless exactly one represented coset \(\beta\) has \(\alpha+\beta\) in the cut space. If no such coset occurs, \(\Lambda(Wm_\alpha)=0\) as well. If it occurs, use (3) and \(\mu(C_D)=0\) to obtain \[ \mu(m_\alpha)=\omega^{c\cdot t}\mu(m_{-\beta}) =c_\beta\omega^{c\cdot t}. \] The same value is \(\Lambda(Wm_\alpha)\): all other modes are noncuts after multiplication, and the matched one reduces with the displayed phase. Local character reduction of an arbitrary ordinary polynomial of degree at most \(L\) does not increase degree, is valid on every evaluation point, and has its usual degree-paid old certificates. Linearity proves (6). The product degree is at most \(L+b\le D\).

Normalization requires the zero coset in the grouped packet, because all other modes sum to zero on every flow fiber. Its coefficient in (5) is \(c_0=\mu(1)=1\). Every other mode is noncut, so \(\Lambda(W)=1\), proving survival. This also follows by taking \(f=1\) in (6).

For the binary assertion, choose representatives in literal opposite pairs \(\beta,-\beta\), adding any absent opposite coset with zero packet coefficient, and retain zero for the zero coset. Distinct nonzero cut-code cosets have order three, so a nonzero coset is different from its negative. The binary involution conjugates coefficients and exchanges \(U,V\), sending \(m_\beta\) to \(m_{-\beta}\). Binary values of \(\mu\) on degree-\(b\) one-hot polynomials give \[ c_{-\beta}=\mu(m_\beta)=\mu(m_{-\beta})^2=c_\beta^2. \] For an absent mode its moment is zero by flow orthogonality, so the same identity still holds. Hence the ordinary polynomial \(W\) is fixed by the involution. Substituting the affine character expressions into one-hot variables makes all its coefficients binary, without increasing degree. This is the existing coefficient descent, not a trace normalization that would kill one. \(\square\)
