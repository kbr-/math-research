4. General two-premise semantic inference has the same simulation

Affine-cover lemma. Suppose linear clauses \(A,B,D\) satisfy \(A\wedge B\models D\). Put \(W=Z(D)\). Then \(W\subseteq Z(A)\cup Z(B)\). An empty \(W\) is the tautology case, and if \(W\) lies in one of the two sets, one-premise weakening suffices.

Otherwise \(W\cap Z(A)\) and \(W\cap Z(B)\) are proper affine subspaces covering \(W\). Each has at most half its points. Therefore both have exactly half, are disjoint affine hyperplanes of \(W\), and are parallel. There is an affine parity \(u\), extended from \(W\) to the whole old variable space, that is zero on the first half and one on the second.

It follows that

\[ A\models D\vee(u=1),\qquad B\models D\vee(u=0). \]

Two semantic weakenings and one complementary-parity resolution yield \(D\), with duplicate context literals identified. An affine constant in \(u\) only switches the two parity labels. Thus this rule convention adds at most two auxiliary clause blocks per binary inference, still old-affine and at one level. The PC ceiling remains \(4h+1\). This argument uses \(\mathbb F_2\); no other-field rule equivalence is inferred.

For size accounting, clauses can be replaced by bases of their falsifying affine systems, of at most \(v+1\) equations on \(v\) old variables. Semantic equivalence preserves binary semantic consequences; the reduction above then supplies bounded-width basic-rule derivations with constant line overhead. Fresh propositional variables, if present, may first be set to constants: semantic implication survives restriction, and a resolution with a constant pivot becomes a weakening. Thus polynomial line count can be treated with polynomial total clause descriptions over the original bit variables.
