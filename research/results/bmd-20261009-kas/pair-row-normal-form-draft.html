9 October 2026 — At a pair collision the Taylor determinant is \(x^{2d-1}\) times an even function vanishing to order \(2(d-1)^2\): the collision exponent splits into a row part and the companion frame's discriminant part

Question. The head is the Kapranov boundary lead (polar effectivity review); its open question is the local structure of the first-step ideal at the two-pair strata, where \(T_3\) has length \(8=4\cdot2\) and the polar complete intersection \(44=4\cdot11\) (five-point symmetry). General statement. lem:cube-pair-row-normal-form, below, for \(n=4\), characteristic zero, every \(d\ge2\).

Setting. As in thm:cube-taylor-collision-factor. Fix a pair \(i\ne j\), the other two indices \(\{k,l\}\), and \(x=a_i-a_j\), \(m=(a_i+a_j)/2\).

Lemma (pair row normal form). Replace each pair of rows \(T^sw_{S'\cup\{i\}}\), \(T^sw_{S'\cup\{j\}}\) (\(S'\subseteq\{k,l\}\)) of the Taylor matrix by their half-sum and by their difference divided by \(x\). There are exactly \(2d-1\) such pairs, every new row is a vector of polynomials invariant under \(a_i\leftrightarrow a_j\) (that is, even in \(x\) at fixed \(m\)), and \(\Delta=\pm2^{-(2d-1)}\cdot(-1)^{?}\) aside, precisely \(\Delta=c\,x^{2d-1}D\) with \(c\) a nonzero constant and \(D\) the determinant of the new rows. Moreover \(D=c'\,x^{2(d-1)^2}\,(e_4V/x)^{d^2+(d-1)^2}\,F\), so \(D\) vanishes along \(x=0\) to the even order \(2(d-1)^2\).

Proof. Count. The pairs are indexed by \((s,S')\) with \(S'\subseteq\{k,l\}\) and \(2s+|S'|+1\le d\), so their number is \(\sum_{r=0}^2\binom2r\bigl(\lfloor(d-1-r)/2\rfloor+1\bigr)\), terms with \(d-1-r<0\) being \(0\) (the floor formula gives \(0\) there). The outer terms give \(\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+\lfloor(d-3)/2\rfloor+2=2\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+1\), the middle one \(2\lfloor(d-2)/2\rfloor+2\); with \(\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+\lfloor(d-2)/2\rfloor=d-2\), the total is \(2d-1\).

Rows. The swap \(a_i\leftrightarrow a_j\) exchanges the two rows of a pair and fixes the rows whose \(S\) contains both or neither of \(i,j\). A half-sum is swap-invariant; a difference is swap-antisymmetric, so its Taylor coefficients, polynomials in the \(a\)'s, are divisible by \(a_i-a_j\), and the quotient is swap-invariant. Each pair transformation \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/x&-1/x\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) has determinant \(-1/x\), so \(\Delta=(-x)^{2d-1}D=-x^{2d-1}D\).

Order. By thm:cube-taylor-collision-factor, \(\Delta=c\,(e_4V)^{\beta}F\) with \(\beta=d^2+(d-1)^2\), and \(x\) divides \(e_4V\) exactly once and does not divide \(F\) (no collision form does: lem:cube-five-point-symmetry, consequence 3). So \(D=-c\,x^{\beta-(2d-1)}(e_4V/x)^\beta F\), and \(\beta-(2d-1)=2(d-1)^2\). \(\square\)

Corollary (frame comparison). With the companion-frame determinant \(q=c''\,e_4^{d^2+(d-1)^2}\operatorname{Disc}^{(d-1)^2}F\) of lem:cube-four-subleading-boundary, where \(\operatorname{Disc}=V^2\) and \(F\) is the same primitive boundary form up to a scalar (lem:cube-taylor-colon-formula (i)), \(\Delta=c'''\,V^{2d-1}\,q\). So the Taylor and companion frames differ by \(V^{2d-1}\): per collision pair, exactly the \(2d-1\) difference rows of the lemma. The previous entry's comparison, left as "the expected shape", is proved.

Reading. Each collision exponent \(\beta=d^2+(d-1)^2\) splits as \((2d-1)+2(d-1)^2\): a row part, which is the frame (dividing out the coincident branch), and an intrinsic part \(2(d-1)^2\), even, which the companion frame sees as \(\operatorname{Disc}^{(d-1)^2}\). The intrinsic part is what a two-pair local analysis must track: at a two-pair stratum, after both pairs' row normal forms, the determinant is \(c\,x^{2d-1}y^{2d-1}D_2\) with \(D_2\) even in both \(x\) and \(y\) and divisible by \(X^{(d-1)^2}Y^{(d-1)^2}\) (\(X=x^2\), \(Y=y^2\)), with quotient \(F\) up to a unit.

Follow-up. Continuing, to the tail (fifth entry). The lead's open question remains the invariant local lengths at a two-pair stratum, \(2\) for \(T_3\) and \(11\) for \((F,m_1)\) at \(d=3\), \(0\) and \(1\) at \(d=2\).
