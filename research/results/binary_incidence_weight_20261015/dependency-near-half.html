Cut lattices

Setting of lem:binomial-closure-classes and lem:binomial-coset-level-bound at \(k=2\), \(q=3\): a connected graph \(G\) with \(m\) edges, each edge a block of two bits, \(n=2m\); an orientation; exponent vectors \(a\in\mathbb Z_3^E\), whose weight is the size of the support, both nonzero residues having binary weight one; the flow system has one row for each vertex \(v\), with exponent \(+1\) on the edges leaving \(v\) and \(-1\) on those entering. Its row lattice \(L\) is the cut lattice \(\{\delta f:f\in\mathbb Z_3^V\}\), \((\delta f)_{uv}=f(u)-f(v)\). The edge expansion is \(h=\min|\partial U|/|U|\) over nonempty \(U\) with \(|U|\le|V|/2\).

Lemma (cut lattice coset leaders). Let \(H\) be a subgraph of \(G\) of maximum degree \(\Delta_H<h/2\) with \(M\) edges. (1) Every \(a\) supported on \(E(H)\) is the unique element of least weight in \(a+L\). (2) The number \(\ell_j(L)\) of cosets of least weight \(j\) is at least \(2^j\binom Mj\). (3) If the flow system is unsatisfiable and its closure is proper at some \(D\ge w\), it has at least \(2^j\binom Mj\) standard monomials of degree \(j\) for every \(j\le D-1\); in particular, at a level \(j\le D-1\) with \(X\le M\) it has more than \(\binom Xj\), and the hypothesis of thm:thin-kernel-source-exclusion fails at that level.

Proof. (1) Let \(f\) be non-constant, with level sets \(U_c\), \(c\in\mathbb Z_3\), and \(Y\) the support of \(\delta f\), the edges between different level sets; \(Y\) is not empty, \(G\) being connected. Each edge of \(Y\) lies in the boundary of exactly two level sets, so \(|Y|=\tfrac12\sum_c|\partial U_c|\), and likewise \(|Y\cap E(H)|=\tfrac12\sum_ce_H(U_c,V\setminus U_c)\). An edge of \(H\) leaving \(U_c\) has an end in \(U_c\) and one outside, so \(e_H(U_c,V\setminus U_c)\le\Delta_H\min\{|U_c|,|V\setminus U_c|\}\le(\Delta_H/h)|\partial U_c|\), the boundary of a set being that of its complement. Hence \(|Y\cap E(H)|\le(\Delta_H/h)|Y|<|Y|/2\). Outside \(Y\) the vector \(a+\delta f\) equals \(a\), and on \(Y\setminus\operatorname{supp}a\) it equals \(\delta f\), nonzero. So \(\operatorname{wt}(a+\delta f)\ge|\operatorname{supp}a\setminus Y|+|Y\setminus\operatorname{supp}a|\ge\operatorname{wt}(a)+|Y|-2|Y\cap E(H)|>\operatorname{wt}(a)\). (2) Two different vectors supported on \(E(H)\) in one coset would each be its unique lightest element; so they lie in different cosets, each of least weight its own weight, and there are \(2^j\binom Mj\) of weight \(j\). (3) By lem:binomial-coset-level-bound at \(k=2\), for \(j\le D-1\) the level \(b_j\), the number of standard monomials of degree \(j\), is at least \(\ell_j(L)\). If \(X\le M\) then \(\binom Xj\le\binom Mj<2^j\binom Mj\) for \(1\le j\le M\). \(\square\)

This is the bound the reviewer of 9 October proposed with three doubts: potentials with many values, handled here by summing over level sets; \(k>2\), not treated; cubic graphs, where the lemma is empty for all of them, two adjacent vertices giving \(h\le2\). How large \(M\) can be: a graph of maximum degree \(t\) is edge-coloured with \(t+1\) colours, and the \(d\) largest colour classes form a subgraph of maximum degree at most \(d\) with at least \(dm/(t+1)\) edges; with \(d\) the largest integer below \(h/2\) this gives \(M\ge dm/(t+1)\). For the complete graph on \(N\) vertices, \(h=\lceil N/2\rceil\), so \(d\) is about \(N/4\) and \(M\) about \(m/4=n/8\). I have not looked for the best constant, and at levels with \(X>M\) a comparison of \(2^j\binom Mj\) with \(\binom Xj\) would exclude more; it is not made.

What it would give the thin endpoint for these systems, conditionally. The theorem's \(X\) is about twice the cutoff \(K\). So a flow system on two-bit blocks with such a subgraph, if it has a proper closure at \(D>K\), is not thin at a cutoff \(K\) below about \(M/2\), about \(n/16\) for complete graphs. By prop:block-binomial-quarter-ceiling it has no proper closure above \(n/4\) up to lower-order terms, the width of a complete graph's rows being \(2(N-1)\), small against \(n\). Whether it has a proper closure anywhere above its width is not on the record: the 27 systems computed on 9 October all fell at their width, and the degree bound for flow systems modulo a prime, which would give one for large expanders, is known here from a search summary only. So part (3) has no instance, and what it says of the binomial class is: if proper closures exist there, they are not thin below a cutoff linear in \(n\).

Degenerate cases. \(h\le2\): \(\Delta_H=0\), \(H\) has no edge, and (1) says that zero is the unique lightest element of the lattice, which holds because a non-constant potential has a nonempty cut. \(G\) disconnected: outside the statement; a potential constant on components gives \(\delta f=0\) and the proof's first step fails as written. \(j=0\): one coset, the lattice. A vector not supported on \(E(H)\): outside the statement.

Nonvacuity. The complete graph on nine vertices: \(h=5\), since a set of \(u\le4\) vertices has \(u(9-u)\) boundary edges, at least \(5u\). A Hamiltonian cycle \(H\) has maximum degree two, below \(5/2\), and \(M=9\) edges of \(m=36\). So each of the \(3^9\) vectors supported on the cycle is the unique lightest element of its coset, and \(\ell_j\ge2^j\binom9j\). This is an instance of parts (1) and (2). Part (3) has none: no flow system with a proper closure at a degree at least its width is recorded.

Structural reason. For the thin endpoint on flow systems: a sparse set of edges meets every cut in a minority, so no translation by a cut can lighten a vector supported on it, and the closure, whose classes lie in cosets, cannot identify such vectors with lighter ones. It is a reason about one class.
