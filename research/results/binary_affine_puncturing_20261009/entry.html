What the relative theorem actually uses

Read the full cut-code proof. A label conjunction on \(T\) expands over the ternary coordinate subspace \(W_T\). Its multiplication columns lie in orbits modulo \(\mathcal B+W_T\), where \(\mathcal B\) is the cut code. Within an orbit, compatibility of the assigned labels gives proportional columns; incompatible phases give zero. Thus its rank is exactly the punctured coset-ball count \(q_T(K)\), or zero. Deleting coordinates then gives \[ q_T(K)/h(K)\le \left(\frac{K+2m}{3K+2m}\right)^{|T|-\dim\mathcal B_T}. \] Both the count and its denominator use the same quotient. The proof does not state this for every polynomial having the same number of affine annihilators.

The present source has binary affine constraints in one-hot indicators, followed by products and arbitrary mixtures. These constraints are not generally ternary linear conditions on edge labels. For instance, on two independent label coordinates, the binary equation \(x_{e,0}+x_{f,0}=0\) allows five of the nine local assignments: both labels zero, or both nonzero. A nonempty affine subspace of \(\mathbb F_3^2\) has cardinality one, three or nine. This local dictionary test rules out identifying every such zero set with one ternary affine coset. It is not a graph-hardness example or a claim that unions of cosets cannot be treated.
