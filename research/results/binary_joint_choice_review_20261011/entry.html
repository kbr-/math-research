Common targets on coordinate graphs

Nonvacuity. For every \(v\ge4\), take the Boolean ideal truncated at \(D=4\), \(w=1\), \(b=0\), \(k=1\), and \(U=\operatorname{span}(1,x_1,\ldots,x_v)\), so \(H=v+1\). One accuracy-one block with inputs \(x_1,\ldots,x_v\) has empty \(z\), \(s=0\), \(\delta=T=1\), original \(B=3\), and \(b+k+TB=4\). Thus \(1<H\) and every hypothesis holds. This is a satisfiable-old-base control of the algebraic statement, not a hard-family lower bound.

Corollary. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(J_D\) be a proper degree-\(D\) Boolean PC-closed space containing the old axioms, and let \(w\notin J_D\), \(\deg w\le b\). Let \(U\subseteq V_k\) consist of multilinear old polynomials, \(k\ge1\), and put \(H=\dim[wU]\). For no blocks set \(\delta=T=1\). Otherwise, for each terminal block \(a\), partition all old variables into \(y_a,z_a\), with \(|z_a|=s_a\), and suppose its actual old-only input tuple contains every \(y_{a,i}+F_{a,i}(z_a)\). Extra inputs are allowed. Put \(\delta_a\ge1\) equal to its actual maximum input degree, \(\delta=\max_a\delta_a\), and \(T=\max(1,\delta(k-1))\). Coefficients are private, accuracies positive, and all companions and fields are present. At an original PC ceiling \(B\ge2\delta+1\), if \[ \sum_a2^{s_a}

Proof: common target. Choose a linear complement \(U_0\) to the kernel of \(f\mapsto[wf]\) in \(U\); it has dimension \(H\). The linear map \[ U_0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_a\operatorname{Fun}(\{0,1\}^{s_a},\mathbb F_2),\qquad Q\longmapsto\bigl(Q(F_a(z_a),z_a)\bigr)_a \] has a target of dimension at most \(\sum_a2^{s_a}\). Thus it has a nonzero kernel vector \(Q\), and \([wQ]\ne0\). Each map evaluates on its own actual coordinate graph; the graphs and coordinate partitions may differ.

Proof: cofactors. In each multilinear monomial of \(Q\), replace the \(y_a\) factors by the corresponding \(F_a\)'s one at a time. Telescoping gives a literal identity \[ Q=\sum_i\beta_{a,i}(y_{a,i}+F_{a,i})+R_a(z_a),\qquad \deg\beta_{a,i}\le\delta_a(k-1),\quad\deg R_a\le\delta_a k. \] Indeed every cofactor is a product of at most \(k-1\) factors of degree at most \(\delta_a\). The remainder vanishes at every Boolean \(z_a\). Successive reductions by \(z_i^2+z_i\) do not increase degree, and the final multilinear representative is zero by uniqueness of Boolean interpolation. Consequently \(R_a\) has a Boolean certificate through \(\delta_a k\).

Proof: complete replay. Place these cofactors in the first coefficient row at the indicated input positions, and zero elsewhere and in all later rows. Then \(H_a=\sum_i\beta_{a,i}g_{a,i}=Q+R_a\) and \(P_a\) maps to \(1+H_a\). For every input, including extras, \[ wQ g_{a,j}(1+H_a)=wg_{a,j}(Q+Q^2)+wQg_{a,j}R_a. \] These Boolean certificates cost at most \(b+d_{a,j}+2k\) and \(b+k+d_{a,j}+\delta_a k\), respectively. Both fit \(b+k+Te_{a,j}\): \(T(\delta_a+1)\ge\max(k,\delta_a k)\), for \(k=1\) directly and for \(k\ge2\) from \(T\ge\delta_a(k-1)\). Weighted field images cost \(b+k+2T\); old generators are weighted by \(wQ\). Replay each PC line \(v\) as \(wQ\Phi(v)\). A nonzero multiplication predecessor has original degree at most \(B-1\), and every variable image has degree at most \(T\), so expansion fits \(b+k+TB\). A refutation would put \(wQ\) in \(J_D\), contradiction. No global satisfying-point algebra for the inconsistent old base is used. \(\square\)

Degenerate cases. For no blocks use old hardness directly, with \(T=1\). Empty \(U\) or \(H=0\) fails the strict dimension premise. A block with no \(y\)'s has the entire old cube as its graph, so the premise cannot hold. Constants, coincident graphs and redundant input positions cause no difficulty; use any indicated occurrence. The case \(k=0\) is excluded since a nonzero constant cannot vanish on any nonempty graph. Zero extra inputs have zero companions.
