General statement. lem:block-additive-degree: over any field, on a finite product \(X=\prod_\nu X_\nu\) with the blocks in disjoint variables, the least degree of a nonzero function supported in a product set \(\prod_\nu A_\nu\) is the sum of the blocks' least degrees, and degree is additive on products of block functions (a standard tensor-degree fact, proved here in this setting); so block-local errors in a domain model have surviving common killers of least degree \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\), a span-type separating idempotent, vanishing off the union of the errors' supports, is forced to be that union's indicator, whose degree can exceed \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\) by a factor of the block size on cubes, while a general idempotent protecting a \(0/1\)-valued least killer, where one exists, costs only \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\), and the least degree of a killer nonzero at a given point is the sum of the blocks' pointwise least degrees.

Block-additive degree

Statement. Let \(F\) be a field, \(X_\nu\subseteq F^{B_\nu}\) finite nonempty sets for disjoint variable blocks \(B_1,\dots,B_S\), and \(X=\prod_\nu X_\nu\). The degree \(\delta(f)\) of a function \(f\) on \(X\) (or \(X_\nu\)) is the least degree of a polynomial agreeing with it. (a) For nonempty \(A_\nu\subseteq X_\nu\), let \(m_\nu\) be the least \(\delta\) of a nonzero function on \(X_\nu\) supported in \(A_\nu\). The least \(\delta\) of a nonzero function on \(X\) supported in \(\prod_\nu A_\nu\) is \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\); and for nonzero \(f_\nu\) on \(X_\nu\), \(\delta(\prod_\nu f_\nu)=\sum_\nu\delta(f_\nu)\). (b) Let \(\mathcal C_D\) be the polynomials of degree at most \(D\) vanishing on \(X\), and \(E_{\nu,i}\) polynomials in the variables of \(B_\nu\), of degree at most \(e\), with supports \(Z_\nu=\bigcup_i\operatorname{supp}E_{\nu,i}\subsetneq X_\nu\) on \(X_\nu\). For \(k+e\le D\), the polynomials of degree at most \(k\) killing every \(E_{\nu,i}\) modulo \(\mathcal C_D\) and not in \(\mathcal C_D\) exist exactly when \(k\ge\sum_\nu m_\nu\), with \(m_\nu\) for \(A_\nu=X_\nu\setminus Z_\nu\). (c) In the setting of lem:idempotent-common-denominator with \(C=\mathcal C_D\) the degree-\(D\) vanishing ideal of a finite set \(X\) and errors \(w_i\) with union of supports \(U\): every \(s\) of degree at most \(D-\max_i\deg w_i\) with \(sw_i-w_i\in\mathcal C_D\) for all \(i\) and vanishing off \(U\) on \(X\), in particular a span generator \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\), agrees with \(1_U\) on \(X\); so \(\deg s\ge\delta(1_U)\), and \(1-s\) has degree at least \(\delta(1_{X\setminus U})\). (d) For block-local errors as in (b), \(\delta(1_{X\setminus U})=\sum_\nu\delta(1_{A_\nu})\), which can exceed \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\) by a factor \(b\): on \(X_\nu=\{0,1\}^b\) with \(Z_\nu=\{0\}\), \(m_\nu=1\) and \(\delta(1_{A_\nu})=b\). (e) Without the condition of vanishing off \(U\): if \(p\) is a surviving common killer, \(0/1\)-valued on \(X\), with \(\deg p+\max_i\deg w_i\le D\) and \(2\deg p\le D\), then \(s=1-p\) satisfies \(sw_i-w_i\in\mathcal C_D\) and \(sp\in\mathcal C_D\), and \(1-s=p\); so the idempotent lemma with protected element \(p\) reaches \(\deg p\), which is \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\) when some least killer is \(0/1\)-valued, as always over \(\mathbb F_2\); over \(\mathbb F_3\) on \(X=\{0,1,2\}\) with \(Z=\{0\}\), \(m=1\) (the function \(x\)) while every nonzero indicator supported in \(A=\{1,2\}\) has degree \(2\). (f) Protecting one point: for \(x_0\in\prod_\nu A_\nu\), the least \(\delta\) of a function supported in \(\prod_\nu A_\nu\) and nonzero at \(x_0\) is \(\sum_\nu m_\nu(x_0)\), where \(m_\nu(x_0)\) is the least \(\delta\) of a function supported in \(A_\nu\) and nonzero at \(x_{0,\nu}\); it can exceed \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\): over \(\mathbb F_2\) on the block \(\{0,1\}^3\) with \(A_\nu=\{x_1=0\}\cup\{(1,1,1)\}\) and \(x_{0,\nu}=(1,1,1)\), \(m_\nu=1\) while \(m_\nu(x_0)=2\).

Proof. (a) Fix a degree-compatible monomial order on all variables; its restriction to each block is one. Let \(G_\nu\) be the reduced Gröbner basis of the vanishing ideal \(I_\nu\) of \(X_\nu\). \(\bigcup_\nu G_\nu\) is a Gröbner basis of \(J=\sum_\nu I_\nu\): S-pairs within a block reduce to zero, and those from different blocks have coprime leading monomials and reduce to zero by Buchberger's first criterion. So the standard monomials of \(J\) are the products of block standard monomials, \(\prod_\nu|X_\nu|=|X|\) of them. \(J\) is contained in the vanishing ideal \(I\) of \(X\), and \(\dim F[x]/I=|X|\) since evaluation onto the functions on a finite set is surjective; so \(J=I\), and the normal forms represent every function on \(X\) exactly once. For a degree-compatible order the normal form of a polynomial has degree at most its own, so \(\delta(f)\) is the degree of \(f\)'s normal form, which is the degree of its leading monomial. Let \(\Phi_\nu\) be the functions on \(X_\nu\) supported in \(A_\nu\), with a basis of normal forms in echelon form, distinct leading monomials; then \(m_\nu\) is the least degree of these leading monomials, every nonzero element of \(\Phi_\nu\) having one of them as leading monomial. The functions on \(X\) supported in \(\prod_\nu A_\nu\) are spanned by the products of such basis elements; each such product is a sum of products of block standard monomials, so it is its own normal form, with leading monomial the product of the factors' leading monomials, and distinct tuples give distinct products. A nonzero combination therefore has as leading monomial the largest product present, of degree at least \(\sum_\nu m_\nu\), attained by the product of minimal-degree elements. The same leading-monomial argument gives \(\delta(\prod f_\nu)=\sum\delta(f_\nu)\). (b) For \(k+e\le D\) every product \(fE_{\nu,i}\) has degree at most \(D\), so membership in \(\mathcal C_D\) is vanishing on \(X\); \(f\) kills \(E_{\nu,i}\) exactly when \(f\) vanishes on the cylinder \(\operatorname{supp}E_{\nu,i}\times\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}X_\mu\), and all of them exactly when \(f\) is supported in \(\prod_\nu A_\nu\); \(f\notin\mathcal C_D\) means \(f\ne0\) on \(X\). Apply (a). (c) \(s\) vanishes off \(U\) by hypothesis; at \(x\in\operatorname{supp}w_i\), \(s(x)w_i(x)=w_i(x)\ne0\) gives \(s(x)=1\); the degree hypothesis puts \(sw_i-w_i\) in degree \(D\), where membership is vanishing. A span generator vanishes off \(U\) because each \(w_i\) does. (d) \(X\setminus U=\prod_\nu A_\nu\), so \(1_{X\setminus U}=\prod_\nu1_{A_\nu}\) and (a) gives the sum. On \(\{0,1\}^b\), \(x_1\) vanishes at \(0\) with degree \(1\), and constants do not, so \(m_\nu=1\); \(1_{A_\nu}=1-\prod_j(1-x_j)\) is multilinear with the monomial \(x_1\cdots x_b\) of coefficient \(\pm1\), and multilinear polynomials are the normal forms on the cube, so \(\delta(1_{A_\nu})=b\). (e) \(sw_i-w_i=-pw_i\), which vanishes on \(X\) as \(p\) kills \(w_i\), within degree \(D\); \(sp=p-p^2\) vanishes on \(X\) as \(p\) is \(0/1\)-valued, within degree \(2\deg p\le D\). On \(\{0,1,2\}\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) the functions supported in \(\{1,2\}\) with values in \(\{0,1\}\) are \(1_{\{1\}},1_{\{2\}},1_{\{1,2\}}\), each of degree \(2\) since a degree-\(1\) polynomial vanishing at \(0\) is \(cx\), not \(0/1\)-valued unless \(c=0\). (f) By the echelon argument of (a), the functions in \(\bigotimes_\nu\Phi_\nu\) of degree at most \(d\) are spanned by the products of basis elements with total degree at most \(d\), since a combination's degree is the largest leading-monomial degree present. Evaluation at \(x_0\) is the product of the block evaluations on such products. A function of degree \(d\) nonzero at \(x_0\) is a combination of products of total degree at most \(d\), one of which is nonzero at \(x_0\), so each factor is nonzero at \(x_{0,\nu}\) and \(d\ge\sum_\nu m_\nu(x_0)\), the least degree of a block element nonzero at \(x_{0,\nu}\) being attained by a basis element by the same echelon argument; the product of such elements attains it. In the example, a nonzero function of degree at most \(1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) is affine, with support the whole cube or a half-space of four points; two distinct half-spaces meet in \(0\) or \(2\) points, so the only half-space inside the five-point \(A_\nu\) is \(\{x_1=0\}\), and the only such function is \(1+x_1\), zero at \((1,1,1)\); so \(m_\nu=1\) and \(m_\nu(x_0)\ge2\). The function \(1_{(0,0,0)}+1_{(1,1,1)}\) is supported in \(A_\nu\), nonzero at \(x_{0,\nu}\), and has degree at most \(2\), since over \(\mathbb F_2\) the coefficient of \(x_1x_2x_3\) is the sum of its values, here \(0\).

Hypotheses, each with its step. Finiteness of the \(X_\nu\) gives the Gröbner basis and the dimension count; disjoint variable blocks give coprime leading monomials across blocks and distinct products; \(k+e\le D\) in (b), the degree bound in (c) and the two bounds in (e) are the steps where membership in \(\mathcal C_D\) becomes vanishing; \(Z_\nu\ne X_\nu\) makes \(A_\nu\) nonempty, without which no killer survives; vanishing off \(U\) in (c) is what forces \(s=1_U\), and (e) shows the conclusion fails without it; (f) needs only \(x_0\in\prod_\nu A_\nu\), and read as the cost of an idempotent protecting \(p=1_{x_0}\), also \(\deg f+\max_i\deg w_i\le D\) and \(\deg f+\deg p\le D\). The field is arbitrary.

Degenerate cases. \(S=1\): (a) is the definition of \(m_1\). \(Z_\nu=\emptyset\): \(A_\nu=X_\nu\), \(m_\nu=0\), and the block drops out. \(Z_\nu=X_\nu\): no surviving killer. \(U=\emptyset\) in (c): \(s=0\) and \(1-s=1\). One-point blocks: \(m_\nu=0\).

Nonvacuity. \(F=\mathbb F_2\), two blocks \(\{0,1\}^2\), \(X=\{0,1\}^4\), errors \((1+x_1)(1+x_2)\) and \((1+x_3)(1+x_4)\) (supports the blocks' origins), \(e=2\), \(D=6\), \(k=2\): the least surviving common killer has degree \(m_1+m_2=2\), for instance \(x_1x_3\), with \(k+e=4\le6\); a span-type idempotent has degree \(\delta(1_{A_1})+\delta(1_{A_2})=4\), with \(4\le D-2\); and (e) with \(p=x_1x_3\), \(s=1+x_1x_3\), has \(2+2\le6\) and \(2\cdot2\le6\).
