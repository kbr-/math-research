Local graph of a majority

Use binary coefficients and local three-state edge coordinates with two independent degree-one label indicators and their affine complementary indicator. Let \(\delta\ge2\) be a power of two, \(b=2\delta-1\), and \[ z=e_\delta(a_1,\ldots,a_b),\qquad a_e=1-x_{e,0}. \] On a state with \(w\) nonzero labels this is \(\binom w\delta\bmod2\), equal to the majority bit. Indeed \((1+t)^w=\prod_{j:w_j=1}(1+t^{2^j})\) in characteristic two, so the coefficient at the power-of-two \(\delta\) is that binary digit, for \(0\le w\le2\delta-1\).

Both fibers. Restriction of normal polynomials through ordinary degree \(\delta-1\) is injective on each fiber of \(z\). For fiber zero, the radius-\(\delta-1\) Hamming ball about the all-zero state is the whole fiber. Index its points and normal monomials by supports of size at most \(\delta-1\) and their nonzero labels; the evaluation matrix is triangular with diagonal one in an inclusion ordering. For fiber one, the radius-\(\delta-1\) ball about a fixed all-nonzero word lies inside it: at most \(\delta-1\) coordinates change, so at least \(\delta\) remain nonzero. Independent ternary state permutations carry that ball to the first one, induce invertible affine transformations on the full local indicator coordinates and preserve degree. This is the exact local argument in the polynomial-row proof, not a Boolean flip of a two-state projection.

Faithful local family. Add one formal variable \(Z\) for this majority. Consider squarefree monomials in the \(2b\) raw indicator variables and \(Z\), excluding each raw indicator pair and using at most \(\delta-1\) total formal variables from the block. Every combination is uniquely \(p+Zq\), where \(p,q\) are local normal polynomials, \(\deg p\le\delta-1\), \(\deg q\le\delta-2\). If its substitution vanishes as a local function, on fiber zero \(p\) vanishes, hence \(p=0\). On fiber one \(q\) then vanishes, hence \(q=0\). Thus this local family is independent. It avoids the defining relation for \(Z\); the whole enlarged polynomial ring remains nonfaithful.
