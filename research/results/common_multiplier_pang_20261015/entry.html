Coordinate radical realization

Statement. Over any field, let \(T:U\to W\) be a linear map between finite-dimensional spaces, of rank \(r\), and \(C=\ker T\). Choose \(r\) independent coordinate rows \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r\) spanning its row space. For every integer \(s\ge2\),

\[ B_s(u_1,\ldots,u_s)=\sum_{j=1}^r\prod_{a=1}^s\ell_j(u_a) \quad\text{has}\quad \operatorname{rad}B_s=C. \tag{1}\]

Here the radical uses all choices of the other arguments. The construction is over the original field, with no positivity or polynomial multiplication assumption. It preserves the rank exactly and supplies no bound on it. With matrices supplied it requires only a row basis. For \(r=0\) use the zero form. For \(L_0\le C\), the induced form on \(U/L_0\) has a nonzero radical exactly when \(r<\dim U-\dim L_0\).

Proof. The row-basis map \(R:U\to k^r\) is surjective and has kernel \(C\). If \(u\in C\), every term in (1) vanishes. Conversely, for each \(j\) choose \(v_j\in U\) with \(R(v_j)=e_j\); then \(B_s(u,v_j,\ldots,v_j)=\ell_j(u)\). A radical vector therefore lies in every row kernel. The quotient and rank statements follow immediately from this equality. In particular \(B_2=R^{\mathsf T}R\) in input coordinates, and \(R^{\mathsf T}\) is injective. No extension field is needed.

Relation to diagonal observation. Over \(\mathbb F_p\), when \(r>0\), put \(s=p\), \(V=\mathbb F_p^r\), and \(\beta(u_1,\ldots,u_p)_j=\prod_a\ell_j(u_a)\). Its diagonal is \(R(u)\), because coefficients are in the prime field; its mixed products vanish on \(C\). Thus it realizes thm:diagonal-observation-principle, and the explicit coefficient sum already works over the base field, normalized at \(v_0=e_1\). At rank zero handle the zero form directly, or take \(V=\mathbb F_p,\beta=0,v_0=1\). This verifies the previous reviewer's auxiliary-coordinate suggestion, in a stronger elementary form. The output rows are chosen from the full map; they need not arise from one old polynomial functional, a fixed trace, an operation-compatible projection or a low-degree multiplication pairing.

Control. Using every row without selection is unsound over finite fields: over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(T:u\mapsto(u,u)\) has zero kernel but \(T^{\mathsf T}T=0\). Its independent row basis \(R=(1)\) works. This is the duplicated-output control already recorded for lem:common-moment-adjoint, not a new counterexample. More generally any prescribed subspace \(C\le U\) is the kernel of a quotient map and hence a radical in (1). Consequently existence of an unrestricted auxiliary form imposes no extra restriction on which common kernels can occur. A structural selection principle must constrain more than this representation.

Matched Pang recurrence

Use precisely Pang, arXiv:2610.00837v1, Theorem 4.3: Boolean variables over \(\mathbb F_p\), old degree-\(D\) PC consequence space \(J=C_D\), \(1\notin J\), \(S\ge1\) families \(g_{\nu,i}\) of degree at most \(d\), and integers \(1\le d\le K\le n\), \(t\ge1\). Put

\[\Delta=(p-1)dt,\quad \Delta+K+d\le D,\quad N_n(k)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom nj,\quad H=\dim V_K/(J\cap V_K),\quad \eta=(1-1/p)\frac{N_n(K-d)}{N_n(K)}.\tag{2}\]

Calibrated conclusion. There are prime-field choices \(q_{\nu,j}\in\operatorname{span}\{g_{\nu,i}\}_i\) and \(P_\nu=\prod_{j=1}^t(1-q_{\nu,j}^{p-1})\) with the actual maps \(T_\nu:u\mapsto([P_\nu g_{\nu,i}u])_i\) satisfying \(\operatorname{rank}T_\nu\le H(1-\eta)^t\). Once these choices are fixed, their stacked map on \(U=V_K/(J\cap V_K)\) has an auxiliary symmetric bilinear form over \(\mathbb F_p\) with exactly its kernel as radical, and also a \(p\)-linear diagonal-observation realization. If \(S(1-\eta)^t<1\), that radical contains a nonzero class; a multilinear representative \(f\in V_K\setminus J\) kills every actual error \(P_\nu g_{\nu,i}\). All original degree bounds remain (2). This is a representation of the source theorem with its quantitative hypotheses, not an improvement of its rate.

Where the contraction comes from. For a fixed prefix \(P\) of degree \(a\), write \(Z_P(k)=\{u\in V_k:Pg_i u\in J\ \forall i\}\) and \(c_P(k)=\dim V_k/Z_P(k)\). These kernels respect degree intersections and absorb multiplication inside \(a+k+d\le D\). Their standard monomials form a downward-closed family of squarefree monomials. Counting consecutive layers gives \((j+1)b_{j+1}\le(n-j)b_j\), so

\[c_P(K-d)\ge\frac{N_n(K-d)}{N_n(K)}c_P(K).\tag{3}\]

On \(V_{K-d}\), define \(M_i u=([Pg_\alpha g_i u])_\alpha\). Their stacked kernel is \(Z_P(K-d)\): absorption gives one inclusion; the diagonal products \(Pg_i^2u\), multiplied by \(g_i^{p-2}\) and corrected using \(g_i^p-g_i\), give the other. Pang's multiplication-map Frobenius descent at \(a+K+pd\le D\) equates generic pencil rank with stacked rank. Its rank-specialization lemma gives a prime-field \(q=\sum c_i g_i\) retaining at least \((1-1/p)c_P(K-d)\). Its proof uses the nonzero maximal minor, whose rank loss at a point is at most its multiplicity there, and the grid multiplicity sum bounded by its degree times \(p^{s-1}\); it does not assume a nonvanishing evaluation on the prime grid.

The retained rank is the dimension of \((qV_{K-d}+Z_P(K))/Z_P(K)\). For \(P'=P(1-q^{p-1})\), bounded absorption preserves \(Z_P(K)\), and \(P'g_i(qu)=Pg_i u(q-q^p)\in J\) puts \(qV_{K-d}\) into \(Z_{P'}(K)\). Both operations fit \(a+K+pd\le D\). Hence \(c_{P'}(K)\le c_P(K)-(1-1/p)c_P(K-d)\le(1-\eta)c_P(K)\). At round \(j\le t\), \(a\le(j-1)(p-1)d\), and

\[a+K+pd\le(t-1)(p-1)d+K+pd=\Delta+K+d\le D.\tag{4}\]

Initially \(c_1(K)\le H\). Iteration proves the stated individual bounds. Every final error map kills \(J\cap V_K\) by the same paid absorption; their stacked rank is at most the sum, below \(H\) under the strict condition. Formula (1) now preserves exactly this already constructed common kernel. A representative may be multilinearized because its change and every error multiple of that change are Boolean consequences within (2).

Adaptive choices. At each round the current prefix and family are fixed before the rank-retaining \(q\) is selected. The final coordinate form is constructed only after all approximators are chosen. It is not used to choose them, and no source coefficient is extended. For the already proved binary leveled lemma, choose families level by level before forming one final stack: \(\Delta_i=\Delta_{i-1}t_i\), \(\Delta_{i-1}\le K\le n\), \(\Delta_i+K+\Delta_{i-1}\le D\), and \(\sum_i S_i(1-\tfrac12N_n(K-\Delta_{i-1})/N_n(K))^{t_i}<1\). Lower-level choices fix the next inputs, and the final sum of ranks is still below the same \(H\). This uses lem:leveled-common-multiplier with its imported premise discharged by thm:binary-approximator-rank; it is not a new all-prime layered theorem.
