What the column factorization gives

Retain the complete setup of lem:symmetric-matching-correction: independent binary (A,Z) of shape (M\times N), (E=AA^{\mathsf T}), (s_i=\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}), (B_{ij}=1+E_{ij}), (W=\prod_{i<j}B_{ij}), and (R=W\prod_i s_i). The full source tuple is (1+s_i), every unique off-diagonal (E_{ij}), and every original Boolean field. Including its diagonal, the nonlinear witness matrix satisfies

\[K=AZ^{\mathsf T}+ZA^{\mathsf T}+ZZ^{\mathsf T} =[A\ Z]\begin{pmatrix}0&I_N\\ I_N&I_N\end{pmatrix}[A\ Z]^{\mathsf T}.\]

Thus its rank is at most (2N). This supplies no strict reduction when (M=2N), or when (M=N+1). For the paid lift (M=2m,N=m+2n+1), the bound exceeds the row count by (4n+2). These are parameter comparisons, not actual-rank lower bounds. Moreover, the matching polynomial uses a zero diagonal and literal edge-dependent masks; its effective edge is (K_{ij}/B_{ij}), not the full Gram difference. No rank bound for that modified matrix follows from the displayed factorization. Changing the row/column ratio alone would not justify dropping those operations.

Masked matching circuit

Statement. For every (M\ge2,N\ge1), define the complete polynomial, including the empty matching,

\[P=\sum_F\prod_{\{i,j\}\in F}K_{ij}\prod_{i\notin V(F)}s_i\prod_{\{i,j\}\notin F}B_{ij}.\]

There is a division-free arithmetic circuit over (\mathbb F_2), counting gates and wires, of size (O(M^9+M^2N)) and binary-fanin depth (O(M^2\log(M+1)+\log(N+1))), computing (P). In particular, the construction is also valid in the allowed unbounded-fanin model. If (M>N), then (Q=P+R) is exactly the earlier symmetric Boolean correction. Adding the input-placeholder product gives its full general IPS certificate at the same asymptotic size and depth. The construction does not preserve Hilbert-like form or establish membership in the matched simulation image, and supplies no PC-degree transfer or fixed-depth bound.

Monomers as a Pfaffian. Work first in the fraction field of the original polynomial ring. For even (M), form the zero-diagonal symmetric matrix (H) with (H_{ij}=K_{ij}/B_{ij}+s_i s_j). Expand its Pfaffian as the sum over perfect matchings, valid in characteristic two. After choosing the edges contributing (K_{ij}/B_{ij}), every remaining vertex contributes its (s_i), independently of how the remaining vertices are paired. An even set of size (r) has ((r-1)!!) perfect matchings, an odd number; the empty set contributes one. Hence

\[P=W\operatorname{Pf}(H).\]

For odd (M), adjoin one dummy vertex with (H_{0i}=s_i). For each chosen matching on original vertices, the remaining original vertices together with the dummy again have an odd number of perfect matchings; each contributes exactly the product of the remaining (s_i). The same identity holds for this even matrix of size (L=M+1). Put (L=M) in the even case and (q=L/2) in either case. This rational identity is only a specification; the following construction removes every division.

Two finite coefficient arrays. Homogenize all original (A,Z) variables by a new formal variable (t). Thus (E,K) become (t^2E,t^2K), and (s_i) becomes (ts_i). Put (D=M^2). Every literal matching term has total degree at most (D): a term with (k) edges has ceiling (M^2-2k). Consequently the polynomial (P(tA,tZ)) has no terms beyond degree (D). Represent all calculations by coefficient arrays in

\[\mathcal A=\mathbb F_2[A,Z][t,u]/(t^{D+1},u^{q+1}).\]

Each array has ((D+1)(q+1)) scalar entries, not exponentially many subsets. Compute each mask inverse by the finite series (\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}t^{2j}E_{ij}^{j}). Multiplying by (1+t^2E_{ij}) leaves one modulo (t^{D+1}). This constructs the entries of (H(t)) in the arrays; dummy edges are (ts_i). No extension-field constants, evaluation interpolation, or formal square root is used.

Let (J) have consecutive off-diagonal two-by-two blocks (\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)). Compute the Pfaffian of (C=J+uH(t)) in (\mathcal A). Its (u^q) coefficient is exactly (\operatorname{Pf}(H(t))), since each perfect matching has (q) edges and the top coefficient chooses the (uH(t)) summand on every edge. Multiply this coefficient by (W(t)), truncate through (t^D), and sum its (t) coefficients. Denominator cancellation in the preceding exact identity makes this last polynomial precisely (P(A,Z)); summing the coefficients is evaluation at one only after recovering a polynomial of degree at most (D).

Unit-pivot elimination. At every stage eliminate the next consecutive pair. At (u=0) the uneliminated matrix is still the corresponding block of (J), so the pivot (p=C_{12}) has (p=1\pmod u). Its inverse in (\mathcal A) is the explicit finite sum (\sum_{j=0}^{q}(1+p)^j). For remaining indices (i,j), make the update

\[C'_{ij}=C_{ij}+p^{-1}(C_{1i}C_{2j}+C_{1j}C_{2i}).\]

Then (\operatorname{Pf}(C)=p\operatorname{Pf}(C')). To prove this over any characteristic-two coefficient ring, expand a Pfaffian of a sum by the subset of vertices whose edges come from the update. The update is a scalar multiple of the alternating rank-two matrix (vw^{\mathsf T}+wv^{\mathsf T}). Every principal Pfaffian of that matrix on at least four vertices vanishes: over the universal polynomial domain its square is the determinant, which is zero by rank at most two; the domain implies that the Pfaffian is zero, and the polynomial identity then specializes to every coefficient ring. The square identity itself follows by pairing long determinant cycles with their reversals, leaving only perfect-match transpositions. Thus only the empty update and one updated edge survive. After multiplying by (p), these terms are exactly the original matchings where vertices one and two are paired, or are paired to two distinct remaining vertices, respectively. This proves the update identity without dividing by two. Iterate it, multiplying the pivots, with empty Pfaffian one. All inverses used are the finite unit series already specified, including when the coefficient ring has nilpotents.

Gate and depth ledger. Let (a=(D+1)(q+1)). Addition of two arrays uses (O(a)) binary gates and multiplication uses ordinary truncated convolution, with (O(a^2)) gates/wires and depth (O(\log(a+1))). Products with powers beyond the truncation rectangle are simply omitted. Each unit inverse uses at most (q) successive array multiplications and a balanced sum. The eliminations use (O(M^3)) array products for matrix updates, (O(Mq)) for all inverse series, and (O(M)) for the pivot product. Building the complete mask uses (O(M^2)) more. Since (q=O(M)), this costs (O(M^3a^2)=O(M^5(D+1)^2)). Computing the shared (E,K,s) uses (O(M^2N)) gates/wires and binary depth (O(\log(N+1))). The initial mask powers use (O(M^2D)) further scalar products; the final coefficient projection and summation use at most (O(a+D)) gates/wires. All fit the stated (O(M^9+M^2N)) size at (D=M^2).

There are (q) sequential pivot stages. Each inverse series has depth (O(q\log(a+1))), and each update a constant number of array operations. A sequential product of the (O(M^2)) mask factors also has depth (O(M^2\log(a+1))). Initial scalar powers can be computed sequentially through (D), at depth (O(D)). These bounds give (O(M^2\log(M+1)+\log(N+1))) overall. In particular, neither coefficient extraction nor denominator clearing has been treated as a primitive operation. The bound is deliberately elementary and not asserted optimal.

Application and scope. When (M>N), the exact identities (Q(A,0)=0,Q(A,b)=R) follow from lem:symmetric-matching-correction, since (Q=P+R) removes just the empty matching. The full certificate is (1+\prod_i(1+Y_i)\prod_{i<j}(1+Y_{ij})+Q), retaining all Boolean placeholders in (Q). The added product costs (O(M^2)) gates/wires and binary depth (O(\log(M+1))). Its output degree and the correction's output degree are those of the earlier literal formula; no low degree for every internal circuit gate or PC derivation is claimed. The separate full certificate (1+T(Y_{ij},1+Y_i)) already noted in the preceding record also has a growing-depth Pfaffian implementation. The present point is the exact specified masked output, not a new general IPS upper-bound paradigm.

Nonvacuity and evidence

Nonvacuity. The circuit statement applies for every (M\ge2,N\ge1), whether or not the source is inconsistent. For an unbounded inconsistent family take (M=2^a,N=M-1), (a\ge3), the paid one-surplus family specified in the preceding entry; the existing polynomial formula encoding stays fixed independently of the proposed proof-size exponent. The construction works equally at the original Gram ratio (M=2N), without asserting a reverse original-rank lift there. The smallest circuit case is (M=2), and the smallest odd case is (M=3). The native generic-edge controls use both parities and a four-row pivot update, with six independent edge symbols of each kind at the largest fixture; they can distinguish the inverse/mask and monomer errors tested, but cannot decide fixed-depth hardness.

Degenerate cases. At zero (K), the output is (R), so (Q=0); this also verifies its zero-placeholder specialization. At (A=0), odd (M) gives zero, while even (M) gives the perfect-matching polynomial of (ZZ^{\mathsf T}) off the diagonal; the circuit still computes it. Zero or duplicated rows cause no pivot failure because the pivots are units in (u), not numerical minors of (H). The empty-matching convention is explicit. Empty row or column sets lie outside the claim; the (M=1) matching polynomial would simply be (s_1). For (M\le N) the circuit still exists but is not asserted to be a Boolean correction: an identity frame has residual one and zero Boolean generators. Independent original variables state the generic polynomial identity; all constant specializations preserve it.
