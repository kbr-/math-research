Attempts and stopping points

Coprime cancellation. The four affine factors \(x_1,x_1+1,x_2,x_2+1\) are distinct primes, so \(B_1^{k_1}\) and \(B_2^{k_2}\) are coprime. Even without knowing \(h\), cross-multiplied compatibility implies \(B_1^{k_1}\mid g_1\): in the unique factorization domain \(A\), its prime multiplicities cannot come from \(B_2^{k_2}\). Dividing then gives \(g_1=B_1^{k_1}h\) and \(g_2=B_2^{k_2}h\) with one unique polynomial \(h\). This is an alternative proof of polynomiality already present in the supplied probe data. Its proof performs exact factor cancellation; it supplies no circuit implementation of that operation.

Localization gluing. Equivalently, \(A[1/B_1]\cap A[1/B_2]=A\) in its fraction field. A reduced denominator of a common fraction must divide powers of both coprime pivots and hence is a unit. This elementary codimension-two extension argument identifies the glued polynomial, but gives no bound for constructing a division-free circuit from its two local presentations. In the present task polynomiality was known before the joint presentations were constructed, so this gluing is not progress on the required effective implication.

Another formulation takes \(I=(B_1^{k_1},B_2^{k_2})\). Their syzygies are generated by \((B_2^{k_2},B_1^{k_1})\): a relation \(uB_1^{k_1}+vB_2^{k_2}=0\) and coprimality force \(u\) to be a multiple of \(B_2^{k_2}\), and then \(v\) the same multiple of \(B_1^{k_1}\). Compatibility therefore makes the assignment \(B_i^{k_i}\mapsto g_i\) an \(A\)-linear map \(I\to A\). Coprime cancellation says it is multiplication by \(h\). This is the same effective extraction question in a module presentation, not a separately solved construction. No free splitting of \(I\) is assumed.

Coprimality in several variables does not imply a unit ideal. This rejects that particular polynomial-combination construction, including a linear \(A\)-module retraction of the displayed scalar inclusion.
