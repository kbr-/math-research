Binary component depth gap

Corollary. No generic operation can take every polynomial-size constant-depth binary arithmetic circuit of polynomial degree and return any requested homogeneous component at polynomial size and bounded depth, uniformly over the requested degree. This already fails for \(G_n=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+x_i)\).

Proof. Its degree-\(q\) component is the elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_q\). Take \(q=2^a\) and \(n=2q-1\). At a Boolean vector of weight \(w\), \(e_q\) has value \(\binom wq\bmod2\). Writing the binary digits of \(w\), the identity \((1+t)^w=\prod_{b:w_b=1}(1+t^{2^b})\) in \(\mathbb F_2[t]\) shows that the coefficient of \(t^q\) is the \(a\)-th digit of \(w\). Since \(0\le w\le2q-1\), this is one exactly when \(w\ge q\): majority on an odd number of inputs. A uniform polynomial-size fixed-depth bound for these components contradicts the cited majority lower bound. This is an attributed consequence of that theorem and the displayed coefficient identity, not a new Boolean circuit lower bound. \(\square\)

The existing extension-field depth gap is consistent with this result: cheap large-field interpolation and free binary coefficient projection cannot be combined. The corollary does not exclude a special component extraction on a restricted circuit class with independently proved hypotheses.
