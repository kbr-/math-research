A useful near-half import: intrinsic shadow ratios

Let \(J_D\subseteq\mathbb F[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Fix a degree-compatible monomial order and polynomials \(E_i\) of degree at most \(e\), with \(e+K\le D\). Set \[ Z_k=\{f\in V_k:E_if\in J_D\ \forall i\},\qquad c(k)=\dim V_k/Z_k,\quad 0\le k\le K. \] Let \(m_j\) be the number of degree-\(j\) standard monomials of \(J_D\). Suppose \(1\le\delta\le K\), \(X\ge K\) is real, and \(m_j\le\binom Xj\) for every \(K-\delta<j\le K\).

Corollary (intrinsic shadow ratio). If \(c(K)>0\), then \[ \frac{c(K-\delta)}{c(K)} \ge\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\rho^i\right)^{-1}, \qquad \rho=\frac{X-K+\delta}{K-\delta+1}. \] If \(c(K)=0\), the error tuple is already killed on \(V_K\), and no ratio is asserted. In particular \(\rho\le1+1/\delta\) gives at least \(1/(1+e_{\rm Euler}\delta)\). A weight or prefactor is included inside the \(E_i\), and its degree must be included in \(e\).

Proof. The spaces satisfy \(Z_k=Z_K\cap V_k\), \(J_D\cap V_k\subseteq Z_k\), and \(xZ_k\subseteq Z_{k+1}\) for \(k<K\), by bounded absorption. The standard monomials of \(Z_K\) consequently form a downward-closed family \(\mathcal M\) of squarefree monomials, contained in the old standard family. A degree-compatible order makes its cumulative level count exactly \(c(k)\). Indeed leading monomials multiply within the cutoff, and Booleanity removes repeated powers. This argument uses no characteristic-two identity.

For a nonempty degree-\(j\) level in the specified window, write its size as \(\binom{x_j}{j}\), with \(j\le x_j\le X\). The Lovász shadow bound and its iteration imply that level \(i_0=K-\delta\) has at least \(\binom{x_j}{i_0}\) elements. Thus its ratio to that lower level is at most \[ \frac{\binom{x_j}{j}}{\binom{x_j}{i_0}} =\prod_{t=i_0}^{j-1}\frac{x_j-t}{t+1} \le \rho^{j-i_0}. \] Sum over the window and bound that lower level by \(c(i_0)\). If every upper level is empty, the cumulative ratio is one. This proves the formula; \((1+1/\delta)^\delta\le e_{\rm Euler}\) gives its stated consequence. \(\square\)

This repairs no false base-Hilbert-ratio theorem. In the existing five-point control, take \(K=2,\delta=1,X=2\): the new bound is \(2/3\), below the actual error ratio \(3/4\), whereas the base cumulative ratio \(4/5\) is invalid. It uses upper bounds on individual top levels and controls every contained error family, rather than substituting one cumulative quotient ratio. Its level-cap hypothesis remains unproved for the relevant main source. The same proof also accepts a verified cap on the actual error standard family, instead of the larger old family; no such source-wide cap is assumed.
