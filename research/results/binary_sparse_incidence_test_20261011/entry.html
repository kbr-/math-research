For a polynomial \(F\) involving every displayed variable, let \(\Delta(F)\) be the convex hull of zero and its monomial exponents. Let \(\omega(F)\) be the least positive rational \(t\) such that \(t\Delta(F)\) contains an integral point with every coordinate positive. Absent original variables contribute their separate factors of \(q\). See the primary paper.

Newton incidence matching bound

Proposition. Let \(A(z)\) be a polynomial matrix over \(\mathbb F_q[z_1,\ldots,z_P]\) with \(H\ge1\) columns. Delete identically zero rows and denote the remaining row count by \(R\). Form a bipartite graph with row vertices \(j\), weight-column vertices \(i\), and edge \(ji\) exactly when \(A_{ji}\) is a nonzero polynomial. Write \(\nu\) for its matching number. Consider \[ f_j(z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^H A_{ji}(z)w_i=0,\qquad N=\#\{(z,w):A(z)w=0\}. \] If \(\nu<H\), every specialization has a nonzero weight kernel. If \(\nu=H\), the Adolphson–Sperber bound just described gives an exponent at most \(P\). Therefore it cannot, by an exponent larger than \(P\), force a point outside the \(q^P\)-point component \(w=0\) unless a nonzero kernel is already forced by the support graph.

Proof of the matching-deficient case. A nonzero \(k\)-by-\(k\) minor at any specialization has a nonzero determinant term, hence a size-\(k\) matching in the polynomial support graph. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}A(z)\le\nu\) for every \(z\), over the base field or any extension. Rank-nullity gives kernel dimension at least \(H-\nu>0\). This argument uses support only; a matching is not asserted to guarantee actual rank.

Proof of the full-matching case. First keep only the \(P_*\le P\) parameters which occur in some entry. Every weight column occurs because the matching covers it, and every row is nonzero. Hence \[ F(z,w,y)=\sum_{j=1}^R\sum_{i=1}^H y_jw_iA_{ji}(z) \] involves every one of its \(P_*+H+R\) variables. For each matched edge, choose one actual monomial \(y_jw_i z^\alpha\) of \(F\). For every remaining row, choose any one of its monomials. These \(R\) choices already cover all \(y\)- and \(w\)-coordinates. For each still uncovered active parameter, choose one monomial containing it. At most \(P_*\) further choices are needed.

Let \(m\le R+P_*\) be the number of choices, allowing repetitions, and let \(u\) be the sum of their exponent vectors. It is an integral vector with every coordinate positive. Since it is a sum of \(m\) points of \(\Delta(F)\), it belongs to \(m\Delta(F)\); adding the origin also places it in \((R+P_*)\Delta(F)\). Consequently \[ \omega(F)\le R+P_*,\qquad \omega(F)-R\le P_*. \] Every support monomial has total \(y\)-degree one. In a nonnegative rational combination giving an integral positive exponent vector, the sum of combination coefficients equals the integral sum of its \(y\)-coordinates. Thus \(\omega(F)\) is an integer, and at least \(R\). The imported lower bound is precisely an integer divisibility exponent between zero and \(P_*\). Restoring unused parameters multiplies the zero count by \(q^{P-P_*}\), making the total certified exponent at most \(P\). The number \(q^P\) is compatible with every such bound, proving the assertion. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For any \(H\ge1\) and \(P\ge0\), let \(R=H\) and \(A=I_H\), with \(P\) unused parameters. The support has a full matching, \(\omega(\sum_i y_iw_i)=H\), and \(N=q^P\) exactly. This gives an unbounded family attaining the threshold. With \(H=R=2,P=0,q=4\), the all-ones matrix and \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}\), \(\alpha\notin\{0,1\}\), have identical monomial support but ranks one and two. In both cases \(\omega=2\), so the support bound cannot detect that one actually has a kernel. These are polynomial-matrix controls, not hard CNF families.

Degenerate cases. With no equations or an unused weight column, the deficient-matching case already supplies a kernel. With no active parameters, the full-matching exponent is zero before free parameters are restored. Polynomial entries that vanish as functions on \(\mathbb F_q\), despite being nonzero polynomials, can only reduce specialized rank; the graph is defined by the chosen collected polynomial entries. Such coefficient or field-function cancellations are not ruled out. The zero-dimensional weight space \(H=0\) is excluded because it contains no candidate surviving class. No degree, independence or genericity condition is imposed on the parameters.

Positive-kernel control. The single equation \(zw=0\) has \(2q-1\) solutions, including nonzero weights at \(z=0\), but its Cayley polynomial \(yzw\) has \(\omega=1\). The certified exponent is zero, and the actual count is not even divisible by \(q\). Thus divisibility above the zero-weight count was only a sufficient route to existence, never a necessary test for it. No numerical enumeration was needed for these exact counts.
