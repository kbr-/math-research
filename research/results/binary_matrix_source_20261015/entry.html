Triangular concentration gap

Proposition. Let \(F\) be any field, \(R\) a commutative \(F\)-algebra, and \(A,B\) upper triangular \((n+1)\)-by-\((n+1)\) matrices over \(R\), with \(n\ge1\). Define \(\psi_n(x)=Ax,\ \psi_n(y)=B(x+y)\), where the free letters commute with matrix entries. For \(d\ge2n\), \(d\ge2\), put \(H_d=[x,y]x^{d-2}\). Then \(H_d^d\ne0\), is homogeneous of degree \(d^2\), and every word coefficient of \(y\)-degree less than \(n\) in \(\psi_n(H_d^d)\) is zero. For \(d\ge\max(3,2n)\), the same assertions hold for \(H_d^d+K_d^d\), where \(K_d=[x,y]y^{d-2}\). Each has a formula of size \(O(d^2)\) and ordered unbounded-fan-in depth at most five, counting all repeated-factor wires.

These assertions also hold after the invertible substitution \(x\mapsto a=x+y,\ y\mapsto b=x+\lambda y\), for any field element \(\lambda\notin\{0,1\}\). In that presentation all scalar coefficients of both letters in every leaf linear factor are nonzero. In particular one can use \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_4\setminus\mathbb F_2\). The binary presentation itself uses \(a=x,b=y\).

Proof. Let \(T\) be the upper triangular algebra and \(J\) its strictly upper triangular ideal. Since \(R\) is commutative, \(T/J\) is commutative, so \([A,B]\in J\), and \(J^{n+1}=0\). The zero-\(y\) word coefficient of \(\psi_n(H_d)\) is \([A,B]A^{d-2}\in J\); every other word coefficient belongs to \(T\). A word of \(y\)-degree \(j\) in a product of \(d\) homogeneous degree-\(d\) factors splits uniquely into \(d\) blocks of length \(d\). At most \(j\) blocks have positive \(y\)-degree. Its coefficient is therefore in \(J^{d-j}\), because \(J\) is a two-sided ideal. For \(j

The word \((xyx^{d-2})^d\) has coefficient one in \(H_d^d\): each block must choose the first of the two distinct words in \(H_d\). For \(d\ge3\), it does not occur in \(K_d^d\), since its third letter in each block is \(x\), whereas each word of \(K_d\) has third letter \(y\). Thus both asserted polynomials are nonzero, in every characteristic. Their written formulas use \(d\) copies of a difference of two length-\(d\) products, and at most two outer summands, proving the size/depth bounds.

For the dense-letter variant, the zero-\(y\) matrices of \(\psi_n(a)\) and \(\psi_n(b)\) are \(A+B\) and \(A+\lambda B\). They are still upper triangular and their commutator lies in \(J\). All remaining coefficients remain in \(T\), so the same filtration proof applies. The change of free generators has determinant \(\lambda-1\ne0\), hence preserves nonzeroness. The formulas use only copies of \(a,b\), whose two coefficients are all nonzero. No field-descent circuit is inferred. \(\square\)

Match the printed concentration parameters

For the one-channel input \(C=H_d^d\), take \(w_2=1,w_1=2\), \(c=1\), and \(c_1=(1,-1)\). Each \(f_i=H_d\) is the sum of the two length-\(d\) products \(xyx^{d-2}\) and \(yxx^{d-2}\). Consequently \(\ell_2=2\). Every \(d\ge4\) gives a nonzero input of exactly the printed form with all coefficients of \(y\)-degree below two zero after \(\psi_2\). This contradicts the backward implication of Lemma 4.10, not an unstated nonhomogeneous extension of it.

There is also a genuine two-channel outer sum. Take \(w_2=2,w_1=4\), \(c=(1,1)\), and \[ c_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0&0\\0&0&1&-1\end{pmatrix}. \] For each \(i\), use the four length-\(d\) products \(xyx^{d-2},yxx^{d-2},xyy^{d-2},yxy^{d-2}\) as the four coordinates before applying \(c_1\). Thus \(f_i=(H_d,K_d)\), and \(C=H_d^d+K_d^d\). Now \(\ell_2=6\), and every \(d\ge12\) violates the same implication. The dense-letter variant supplies the identical dimension accounting with nonzero coefficients in all leaf factors. This avoids attributing the issue merely to zero leaf coefficients or an output product gate.

The subsequent prescribed tensor substitution also has a direct limitation. If its two pairs of matrices lie in \(T_{n+1}(R)\), then the final inputs lie in \(T_{n+1}(R)\otimes T_{n+1}(R)\). Its ideal \(I=J\otimes T+T\otimes J\) satisfies \(I^{2n+1}=0\): every product of that many ideal terms has at least \(n+1\) strict factors in one tensor component. The quotient is commutative. Hence each commutator belongs to \(I\), and either polynomial above vanishes under the full prescribed tensor substitution whenever \(d\ge2n+1\). This includes the upper bidiagonal matrices used in the source's final step. It rejects that detector family, not arbitrary matrix evaluations of the same dimension.

Indeed, over \(\mathbb F_2\) take \(X=I+E_{12}\), \(Y=E_{21}\). Then \([X,Y]=I\), \(X^2=I\), and \(Y^2=0\). For even \(d\ge4\), \(H_d(X,Y)^d=I\) and \(K_d(X,Y)^d=0\). Thus the two-channel polynomial has a nonzero two-by-two witness. The counterexample is fully compatible with the existence of small generic-matrix witnesses. Dense outer matrices can escape the triangular limitation already on this family; the source's path/support proof does not establish a general concentration result for that replacement.

What fails in the argument, and what remains open

The unit cancellation in the proof of Lemma 4.6, printed page 22, is not justified for general polynomial factors. On a block representing a commutator, if the specialization's jump set avoids that block, the zero-\(y\) coefficient is a commutator of scalar loop weights and is zero. Nonzero leaf coefficients do not make that coefficient vector a unit. The preceding proposition already disproves the final concentration implication, independently of which intermediate proof step is repaired. No separate interpolation refutation or certification of the paper's cited Amitsur theorem is claimed.

Changing to dense nontriangular outer matrices restores nonzeroness of the displayed family, but removes the particular ordered-jump decomposition used for disjoint coefficient supports. No replacement low-degree concentration bound or hard formal extractor is established. The exact proof-image certificate still additionally needs a nonzero homogeneous identity and a matrix dimension sufficient in its original encoding. The source's main theorem is neither imported nor declared false here.

Nonvacuity. The families cover unbounded \(d\), with homogeneous degree \(d^2\), size \(O(d^2)\), and fixed circuit depth. The two-channel example has the source's fixed \(w_1=4,w_2=2\) and works at every \(d\ge12\). The dense \(\mathbb F_4\) presentation satisfies the nonzero leaf-coordinate condition. None is claimed to be an IPS certificate or a hard CNF family.

Degenerate cases. Dimension \(n+1=1\) is outside the statement; every commutator would already evaluate to zero there. Below \(d\ge2n\), the filtration does not imply the low-degree vanishing, and the symbolic two-factor control below is nonzero. At \(d=2\) the two-channel sum can cancel, so it requires \(d\ge3\). The zero polynomial is not used. The dense change excludes \(\lambda=0,1\), ensuring nonzero leaf coefficients and invertibility. General nontriangular matrices are outside the vanishing claim and supply the explicit positive control.
