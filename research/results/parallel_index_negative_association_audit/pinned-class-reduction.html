2. Reduction principle: dense pinned classes are satisfied, sparse ones are rare

Group the terms of \(F\) by tail, the conjunction of their light rows: a class \(\tau\) consists of the terms \([i\mapsto x]\wedge\tau\) for \((i,x)\in P_\tau\). Under \(\rho'\), the class is satisfied as soon as one of its pairs is matched-consistent and \(\tau\) is satisfied; its alive pairs are those of \(P_\tau\cap(R'\times Q)\) with \(\tau\) alive or satisfied.

Lemma (matched edges hit dense pair sets). Fix \(Q\) and \(R'\), and let \(P\) be a set of pairs \((i,x)\) with \(i\notin R'\) and \(x\notin Q\). Under the uniform bijection \(\mu'\) of the matched rows onto the labels outside \(Q\), the probability that no pair of \(P\) is matched is at most \(\exp(-|P|/(n-N))\).

Proof. The indicators \(1[\mu'(i)=x]\) of a uniformly random bijection are negatively associated (Joag-Dev and Proschan, Negative association of random variables, with applications, Ann. Statist. 11 (1983), permutation distributions; imported statement). For each row \(i\) the event \(\mu'(i)\in P_i\), with \(P_i\) the labels paired with \(i\), is an increasing function of the entries of row \(i\), and different rows use disjoint entries, so these events are negatively associated and \(\Pr[\text{none}]\le\prod_i(1-|P_i|/(n-N))\le\exp(-\sum_i|P_i|/(n-N))\). \(\square\)

The two sides of a class. Write \(\lambda_\tau=\Pr[\tau\text{ satisfied}]\ge2^{-|\tau|-2r}\) and \(\lambda'_\tau=\Pr[\tau\text{ alive or satisfied}]\le4^r\lambda_\tau\) (each light row is residual with probability \((N+1)/n\le2^{-|p|}\) or matched consistently with probability at most \(2\cdot2^{-|p|}\)). A class with \(|P_\tau|\ge n\ln(8SK)\), where \(K\) is the number of classes, has a matched pair with probability at least \(1-1/(8SK)\) after discarding the at most \((N+1)n+nN\) pairs touching \(R'\) or \(Q\), so the class is satisfied whenever its tail is, and otherwise its alive pairs are irrelevant only if the tail is not alive either. A class with \(|P_\tau|<n\ln(8SK)\) has at most \(|P_\tau|(N+1)N/n^2\cdot\lambda'_\tau\le4^r(N+1)N\ln(8SK)\lambda_\tau/n\) alive pairs in expectation. Summing, the expected number of alive pairs in sparse classes is at most \(4^r(N+1)N\ln(8SK)\,\mu_{\rm sat}^{\rm sparse}/n\), where \(\mu^{\rm sparse}_{\rm sat}\le\sum_\tau\lambda_\tau\) bounds the expected number of satisfied terms among them; and the dense classes are, up to probability \(1/8\), reduced to their tails, a light disjunction handled by the light-row lemma.

What remains. For the sparse classes one needs \(\Pr[\nu(B)\ge k_0]\) exponentially small in \(k_0\), not just \(\mathbb E|B|/k_0\), because the union over \(S\) readers costs a factor \(S\). Given \(Q\) the alive-pair indicators are negatively associated in \(R'\), which gives \(\Pr[|B|\ge k_0\mid Q]\le(e\,\mathbb E[|B|\mid Q]/k_0)^{k_0}\), so what is missing is control of \(\mathbb E[|B|\mid Q]=\frac{N+1}{n}\sum_{x\in Q}w(x)\) with \(w(x)=\sum_{(i,x)\in P^{\rm sparse}}\lambda'_{(i,x)}\), that is, of how much sparse-class weight a random flat can capture; the crude bound \(N\max_xw(x)\) is too weak when the weight concentrates on few labels, and concentration on few labels is exactly the single-target situation, where \(\nu(B)=1\) although \(|B|\) is large. A proof must therefore bound the matching number directly, for instance through the weights of the labels and rows separately: \(\nu(B)\le\min(\)number of rows touched, number of holes touched\()\), and a hole \(x\in Q\) is touched only if some row pinned to \(x\) is residual, an event of probability at most \((N+1)\deg(x)/n\), while a row is touched only if one of its labels lies in \(Q\). This is the next cycle's target, with tails on shared rows handled by the tree as before.
