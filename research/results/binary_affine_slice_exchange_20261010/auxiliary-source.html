Auxiliary axioms are free

Lemma (auxiliary axiom endpoint). Let \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) contain the Boolean axioms and let \(L\) be any set of polynomials in the same variables. If the system obtained from \(\Gamma\cup L\) by adding a family of ENS blocks has no PC refutation through degree \(B\), then neither has the system obtained from \(\Gamma\) by adding the same blocks. In particular, if \(L\) consists of affine polynomials of rank \(\varrho\) with \(1\notin\operatorname{span}L\), \(n=v-\varrho\), and \(1\notin C_D(\Gamma\cup L)\) for \(D=\lfloor n/2-s\rfloor\), \(s=o(n/(\log n)^{\ell-2})\), \(D\ge2\) and at least the degrees of \(\Gamma\), then the product-only ENS source over \(\Gamma\) with at most \(n^A\) blocks of accuracy \(\lfloor n^\epsilon\rfloor\), \(0<\epsilon<1/(3\ell)\), has no PC refutation through degree \(\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), for large \(n\).

Proof. A refutation from fewer axioms is a refutation from more. The second statement is the affine free dimension lemma and its consequence applied to \(\Gamma\cup L\), followed by the first statement. \(\square\)

Polynomial size enters only through the block count: a bound of \(v^{A'}\) blocks is covered when \(v\le n^C\). The reviewer's excerpts did not contain item 1 of the free-dimension lemma or the product-only grammar; both are as recorded in that entry and in the moving-cutoff theorem.

The formula whose Frege proofs are bounded is \(\Gamma\); \(L\) is used only in the analysis and may depend on \(v\) arbitrarily. Restricted to the zero set of \(L\), a clause of \(\Gamma\) on \(k\) variables becomes a relation on \(k\) linear forms in the free variables. So every bounded-width CNF carries, for each subspace, a parity-row system of the kind studied in the dense-parity entry, and the family is good if one of them is hard at half its dimension. For a random subspace of a random \(k\)-CNF these are the random instances for which the heuristic count is discouraging; the freedom lies in choosing formula and subspace together.

Specification. By the rule in Where we stand the weakened target is matched in the specification: the free-dimension count may use affine equations that are auxiliary and not rows of the formula, with the pairwise filtration condition read on the formula's rows together with them. This is a relaxation of the specification adopted in the free-dimension entry, so its witness, the guarded family with no affine equations, serves.

Why half stays

On the plain cube with only the Boolean axioms and cutoff \(K=\alpha v\), \(\alpha<1/2\), let the inputs be polynomials of degree \(d\) and \(q\) a combination of them. The engine's step multiplies the prefactor by \(1+q\), and the functions of degree at most \(K\) killed by it include the products \(qg\) of degree at most \(K\), which bounds the rank drop from below. Assume, as the semi-regularity hypothesis, that the kernel of \(1+q\) on \(V_K\) is exactly \(q\,V_{K-d}\) and that these products have dimension about \(N_v(K-d)-N_v(K-2d)+\cdots\), so one step lowers the rank by a factor about \(1/(1+\rho^d)\), \(\rho=\alpha/(1-\alpha)\), which is the engine's bound up to constants. With \(d\) polylogarithmic at the upper levels this is too little contraction unless \(\rho^d\) is at least an inverse polylogarithm, that is \(\alpha=1/2-O(\log\log v/d)\). This is a heuristic computation, not a proof that no choice of \(q\) does better; it agrees with the reviewer's expectation for its second proposal and with the main notebook's two-layer budget.

Falsification attempt on this line

The line's general claim after this block: some polynomial-size family is hard at half the dimension of some subspace. Three attacks. A general ceiling below half for all parity-row systems of bounded \(k\) would refute it; none is known, and the dense-parity proposition shows that no local argument gives one. The dimension count would refute it for random instances if made rigorous; it says nothing about structured ones. A general ceiling of about half for every unsatisfiable system would show the window is at most \(O(1)\) wide; it is neither proved nor refuted, as recorded. The claim survives because nothing is known in either direction, which is the weakest kind of survival.

Absurd bridges

  • Coset leaders of cut codes. Translation: for binomial systems such as Tseitin in multiplicative variables, standard monomials are minimum-weight representatives of cosets of the cut space, so thin counts become counts of coset leaders of a given weight, and a covering-radius theorem would bound the shattered dimension. For a cubic graph on \(N\) vertices there are \(2^{N/2+1}\) cosets, thin from weight about \(N/4\), a sixth of the variables. Smallest test: whether the translation exists in characteristic two. Answers. It would give a family whose quotient is small for a structural reason. Test. Falsified for the parity formulation over the goal's field: its multiplicative variables need characteristic different from two, and over \(\mathbb F_2\) Tseitin parity systems are refuted in constant degree. Binomial systems modulo an odd prime over an extension of \(\mathbb F_2\) containing the roots of unity were not tried.
  • Whitney numbers of matroids. Translation: with affine axioms kept in the original variables, a monomial on a dependent set is reducible and monomials on bases of one flat agree modulo lower degree, so standard monomials of degree \(j\) would be counted by flats of rank \(j\), and unimodality theorems for Whitney numbers would describe the levels. Smallest test: compare with the count in free coordinates. Answers. It would let the choice of subspace thin the quotient. Test. Falsified: an affine substitution preserves the degree filtration, so the Hilbert function is that of the cube on the free variables in any coordinates; the flats count the same \(\binom nj\) classes and nothing is gained.
