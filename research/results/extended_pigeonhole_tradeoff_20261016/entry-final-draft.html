The tradeoff, read

Impagliazzo, Pudlák and Sgall, Lower Bounds for the Polynomial Calculus and the Groebner Basis Algorithm, ECCC TR97-042 (September 1997), read from its pages rendered as images, its text not extracting: Definition 1.1 (a refutation's lines are polynomials, degree the largest degree of a line, axioms \(x^2-x\) included for all variables), Definition 1.2 (functional PHP: \(Q_i=1-\sum_jx_{ij}\), \(x_{ij}x_{ij'}\), \(x_{ij}x_{i'j}\)), Lemma 3.1 with its proof, Section 5 with Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2 and its proof, Theorem 5.6 and its proof and Corollaries 5.3 and 5.7. Recorded as imp:ips-size-degree-tradeoff:

  • Lemma 3.1: if every nonzero monomial of a derivation of \(g\) from \(P\) has at most \(b\) distinct variables, there is a derivation of degree \(\max(b+1,d)\), \(d\) the largest degree of \(g\) and \(P\).
  • Lemma 5.1 (from Clegg–Edmonds–Impagliazzo): if \(P\) has degree at most \(d\), \(P|_{x=0}\) has a refutation of degree \(d\) and \(P|_{x=1}\) one of degree \(d+1\), then \(P\) has one of degree \(d+1\).
  • Theorem 5.2: \(P\) of degree at most \(d\) in \(n\) variables with a refutation of \(M\) nonzero monomials has one of degree \(O(d+(n\log M)^{1/2})\); its proof gives at most \(1+b-\log_{1-b/n}S\le1+b+(n\ln M)^{1/2}\) with \(b=\max(d,\lceil(n\ln M)^{1/2}\rceil)\) and \(S\le M\) the number of monomials with at least \(b\) distinct variables.
  • Theorem 5.6: if functional \(\mathrm{PHP}^m_n\) has a refutation of \(M\) nonzero monomials, it has one of degree \(O((n\log M)^{1/2})\); the proof calls a monomial large when it has at least \(b=\lceil(2n\ln M)^{1/2}\rceil\) distinct holes, branches on a variable \(x_{ij}\) of a hole met by at least \(bS/n\) large monomials, and, when \(x_{ij}\) itself is in fewer than \(bS/2n\) of them, sets in \(P|_{x_{ij}=1}\) every \(x_{i'j}\) and \(x_{ij'}\) to \(0\), degree-one axioms there. Corollary 5.7: every refutation of functional PHP over any field has \(2^{\Omega(n)}\) monomials.

Extension variables as items

Setting. Over a field \(F\), \(P\) is functional \(\mathrm{PHP}^m_n\), \(m>n\), with \(x^2-x\) for all variables, together with \(z_l^2-z_l\) and \(z_l-Q_l\) for \(l\le M\), each \(Q_l\) a polynomial of degree at most \(k\) in the \(x\)'s and the earlier \(z_1,\ldots,z_{l-1}\) (nonrecursive definitions, in the \(x\)'s alone, are a special case), \(0/1\)-valued on \(\{0,1\}\)-points. Define \(\varphi(z_l)=Q_l(x,\varphi(z_1),\ldots,\varphi(z_{l-1}))\), a polynomial in the \(x\)'s, \(0/1\)-valued on \(\{0,1\}\)-points by induction on \(l\), so that \(\varphi(z_l)^2-\varphi(z_l)\) has multilinear representative \(0\); \(K=\max_l\deg\varphi(z_l)\), at most \(k^t\) for nesting depth \(t\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\) every polynomial is \(0/1\)-valued, and a parity of earlier variables has degree one at any nesting and fan-in. Refutations and their degrees are as in Definition 1.1; size is the number of nonzero monomials over all lines.

Theorem (extended pigeonhole tradeoff). If \(1\le K\le n/4\) and \(\Gamma=(n/2+1)/K-\max(k,2)-3>0\), every refutation of \(P\) has size \(s\) with \[\ln s\ \ge\ \frac{\Gamma^2}{8(n+M)}.\]

Proof. Degree step. The substitution \(z_l\mapsto\varphi(z_l)\) is a ring homomorphism \(\varphi\) of polynomial rings fixing the \(x\)'s. In a refutation of degree \(D\), a line \(f\) of degree at most \(D\) goes to \(\varphi(f)\) of degree at most \(KD\); a multiplication \(f\mapsto x_{ij}f\) stays one, of degree at most \(1+K(D-1)\le KD\); a multiplication \(f\mapsto z_lf\) becomes \(\varphi(f)\mapsto\varphi(z_l)\varphi(f)\), of degree at most \(K+K(D-1)=KD\), carried out monomial by monomial of \(\varphi(z_l)\) through at most \(K\) multiplications by variables and then additions, all of degree at most \(KD\). The axioms \(z_l-Q_l\) go to \(\varphi(z_l)-\varphi(z_l)=0\); \(z_l^2-z_l\) goes to \(\varphi(z_l)^2-\varphi(z_l)\), whose multilinear representative is \(0\), so by the proof of Lemma 3.1 it is derivable from \(x^2-x\) in degree \(2K\). This is a refutation of functional PHP of degree at most \(\max(KD,2K)\); the PHP degree bound of imp:razborov-generic-pigeonhole-degree, plain principle, is \(n/2+1\), and \(2K\le n/2\), so \(KD\ge n/2+1\).

Items. The items of a monomial are the holes of its \(x\)-variables and its \(z\)-variables; it is large when it has at least \(b\) distinct items, \(b=\max(k,2,\lceil(2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\rceil)\); put \(\beta=b/(2(n+M))\). Claim: for every restriction \(P_\rho\) of \(P\), setting some variables to constants, with a refutation of \(S\) large monomials, \(P_\rho\) has a refutation of degree at most \(f(S)=2+b-\log_{1-\beta}S\) if \(S\ge1\), and \(b+1\) if \(S=0\). Induction on \(S\) and on the number of variables left, as in Theorem 5.6. Restricting a refutation gives one of the restricted axioms with no more large monomials, items only being lost.

\(S=0\). Remove the monomials containing some \(x_{ij}x_{i'j}\), \(i\ne i'\), with the hole axioms, as in Theorem 5.6 (a restricted hole axiom is the same, a single variable \(x_{i'j}\), or \(0\)); then a monomial's \(x\)-variables lie in distinct holes, so its distinct variables are at most its items, fewer than \(b\), and Lemma 3.1 gives degree \(\max(b+1,d)=b+1\), the axioms having degree at most \(\max(k,2)\le b\).

\(S\ge1\). The items of the large monomials number at least \(bS\) in all, over at most \(n+M\) items, so some item is in at least \(2\beta S\) of them. If it is \(z_l\): \(P_\rho|_{z_l=0}\) loses those, keeping at most \((1-2\beta)S\le(1-\beta)S\), so by induction it has degree at most \(1+b-\log_{1-\beta}S\) (or \(b+1\), no more); \(P_\rho|_{z_l=1}\) has fewer variables and at most \(S\) large monomials, so degree at most \(f(S)\); Lemma 5.1, \(P_\rho\) having degree at most \(\max(k,2)\le1+b-\log_{1-\beta}S\), gives \(f(S)\). If it is a hole \(j\), take an unset \(x_{ij}\): if \(x_{ij}\) is in at least \(\beta S\) large monomials, the branch \(x_{ij}=0\) loses them and the branch \(x_{ij}=1\) has fewer variables, and Lemma 5.1 gives \(f(S)\); otherwise at least \(\beta S\) large monomials meet hole \(j\) through some \(x_{i'j}\), \(i'\ne i\), and do not contain \(x_{ij}\), so in \(P_\rho|_{x_{ij}=1}\) setting every \(x_{i'j}\) and \(x_{ij'}\) to \(0\), degree-one axioms there, gives \(P'\) with at most \((1-\beta)S\) large monomials, of degree at most \(1+b-\log_{1-\beta}S\) by induction; \(P_\rho|_{x_{ij}=1}\) derives the axioms of \(P'\) from its own with those degree-one axioms in degree at most \(\max(k,2)\), so it has the same degree; \(P_\rho|_{x_{ij}=0}\) has fewer variables, degree at most \(f(S)\), and Lemma 5.1 with \(x_{ij}\) and \(1-x_{ij}\) exchanged, an affine change of variable that keeps degree, gives \(f(S)\). The induction reads the axioms only through their degree, at most \(\max(k,2)\): a restricted extension axiom, \(z_l-Q_l|_\rho\) or \(c-Q_l|_\rho\) once \(z_l\) is set, is another axiom of degree at most \(k\), and the degree step is applied only to the original system.

Count. With \(S\le s\), \(D\le2+b-\log_{1-\beta}s\le2+b+\ln s/\beta=2+b+2(n+M)\ln s/b\), as \(-\ln(1-\beta)\ge\beta\) (if \(\beta\ge1\), then, \(k\) and \(2\) being below \(2(n+M)\), \((2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}>2(n+M)-1\), so \(\ln s>2(n+M)-2\ge n/8\ge\Gamma^2/(8(n+M))\), as \(\Gamma<n/2+1\le n\)). Since \(b\ge(2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\), the last term is at most \((2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\), and \(b\le\max(k,2)+1+(2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\), so \(D\le3+\max(k,2)+2(2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\). With \(D\ge(n/2+1)/K\), \(2(2(n+M)\ln s)^{1/2}\ge\Gamma\), and \(\ln s\ge\Gamma^2/(8(n+M))\). \(\square\)

Hypotheses, each with its step. \(0/1\)-valued definitions: only the degree step, through \(\varphi(z_l)^2-\varphi(z_l)\); without it the substituted Boolean axiom is not derivable. The composed degree \(K\le n/4\): the degree step, \(2K\le n/2\) keeping the Boolean images below the PHP bound; \(K\ge1\) only for the count \(KD\). The definitions' own degree \(k\): only the axiom degree \(\max(k,2)\) in the induction. Acyclic definitions, each from earlier ones: only to define \(\varphi\); a first draft assumed them nonrecursive, copying the earlier entry's setting rather than reading the proof, which needs only acyclicity. Functional PHP with \(m>n\): the PHP degree bound uses the whole system; the induction itself uses the hole axioms, and the functional axioms \(x_{ij}x_{ij'}\) only in setting \(x_{ij'}\) to \(0\), which does not enter the count. No condition on how many holes a definition touches, and none on \(r\).

Degenerate cases. \(M=0\): items are holes only, and the argument is Theorem 5.6's; with \(K=k=1\) it gives \(\ln s\ge((n/2+1)-5)^2/(8n)\), of order \(n/32\). \(\Gamma\le0\): no conclusion, as when \(K\) is near \(\sqrt{n/2}\) with \(k=K\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\) with parity definitions, \(K=k=1\) whatever the nesting, \(\Gamma=n/2-4\) needing \(n>8\), and the bound is that of \(M=0\) with \(n\) replaced by \(n+M\) in the denominator. A constant \(Q_l\): the theorem holds as stated, the item \(z_l\) being branched on like any other. \(m\le n\): the system is satisfiable and has no refutation.

Nonvacuity. For the item budget, over \(\mathbb F_2\) take \(n=2^a\), \(m=n+1\), \(k=K=a\), \(M=n^{3/2}\) nonrecursive definitions, each any product of at most \(a\) of the \(x\)'s, on \(N=mn+M\) variables. Then \(K\le n/4\), \(\Gamma=(n/2+1)/a-a-3>0\) for \(a\ge5\), and the bound is \(\Gamma^2/(8(n+n^{3/2}))\), of order \(n^{1/2}/(32a^2)\), against \(\ln N\) of order \(2\ln n\): computed at \(a=20,30,40,60\), it is \(0.0798\), \(1.14\), \(20.5\), \(9321\) against \(\ln N=27.7\), \(41.6\), \(55.5\), \(83.2\), superpolynomial in \(N\) in the limit and not before \(a\) between \(40\) and \(60\). The witness satisfies the hypotheses with \(M>0\) and nonconstant definitions; which branches the induction takes depends on the refutation. A nested witness: over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(M=n^{3/2}\) parities, each of any set of \(x\)'s and earlier \(z\)'s, have \(K=k=1\), and the bound \((n/2-4)^2/(8(n+n^{3/2}))\) is of order \(n^{1/2}/32\). Plainest bound.
