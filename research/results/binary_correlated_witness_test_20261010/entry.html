Carry one common evaluation relation

Let \(\pi\) be a finite Boolean/MOD2 proof DAG, of total symbol and wire size \(S\), and let \(y\) denote its original variables. Introduce a Boolean witness \(z_g\) for each formula-evaluation gate. Shared occurrences may use the same witness; distinct copies may instead be linked by equality. Retain the truth table of each AND, OR, NOT or binary XOR gate as a constant-width CNF. A gate with at most two inputs has at most eight assignments, and one clause forbids each assignment inconsistent with its output. Thus all gate constraints together have \(O(S)\) variables and clauses. Unbounded fan-in AND/OR/parity gates may first be replaced by binary chains with linear wire cost; this controls relation size, not evaluation depth.

For every assignment to \(y\), a topological evaluation determines exactly one assignment to all gate witnesses satisfying these constraints. Conversely each satisfying witness must equal that evaluation, by induction in the same order. Correlation is retained by leaving the shared witnesses unquantified until they are joined. AND, OR, NOT and parity therefore compose exactly. A copy reuses a value or imposes its equality, and an MP triple has output values \(a,c,b\), with \(c=\neg a\lor b\), so \(a=c=1\) forces \(b=1\) on the same witness. No fresh, independent choice of the implication's private assignment is made.

This is the standard definitional encoding of circuit evaluation, not a new proof simulation or a modification of the charged CNF. It does not assert that every line of a refutation is true on every assignment. An initial clause is true only when its corresponding original constraint holds; conjunction of the inconsistent full old system yields an empty relation, which supplies no useful normalized annotation by itself. All the construction has established is correct shared evaluation and local inference semantics, with a linear-size witness relation.

The missing resource survives intact

For a gate output \(z_o\), existentially quantifying its evaluation witnesses in \(T(y,z)\land z_o\) returns the original function of \(y\). Consequently small constant-width evaluation relations can project to every function having a small ordinary Boolean circuit. Their CNF formula depth is two even when the evaluated circuit has growing depth. A topological order of constraints is not a constant-depth procedure for eliminating their witnesses.

The already proved trace/ladder control is more specific than this general observation. On the ladder, once a valid shared rung assignment is fixed, the left private rail labels are uniquely determined by their prefix sums, if the left charge condition holds; the same is true on the right. Both sides have constant-width local clauses and path-shaped state propagation. The conjunction has a linear-line, width-fifteen resolution refutation and a polynomial-size bounded-depth Frege refutation. Yet any fixed-depth \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\) separator of its two side projections is superpolynomial. This is the full existing result, not a new experiment or an assumption about expanders.

It follows that uniqueness of the private witness, polynomial relation size, bounded clause width, an acyclic propagation order, and exact local MP/copy composition cannot by themselves justify the desired generic extraction. The counter still allows the actual proof to be short and constant depth; it is not just a complicated function presented by a large proof. Any parity gates that occur can be encoded faithfully by the same witness method, but adding that capability cannot rescue an implication already false in the Boolean fragment.

Nor does moving to the expander automatically make witness selection cheap. The previous cycle constructs connected sides independently of the proof and proves that every zero-chord spanning-tree choice has a hard private coordinate, even on its compatibility promise. Thus a canonical selection can preserve all shared inference semantics while losing exactly the circuit-depth resource being sought. That result does not rule out a selection adapted to the proof or a different relational extraction.

What a genuinely stronger restriction would have to supply

A constant-depth, polynomial-size witness-elimination procedure for the relevant shared output would suffice. An annotation whose witnesses are themselves constant-depth, polynomial-size functions of the boundary would also suffice by simultaneous substitution, provided composition does not introduce proof-height dependence. These are sufficient extra hypotheses, not obtained consequences of the construction above. Calling the relation “correlated” or its solution “unique” supplies neither. The needed depth is \(f(d)\), independent of \(n,S,A\); logarithmic balancing is still too large for the circuit contradiction.

I did not find a proof-derived restriction beyond these restatements that both survives the ladder control and applies to every hypothetical small expander Frege proof. Expansion remains available as a distinguishing hypothesis, but the present witness representation carries no quantitative use of it that bounds elimination. Requiring a small elimination circuit is the original missing extraction obligation in another form, so there is no justification for another cycle spent refining this representation alone.
