Question. Braun's finite-parameter theorem uses its counting bound \(m\ln(4M)\le k^2\) only to obtain a nonzero common kernel in \(\mathcal L_k\). When the proper high-rank blocks are coordinate blocks, the transversal of lem:greedy-transversal-certificate supplies that kernel from \(M\le k\) alone. Does that give exclusion at degree about \(n/(2M)\), what do the published and exact summed counts give there, and is it a joint saving below the summed ranks?

General statement. thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion: over \(\mathbb F_2\), a complete old-affine family whose proper blocks of rank above \(h(k+1)\) are at most \(k\) coordinate blocks has no ordinary-PC refutation through any \(D\ge2h+1\) with \(2k(D+1)-1\le n\); the greedy inequality may replace “at most \(k\)”. On spread coordinate families with \(\ln M\ge-(kr/m)\ln(1-1/\ell)\), roughly \(M\lesssim\sqrt{m\ln M/3}\) at \(k=M\), the exact summed restriction ranks already reach \(\dim\mathcal L_k\). The theorem is elementary: it fixes one pinned bit of each block, in fresh rows, at its unpinned value, and applies Braun's row separation.

Small-inventory coordinate exclusion

Setting. As in Braun's finite-parameter theorem (Section 5, claim audit:ordinary-restriction-affine-family), over \(\mathbb F_2\), the field of that publication: \(m>0\), \(\ell\ge2\), \(n=2^\ell\), \(h=3\ell\), \(v=m\ell\), a finite complete old-affine family adjoined to \(\mathcal Q_{m,\ell}\), and \(\mathcal L_k\) the span of monomials of degree at most \(k\) with at most one variable from each pigeon row. A block is a coordinate block when its input span is spanned by literals \(x_b+c_b\), \(c_b\in\{0,1\}\); if it is proper, these involve distinct bits, its rank is the number \(|P|\) of pinned bits, and its zero flat is \(\{x_b=c_b,\ b\in P\}\).

Statement. (a) Let \(k\ge1\) and suppose the proper blocks of rank greater than \(h(k+1)\) are coordinate blocks, \(M\) of them. If \(D\ge2h+1\) and \(2k(D+1)-1\le n\), and either \(M\le k\) or, with \(r=h(k+1)+1\), \(V=2v\) (or \(V=v\) when every bit pinned by several of these blocks has one constant), some integer \(0\le t\le k-1\) has \(M\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\bigl(1-(r-\ell i)/V\bigr)\le k-t\), then the augmented system has no ordinary-PC refutation through degree \(D\). (b) In particular, for \(M\ge1\), a family whose proper blocks of rank greater than \(h(M+1)\) are at most \(M\) coordinate blocks has no refutation through \(D_M=\lfloor(n+1)/(2M)\rfloor-1\) whenever \(D_M\ge2h+1\). (c) Let \(M\le k\) spread coordinate blocks each pin exactly \(r\) bits in distinct rows. If \(\ln M\ge-(kr/m)\ln(1-1/\ell)\), the sum of their exact restriction ranks on \(\mathcal L_k\) is at least \(\dim\mathcal L_k\), so the exact summed count certifies no common kernel at this \(k\), while (a) gives one when \(r>h(k+1)\).

Proof of (a). Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\) be the pinned sets of the proper blocks of rank above \(h(k+1)\). Under \(M\le k\): each has more than \(h(k+1)\ge\ell(k-1)\) bits, at most \(\ell\) per row, so it meets at least \(k\) rows. Choose for each block in turn a pinned bit \(b_a\in P_a\) in a row not used before; at most \(k-1\) rows are used when a block is handled, so one exists. Put \(f=\prod_a(x_{b_a}+c_{b_a})\), with \(f=1\) if \(M=0\). The factors lie in distinct rows, so \(f\) expands into monomials with at most one variable per row, of degree at most \(M\le k\): \(f\in\mathcal L_k\), and \(f\ne0\). Substituting \(x_{b_a}=c_{b_a}\) makes a factor zero, so the ordinary restriction of \(f\) to block \(a\)'s zero flat is the zero polynomial. Under the product inequality, lem:greedy-transversal-certificate gives such an \(f\), a product of at most \(k\) literal factors in distinct rows. Braun's restriction-ideal lemma (claim lem:ordinary-affine-restriction-ideal) then gives \eqref{eq:literal-ideal} for each such block: \(f=\sum_ia_ig_i\) with \(\deg a_i\le k-1\). This is exactly what the common-kernel lemma supplies in Braun's proof, so the rest of that proof applies unchanged: weighted removal (claim lem:affine-family-removal-with-unit-blocks), with blocks of rank at most \(h(k+1)\) handled by packing and unit spans by constants, derives \(f\) from the old base through \(B=k(D+1)\), contradicting Braun's row separation (claim lem:cube-residual-dual-separation), which needs \(1\le k\le B\), \(4(k-1)<n\) and \(2B-1\le n\); the middle condition follows from the last with \(D\ge2h+1\). Part (b) is (a) at \(k=M\), where \(D_M\) is the largest \(D\) with \(2M(D+1)-1\le n\).

Proof of (c). Translating the pinned bits of one block to zero changes neither its restriction rank nor row-linearity, so the rank of block \(a\) is the number of row-linear monomials of degree at most \(k\) avoiding \(P_a\), as in the ceiling proof. Let \(R\) be the \(r\) rows it meets. The monomials of degree exactly \(j\) on a row set \(S\) avoiding \(P_a\) number \(\ell^j(1-1/\ell)^{|S\cap R|}\). For a uniform \(j\)-subset \(S\) of rows, \(E|S\cap R|=jr/m\), and \(t\mapsto(1-1/\ell)^t\) is convex, so the count over all \(S\) is at least \(\binom mj\ell^j(1-1/\ell)^{jr/m}\ge\binom mj\ell^j(1-1/\ell)^{kr/m}\). Summing over \(j\le k\) gives rank at least \((1-1/\ell)^{kr/m}\dim\mathcal L_k\), since \(\dim\mathcal L_k=\sum_{j\le k}\binom mj\ell^j\). The \(M\) blocks together give at least \(M(1-1/\ell)^{kr/m}\dim\mathcal L_k\ge\dim\mathcal L_k\) under the hypothesis.

What the theorem is. Two elementary facts the comparison below must be read with. First, with \(f=1\), Braun's removal and row separation already exclude every complete family whose proper blocks all have rank at most \(h(k+1)\), at every \(k\) with \(D\ge2h+1\) and \(2k(D+1)-1\le n\), with no counting condition. Second, over \(\mathbb F_2\) the weight \(\prod_a(x_{b_a}+c_{b_a})\) is the indicator of \(\{x_{b_a}=c_{b_a}+1\ \forall a\}\): weighting by it is restricting each chosen bit to the value its block forbids, which makes every high-rank block's flat unreachable. So the theorem says: kill each high-rank coordinate block by fixing one of its bits, in distinct rows, then apply row separation. Its saving below the summed ranks is a cover by \(M\) single literals, the most elementary construction available, and part (c) holds almost automatically, since a single spread block of rank \(r\ll v\) has rank close to \(\dim\mathcal L_k\).

Degenerate cases. No proper high-rank block: \(f=1\), which excludes the family at every \(k\) with \(D\ge2h+1\) and \(2k(D+1)-1\le n\), with no counting condition (see above). \(M>k\) without the product inequality: (a) says nothing. \(D_M<2h+1\), as for \(M\) above about \(n/(12\ell)\): (b) says nothing. New mathematics. thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion: for complete families over \(\mathbb F_2\) whose proper high-rank blocks are coordinate blocks, fixing one pinned bit per block in distinct rows replaces Braun's counting bound in his common-kernel step, raising the certified exclusion degree for \(M\) such blocks to \(\lfloor(n+1)/(2M)\rfloor-1\); a Jensen bound shows that on spread families with \(\ln M\ge-(kr/m)\ln(1-1/\ell)\) the exact summed ranks already reach \(\dim\mathcal L_k\). The proof is Braun's, with one lemma replaced by an elementary transversal.

Selection gain. Braun's common-kernel selection is certified for coordinate families from the block count alone; on spread families in the range of (c) this is beyond the summed ranks, and the certified degree rises by factors of order \(\sqrt{n\ln M}/M\) for \(2\le M\le n^{1/4}\) in the computed table.
