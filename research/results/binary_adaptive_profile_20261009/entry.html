9 October 2026 — A codimension budget permits adaptive common-input batches

Working weighted batching criterion. Arbitrary source density is not established.

Question. On general-step, can the cheap selected-input bypass work across different parent tuples without one globally surviving common input or a thin initial error profile? Supply an explicit cumulative cost using the actual input subspaces, and distinguish it from a claim that every source meets that cost.

General statement. thm:codimension-batch-removal uses the weighted cube's uniform image floor to select a surviving common input separately in each of a bounded number of groups. The group criterion is a computable codimension sum; targets are selected after earlier weights, and their degrees add.

New mathematics. This is a complete adaptive composition of the existing common-input weighted specialization and the imported weighted-cube floor. It supplies a conditional multi-parent alternative to a uniform initial-ratio demand. It is not a new scalar-normalization engine or a theorem about arbitrary source subspaces.

Goal progress. Unchanged. The cumulative codimension premise is not supplied by the compiler, and a deeper-source recurrence is absent. The theorem covers a dense-reference class rather than the unrestricted binary statement.

A paid group capacity

Let \(J_D\) be a proper binary Boolean pseudoideal on \(v\) old variables containing an unsatisfiable old family \(\Gamma\), with \(u=\max(2,\max\deg\Gamma)\). Optional disjoint pair-exclusion relations define admissible monomials as in the preceding profile theorem. Fix integers \(r\ge1\), \(\delta\ge r\), \(L\ge1\), and an original proof ceiling \(B\ge u\). Let \(w_0\notin J_D\), \(\deg w_0\le b_0\), and assume \[ D\ge b_0+Lr+\max(B,2\delta). \tag{1} \] Put \(a=D-b_0-Lr-1\) and \(H=\binom ar\); (1) gives \(a\ge r\).

Let \(V\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le r}\) contain every admissible degree-\(r\) monomial. For each of finitely many nonempty actual old tuples \(g_{j,i}\), of degree at most \(\delta\), supply reference tuples \(q_{j,i}\in V\) satisfying \[ w_0(g_{j,i}-q_{j,i})\in J_D. \tag{2} \] These are memberships in the same paid pseudoideal, not unrestricted ideal membership. Set \(G_j=\operatorname{span}_i q_{j,i}\subseteq V\) and \(c_j=\operatorname{codim}_V G_j\). Each actual tuple has a fresh complete ENS block of positive accuracy, including its coefficient fields. No tuple reads another block of this family.

Theorem (codimension batch removal). If the blocks admit a partition into at most \(L\) groups such that \[ \sum_{j\text{ in a group}}c_j<H, \tag{3} \] there are constant binary rows, with only one active row per block, and a surviving weight \(W\), \(\deg W\le b_0+Lr\), killing every weighted companion and field image. Earlier memberships \(w_0E\in J_D\) persist whenever \(\deg(w_0E)+Lr\le D\). A complete original source proof with constant earlier coefficient substitutions is excluded through degree \(B\) if all its earlier weighted generators satisfy that preservation bound. In particular this excludes the one-level source over \(\Gamma\) with these old-only tuples. All original accuracies and companion degrees remain charged.

A sufficient grouping test, when \(H\ge2\), is \[ \max_j c_j\le(H-1)/2,\qquad \sum_jc_j\le L(H-1)/2. \tag{4} \] For \(H=1\), all \(c_j=0\) suffice as one group. Condition (4) is only a sufficient packing rule; a supplied partition satisfying (3) is enough.

Choose a new target after each weight

Proof. Process the nonempty groups in any fixed order. Before group \(s\le L\), suppose \[ w_{s-1}=w_0 f_1\cdots f_{s-1}\notin J_D,\qquad\deg f_i\le r. \] Its degree is at most \(b_0+(s-1)r\). The weighted cube supplies at least \(a\) free coordinates after this weight, since the residual window covers \(u\) by (1). No selected cube contains a designated forbidden pair. Therefore the map \(V\to Q_D\), \(f\mapsto[w_{s-1}f]\), has rank at least \(H\).

The common reference input space \(G=\bigcap_{j\text{ in group }s}G_j\) has codimension at most the sum in (3), hence below that rank. It cannot lie inside the kernel of the weight map. Choose binary \(f_s\in G\) with \(w_{s-1}f_s\notin J_D\). This proves survival inductively; targets for different groups need not agree, and no product of independently chosen weights is assumed to survive.

For each block \(j\) of group \(s\), write \(f_s=\sum_i\alpha_{j,i}q_{j,i}\) with binary constants. Put these constants in its first actual coefficient row and zero every other row. The actual selected row is \(A_j=\sum_i\alpha_{j,i}g_{j,i}\), so (2) gives \(w_0(A_j-f_s)\in J_D\). The complete product is \(1-A_j\); all coefficient fields are zero at this assignment.

Set \(W=w_0\prod_s f_s\). To check a companion of group \(s\), write \(V_s=\prod_{t\ne s}f_t\). Then \[ Wg_{j,i}(1-A_j) =w_0V_s g_{j,i}f_s(1-f_s) +V_s g_{j,i}f_s\,w_0(f_s-A_j). \tag{5} \] The first summand has a Boolean certificate through degree at most \(b_0+(L-1)r+\delta+2r\le b_0+Lr+2\delta\). The second multiplies the supplied comparison by degree at most \(Lr+\delta\), so bounded absorption fits the same ceiling. This is within (1). Thus every weighted companion vanishes with the single final weight; no reference input position was substituted for an actual one.

Earlier weighted memberships are multiplied by \(\prod_s f_s\), giving exactly the stated preservation cost. Finally, all new coefficients are constants. Multiplying a constantly specialized original degree-\(B\) proof by \(W\) fits \(b_0+Lr+B\le D\). Old axioms are in \(J_D\), all earlier generators are preserved by premise, and new generators were checked in (5). The final line would put \(W\) in \(J_D\), contradicting survival. Ordinary NS replay at its original degree is included. For an earlier nonconstant substitution its global degree multiplier and fields must instead be paid explicitly; this theorem does not erase that cost.

To prove (4), greedily fill groups up to total integer cost \(H-1\). If adding an item would exceed that capacity, the completed group's load is strictly greater than \((H-1)/2\), because every next item is at most half the capacity. More than \(L\) groups would produce \(L\) such completed groups and total cost greater than \(L(H-1)/2\), contradiction. Zero-cost items cause no exception. Thus at most \(L\) groups suffice. The \(H=1\) statement is immediate. \(\square\)

Comparison with the existing stronger special cases

The older codimension criterion and its target-relative refinement already select a surviving common class by comparing reduction losses with target-image rank. The present theorem uses the actual input spans, constant coefficients and a uniform floor available after every prior target. Its contribution is the explicit adaptive group/degree composition, not the intersection inequality itself.

The handoff's core–residual theorem already learns supplied common core directions and handles residuals with their packing costs. Here a common surviving direction is certified anew in each group by the weighted cube; a low-residual-rank hypothesis is not imposed. This does not prove that arbitrary groups have small codimension sums.

Before using the conservative floor, use stronger available information. If \(w_0=1\) and the whole \(V\) injects into the old quotient, then \(\sum_jc_j<\dim V\) already gives one surviving common input. On the low-degree admissible layer in the growing family this injection holds while \(2r<\varepsilon\), as the preceding proof shows. Sequential batching is unnecessary in that case. The theorem is useful as a paid criterion after an arbitrary earlier weight or when only a smaller image floor is known; it is not claimed superior to exact image information.

Use on a genuine parent remainder

On the growing family, earlier high products may first be killed with \(w_0\) of degree \(K\). If intermediate bottoms have exact admissible monomial values of degrees at most \(r\), and actual parents have old-affine terms plus mixtures of these and the killed high products, deleting the high products gives references in the corresponding degree-\(r\) space. This supplies (2) at the original specialized ceiling \(\delta\), which may exceed \(r\). It does not imply (3) or (4).

For \(r=\lceil\log n\rceil\), \(b_0=K\), \(B=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor\), \(\delta=O_A((\log n)^2)\), and \(1\le L\le\lfloor D/(4r)\rfloor\), condition (1) holds eventually. Thus any actual reference spaces meeting (3) yield complete parent removal, preserving the earlier bottom memberships through \(b_0+Lr+\delta+1\le D\). The construction uses one active parent row regardless of the original accuracy. Original bottom and parent companion ceilings, including \(2h+1\) and \(2h^2+3h\) for the common-accuracy presentation, are not reset.

This covers different dense reference tuples, including rank-deficient ones. The preceding full-initial-profile control remains compatible: a large initial error image does not prohibit selecting a shared surviving input for a group. Conversely, a small literal input span has large codimension in this ambient space and may fail the new criterion despite an easy alternative normalization. No relation between written source size and a favorable total codimension has been proved.
