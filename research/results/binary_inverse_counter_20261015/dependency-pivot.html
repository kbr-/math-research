There is an explicit Hilbert-like IPS certificate \(C_{m,n}(x,Y,Z)\) with one shared child \(C_{m-1,n-1}\), polynomial gate/wire size, depth \(O(n)\), and joint degree at most \(3^n(n+1)!\). Its direct normalization gives \(Q(x,b(x))=P(x)\), where \(P=\prod_{i,k}(1+F_{ik})\), with polynomial size and depth \(O(n)\). For \(n\ge1\) that \(Q\) belongs to \(J^{m-1}\), where \(J\) is the ideal of matrix-\(B\) entries with \(A,Z\) independent. Direct residual normalization. Let \(P_{ik}\) omit factor \((i,k)\) from \(P\). Canonical pivot depth gap

Corollary. The \(Y_{n+1,n+1}\) coefficient of this exact \(C_{m,n}\) computes the determinant function of the first \(n\) rows of \(A\) on Boolean inputs. Consequently at \(m=2n\) this particular certificate polynomial has no polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth binary arithmetic circuit.

Proof. Extract the coefficient by setting the named placeholder to one and every other \(Y,Z\) to zero; Hilbert-linearity makes this a depth-free operation. The special row is not the first row at any stage before the base case, so it enters \(H\) only through \(wY_{ik}\), and never enters \(H_0\). Its coefficient therefore satisfies \[ c_{m,n}(A,B)=w\,c_{m-1,n-1}(a,b),\qquad c_{m,0}=1. \] Inductively it depends only on \(A\). On Boolean inputs, a zero first row gives zero. Otherwise the selected nonzero pivot is one, and elementary column elimination shows that the top \(n\times n\) square is nonsingular exactly when the child's top \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) square is nonsingular. Induction proves equality of Boolean functions, not formal polynomial equality. The recorded explicit MOD3 reduction excludes polynomial-size fixed-depth binary circuits for this function, since such circuits become parity/AND circuits on Boolean inputs. Placeholder evaluation and fixing irrelevant entries preserve those bounds. \(\square\)

This is not a lower bound for every IPS certificate. The unit-slice syzygies already invalidate assuming a designated coefficient is protected universally. Nor does the obstruction automatically transfer to normalized \(Q\): normalization can move or cancel the determinant dependence, and no cheap reverse extraction is supplied.
