A pencil relation at prefix cost

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) in a Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J_D\). Let \(\deg w\le b\), and let \(H\) be an affine-form subspace, with affine forms \(a,b_1\). Put \[ L_0=H,\quad L_1=H+\langle a\rangle,\quad L_2=H+\langle b_1\rangle,\quad L_3=H+\langle a+b_1\rangle. \] For each \(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), suppose an actual polynomial \(P_j\), of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), has a supplied literal prefix \[ 1-P_j=\sum_\nu A_{j,\nu}\ell_{j,\nu},\qquad \ell_{j,\nu}\in L_j,\quad \deg A_{j,\nu}\le\delta-1, \] and \(wP_j\ell\in J_D\) for every \(\ell\in L_j\). All four products and these certificates are supplied; no span-lattice closure is assumed. Independence and properness of the pencil are unnecessary for the identity; they only make its four geometric labels distinct and consistent.

Lemma (paid binary pencils). If \(b+2\delta+1\le D\), then \[ w(P_1+P_2+P_3-P_0)\in J_D. \tag{1} \] For a parent tuple of degree at most \(\delta\), prefix degree \(p_0\), multiplier domain \(U\subseteq V_K\) and feature-word length \(h\ge1\), the core feature \(P_0\) may consequently be deleted from its actual feature-word image if the other three features are retained and \[ b+p_0+K+(h+2)\delta+1\le D. \tag{2} \] Here the feature-word image is the span of the tuples \( ([wQg_iP_Sf])_i\) in \((V_D/J_D)^{r_{\rm tuple}}\), for \(f\in U\) and \(|S|\le h\). The assertion is equality of these quotient images, not of raw polynomial spans or a bound on every contracted image.

Proof. For \(c=a,b_1,a+b_1\), write \(P_c\) for its extension product. The prefix of \(P_0\), absorbed by \(wP_cH=0\), gives \(wP_c(1-P_0)\in J_D\) through \(b+2\delta\). Each form in the prefix of \(P_c\) is \(h+\lambda c\), \(h\in H\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_2\). Multiplying that prefix by \(wP_0(1-c)\), the \(h\)-terms vanish using \(wP_0H=0\), and the \(c\)-terms vanish using the ordinary degree-two Boolean certificate for \(c(1-c)\). Their complete ceiling is \(b+2\delta+1\). Thus \[ wP_0(1-c)(1-P_c)\in J_D. \] Also \(wP_cc\in J_D\); after multiplying by \(P_0\), it and the first absorption identify \(wP_cP_0(1-c)\) with \(wP_c\). Therefore \[ w\bigl(P_c-P_0(1-c)\bigr)\in J_D \] at the same ceiling. Summing the three identities uses \((1-a)+(1-b_1)+(1-a-b_1)=1\) in characteristic two and proves (1).

For a word containing \(P_0\), multiply (1) by its other at most \(h-1\) feature factors, the fixed parent prefix, one tuple coordinate and one element of \(U\). The total certificate ceiling is at most the left side of (2). Replace the core occurrence by the three extension occurrences; the resulting words have length at most \(h\) and no core occurrence. Repeated factors reduce by Booleanity within their original product degree. Every old generator image is therefore in the new feature-word span; the reverse containment is immediate. This proves the equality without adding an unsupplied core or union member. \(\square\)

The lemma answers the additive test left after the earlier union-module attempt. It can remove an image summand when the pencil is actually supplied. It does not imply that generic source registries contain enough pencils for a useful global rank bound.

Choose shared prefixes instead of assuming a closed pencil family

The same core geometry suggests a complete source class. Let \(\Gamma\) be an old binary system on \(v\) variables, containing Booleanity, and let \(J_D=\mathcal C_D(\Gamma)\) be proper. There are two source levels, with genuine complete ENS blocks and private coefficient domains. Bottom inputs are old affine forms. Their spans \(V_c\) are assigned to \(q\ge1\) affine cores \(H_1,\ldots,H_q\) such that \[ H_{j(c)}\subseteq V_c,\qquad V_c=H_{j(c)}+\operatorname{span}(R_{c,1},\ldots,R_{c,r_c}),\quad r_c\le r_0. \tag{3} \] The residual representatives are actual affine forms in \(V_c\); their constant representations in the indexed bottom tuple are available. A core need not be an original source block. The source has at most \(M\ge1\) parents, with inputs \[ g_{a,i}=A_{a,i}(x)+\sum_c\alpha_{a,i;c}P_c, \qquad \deg A_{a,i}\le r_1,quad r_1\ge1. \tag{4} \] Products are shared coherently, no raw bottom coefficients occur in (4), and no parent reads another parent. All source accuracies are the same \(h\); the finite argument also allows larger accuracies while retaining their original costs.

For \(N_v(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,v)}\binom vj\), put \(\eta_r(K)=\tfrac12N_v(K-r)/N_v(K)\), with \(K\ge r\). Choose \(K,t_0,t_1\ge1\), set \[ s=t_0+r_0,\quad \delta=\max(s,r_1),\quad r=\max(1,r_0,r_1), \] and require \[ r\le K\le v,\quad q(1-\eta_1(K))^{t_0}<1,\quad M(1-\eta_r(K))^{t_1}<1,\quad h\ge\max(s,t_1), \tag{5} \] \[ 2K+qt_0+(t_1+1)\delta\le D,\qquad 2K+qt_0+B\le D. \tag{6} \]

Theorem (core star exclusion). Under (3)–(6), the complete two-level source has no ordinary-PC or NS refutation through original degree \(B\). One coherent constant coefficient assignment and a surviving old weight suffice. The proof charges the number of shared cores, not the number of bottom spaces or parent mixtures.

Proof: approximate the core family once. Apply surviving branch removal with the single initial branch of weight one, actual and reference degree one, to affine bases of the \(q\) cores, with cutoff \(K\) and \(t_0\) rows. The first inequality in (5) supplies one nonzero \(w\notin J_D\), \(\deg w\le K\), and old binary products \(Z_j\) of degree at most \(t_0\) such that \(wZ_jH_j=0\) in \(J_D\). Its buffer \(K+t_0+1\le D\) follows from (6). Zero cores have product one; unit cores admit their usual constant cleanup. These auxiliary core tuples are used only to choose old polynomials, not added as axioms to \(\Gamma\).

In every bottom \(c\), pull the chosen core rows back by constants to its actual input positions. Follow them with the \(r_c\) rows selecting its residual representatives, and zero-pad. This is legal by (3) and (5), and gives literally \[ P_c=Z_{j(c)}\chi_c,\qquad \chi_c=\prod_{u=1}^{r_c}(1-R_{c,u}),\qquad \deg P_c\le s. \tag{7} \] Every weighted bottom companion belongs to \(J_D\) through \(K+s+1\): its core component uses \(wZ_jH_j=0\), and each residual component uses \(R_{c,u}(1-R_{c,u})\) and Booleanity. Empty residual lists cause no exception. All copies use the same chosen core rows and bottom product.

Proof: one branch handles arbitrary parent mixtures. For \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^q\), define \[ w_\epsilon=w\prod_{j=1}^q Z_j^{\epsilon_j}(1-Z_j)^{1-\epsilon_j}. \] Their sum is literally \(w\), and their degrees are at most \(K+qt_0\). Booleanity gives \(w_\epsilon(Z_j-\epsilon_j)\in J_D\) through \(K+(q+1)t_0\). Multiplying by \(\chi_c\) in (7) yields weighted comparisons with \(\epsilon_{j(c)}\chi_c\) through \(K+qt_0+s\). Consequently the actual parent inputs agree on each weighted branch with \[ A_{a,i}+\sum_c\alpha_{a,i;c}\epsilon_{j(c)}\chi_c, \] of degree at most \(r\). The actual inputs have degree at most \(\delta\). No independent model for the core values is required, and a branch image need not retain a fixed fraction of the original rank.

Apply surviving branch removal with branch-degree ceiling \(K+qt_0\), another multiplier cutoff \(K\), and \(t_1\) rows. Conditions (5) and the first inequality in (6) are exactly its rate and comparison requirements. It chooses one branch, one coherent parent assignment and a polynomial \(f\) of degree at most \(K\), with \(W=w_\epsilon f\notin J_D\), annihilating every parent companion. Its degree is at most \(2K+qt_0\). The earlier bottom certificates survive multiplication by the branch factors and \(f\) through \(2K+qt_0+s+1\), which is at most the first bound in (6), since \(t_1\ge1\), \(\delta\ge s\ge1\). Coefficient-field images are zero at the binary constant assignments.

Proof: replay the original source. A degree-\(B\) source refutation under this single constant coefficient assignment never increases degree. Weighted old axiom images and every subsequent multiplication fit \(\deg W+B\le D\) by (6); all weighted extension images were just certified. Induction would put its final polynomial \(W\) in \(J_D\), contradiction. Ordinary degree-\(B\) NS refutations are included by their standard PC replay. Original accuracies, input degrees and cofactor allowances are not reset to their smaller images. \(\square\)

A polynomial-inventory range

Fix \(0<\gamma<1\), constant \(r_0\ge0,r_1\ge1\), an inventory exponent \(A\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1/6\). Suppose the old family on \(v\) variables has \(\mathcal C_{\lfloor\gamma v\rfloor}\) proper, at most \(v^A\) bottoms and parents, and a cover (3) by \(q=o(v/\log v)\) cores. At accuracy \(h=\lfloor v^\varepsilon\rfloor\), the source has no degree-\(\lfloor v^{1/3}\rfloor\) refutation for sufficiently large \(v\).

Indeed choose \(K=\lfloor\gamma v/4\rfloor\). For fixed \(r\), \(\eta_r(K)\) is bounded below by a positive constant, by the same binomial comparison as the chain theorem. Thus \(t_0,t_1=O_{A,\gamma,r_0,r_1}(\log v)\) satisfy (5), and \(qt_0=o(v)\), \((t_1+1)\delta=O((\log v)^2)\). Both inequalities (6) hold. The original bottom and parent companion ceilings are respectively \(O(h)\) and \(O(h^2)\), so they are below \(B=v^{1/3}+O(1)\) eventually; the statement is not made vacuous by ignoring every source axiom. The old family and fixed accuracy exponent are independent of \(A\); the row-count constants and eventual threshold may depend on it.
