Sparse mask survival

Lemma. Let \(A=\mathbb F_3^r\), \(r\ge1\), \(N=3^r\), and \(1\le L\le N\). Use \(\mathbb F_4\)-valued functions and its primitive cube root \(\omega\) to define Fourier characters. Independently select \(\eta(a)\in\{0,1\}\subseteq\mathbb F_4\) with probability one half at each \(a\in A\). Then \[ \Pr\bigl(\exists f\ne0:\ |\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|\le L, \ \eta f=0\bigr) \le \min\{1,\ L(3N)^L2^{-\lceil N/L\rceil}\}. \] Thus, outside this exceptional event, multiplication by \(\eta\) is injective on every linear code whose Fourier support is contained in some fixed set of at most \(L\) frequencies, simultaneously. This does not assert injectivity between arbitrary members of the nonlinear union of all sparse-support classes: their difference can have twice the support. For \(L=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), the exceptional bound tends to zero.

Proof: support from translation rank. For nonzero \(f\), convolution by \(f\) on the group algebra \(\mathbb F_4[A]\) has rank \(u=|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|\). The Fourier matrix is invertible because \(3^r\) is nonzero in characteristic two and every character is \(\mathbb F_4\)-valued; diagonalizing convolution proves this rank identity. Its columns are translates of \(f\). Select \(u\) translates forming a column-space basis. Their supports cover all \(N\) positions: a row zero in every basis column would be zero in every translated column, impossible since translates of a nonzero function cover the group. Each basis column has \(|\operatorname{supp}f|\) entries in its support. Hence \[ N\le u|\operatorname{supp}f|, \qquad |\operatorname{supp}f|\ge\lceil N/L\rceil \quad(u\le L). \]

Proof: uniform erasure bound. A fixed nonzero \(f\) is killed precisely when \(\eta\) is zero at all positions of its support, of probability \(2^{-|\operatorname{supp}f|}\). There are at most \[ \sum_{j=1}^{L}\binom Nj3^j\le L(3N)^L \] nonzero functions with at most \(L\) nonzero Fourier coefficients: choose the support and one of the three nonzero field coefficients at each frequency. Union-bound their killing probabilities to obtain the lemma. For \(L=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), the logarithm of its untruncated bound is \[ \log L+L\log(3N)-\lceil N/L\rceil\log2 =-\Omega(N^{2/3}), \] since \(N^{1/3}\log N=o(N^{2/3})\). A linear code on a fixed frequency set has every nonzero member in this tested class, proving the stated injectivity. \(\square\)
