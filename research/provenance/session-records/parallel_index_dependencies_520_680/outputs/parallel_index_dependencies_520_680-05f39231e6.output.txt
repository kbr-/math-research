   \[
       G_{s,i}x_s
           =-(x_s^2-x_s)(1-y_{s,i}),
       \tag{CORE-old-bit-companion}
  \] 
   through degree three. Booleanity of \(v_s\) is the old bit equation through two. Thus all prefix, companion, value-degree, and Booleanity requirements of the  costed source interface  hold within the original ceilings.

### 545 thm:shared-core-projected-source-criterion
shared-core-projected-source-criterion  3. A multilevel criterion after the correct affine projection 
   Use the exact binary source stage and original degree conventions of the  adapted-rank compiler , including every required subgroup and coherent copy. Fix source depth \(d\), take \(h=3\ell\), and choose in advance a polynomial upper bound \(M\ge1\) for the final affine inventory. Put 
   \[
       k=\left\lceil\sqrt{(n+1)\log(4M)}\right\rceil,\qquad
       r_*=3\ell(k+1)+1.
  \]

   For every other nonbottom source block, replace its formal inputs by 
   \[
    L_{a,i}(b)+\sum_{c&lt;a}\alpha_{a,i;c}Z_c
       \ \longmapsto\
    \underbrace{L_{a,i}(b)+
        \sum_{s\in S,\ s&lt;a}\alpha_{a,i;s}x_s}_{\text{new old-affine part}}
       +\sum_{\substack{c&lt;a\\c\notin H\cup S}}
                \alpha_{a,i;c}Z_c .
       \tag{CORE-source-projection}
  \] 
   The columns in \(H\) disappear with value zero; the columns in \(S\) disappear only after their old-bit contribution is added to the affine part. Canonical pair-bottom columns remain in the final sum: being old-only does not make their quadratic values affine. 
    Working source criterion. A hypothetical polynomial-size proof of fixed source depth satisfying this criterion is excluded for sufficiently large \(n\).

All use one common retained system and respect strict source order. For every unselected block, apply the old-affine intersection and degree-ordered original-input basis construction to the projected formal tuple. Interpret its remaining selector names only after this basis choice, so their formal cancellation gives genuinely old-affine core inputs. 
   The old-affine offset in (CORE-source-projection) has degree at most one. Every interpreted earlier value stays within its original weight. The adapted construction therefore retains all original individual \(d_i\)'s and yields cost at most \(2h+r_a^{\rm rem}\delta_a\). If a remaining selector direction occurs, the  original  \(\delta_a\) is at least \(h\), even if that direction's current value has smaller collected degree. Hence \(r_a^{\rm rem}\le h-1\) gives the original \(w_a=h(\delta_a+1)\) bound. Affine, zero, and unit cases have their established profiles.    Canonicalized pair bottoms require no retained block; every other original bottom is retained at most once. Each selected star or unselected later block contributes at most one fresh affine core. Thus the final inventory is at most one affine block per source registry block. The chosen \(M\) remains valid, and the designated core ranks are old-affine ranks in that final family. 
   The  joint-zero theorem  excludes a degree-\(D\) refutation of this endpoint whenever its usual multiplier and old-board bounds fit. The existing source reconstruction preserves its supplied modular NS degree, or the logical-DAG compiler gives 
   \[
       D\le\max(M_{\rm fr},10)(d+1)h^d.
  \] 
   For fixed \(d\), polynomial \(M\), and \(h=3\ell\), this is polylogarithmic, while \(k(D+1)=o(n)\) and \(4(k-1)&lt;n\). The forbidden affine endpoint follows, completing the criterion. 
   The criterion must hold for the full retained registry, including mixed auxiliary unions. It is not automatically satisfied by the displayed logical formulas alone. This is a source-profile theorem; it does not assert a raw degree-preserving map of arbitrary later coefficient proofs. General surviving selector dependence and the ordinary unary conversion gap remain open.

### 547 def:mixed-star-union-original-setup
mixed-star-union-original-setup  1. Keep the actual flattened tuple and its original degree 
   Let selected shared-bit subgroups \(s\) cover the original union input index set \(I\). Their index sets \(I_s\) may overlap. After the consistent pair-bottom specialization, each occurrence in subgroup \(s\) has 
   \[
       G_i=(1-x_s)L_{s,i},\qquad L_{s,i}=1-y_{s,i},
       \qquad i\in I_s.
  \] 
   A shared input must have the same polynomial in every occurrence. For example \((1-x_0)(1-x_1)\) can occur in stars with either probe \(x_0\) or probe \(x_1\). The source convention  flattens an OR cluster into its maximal non-OR children . Thus the union still reads the original bottom products, with original bounds 
   \[
       \mu_i=2h,\qquad
       w_U=h(2h+1).
       \tag{UNION-original-weight}
  \] 
   The union and its proper OR subgroups can be at the same ENS level. We do not insert another level or replace the input tuple by the subgroup products to obtain a larger weight. 
   For each subgroup retain its affine core \(A_s\) on the \(L_{s,i}\)'s, with prefixes \(U_{s,i}\), and assume the eligible equation \(A_s=0\) is present. Put \(\chi_s=1-x_s\). The  old-bit profile  supplies the exact relation 
   \[
       \chi_s-\sum_{i\in I_s}U_{s,i}G_i
           =\chi_s A_s,\qquad
       \deg U_{s,i}\le2h-1.
       \tag{UNION-subgroup-prefix}
  \] 
   The hypotheses concern the actual tuple, its cover, and the supplied core equations. A general collection of low-rank bottom blocks need not admit such a cover. 


### 548 thm:mixed-star-union-affine-core-profile
mixed-star-union-affine-core-profile  2. Compress the old truth inputs into one affine core 
   Introduce one fresh complete accuracy-\(h\) affine block on the old bits \((\chi_s)_s\), with product \(B\) and prefixes \(V_s\), so \(1-B=\sum_sV_s\chi_s\) and every \(\chi_sB\) is a companion axiom. Give the original union value \(B\). For its original input position \(i\), put 
   \[
       W_i=\sum_{s:i\in I_s}V_sU_{s,i}.
       \tag{UNION-original-prefix}
  \] 
   Every overlapping occurrence contributes to this sum. Substitution of (UNION-subgroup-prefix) gives the exact residual 
   \[
       1-B-\sum_{i\in I}W_iG_i
          =\sum_sV_s\chi_s A_s
                   \in\mathcal I_{4h}(\Gamma).
       \tag{UNION-residual}
  \] 
   Each displayed generator multiple costs at most \((2h-1)+1+2h=4h\). The original input-weighted prefix bound is 
   \[
       \deg W_i+\mu_i
            \le(2h-1)+(2h-1)+2h=6h-2.
       \tag{UNION-prefix-cost}
  \] 
   For a companion, choose any subgroup \(s\) containing \(i\). Its factorization gives the particularly direct witness 
   \[
       G_iB=L_{s,i}(\chi_sB)
                    \in\mathcal I_{2h+2}(\Gamma).
       \tag{UNION-companion}
  \] 
   There is no need to sum over all groups for this step. Booleanity of the new value has the ordinary identity 
   \[
       B^2-B=-\sum_sV_s(\chi_sB)
                    \in\mathcal I_{4h}(\Gamma).
       \tag{UNION-Booleanity}
  \] 
    Working profile theorem.  These data form a complete profile of cost \(s_U=6h-2\), with the original \(\mu_i=2h\), for every \(h\ge2\). It fits the original union weight because 
   \[
       h(2h+1)-(6h-2)=(2h-1)(h-2)\ge0.
       \tag{UNION-budget}
  \] 
   The value degree is at most \(2h\), the residual ceiling \(4h\) is at most \(s_U\), and the companion and Booleanity ceilings fit \(s_U+\mu_i\) and \(2s_U\). All equations are ordinary NS identities and work over every prime. No bound on the number of subgroups or grouped bottom literals appears in these degree estimates. Witness size and input inventory are still retained; no small expanded-monomial-size claim is made. 
   All affine blocks use only old inputs and have disjoint own coefficients. The union value does not recursively depend on same-level subgroup values. Its NS witnesses use the supplied subgroup coefficients in one fixed retained system, which is permitted by the source interface.
