Question. The goal review of 16 October moved cycles to effective-annihilator-degree. Six research entries later, what does the line prove, what does the goal need from it, and which test decides whether it can give that?

General statement. No claim is registered: the review assesses lem:transversal-killer-basis, lem:cylinder-killer-degree, lem:top-degree-killer-transfer, lem:graded-domain-pairing, lem:leading-form-killer-bound, lem:block-additive-degree and the joint-savings results of the period, and sets the next test.

The line's general claim. In domain models, where the closure is the degree-\(D\) part of the vanishing ideal of a finite set, the first surviving common killer degree of errors is exact: a transversal number on cubes and one-hot product domains (lem:transversal-killer-basis, lem:cylinder-killer-degree), additive over disjoint blocks (lem:block-additive-degree). At the top degree of the product pigeonhole system, whose degree-\(n\) closure is proper and unsatisfiable, monomial errors on distinct holes reduce to the domain model (lem:top-degree-killer-transfer), so there the killer degree is the transversal number, \(\tau(K_4)=3\) at \(m=5\), \(n=4\). Graded duality pairs only cubes (lem:graded-domain-pairing) and gives proper closures a lower bound blind to axioms' constant terms (lem:leading-form-killer-bound). Idempotent denominators pay indicator degrees, unless they protect a least killer.

What the goal needs. effective-annihilator-degree asks for an explicit bound on the first filtered degree of an annihilator preserving a protected class in a faithful model of the closures the repair uses, Pang's proper \(C_D\) at his cutoff \(\Delta+K+d\le D\), or a strictly cheaper certificate on an unbounded family, with the consequence for repair. The line's exact results hold in domain models and at the top degree of the product pigeonhole system, where the inhomogeneous axioms enter the window only as themselves; no result covers a window containing multiples of those axioms, which is where Pang's closures live.

Falsification attempt. The line's implicit hope is that killers modulo a proper closure behave as in a domain model. Tested against the record: check:functional-factor-kernels found the functional pigeonhole system's common kernels at \(K=1\) equal to the domain model's plus the relations, consistent with the hope; ex:support-saving-not-inherited shows ranks in a proper quotient differ from the domain model's (a saving of \(54\) against \(11\)), refuting the transfer of ranks; check:leading-form-functional-pigeons shows a proper closure removing every survivor when the errors span a unit through an inhomogeneous axiom, and finds least surviving killer degree \(2\) for two sets without linear survivors. What is untested is the kernel space at \(K=2\), where multiples of the pigeon axioms enter.

Assessment. The line is exact in domain models, and at the top degree of the product pigeonhole system it is exact modulo a proper closure; elsewhere it bounds no killer degree modulo a proper closure whose window contains multiples of its inhomogeneous axioms. The six research entries of the period, #entry-2026-10-16-cylinder-killers, #entry-2026-10-16-gorenstein-domains, #entry-2026-10-16-initial-pseudoideal-bound, #entry-2026-10-16-principal-joint-kernels, #entry-2026-10-16-marginal-refinement and #entry-2026-10-16-block-killer-degree, each declared no bearing. The Alon–Füredi lead of the last review, left Not run, has its one-point case, the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz degree \(\sum_i(|S_i|-1)\) of a grid function supported at one point, as a case of lem:block-additive-degree (a) with one-variable blocks; the theorem itself, on the least number of nonzeros of a polynomial of given degree on a grid, covers arbitrary supports and is not given by (a).

Obstacle

No recorded statement relates killers modulo a proper closure to vanishing on a set when the window contains multiples of the inhomogeneous axioms: lem:top-degree-killer-transfer works at the top degree of the product pigeonhole system, where those axioms enter only as themselves, using the independence of the unplaced-pigeon indicators, and check:functional-factor-kernels covers the functional system at \(K=1\). At \(K=2\) the least surviving degree is known for two sets, but not the kernel space or which killers survive.

Architecture review

Necessary target. For every proper Pang closure \(C_D\) in his setting (any prime \(p\), families of degree at most \(d\), \(t\) factors, \(\Delta+K+d\le D\)), a degree \(k\) and a polynomial \(f\) of degree at most \(k\) killing every error modulo \(C_D\) and preserving the protected class, with \(k\) bounded explicitly below Pang's \(K\) on an unbounded family, or a certificate as cheap. Two readings of the protected class: the base class \(1\), \(f\notin C_D\); and the weight class the repair needs, \(wf\notin J_D\). Downstream. Through thm:certified-repair-composition the target would supply the killing step of the repair; substituting such an \(f\) for the multiplier that thm:finite-grid-repair constructs needs a lemma not in the record (any \(f\) killing the weighted adaptive errors with \(wf\notin J_D\) can take its place), and lowering \(K\) also changes Pang's \(\eta\), his rank budget and his simulation cutoff (cor:pang-observation-calibration). So the target gives the killing step at the goal's parameters; the repair at a lower cutoff is not established until that lemma and the recomputed budget are proved.

Extra requirements. The current approach demands exact least degrees and works where membership is literal vanishing; the target needs only an upper bound below \(K\) and only derivability modulo \(C_D\). The exactness can be weakened: a killer of degree below \(K\) suffices. The domain-model setting cannot stand in for the target: it is what Pang's closures are not, so the demand that is wrong for the goal is the setting, not the precision.

Independent alternative. The review's medium reviewer, given only the goal statement and fourteen registered results with the route withheld (review record), proposed a sandwich: killing is proved in the domain sub-closure \(B_D\subseteq C_D\) generated by the Boolean and monomial axioms, where the line's exact degrees hold, since \(fE\in B_D\) implies \(fE\in C_D\); survival is proved through a normal-form operator of the proper closure, such as Razborov's for the pigeonhole principle; the open step is a locality product rule for that operator. It explains why no ring-level theorem can be expected: the control \(k[t]/(t^{m+1})\) of prop:multiplier-transfer-limits has a one-point spectrum and unbounded annihilator degree. Compared with the current route, the alternative keeps the line's domain-model results as the killing half and supplies the missing survival half by an operator. The record has a candidate: prop:pigeonhole-quotient-count (near-half notebook), conditional on Razborov's Claims 3.5 and 3.11, counts the pigeonhole quotient with linear pigeon sums by its irreducible terms below degree \((n+1)/2\).

Rank normalization. The line's degrees are measured in domain models, and the goal's in the proper quotient; ex:support-saving-not-inherited shows the two give different ranks for the same errors. The target is a degree, so relative contraction does not replace it; the test below therefore measures every quantity modulo \(C\cap V_2\), the proper quotient's relations, not in the domain model.

Decision. Adopt the sandwich, and give the next research cycle to the test below, which measures both halves and a joint effect at once; then return cycles to joint-error-savings, restoring the order the user stated for the goal's objectives, which the goal review had departed from by its own rule; the test's joint quantity (ii) bears on that line too.
