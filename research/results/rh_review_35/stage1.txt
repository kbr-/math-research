Goal. Prove the Riemann hypothesis: every zero of zeta in 0 < Re s < 1 has real part 1/2. The necessary
endpoint is the exclusion of one hypothetical off-line zero at an arbitrary height.

Available results of the period (six entries), each with its status.

1. Imported, unaudited: a preprint claiming zeta(s) != 0 for Re s > 11/12 (companion: 7/8), through a power
   saving for sums of mu(n) nu(n) over the Eisenstein integers. Read in this period, with no error found:
   (a) the saving is carried by a "dual estimate", a mean square over rows k and an auxiliary modulus f of
   sums of cubic Gauss coefficients twisted by sextic symbols chi_n(k) chi_n(f)^4; (b) it is proved by
   induction on the scale of the rows from a cube inversion, a transfer estimate (two Poisson summations
   returning the same family at smaller scales) and a completed mean square; (c) the completed mean square
   holds because a sextic twist of exponent one of cubic theta coefficients becomes a quadratic twist on the
   dual side, where an optimal quadratic large sieve (Goldmakher-Louvel) is cited; the theta function's
   polar term is removed by a derivative that also produces an angular factor. Unread: the reduction from
   the Moebius mean square to the dual estimate by Poisson summation, the Gauss-sum identity that absorbs
   the Moebius function (gamma_1 gamma_2 = mu alpha G), and the deduction of the half-plane from the bound
   for one sum. The mean square is false for coefficients identically one (sixth-power rows).
2. Published (Turan 1984, Theorem 36.1), read: under the Lindelof hypothesis, a bound N log^10 N / tau^b
   for sums of Lambda(n) n^(-i tau) on lengths tau^a <= N <= tau^(a(1+2 delta)) gives zeta != 0 on
   sigma >= 1 - (b/a)(1 - 2 delta) at height tau. The hypothesis is used only to bound by delta^3 log tau the
   zeros in a fixed box to the right of the line. The notebook's own conditional exclusions rediscover the
   method in a weaker form.
3. Published (same book, Section 37), statement read: a count of off-line zeros in unit windows at every
   distance from the line is equivalent to the Lindelof hypothesis; a "weak" hypothesis (fewer than
   A delta log tau zeros in a disk of radius delta at the edge of a rectangle free of zeros to its right) is
   stated to hold unconditionally with A = 0.71 and implies a density bound when A < 0.01.
4. Proved earlier in the notebook: from a 2m-th moment over D^beta rows a single sum is recovered with
   exponent 1/2 + beta(1 - 1/k)/(2m), at best 1 - 1/(2k) for a symbol of order k.

STAGE 1 REPLY (medium reviewer, fresh context; [D] derived from the brief, [R] recalled and unread).

(i) Different architecture: none found that reaches 1/2. [D] Items 2 and 3 are transfers, not inputs: a
prime-sum saving with b/a near 1/2 at every height is essentially the goal through the explicit formula.
[D] The one arithmetic input (item 1) sits at item 4's ceiling: 1 - 1/(2k) is 11/12 at k = 6 and 7/8 at k = 4;
lowering it needs smaller k, and the limit is structural, at best 3/4 from a quadratic symbol. [D] The only
non-circular combination: if item 1 held, zeta would have no zeros in Re s > 11/12 and item 2's count would
be free there, but item 2 would then be unconditional only where nothing is left to exclude. [D] Item 3's
0.71 against the needed 0.01, and delta^3 log tau against delta log tau, are gaps with no mechanism supplied.

(ii) Step expected to fail: the deduction of the half-plane from the bound for one sum, with what nu is
there. [D] A power saving for sum mu(n) nu(n) continues 1/zeta_K only if nu is trivial or of finite order on
the full sum; check that the instance needed for zeta is one the mean square covers. [R] Precedent:
Heath-Brown and Patterson give a power saving for cubic Gauss sums over primes with no consequence for zeta
zeros, Gauss-sum weights being only twisted-multiplicative. [R] Second suspect: the identity absorbing
Moebius; classically g(n)^3 = mu(n) N(n) n. [D] A bilinear splitting then needs a factorisation of n, paid
for by symbols chi_a(b); check that no combinatorial identity reintroduces mu on a short variable.
Cheapest test [D]: by reading, trace nu from the theorem's final sum back to the dual estimate; check
whether the instance needed for zeta lands on the excluded rows, and whether the exponent attains item 4's
ceiling at finite parameters. Numerics on sums of mu cannot refute.

(iii) [R] No published theorem contradicts a zero-free half-plane. [R] The nearest hazard is Dunn and
Radziwill: Heath-Brown's cubic large sieve is sharp; any step implying a better cubic or sextic large sieve
for general coefficients would be false. [D] The route through a quadratic large sieve avoids this only if
the cubic part is wholly absorbed by the theta coefficients; chi_n(f)^4 is a cubic symbol, so check that it
does not survive as a free cubic twist. [R] Goldmakher-Louvel's hypotheses were not checked.
