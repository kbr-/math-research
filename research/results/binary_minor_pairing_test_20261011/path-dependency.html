Tied root paths

Observation. In characteristic three take \(a_0=0\), and nonzero root series \(a_i(t)\) whose finite valuations have a unique strictly least value \(w_*\), with initial coefficient \(\alpha_*\). In the Motzkin pair network, assign edges their valuations before summing paths. For \(N\ge3\), the contribution of all least-weight determinant path families cancels. In particular no single-family unique-minimum proof works for this ordering. Higher weight groups and the full determinant are not asserted to vanish.

Proof. Let \(i_*\) index the unique least-valuation root, treating \(w_0=\infty\). For every pair \(e=(i_*,j)\), the initial terms of \(s_e\) and \(\delta_e\) are \(\alpha_*t^{w_*}\) and \(\alpha_*^2t^{2w_*}\). An excursion of length \(l\), with \(h\) horizontal steps and \(d\) down steps, satisfies \(h+2d=l\). In each of these pair copies its initial weight is therefore \(lw_*\) and its initial coefficient \(\alpha_*^l\), independent of its shape. This statement concerns individual paths, so it does not incorrectly discard the cancellation already visible in \(q_3=s^3+3s\delta\).

The nonzero root of smallest valuation is the smaller endpoint of \(N-1\) pairs. Fix two of those pair sources. In any determinant family exchange their assigned output lengths and their excursion shapes, transferring each shape to the other private copy. All graph paths remain valid, their outputs remain distinct, the total weight and product of initial coefficients are unchanged, and the output permutation changes sign. The exchange is a fixed-point-free involution because the two assigned outputs differ. It preserves the least-weight set and pairs its contributions with opposite signs. All other pair paths stay unchanged, so ties among the remaining root valuations do not affect the argument. If there is no nonzero path family, the asserted contribution is already zero. This proves the assertion for every unique-minimum root ordering, including all strictly separated root valuations. \(\square\)

This is the pair-evaluation alternation behind the old Schur frame, exposed directly in the new path representation; no novelty of the cancellation principle is claimed. The earlier joint-leading formula separately warns that a uniquely maximal block choice can have a vanishing internal scalar. The sparse-cluster theorem also prevents replacing the tied group by its simplest sparse limit. These are different obstructions, all retained.
