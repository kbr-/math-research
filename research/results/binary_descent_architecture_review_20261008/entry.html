Binary map weight locus

Use the exact setup of complete weighted-map descent: a binary degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J_2\) containing old Booleanity, old variables \(x\), fresh variables \(y\), a binary source \(\mathcal F\) of original joint degree at most \(B\ge2\) containing every fresh field equation, and \(T\ge1\), \(b+TB\le D\). Put \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), \(J_k=k\otimes J_2\), and assume \(H_b=\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le b}/(J_2\cap\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le b})\ne0\).

Corollary (binary map weight locus). For each binary map \(\Psi\) fixing old variables and sending every fresh variable to an old polynomial of degree at most \(T\), define \[ K_\Psi=\{[W]\in k\otimes H_b:W\Psi(F)\in J_k\text{ for every }F\in\mathcal F\}. \] The nonzero weights admitting a complete weighted map of degree at most \(T\) over \(k\) are exactly \((\bigcup_\Psi K_\Psi)\setminus\{0\}\). Each \(K_\Psi\) is a binary-defined linear subspace, and there are finitely many of them. The corresponding locus in projective weight space is dense, or contains a nonempty open set, if and only if one binary map satisfies \(\Psi(F)\in J_2\) without any weight for every \(F\).

Proof. Changing a weight representative by an element of \(J_2\) of degree at most \(b\) changes every product by an element of \(J_k\), by bounded absorption within \(b+TB\le D\). Thus the kernels are well-defined. Their equations are linear in the weight coordinates over \(\mathbb F_2\). There are finitely many degree-bounded binary polynomial maps in finitely many variables.

Given an algebraic-closure solution, put its finitely many coefficients in a finite extension. The proof of weighted-map descent first replaces all coefficient images by binary images while keeping the same original weight: weighted Frobenius differences and a unital trace projection give \(W(\Phi(R)-\Psi(R))\in J_k\) for every source polynomial of degree at most \(B\). Therefore \([W]\in K_\Psi\). Conversely membership in a nonzero \(K_\Psi\) already supplies a complete map, so the union is exact.

A finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot be dense over the infinite field \(k\): choose a nonzero linear form annihilating each proper subspace; their product is a nonzero polynomial vanishing on the union. Thus density is equivalent to \(K_\Psi=k\otimes H_b\) for some \(\Psi\). Since \(H_b\ne0\), the class of one is nonzero: if \(1\in J_2\), bounded absorption puts every degree-at-most-\(D\) polynomial in \(J_2\). Evaluating the full-kernel condition at one gives the unweighted memberships, which descend to binary because all data are binary. Conversely those unweighted memberships absorb every degree-\(b\) weight in the paid window. This also proves the projective assertion, including dimension-zero projective space. \(\square\)

This is an exact structural consequence of the earlier theorem, not a new nonemptiness theorem. Searching over the algebraic closure does not produce a dense continuously varying family of admissible weights unless an unweighted binary map already exists. A nonempty proper kernel is enough for the intended weighted endpoint; insisting on generic weights would add a stronger requirement.

Coefficient chart boundary

Use the charged one-hot quotient and its Fourier description, over \(\mathbb F_4\) or any algebraic extension, with \(\max(4,2\Delta)\le D<\varepsilon n/8\) and nonzero total charge. Suppose the graph has \(Mr\) edge-disjoint cycles, with one chosen edge-label indicator \(x_i\) per cycle. Partition these indicators into \(M\ge2\) disjoint groups \(S_a\) of size \(r=T+2\), where \(T\ge3\). On each group use one complete accuracy-one block with inputs \((x_i)_{i\in S_a}\), coefficient images \(a_i(x)\) of degree at most \(T\), and \(P_a=1-\sum_{i\in S_a}a_ix_i\). The original companion degree is three; include all fresh fields and all old generators, taking \(B\ge\max(3,\Delta)\).

Proposition (coefficient chart boundary). If \(b+TB\le D\) and a degree-at-most-\(b\) weight survives while \(Wx_iP_a\in J_D\) for every group/input, then \(b\ge M\). In particular this applies when all weighted fresh fields also hold. When \(M+TB\le D\), the minimum is attained by a binary weight of degree \(M\) and constant binary coefficient rows. Nevertheless the homogenized coefficient equations have a surviving degree-one solution at their boundary. If instead coefficient degree \(T+1\) is allowed and \((T+1)B\le D\), there is an unweighted complete map.

Proof of the lower bound. Put \(Z_a=\prod_{i\in S_a}(1-x_i)\). The identity \(1-Z_a=\sum_i x_i\prod_{j<i}(1-x_j)\) and the weighted companions put \(WP_a(1-Z_a)\) in \(J_D\). Writing \(q_a=\sum_i a_ix_i\), Booleanity gives \(Wq_aZ_a\in J_D\). Every product and Boolean reduction fits \[ b+(T+1)+r=b+2T+3\le b+3T\le b+TB\le D. \] Adding gives \(W(Z_a-P_a)\in J_D\), where \(\deg P_a\le T+1=r-1\).

Here the maximal-character argument applies also to these negative conjunctions; spell out the change. On the chosen cycle, \(1-x_i\) is the sum of its two nonzero Fourier character terms, each with nonzero coefficient. The group of disjoint-cycle circulations preserves \(J_D\) and degree. Choose a maximum-support nonzero eigencomponent \(W_\alpha\) of \([W]\), whose support \(S\) has size \(s\le b\). If \(S\cap S_a=\varnothing\), examine character \(\alpha+\beta\), where \(\beta\) is one on \(S_a\). Any contributing nonzero component of \(W\) must equal \(\alpha\) outside \(S_a\); maximality forces it to be zero on \(S_a\). Thus the contribution is uniquely \(W_\alpha\) times the all-positive Fourier character of \(Z_a\). That character multiplication is injective on cut-code classes in the window \(b+r\le D\), so it is nonzero. In contrast every component of \(WP_a\) has support at most \(s+r-1\). This contradicts the relation above. Hence \(S\) hits all \(M\) disjoint groups and \(b\ge s\ge M\). No global point model of the inconsistent base or invariant-weight assumption is used.

Affine attainment and repair. Select one indicator \(x_{i_a}\) in each group, put \(W=\prod_a x_{i_a}\), and choose the constant row selecting that input. Then \(P_a=1-x_{i_a}\); every weighted companion has a Boolean zero factor, through degree \(M+2\), and fresh fields vanish. The selected-edge characters are distinct modulo cuts, so \(W\) survives. This proves attainment in the stated window. Alternatively assign \(a_i=\prod_{j<i}(1-x_j)\) within each group. Their maximum degree is \(r-1=T+1\), their sum \(\sum_i a_ix_i=1-Z_a\), and \(P_a=Z_a\). Every companion is Boolean zero and every coefficient image is Boolean, giving an unweighted map within the larger original-charge window.

Projective boundary. Homogenize coefficient parameters by \(t\), so \(P_a^{\rm hom}=t-\sum_i a_ix_i\) and the fresh field equations become \(a_i^2-ta_i\). Set \(t=0\), \(W=x_{i_1}\), \(a_{i_1}=1-x_{i_1}\) in the first group, and all other coefficient images to zero. Every product \(P_a^{\rm hom}\) is Boolean zero; the only nontrivial weighted field is \(x_{i_1}(1-x_{i_1})^2\), also Boolean zero. The parameter vector is nonzero and the degree-one weight survives. Thus for \(b=1<M\) the affine chart is empty but this projective completion has a boundary point. These identities cost at most three, within the stipulated window. \(\square\)

The statement is nonvacuous on the recorded triangle-replacement cubic expander family, which has linearly many disjoint cycles and a linear proper window. For any fixed \(M,T\) the conditions hold eventually. At the intended larger weight allowance, once \(b\ge M\) and the full window fits, the explicit affine map defeats this particular obstruction. It neither establishes arbitrary nonlinear-source coverage nor refutes a proof-local assertion: these are complete source-shaped blocks, not a demonstrated fragment forced by every short Frege proof.

  • Finite-field divisibility: Ax–Katz. The classical bound, stated precisely in the Adolphson–Sperber introduction, makes the number of common zeros divisible by \(q^\nu\), where \(\nu=\max(0,\lceil(n-\sum d_i)/d_{\max}\rceil)\). Let there be \(P\) parameter coordinates, \(H\) surviving weight coordinates, and \(R\) nonzero equations linear homogeneous in weights, of total degrees \(d_i\ge1\). The zero-weight fiber already has \(q^P\) points. Answers. Divisibility stronger than \(q^P\) would force another point, giving a surviving map over a finite extension and hence binary descent. Test. Falsified an advantage from this raw total-degree count: \(\nu>P\) implies \(H>\sum d_i+P(d_{\max}-1)\ge R\), where ordinary rank-nullity already supplies a nonzero weight for every parameter. Refined support counts or source syzygies are not ruled out.
