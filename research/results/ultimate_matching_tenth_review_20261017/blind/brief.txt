The goal. Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture (Conjecture 5.8 of "The Status of my favorite problems"):
for alpha_1, alpha_2 > 0 with 1/alpha_1 + 1/alpha_2 = 1/2, with probability tending to one as N grows there is
a permutation pi of {1..N} such that, for j = 1, 2,
  sum_{i<=N} exp[ ( sqrt(N) |X_i^j - Y_pi(i)^j| / (L sqrt(log N)) )^alpha_j ] <= 2N,
where X_1..X_N, Y_1..Y_N are independent and uniform on [0,1]^2, superscripts are coordinates, and L does not
depend on N. Known by others: Ajtai-Komlos-Tusnady and Leighton-Shor control the sum and the maximum of the
distances (both optimal); the pair (alpha, infinity) for alpha < 1/2 and (2,2) are proved in the literature;
Talagrand's monograph reduces the case (2, infinity) to a discrepancy bound over a class of
gradient-penalized functions and says its chaining method loses too much to reach exponent 2 there.
