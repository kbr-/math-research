Split the rectangular images directly

Lemma (radical rank splitting). Let \(W,U_1,\ldots,U_L\) be finite-dimensional spaces over a field, with nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms or nondegenerate Hermitian forms for one fixed involution. Let \(A_i:U_i\to W\), \(L\ge1\), and write \(A_i^*\) for the adjoint. Put \[ r_i=\operatorname{rank}A_i,\qquad \kappa_i=r_i-\operatorname{rank}(A_i^*A_i),\qquad d_i=\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix}(A_j^*A_i)_{j\ne i}\end{bmatrix}, \] where the last matrix is a vertical stack, with rank zero for an empty stack. There are pairwise orthogonal self-adjoint projections \(P_1,\ldots,P_L\), summing to the identity on \(W\), such that \[ \sum_i\sum_{s\ne i}\operatorname{rank}(P_sA_i) \le (L-1)\sum_i\min(r_i,2d_i+\kappa_i) \le (L-1)\left(2\sum_{i\ne j}\operatorname{rank}(A_j^*A_i)+\sum_i\kappa_i\right). \tag{1} \] Zero projections are permitted. No order, positivity or spectral comparison is assumed.

Proof. Let \(W_i=\operatorname{im}A_i\). Since \(\ker A_i^*=W_i^\perp\), rank-nullity on \(A_i^*|_{W_i}\) gives \[ \dim\operatorname{rad}(W_i)=\dim(W_i\cap W_i^\perp)=\kappa_i. \] Let \(T_i=W_i\cap(\sum_{j\ne i}W_j)^\perp\). Surjectivity of \(A_i:U_i\to W_i\) and the definition of the stacked map imply \[ \operatorname{codim}_{W_i}T_i=d_i \le\sum_{j\ne i}\operatorname{rank}(A_j^*A_i). \] The radical of the restricted form on \(T_i\) has dimension at most \(d_i+\kappa_i\). To see this, quotient \(W_i\) by its radical: the image of \(T_i\) has dimension at least \(\dim T_i-\kappa_i\) in a nondegenerate space of dimension \(r_i-\kappa_i\). Its perpendicular has dimension at most \(d_i\); pulling that perpendicular back adds at most \(\kappa_i\) dimensions. The radical of \(T_i\) lies in that pullback.

Choose a complement \(E_i\) to \(\operatorname{rad}(T_i)\) inside \(T_i\). Its restricted form is nondegenerate, and \[ \operatorname{codim}_{W_i}E_i\le2d_i+\kappa_i. \] The \(E_i\)'s are pairwise orthogonal by their definition. Their direct sum is nondegenerate. Decompose \(W\) into these summands and their orthogonal complement, assign the complement to the first summand, and take the corresponding orthogonal projections \(P_i\). For \(s\ne i\), \(P_s\) kills \(E_i\); hence \[ \operatorname{rank}(P_sA_i)\le\dim W_i-\dim E_i\le\min(r_i,2d_i+\kappa_i). \] Sum over the \(L-1\) off-label projections and then use the bound on \(d_i\). This proves (1), including \(L=1\). \(\square\)

This controls misplaced rectangular coefficient ranks rather than squared norms of compressed positive matrices. It is an elementary subspace construction, not a claim to the source's Fourier concentration theorem over a new field. A weighted graded application still has to supply its overlap and radical estimates at all relevant cutoffs.

Why the image-radical allowance is real

Over characteristic two with the standard bilinear or Hermitian form, take two identical maps \[ A_1=A_2=\begin{pmatrix}I_r\\ I_r\end{pmatrix},\qquad r\ge1. \] Every cross-Gram and self-Gram is zero, but each map has rank \(r\). For complementary projections \(P,Q\), the sum \(\operatorname{rank}(PA_2)+\operatorname{rank}(QA_1)\) is at least \(r\), since \(A_1=PA_1+QA_1\). The bound without radicals would incorrectly give zero. Here \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=r\), so (1) retains the missing cost. This is the same isotropy phenomenon measured by the earlier Gram defect, not a new obstruction to all pairings.

A positive control shows why we used rectangular images instead of the source's row Gram. Over \(F_4\), let \(H=[I_r\ I_r]\), let \(v_0,v_1,v_2\) be the three unit-orthogonal Fourier columns on \(F_3\), and put \(V_z=v_z\otimes H\). Then \(V_zV_z^*=0\) for all \(z\), while each \(V_z\) has rank \(r\). Nevertheless \(V_z^*V_z\) has rank \(r\), cross-Grams vanish for distinct frequencies, and \(\kappa_z=0\). The projections \((v_zv_z^*)\otimes I_r\) split the three images exactly. Thus a zero row-Gram potential does not preclude an exact field splitting, and is not a reason to discard the mechanism.

Small real variance need not mean small field overlap

Proposition (odd channel rank gap). For every odd integer \(N\ge5\), let \(X=\mathcal M=F_3\), with uniform real probability, and set, for \(y\in\{0,1\}\), \[ \pi_y^m(x)=\frac13+\frac1N\left(\mathbf1_{x=m+y}-\mathbf1_{x=m+y+1}\right), \tag{2} \] with subscripts interpreted modulo three. These are positive probability distributions with uniform averaged marginals. For every real \(g\), \[ \mathbb E_m\left(\sum_x\pi_y^m(x)g(x)-\mathbb E_xg(x)\right)^2 =\frac3{N^2}\operatorname{Var}(g) \le\frac3{N^2}\mathbb E_x g(x)^2. \tag{3} \] All coefficients have odd rational denominator, but reduction modulo two makes (2) the deterministic channel \[ \bar\pi_y^m(x)=\mathbf1_{x=m+y+2}. \tag{4} \] For the binary function \(g=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}\), the scalar field deviation from its field average has rank one with probability \(2/3\), independently of \(N\). Thus no bound tending to zero with the real variance constant can control even this expected scalar field rank.

Proof. Positivity follows from \(1/3-1/N>0\), and row sums are one. Summing the perturbations over \(m\) gives zero for each \(x,y\), proving the marginal condition. The deviation in (3) is \(N^{-1}(g(m+y)-g(m+y+1))\). On three real values, the sum of their three cyclic squared differences is three times the sum of squared deviations from the mean. Averaging proves (3). In the residue field, both \(1/3\) and \(1/N\) equal one and subtraction equals addition. The two perturbed coordinates therefore have coefficient zero and the third has coefficient one, proving (4). The field mean of \(g\) is one because \(3=1\), whereas the selected value equals zero at two of the three \(m\)'s. The deviation is nonzero with probability \(2/3\). \(\square\)

The real channel hypotheses hold even for the literal source choice \(Y=F_2\), with variance tending to zero rapidly enough for any fixed smoothing parameter. This does not refute the real smoothing theorem. It refutes transferring its quantitative variance estimate by reduction of odd rational coefficients. There is no zero denominator in this example.

An odd-character alternative still has the gap. To avoid the source's binary Fourier normalization in characteristic two, instead allow \(y\in F_3\) in (2) and use \(F_4\) characters. This is an alternative being tested, not the source's stated binary-character theorem. Put \(U(x)=e_x^{\mathsf T}\), so \(Z(x)=E_{xx}\). In the reduced tagged stack, \(H(y)\) has its only nonzero row block at \((y,m+y+2)\), equal to \(e_{m+y+2}^{\mathsf T}\). The three Fourier coefficient matrices \(V_z=\sum_y\omega^{-zy}H(y)\) all have rank three and the same nondegenerate three-coordinate image. Their self-Grams have rank three, and every cross-Gram \(V_z^*V_{z'}\) is a diagonal matrix of three nonzero phases, also of rank three. Thus their image radicals are zero but their overlaps do not tend to zero with \(3/N^2\).

For any resolution of the row identity by orthogonal projections \(P_s\), surjectivity of each \(V_z\) onto that same image \(W_0\) gives \[ \sum_z\sum_{s\ne z}\operatorname{rank}(P_sV_z) =2\sum_s\operatorname{rank}(P_s|_{W_0})\ge2\dim W_0=6. \] Only the identity resolution is used in this lower bound; orthogonal projections are retained to match the proposed splitting application. This tests actual misplaced coefficient ranks, not merely a surrogate trace. It does not rule out a field-native channel with a separate rank-overlap estimate.

Actual graded source scope

The canonical charged form is nondegenerate on its full cutoff quotient, but an actual coefficient image can still have a radical. For example, the nonzero Boolean old polynomial \(x_{e,0}+x_{e,1}\) has canonical moment zero when the single-edge characters are noncut, although each indicator has moment one. Its one-dimensional image can therefore be isotropic. That does not make its ENS block hard: selecting that same input can erase its companion. The example cautions against treating radical control as a necessary condition for every successful coefficient choice.

The finite-family moment theorem can repair specified binary image and kernel restrictions over a larger field in its paid window, but it does not simultaneously preserve every prescribed channel identity. Conversely, the split (1) can be used merely as linear-algebraic bookkeeping on a fixed source matrix; it does not need an invented polynomial implementation of each projection. What remains missing is a quantitative bound on the actual cross-Gram ranks and radicals, or another quantity that controls the graded tails and a legal common coefficient choice.

The earlier scalar tilted contraction and partial-image bounds keep their unproved source-sensitive growth/escape estimates. Replacing a norm by rank does not prove those estimates. Nor can nonzero scalar frequency weights encode a degree penalty by themselves: multiplying columns by such scalars does not change rank. A genuinely graded potential must retain degree information separately.
