Controls for the outside comparisons

Proposition (invariant moment conflict). In the charged binary quotient through degree at least two, let an edge lie on a cycle. Every normalized linear answer map invariant under all circulation translations violates monomial multiplicativity on a fixed three-query test.

Proof. A cycle circulation permutes the three indicators \(x_{e,0},x_{e,1},x_{e,2}\) transitively, so their answers are one common value \(a\). Their sum is one; binary linearity gives \(a+a+a=a=1\). Distinct indicators have product zero in the old local ideal. Querying \(x_{e,0}\), \(x_{e,1}\), and their product therefore gives \(1,1,0\), contradicting multiplicativity. The test uses no higher-degree or dense query. It rejects invariant linear designs as universally consistent answers, not adaptive or nonlinear strategies. \(\square\)

The stronger recorded unrestricted-design rate obstruction already applies on Boolean quadratic systems. Its full proof and the distinction between arbitrary and source-generated query trees were reread. Changing a distribution does not evade its hypotheses; a narrower class needs a real source argument.

Corollary (rectangular inverse transfer). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(A\) be \((n+1)\times n\), \(B\) be \(n\times(n+1)\), with Boolean entries and equations \(AB=I_{n+1}\). Every ordinary-PC degree-\(D\) refutation yields one of functional unary PHP through \(\max(D,2)\). Hence no such refutation exists when \(D\ge2\) and \(n\ge2D-1\).

Proof. Substitute \(A_{ij}=B_{ji}=X_{ij}\). Off-diagonal equations become sums of column-collision axioms. A diagonal equation becomes \(\sum_j(X_{ij}^2-X_{ij})+(\sum_jX_{ij}-1)\), derivable through degree two. Boolean equations map to Boolean equations. Variable identification preserves the degree of every subsequent PC inference. The functional PHP barrier, also proved by the publication's matching-moment extension, finishes the argument. \(\square\)

This is the order-two case of the published tensor-rank/PHP reduction, credited below, not a new hardness method. The matrix system is unsatisfiable by ordinary rank. Its quadratic equations have constant-depth binary propositional expressions of total length \(O(n^3\log n)\). This is an alternative family, not a proved transfer of our whole ENS compiler to a new literal-CNF encoding.

Imported subspace local lemma. For \(0<\dim V<\infty\) over any field put \(R(X\mid Y)=\dim(X\cap Y)/\dim Y\), \(R(X)=R(X\mid V)\). Let \(X_i\le V\), \(N_i\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}\). Assume \(R(X_i\mid X_T)=R(X_i)\) for every \(T\subseteq[m]\setminus(N_i\cup\{i\})\) with \(X_T\ne0\). If \(0\le y_i<1\) and \[ 1-R(X_i)\le y_i\prod_{j\in N_i}(1-y_j), \quad\text{then}\quad R\left(\bigcap_iX_i\right)\ge\prod_i(1-y_i)>0. \] This is Ambainis–Kempe–Sattath, Theorem 13 in the cited version.

Dimension proof. Induct on the conditioning-set size. Split it into neighbor indices \(A\) and nonneighbors \(B\). Dimension submodularity and the inductive chain rule give \[ 1-R(X_i\mid X_A\cap X_B) \le\frac{1-R(X_i\mid X_B)}{R(X_A\mid X_B)} \le\frac{y_i\prod_{j\in N_i}(1-y_j)}{\prod_{j\in A}(1-y_j)}\le y_i. \] Here \(X_T=\bigcap_{j\in T}X_j\); independence handles the numerator, and induction keeps denominators positive. The empty case is immediate. The chain rule concludes. Only dimension identities are used, so finite fields are included.

For \(V=\operatorname{im}M_w\), take \(X_i=\ker([wf]\mapsto[wb_if])\). Bounded multiplication closure and \(\deg w+K+\deg b_i\le D\) give representative independence. Then \(R(X_i)=1-E_i/h_w(K)\). The dependency hypotheses remain unproved. The earlier weighted Shearer criterion already permits one-sided conditional-rank bounds rather than exact independence. It and its general truncation obstruction were recovered during registration and reread in full; this import supplies an attributed classical comparison, not a stronger new route.

Calibration (earlier truncated kernel dependence). Over any field, on the degree-one Boolean polynomial space in \(v\ge2\) variables, the kernels of multiplication by \(x_1\) and \(x_2\), with outputs through degree two, are not relatively dimension-independent, despite disjoint variable supports.

Proof. Unique multilinear coefficients give \(\ker M_{x_1}=\langle1-x_1\rangle\) and \(\ker M_{x_2}=\langle1-x_2\rangle\). Their intersection is zero, so \(R(X_1\mid X_2)=0\), whereas \(R(X_1)=1/(v+1)\). On the full two-variable function algebra instead, the kernel dimensions are two, two and one in dimension four, giving independence. It is the degree truncation that invalidates the naive support argument. \(\square\)

Lemma (pivot support degree). Over any field, on Boolean entries of an \(s\times s\) matrix, a nonzero polynomial function supported only on invertible matrices has multilinear degree at least \(s\). Spectator variables are allowed. The minimum is exactly \(s\).

Proof. Setting any whole matrix row to zero makes the matrix singular, so the multilinear polynomial restricts to zero. Hence every nonzero monomial contains a variable from that row. This holds for all \(s\) disjoint rows, forcing degree at least \(s\). The determinant is a degree-\(s\) nonzero function with the required support, attaining the bound. \(\square\)

This is a support-degree variant of the existing localization cost, not a general obstruction to rational methods. A full symbolic pivot needs this weight degree before its inverse can be used on that branch. A small pivot, a different certificate, or old relations may change the application; positivity-based network invertibility is not being asserted over \(\mathbb F_2\).
