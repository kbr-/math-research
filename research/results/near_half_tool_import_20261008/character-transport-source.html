Character binomials can change position cheaply

Let \(q\ge3\) be prime and let a field \(E\), of characteristic different from \(q\), contain a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\). Work in ordinary Boolean variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_v\) and write \[ u_i(a)=1+(\zeta^a-1)x_i,\qquad X(a)=\prod_{i:a_i\ne0}u_i(a_i),\quad a\in\mathbb F_q^v. \] Every character product is multilinear, of degree its support size. Retain all Boolean equations. The literal identity \[ u_i(a)u_i(t)-u_i(a+t)=(\zeta^a-1)(\zeta^t-1)(x_i^2-x_i) \] will pay for every character reduction.

Lemma (character binomial transport). Suppose \(X(a)-\gamma X(b)\), with \(\gamma\in E^\times\), has a PC derivation through degree \(B\), and \(a,b\) have disjoint supports differing in size by at most one. Let \(p-h=a-b\), and put \[ M=\max\{|\operatorname{supp}p|,|\operatorname{supp}h|, \lceil|\operatorname{supp}(a-b)|/2\rceil\}. \] Then \(X(p)-\gamma X(h)\) has a PC derivation through \(\max\{B,M+2\}\).

Proof. On coordinates common to \(p,h\), subtract \(h_i\) from both exponents, obtaining \(p',h'\) with disjoint supports and the same difference. Their respective support sizes are no larger than those of \(p,h\). For any coordinate of nonzero difference, a disjoint representation puts that difference on the first side or its negative on the second. Multiplying the relation by the inverse of its present factor and reducing transfers the coordinate between sides, preserving \(\gamma\) and the exponent difference.

Pair the transfers needed in opposite directions, performing the two moves of each pair consecutively. After each pair the side sizes return to their starting values; between its two moves a side can exceed them by at most one. Perform any remaining moves, all in the same direction, afterward; their side sizes lie between the starting and target sizes. Thus reduced side sizes never exceed \(M+1\), and every temporary multiplication and its explicit Boolean correction has degree at most \(M+2\). This derives \(X(p')-\gamma X(h')\). Reattach the previously removed common factors one at a time. Reduced supports grow only to the target supports; each temporary multiplication costs at most \(M+1\). The displayed identity, multiplied by the other factors, is a Boolean certificate at exactly those ceilings. Reuse the original derivation's final line; no earlier proof line is multiplied by an accumulating cofactor. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The nonzero phase and prime-modulus restrictions are retained as shared application conventions; the transport argument does not require their full strength. The theorem’s rank positivity follows from its consistency and Boolean-inconsistency assumptions.

The coset degree ceiling

Let \(q\ge3\) be prime, \(F\) any field with \(\operatorname{char}F\ne q\), and \(Ax=b\) a system over \(\mathbb F_q\) on \(v\) coordinates, of rank \(r\ge1\) and row support at most \(w\ge1\). Assume it has a field solution but no Boolean solution. Over \(F\), encode each row by all its forbidden Boolean-assignment indicators, and include Booleanity. Put \(k=v-r\) and \[ C=\min\left\{v,\left\lceil (1-1/q)v+(k+1)/q +w\sqrt{2r\log(4r+2)}\right\rceil\right\}. \]

Theorem (coset character ceiling). This Boolean encoding has an ordinary PC refutation over \(F\) through degree \[ B=\max\{\lceil C/2\rceil+w+1,\ k+3\}. \] For fixed \(q,w\), this is at most \[ \max\{v-r,\ v/2-r/(2q)\}+O_w(\sqrt{r\log(r+1)}+1). \] No polynomial proof-size bound is asserted.

Parametrize the actual field coset. Select \(r\) independent original rows, still of width at most \(w\). Full-field consistency makes every other equation their affine consequence, so this subsystem has the same solutions. Choose \(r\) pivot columns and let \(T\) be the \(k\) free coordinates, denoted \(y\). The field solutions are precisely \[ x_j=c_j+\ell_j\cdot y \quad(j\notin T),\qquad x_j=y_j \quad(j\in T), \] where \(c_j\in\mathbb F_q\), \(\ell_j\in\mathbb F_q^T\); use \(c_j=0,\ell_j=e_j\) for free coordinates. In particular, for each pivot and each \(s\in\{1,2\}\), the row-space vector \[ d=s(e_j-\ell_j) \] has support at most \(k+1\), and is a combination \(\lambda\) of the selected rows whose corresponding charge is \(sc_j\). Here \(\ell_j\) is embedded in the full coordinate space on \(T\).

A random path to a small nonzero target. Choose independent uniform \(\rho_i\in\mathbb F_q\), first set the row coefficients from zero to \(\rho\) one by one, then from \(\rho\) to \(\lambda\) one by one. At a first-stage state, each coordinate of the row sum is zero or uniform. At a second-stage state, it is uniform when an unprocessed random row touches it; otherwise it equals its final value \(d_i\). Consequently each state's expected support is at most \[ (1-1/q)v+|\operatorname{supp}d|/q\le (1-1/q)v+(k+1)/q. \] Changing one random coefficient affects at most \(w\) support coordinates. The same bounded-difference proof as in the random modular theorem gives tail \(\exp[-t^2/(2rw^2)]\). At \(t=w\sqrt{2r\log(4r+2)}\), a union bound over \(2r+1\) states has failure probability at most one half. Thus a path exists with all supports at most \(C\). A separate path may be used for each pivot and each \(s\); no common random choice or short proof inventory is needed.

Realize the path in ordinary PC. Adjoin \(\zeta\) in a finite algebraic extension \(E/F\). For every row \((a_i,b_i)\) and scalar \(t\), the polynomial \(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}\) is a scalar sum of its forbidden assignment indicators through degree \(w\), by multilinear interpolation. An allowed tuple contributes zero. Maintain a balanced relation \(P-\zeta^bQ\) at each state, with disjoint character supports of sizes at most half its support, rounded up. A row change gives \[ (P-\zeta^bQ)X(ta_i)+\zeta^bQ(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}) =PX(ta_i)-\zeta^{b+tb_i}Q. \] Both summands fit degree \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w\). Reduce repeated factors, remove common factors using inverse-character multiplication, and balance the sides by transferring factors. Each move adds at most one temporary degree; the explicit local Boolean identity pays for its reduction. This constructs the endpoint balanced relation through \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w+1\), with difference \(s(e_j-\ell_j)\) and phase \(\zeta^{sc_j}\). This argument uses no global inconsistency witness.

For every free exponent vector \(\beta\in\mathbb F_q^T\), apply character binomial transport to place this relation at \[ X(\beta)u_j(s)-\zeta^{sc_j}X(\beta+s\ell_j). \] The first term has support at most \(k+1\) and the second at most \(k\); the endpoint difference has support at most \(k+1\). Hence all such cofactored relations are derived through \(B\). Crucially, their proofs do not cost the previous ceiling plus \(k\).

Use the Boolean exclusion on the full parameter space

On the full \(q\)-ary parameter grid \(\mathbb F_q^T\), define functions \[ g_j(y)=\zeta^{2(c_j+\ell_j\cdot y)} -(1+\zeta)\zeta^{c_j+\ell_j\cdot y}+\zeta. \] They vanish exactly when \(x_j=c_j+\ell_j\cdot y\) is zero or one: the polynomial in its character value is \((z-1)(z-\zeta)\), with two distinct roots. Thus the \(g_j\) have no common zero on the full parameter grid. This uses the actual field consistency to define a nonempty global coset; no inconsistent-system pseudo-ideal is being treated as a global evaluation algebra.

For a pivot \(j\), combine the two derived cofactored relations with the literal polynomial identity \[ u_j(2)-(1+\zeta)u_j(1)+\zeta=0. \] It gives, through \(B\), the reduced polynomial \[ P_{j,\beta}=\zeta^{2c_j}X(\beta+2\ell_j) -(1+\zeta)\zeta^{c_j}X(\beta+\ell_j)+\zeta X(\beta). \] All exponents in free coordinates are taken modulo \(q\). For a free coordinate \(j\), multiply the same literal identity by \(X(\beta)\) and apply the local character-product correction at that one variable; this derives \(P_{j,\beta}\) through \(k+1\le B\). Its ordinary polynomial can even be zero after the Boolean substitution; that causes no problem.

At every point \(y\), choose one index \(j\) with \(g_j(y)\ne0\). Define \(H_j(y)=1/g_j(y)\) on points assigned to that index, and zero otherwise. Then \(\sum_jH_jg_j=1\) as functions on the full parameter grid. Its characters \(\zeta^{\beta\cdot y}\), \(\beta\in\mathbb F_q^T\), are a basis: explicitly \[ H_j(y)=\sum_\beta h_{j,\beta}\zeta^{\beta\cdot y},\qquad h_{j,\beta}=q^{-k}\sum_z H_j(z)\zeta^{-\beta\cdot z}. \] The inverse formula follows by multiplying the one-coordinate geometric sums; \(q\) is invertible in \(E\). It also covers \(k=0\).

Substituting these expansions into \(\sum_jH_jg_j=1\) and collecting exponents modulo \(q\) yields a coefficient identity in the character basis. Replace each free character by its ordinary multilinear polynomial \(X(\beta)\). This is a linear replacement of a coefficient identity, so \[ \sum_{j,\beta}h_{j,\beta}P_{j,\beta}=1 \] is a literal ordinary-polynomial identity. Every summand \(P_{j,\beta}\) is already a derived line through \(B\). No product \(H_jg_j\) of potentially doubled degree is inserted into the PC proof, and no unbounded certificate is used as a bounded one. Taking this final linear combination yields a refutation over \(E\). Coefficient components in an \(F\)-basis of \(E\) containing one give the refutation over \(F\) at the same degree, since all original forbidden indicators and Boolean axioms are defined over \(F\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

Outcome for the first proposal

At fixed \(q=3\), \(r=2v/3\) and \(w=6\), we have \(k=v/3\) and the bound is \(7v/18+o(v)\). Its fixed gap below half rules out the proposed ternary family at every fixed approximation depth \(\ell\ge2\), independently of the chosen excluded syndrome, expansion quality or construction algorithm. This is an actual PC upper bound, not a ceiling on a lower-bound certificate.

More generally, for fixed \(q,w\), every rank \(r\ge(1/2+\epsilon)v\) with fixed \(\epsilon>0\) is excluded from the moving near-half window. Lower ranks are not ruled out by this bound. In particular, modulus five and rank \(v/2\) have enough syndromes for the same missing-image counting argument, while this theorem gives only \(v/2+O(1)\) eventually at bounded width. This is an untested alternative, not a new hard-family claim; its detailed construction and lower-bound test are queued after the existing alternatives.
