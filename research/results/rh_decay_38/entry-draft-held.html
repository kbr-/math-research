What the formula says at this row

From the appendix, read. At the trivial twist the set of varying primes is empty and the twisted theta function is a combination of translates of \(\overline\theta\) by \(\lambda^2h_0/L\), \(h_0\) modulo \(L\), where \(L\) makes the fixed factor \(\chi_n(\lambda)^2\) times the indicator of primary \(n\) prime to 2 periodic; that needs \(n\) modulo 9 and modulo 2, so \(L=18\) suffices (the source takes a fixed \(L\) with high powers of the primes above 2 and 3, which only adds terms with zero coefficient or larger denominators of the same kind; I use 18 for the estimate and mark it as mine). Since \(\lambda^2=-3\), the shifts are \(-h_0/6\) and the reduced denominators \(c_0\) divide 6, so \(N(c_0)\le36\). The dual sums are \(C\sum_\ell d(\ell)\alpha(\ell)N(\ell)^{-1/2}\psi(\lambda^4\ell)V^\sharp(N(\ell)X/N(c_0)^2)\) with \(|C|\le1/81\), \(\ell\in\lambda^{-4}\mathcal O\) of least norm \(1/81\), so the weight's argument is at least \(X/104976\). The transformed weight is \(V^\sharp(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(0)}\widehat V_*(-t)\frac{\Gamma(7/6+t)\Gamma(5/6+t)}{\Gamma(7/6-t)\Gamma(5/6-t)}((2\pi)^4x/27)^{-t}dt\).

For the weight \(W(y)=\exp(-(\log y)^2/2)\), \(\widehat V_*(t)=\sqrt{2\pi}\exp((t+\tfrac12)^2/2)\) and \(V^\sharp\) is computed numerically (script, output): of order one below \(x=0.1\), \(4\cdot10^{-2}\) at \(x=1\), \(10^{-3}\) at \(5\), \(2\cdot10^{-4}\) at \(30\), \(5\cdot10^{-6}\) at \(100\). A first attempt with a double-exponential quadrature gave values near \(0.8\) at \(x=10^5\), where the weight must be negligible; the trapezoid rule, which converges geometrically for this integrand, replaced it, and its values respect the bound \(|V^\sharp|\le e^{1/8}\) and decay. So the dual sum has about \(10^5/X\) terms of appreciable weight, and the twisted sum must fall as \(X\) grows through the range \(10^3\) to \(10^5\). For the compactly supported bump of the previous entry the transform decays much later, its Mellin transform being wide; that is why no fall was seen there, and the reviewer's estimate of the onset was of the right kind.

What it shows and does not

At the trivial row the completed twisted sum has cancellation a thousand times beyond the random-walk scale, setting in where the import's transformation formula puts it, while the untwisted sum has the growth of a pole. This is what an entire completed series with a functional equation of about that conductor looks like, and it is what the import's removal of the polar term asserts (polar term audit). It is evidence for the theta input at one row with the trivial twist. It does not test the dependence on the row, the local factors at primes of the row, the quadratic large sieve, or anything else in the import; and it does not test the formula quantitatively, since I compared the onset and not the dual sum's value.

Nonvacuity. Ten sizes with 603 to 308234 terms; the two predicted behaviours differ by a factor of more than \(10^4\) at the largest size (\(19.9\) against \(0.00075\)), and the outcome that would have contradicted the formula, a twisted sum near the random-walk scale \(0.83\), is excluded by three orders of magnitude.
