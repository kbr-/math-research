The growth floor in the full Boolean algebra

Let \(\mathcal P_{\le K}\) be the space of multilinear polynomial functions of degree at most \(K\) on \(\mathbb F_2^n\), and \(U=\operatorname{span}(1,x_1,\ldots,x_n)\). Products below are Boolean products. For \(0\le K<n\) and \(0\ne V\subseteq\mathcal P_{\le K}\), \[ \dim(UV)-\dim V\ge n-K. \tag{1} \] This is the same leading-product principle as the earlier disjoint-row multiplication argument, here in the full Boolean algebra with one coordinate per row.

Proof. Let \(k\le K\) be the maximum degree of an element of \(V\), and choose \(f\in V\) of degree \(k\). In a degree-compatible monomial order, let its leading monomial be \(x_S\), \(|S|=k\). For every \(j\notin S\), the Boolean reduction of \(x_jf\) still has leading monomial \(x_{S\cup\{j\}}\): ordinary multiplication preserves monomial order, and any nonsquarefree term drops in total degree when reduced. These \(n-k\) leading monomials are distinct and have degree \(k+1\). The products are consequently linearly independent modulo \(V\), whose elements have degree at most \(k\). This proves (1). Equality is attained by \(V=\langle x_1\cdots x_K\rangle\), with the empty product interpreted as one: its affine products have exactly the basis consisting of that monomial and its products with coordinates outside its support. \(\square\)

Apply the floor to the actual source image

Take \(n\ge2\), old Boolean equations only, and one complete accuracy-\(h\) block, \(h\ge1\), with genuine input tuple \[ (g_1,\ldots,g_n)=(x_1,\ldots,x_n). \] Its original product degree is \(2h\), companion degree \(2h+1\), and fresh-field degree two. Its input space in the stable-core criterion is exactly \(U\). The joint map is \[ \mathcal T f=(x_1f,\ldots,x_nf). \] Its full Boolean-function kernel is the line spanned by \(e_0=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+x_i)\): all coordinates vanish precisely when the function is supported only at the origin. The unique multilinear representative of \(e_0\) has degree \(n\).

Therefore, when \(K\le n-2\), \(\mathcal T\) is injective on \(\mathcal P_{\le K+1}\). Every nonzero \(X\subseteq\mathcal T(\mathcal P_{\le K})\) has a unique preimage subspace \(V\subseteq\mathcal P_{\le K}\), and \[ UX=\mathcal T(UV),\qquad \dim UX-\dim X=\dim UV-\dim V\ge n-K. \] Since \(\dim U-1=n\), capping as in the criterion leaves \[ q=\min(n,\dim UX-\dim X)\ge n-K. \tag{2} \] These are actual tuple-image dimensions, not a scalar image substituted for the source map.

Proposition (coordinate core cost). In this complete coordinate-input source, the displayed stable-core bound, optimized over every \(h\ge1\), original \(B\ge2h+1\), \(K\ge0\), and nonzero \(X\subseteq\mathcal T(\mathcal P_{\le K})\), always has \[ \max\{K+4,\ K+1+TB\}\ge n+2,\qquad T=\max\{1,\lceil q/h\rceil-1\}. \tag{3} \] The value \(n+2\) is attained for that bound at \(h=1,B=3,K=n-2\) and \(X=\mathcal T\langle x_1\cdots x_{n-2}\rangle\). In contrast, the actual source has a surviving degree-one weight and constant coefficient map with weighted original replay cost \(2h+2\), equal to four at \(h=1\). The Boolean growth bound (1), including sharpness, is part of this control.

Proof of the budget floor. If \(K\ge n-1\), then \(K+1+TB\ge n+3\), since \(T\ge1\) and \(B\ge3\). Otherwise put \(m=n-K\ge2\) and use (2). If \(m\le2h\), then \(TB-m\ge2h+1-m\ge1\). If \(m>2h\), let \(s=\lceil m/h\rceil\ge3\); \(T\ge s-1\) and \(m\le sh\), so \[ TB-m\ge(s-1)(2h+1)-sh=(s-2)h+s-1\ge3. \] In both cases \(K+1+TB=n+1+(TB-m)\ge n+2\). For the asserted attaining choice, the monomial preimage has growth exactly two; injectivity on degree at most \(n-1\) gives \(q=2,T=1\), and both terms of (3) equal \(n+2\).

Proof of the cheap repair. Take \(W=x_1\), set the first row's coefficient of \(g_1\) to one, and set every other coefficient to zero. The substituted product is \(1-x_1\), and \[ Wg_i(1-x_1)=x_1x_i(1-x_1) \] is an old Boolean consequence through degree at most three. The fresh-field images are zero. The nonzero weight survives the Boolean-only base. The original source ceiling is still \(2h+1\), so weighted proof replay pays \(1+(2h+1)=2h+2\), not a reset ceiling for the specialized product. At \(h=1\) this is four for every \(n\). \(\square\)

This comparison is about the stated sufficient budget (3). It is not a lower bound on every possible implementation of the colon construction, every alternative accounting of its particular output, or every stable-core argument. The base is satisfiable, so it is not a Frege or PC hardness result. Additional hard old equations can invalidate the leading-monomial argument and change the kernel of \(\mathcal T\); no full-cube floor is imported into the charged quotient without proof.

Compare the existing positive control

The tiny-minimizer example has the opposite behavior. Its actual inputs are \(Q=x_1x_2+x_3x_4\) and \(1+QH\), \(H=z_1z_2+z_3z_4\), on the Boolean domain with \(ax_i=az_j=0\). It recovers a surviving anchor weight and constant row selecting the second input. This is a previously easy source, not new coverage. A one-dimensional minimizer is enough for that endpoint; loss of a fraction of a larger domain is not itself failure.

Together the controls say exactly what they test. Small absolute growth can construct legal maps, even on a tiny image. But the all-input growth condition can also cost arbitrarily more than a direct known normalizer, even after optimizing its candidate subspace and degree. Neither a ratio close to one nor large input rank resolves this distinction.
