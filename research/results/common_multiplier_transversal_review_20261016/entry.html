General statement. lem:transversal-killer-basis: on the Boolean cube over any field, the functions of degree at most \(d\) vanishing on an up-set \(U\) are spanned, with no relation, by the products \(\prod_{v\in T}(1-x_v)\) over the transversals \(T\) of the minimal sets of \(U\) with \(|T|\le d\); so the first degree of a common killer of errors with monomial supports that keeps \(1\) alive is a transversal number, and for a protected function \(p\) it is the least size of a transversal avoiding a point of its support.

Obstacle

Every joint saving the line has found is of an excluded kind, and the record now says why on its test systems. Where the closure is the degree part of a vanishing ideal of a point set, stacked ranks are restriction ranks to unions of supports (lem:domain-model-stacked-rank); the product pigeonhole system is such a domain model in its whole inert range (cor:hole-occupancy-kernels), and the functional system at six and seven pigeons still showed only the frame and its relations (check:functional-factor-kernels). The first exclusion of the open statement, union-of-supports counting, already removes every saving in every domain model; for monomial supports on the cube the lemma below adds that the killers themselves are combinations of products of single literal killers, though the degree qualifier of the third exclusion, a degree growing with the number of distinct factors, fails for a star family, whose transversal number stays \(1\). A saving of the admitted kind must therefore come from the part of a truncated closure that is not a vanishing ideal: at the top degree of the product system that part is the span of the unplaced-pigeon indicators (cor:hole-occupancy-kernels (d)), of dimension at most \(m\). The line is stuck on finding a system and error family where that non-domain part acts on the errors with rank growing with the family.

Transversal killer basis

Statement. Over a field \(F\), let \(Q=\{0,1\}^N\), identify points with subsets of \([N]\), and let \(U\subseteq Q\) be an up-set with minimal sets \(\mathcal M\). Call \(T\subseteq[N]\) a transversal when it meets every member of \(\mathcal M\). For \(d\ge0\), let \(W_d\) be the functions on \(Q\) given by polynomials of degree at most \(d\) that vanish on \(U\). Then (a) the products \(\pi_T=\prod_{v\in T}(1-x_v)\), over the transversals \(T\) with \(|T|\le d\), form a basis of \(W_d\); so \(\dim W_d\) is the number of such transversals, and the least \(d\) with \(W_d\ne0\) is the transversal number \(\tau(\mathcal M)\). (b) For a function \(p\) on \(Q\), the least degree of an \(f\in W_d\) with \(fp\ne0\) is \(\min\{|T|:T\text{ a transversal},\ T\subseteq[N]\setminus x,\ x\in\operatorname{supp}p\}\), and it is infinite when \(\operatorname{supp}p\subseteq U\). (c) In particular, for errors \(E_j=c_jx_{A_j}\) with \(c_j\ne0\), as functions on \(Q\), among killers \(f\) with \(\deg f+\max|A_j|\le D\), a common killer modulo the cube's degree-\(D\) vanishing ideal that keeps \(1\) alive exists exactly when \(\tau(\{A_j\})+\max|A_j|\le D\), and its least degree is then \(\tau(\{A_j\})\), attained by \(\pi_T\) for a least transversal \(T\); a nonzero constant error, \(A_j=\emptyset\), gives \(\tau=\infty\) and no killer.

Proof. Put \(y_v=1-x_v\); this affine change of variables preserves degree and sends the point \(x\) to its complement, so \(U\) becomes the down-set \(D'=\{[N]\setminus x:x\in U\}\). For a down-set, the monomials \(y_S\), \(S\in D'\), are a basis of the functions on \(D'\): their evaluation matrix on \(D'\) is triangular for inclusion with ones on the diagonal, as \(y_S\) at the point \(R\) is \(1\) exactly when \(S\subseteq R\). So the functions on \(D'\) given by degree-\(\le d\) polynomials have dimension \(\#\{S\in D':|S|\le d\}\), and restriction from \(Q\), where all \(y_S\) with \(|S|\le d\) are independent, has kernel of dimension \(\#\{S\notin D':|S|\le d\}\). For \(S\notin D'\), \(y_S\) vanishes on \(D'\): a point \(R\in D'\) with \(S\subseteq R\) would put \(S\) in \(D'\). So these \(y_S\) are a basis of the kernel. Finally \(S\notin D'\) means that \([N]\setminus S\notin U\), that is, \([N]\setminus S\) contains no member of \(\mathcal M\), that is, \(S\) is a transversal; and \(y_S=\pi_S\). This gives (a). (b) A combination \(f=\sum_T\lambda_T\pi_T\) has \(f(x)=\sum_{T\subseteq[N]\setminus x}\lambda_T\), since \(\pi_T(x)=1\) exactly when \(T\) avoids \(x\); so some \(f\in W_d\) is nonzero at \(x\) exactly when some transversal of size at most \(d\) avoids \(x\), and for \(x\in U\) none does, \(x\) containing a member of \(\mathcal M\). (c) The support of \(c_jx_{A_j}\) is the up-set generated by \(A_j\); \(fE_j\) has degree at most \(\deg f+|A_j|\le D\), so it lies in the degree-\(D\) vanishing ideal exactly when \(f\) vanishes on that up-set; the union \(U\) has minimal sets among the \(A_j\), with the same transversals; protecting \(1\) means \(f\ne0\) somewhere, and (b) with \(p=1\) gives \(\tau\).

Degenerate cases. \(U\) empty: \(\mathcal M\) is empty, \(\emptyset\) is a transversal, \(\tau=0\), and \(W_d\) is everything. \(U=Q\): \(\emptyset\in\mathcal M\) and no set meets it, so \(W_d=0\) and no killer survives. One error \(x_A\): \(\tau=1\), any literal \(1-x_v\), \(v\in A\). \(p\) supported in \(U\): no surviving killer, as (b) says.
