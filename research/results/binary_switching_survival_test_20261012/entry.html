Categorical switching survival

A categorical term on \([q]^n\), integer \(q\ge2\), specifies one value on each coordinate of its scope. A categorical clause forbids one such pattern; its width is the scope size. Under \(\mathcal R_p\), independently retain each whole coordinate with probability \(p\in(0,1)\), otherwise fix it to a uniform value in \([q]\). This is not independent restriction of the one-hot bits.

Lemma. Let \(F:[q]^n\to\{0,1\}\) have a categorical CNF or DNF of width at most \(k\ge1\), and let \(\mu=\Pr_x[F(x)=1]\) for uniform \(x\). There is a canonical categorical decision tree such that, for every integer \(s\ge1\), \[\Pr_{\rho\sim\mathcal R_p}[\operatorname{depth}(F|_\rho)\ge s] \le a^s,\qquad a=\frac{2q^2kp}{1-p}.\tag{1}\] Here the depth on the left may be taken to be that canonical tree's height, so the bound also holds for minimum depth. For every integer \(t\ge0\), \[\Pr[F|_\rho\not\equiv0]\le q^t\mu+a^{t+1}.\tag{2}\] Bounds above one are vacuous but valid. Clause count does not enter either bound.

Source and adaptation. Beame's Switching Lemma Primer, Section 2, Lemmas 1–2 and the full proof, was read from the held primary PDF text. Its stated restriction family fixes the number of unset Boolean variables. The proof below uses its term-query encoding with a weighted product measure and \(q\)-valued path data; (1) is not quoted as Beame's stated constant. Its displayed \(7pr\) constant is confirmed by the concluding calculation on page 8; no numerical parameter is inferred from a damaged text glyph.

Canonical tree. Work first with a DNF with fixed term and within-term coordinate orders. Discard impossible terms. At a restriction, find the first term not falsified. If there is none, output zero; if that term has no unset coordinates, output one. Otherwise query all its unset coordinates, in order, using a full \(q\)-ary tree, and continue recursively at each resulting assignment. The variables within that batch are queried even if an intermediate answer already decides its term. A CNF uses the complementary DNF and interchanges leaf labels. This preserves height and does not change the original restriction law.

Encode a bad restriction. When the canonical tree has height at least \(s\), choose its lexicographically first path of length at least \(s\), and truncate after exactly \(s\) queries. Break that prefix into term-query batches, the last possibly partial. For each batch record the nonempty subset of the original term's at most \(k\) positions queried in that batch, and record the actual path values of its queried variables. Replace those \(s\) stars in \(\rho\) by the values that satisfy their selected terms, obtaining \(\rho'\). Different batches use disjoint newly queried variables. The recorded masks have total size \(s\); the path values have \(q^s\) possibilities.

For a composition \(s=s_1+\cdots+s_j\) into positive parts, the number of mask sequences is at most \(\prod_i\binom{k}{s_i}\le k^s\). There are at most \(2^{s-1}\) compositions, so at most \((2k)^s\) mask sequences, and at most \((2kq)^s\) auxiliary records. Terms shorter than \(k\) are padded by unavailable positions; this only enlarges the bound. The partial final batch is included.

Decode and weight. From \(\rho'\) and the auxiliary record, find the first nonfalsified term. It is the first selected term: earlier terms were already falsified by unchanged old assignments; the selected term has all its queried coordinates set to satisfying values, and no conflicting future assignment to its still-unqueried coordinates. The latter is immediate for a full batch, which queried every remaining coordinate of that term, and for the final partial batch, after which no later assignment was made. Its mask identifies the variables. Change their values to the recorded actual path values, and repeat. At the next stage earlier terms remain falsified under the restored path prefix. This identifies every selected batch, including the last partial one. Reset all recovered queried variables to stars to recover \(\rho\). The encoding is injective.

The product probability weights obey \[\Pr(\rho)=\left(\frac{qp}{1-p}\right)^s\Pr(\rho'),\] since exactly \(s\) stars become fixed values. For each auxiliary record, sum over the distinct possible \(\rho'\); its total weight is at most one. Multiplying by the auxiliary bound proves (1). No independence conditional on earlier term failures is assumed.

For (2), when a nonzero restricted function has a decision tree of depth at most \(t\), one accepting leaf has conditional cube mass at least \(q^{-t}\). Hence its conditional mean \(\mu_\rho\) is at least \(q^{-t}\). Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr[\mu_\rho\ge q^{-t}]\le q^t\mathbb E\mu_\rho=q^t\mu\). The complementary event of depth greater than \(t\) has probability at most \(a^{t+1}\) by (1). This proves (2). It is an elementary use of switching, not a rank estimate from scalar rarity on each fixed restriction.

Narrow error source exclusion

Consider a finite acyclic binary ENS source over an old \(q\)-label one-hot domain. Every block has \(h\ge1\) fresh binary coefficient rows; its input polynomials may use old variables and coefficients of earlier blocks, but not its own or later coefficients. All coefficient fields, old axioms and at most \(M\ge1\) companions are retained. Their original polynomial degrees are at most a supplied \(B\ge2\). This fixed scalar-assignment argument does not require product-only earlier inputs, although the audited compiler's product-only sources are included.

Supply a probability distribution on coherent global assignments of every fresh coefficient to binary old polynomials of degree at most \(T\ge1\), fixing the old variables. For every map in its support, suppose each actual companion's evaluation on the entire old label cube has a categorical CNF of width at most \(k\ge1\). There is no bound on that CNF's clause count and it is not introduced into the proof. Suppose each companion has mean at most \(\epsilon\), averaged over that map distribution and a uniform full label assignment. A \(\mathcal R_p\) restriction, independent of the map sample, must leave the old system without an ordinary binary PC refutation through degree \(TB\), with probability at least \(1-\eta\).

Theorem. If, for some integer \(t\ge0\), \[a=\frac{2q^2kp}{1-p},\qquad \eta+M(q^t\epsilon+a^{t+1})<1,\tag{3}\] then the complete source has no degree-\(B\) PC or ordinary NS refutation. There is a single sampled map and one old restriction for which every companion is locally zero and the old degree barrier survives. The weight is one; the original proof ceiling is charged as \(TB\), not reset. The width assumption concerns the supplied sampler, not every possible coefficient assignment.

Uniform scalar specialization. For independent uniform fresh coefficient bits, \(T=1\) and \(\epsilon=2^{-h}\) suffice. If the width premise holds for this particular sampler, a convenient sufficient condition is \[a\le q^{-2},\qquad \eta+M(2^{-h/2}+2^{-h})<1.\tag{3a}\] Other samplers can satisfy (3) even when uniform scalar choices fail its width premise.

Rare errors before restriction. Choose all fresh coefficient bits independently and uniformly, once for the whole source. At any fixed old label assignment, condition on all earlier coefficients of a block. Its input vector is now fixed over \(\mathbb F_2\). If the input of a companion is zero, that companion is zero. Otherwise the vector is nonzero, and each fresh scalar row evaluates to an independent fair bit. Its complementary product is one with probability \(2^{-h}\). Thus every companion error has mean at most \(2^{-h}\), averaged over the scalar choices and a uniform full label assignment. This reasoning permits shared earlier blocks because their coefficients are conditioned on once, not chosen separately for each reader.

One simultaneous restriction. For each fixed map apply (2) to each companion function and average over the supplied map distribution. Its survival probability is at most \(q^t\epsilon+a^{t+1}\). A union bound over the actual \(M\) companions and the old-hardness failure probability is less than one by (3). This supplies a single pair of choices making all errors zero while old hardness survives. For the uniform scalar specialization, choose \[t=\left\lfloor\frac{h}{2\log_2 q}\right\rfloor,\] so \(q^t2^{-h}\le2^{-h/2}\) and \(a^{t+1}\le q^{-2(t+1)}\le2^{-h}\), proving (3a). No adaptive independent-formula assertion or independent success event across companions is used.

Certificates and original replay. A degree-at-most-\(T\) polynomial substitution followed by whole-coordinate restriction sends an original degree-\(e\) generator to degree at most \(Te\). Its companion image is zero on the remaining one-hot label domain and has a local-axiom certificate through \(Te\): eliminate one indicator per coordinate with its linear sum equation, reduce squares and products of two different remaining indicators, and retain at most one of those indicators per coordinate in a monomial. This reduction never increases degree. The resulting tensor basis consists of one and \(q-1\) label indicators per coordinate; its evaluation matrix on \([q]\) is invertible, so a zero function has zero remainder. This proves literal degree-paid membership, not vanishing on hypothetical solutions of the inconsistent old charge system.

Coefficient field images are Boolean-zero through \(2T\le TB\). Substitute in an alleged degree-\(B\) source proof and supply these local certificates. For PC multiplication a nonzero predecessor has original degree at most \(B-1\), so multiplication by the degree-at-most-\(T\) variable image fits \(TB\). Old variables are fixed before restriction; the other axioms are the restricted old ones. This contradicts their degree-\(TB\) barrier. Ordinary NS has its same-degree PC replay. The argument constructs no small-description certificate or map-search algorithm.

Fixed-family range. In the recorded hard categorical restriction law, take \(\Delta=\lceil n^\alpha\rceil\), \(p=n^{-\beta}\), \(0<\beta<\alpha<1\), with \(\log N=\Theta(n^\alpha)\), residual proper degree \(D_\rho=\Omega(n^{1+\alpha-\beta})\) on an event of probability \(1-o(1)\). Fix a supplied original source exponent \(\ell\ge2\) and choose \[\alpha(\ell-1)+\beta<1,\quad h=\lceil n^\alpha\log n\rceil, \quad B=\lceil h^\ell\log n\rceil, \quad k\le\left\lfloor\frac{1-p}{162p}\right\rfloor.\tag{4}\] For \(q=3\), \(T=1\) and the uniform scalar sampler with its supplied width premise, (3a) holds eventually for every \(M\le N^A\), fixed \(A\), and \(B=o(D_\rho)\). The family, \(h,B\) and width allowance do not depend on \(A\). This is a supplied width class; no assertion that every depth-\(d\) proof has (4) is made. The exponent \(\ell\) describes the source's supplied original ceiling, not a newly claimed compiler simulation.

Affine error width

The width premise is not an automatic consequence of low polynomial degree. Let \(m\ge32\), \(1\le h\le m/16\), and choose \(C\) uniformly in \(\mathbb F_2^{h\times m}\). On \(m\) distinct label coordinates let \(z_i\) be the indicator of one distinguished label, and consider the complete affine block with inputs \((z_1,\ldots,z_m)\). Its first actual error under these scalar rows is \[E_C(z)=z_1\prod_{u=1}^h(1+(Cz)_u),\qquad \deg E_C\le h+1.\tag{5}\] It is a function on the whole label cube, with every binary \(z\)-pattern attainable.

Proposition. With probability at least \[1-(2^h-1)e^{-m/16},\tag{6}\] every categorical CNF representing (5) has a clause of width at least \(d=\lceil m/4\rceil-1\). On this event the error is nonzero, and \(C\) has rank \(h\). This width cost does not imply hardness of the block: if the selected \(z_i\)'s belong to a weighted-free old cube at weight one, one nonzero row of \(C\) gives a surviving degree-one weight killing every companion.

Distance event. For fixed nonzero \(u\in\mathbb F_2^h\), \(uC\) is uniform in \(\mathbb F_2^m\). Its weight \(X\) is binomial. Exponential Markov with parameter \(\log2\), and \((1+e^{-s})/2\le\exp((e^{-s}-1)/2)\), gives \[\Pr[X\le m/4]\le\exp\bigl(m(\log2-1)/4\bigr)\le e^{-m/16},\] using \(\log2<3/4\). A union bound proves that every nonzero row combination has weight at least \(m/4\) with probability (6). On that event no such combination is zero or the first unit vector, so \(C\) has rank \(h\) and \(e_1\notin\operatorname{rowspan}C\).

Project the actual support. The support of (5) in binary indicator coordinates is the nonempty affine flat \[S=\{z:Cz=0,\ z_1=1\}.\] Its affine equations have linear parts \(\operatorname{rowspan}(C,e_1)\). Every vector in this row space other than zero or \(e_1\) has weight at least \(m/4-1\), hence at least \(d\). For any set \(T\) of fewer than \(d\) coordinates, linear elimination therefore shows that the projection of \(S\) to \(T\) is the full cube if \(1\notin T\), and just the condition \(z_1=1\) if \(1\in T\). To justify the elimination assertion, any linear equation on a projected affine flat pulls back to an equation on \(S\) supported on \(T\), and all equations on \(S\) are constant combinations of its defining independent equations. There are no others with that support.

A categorical clause of width less than \(d\) which is true on \(S\) can thus forbid only a label pattern already incompatible with \(z_1=1\). A prescribed label pattern fixes its binary indicator pattern, and any compatible binary extension can be realized by labels, so the same projection conclusion holds for categorical clauses. Consequently every such narrow clause is true on all label assignments with \(z_1=1\). Their conjunction cannot equal (5): \(C\) has a nonzero row independent of \(e_1\), so some point with \(z_1=1\) has \(Cz\ne0\). This proves the width lower bound. Additional unused label coordinates do not help: fix them arbitrarily in a putative CNF representation, which can only reduce its clause widths.

Actual hard-quotient control. The full weighted cube proof supplies \(m\le D-1\) free indicators in a proper old quotient containing the unsatisfiable base, at \(D\) above its old width. Distinct selected indicators lie on distinct edges, since same-edge exclusion would contradict their independent weighted monomials. Any nonzero row \(L=(Cz)_1\) then survives. Put \(W=L\); the factor \(L(1+L)\) makes every weighted companion Boolean-zero through degree \(h+2\). Coefficient fields vanish; old inputs and original proof replay fit whenever \(1+\max(u,2h+1)\le D\). Thus a cheap weight is available despite the large error-CNF width. No global satisfying-point interpretation of the charged quotient is used.

At the growing-family parameters, one can take \(m=\Theta(D)\) and \(h=o(m)\). The lower bound \(d=\Theta(m)\) is far beyond the width allowance \(O(n^\beta)\) in (4), and the distance event has probability tending to one. The error polynomial still has degree at most \(h+1\) and its scalar source is legal. This refutes the hoped-for uniform width premise even for simple sources; it does not refute optimized coefficients, a suitable weighted map or any Frege lower bound.

Every companion is its own indicator times the conjunction of these pair equalities. Its categorical CNF has width at most two, even for inputs outside the paired coordinates. Distinct edge labels make the indicator bits independent with bias \(1/3\). A pair agrees with probability \(5/9\). For an input in a pair its error mean is \((1/9)(5/9)^{h-1}\); for an outside input it is \((1/3)(5/9)^h\). Both are at most \((5/9)^h\le2^{-h/2}\). These are the recorded paired errors, with the unused coordinate companions explicitly retained.

For this point-mass sampler, take \(\epsilon=2^{-h/2}\), \(T=1\), \(k=2\), \(a\le1/9\), and \(t=\lfloor h/(4\log_2 3)\rfloor\). Equation (3) applies when \(\eta+M(2^{-h/4}+2^{-h/2})<1\). Scalar error width obstruction

Corollary. Let \(V\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m\) be a linear code of dimension \(r>h\ge1\) and minimum nonzero weight at least \(d\ge1\). Use a basis of \(V\) as coefficient vectors of the input linear forms \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\) on distinguished-label indicators \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\) of distinct coordinates, over any \(q\ge2\). For every \(h\)-row binary scalar assignment to this block, some nonzero companion function has categorical CNF width at least \(d\). Consequently no distribution on these scalar assignments makes all its errors width less than \(d\) on every map in its support. This is a pre-restriction, unweighted error-width statement; polynomial coefficient maps, restriction-dependent choices and surviving weights are not excluded.

Proof. Its selected row forms span a subspace \(R\subseteq V\) of dimension at most \(h

Unbounded code and paid repair. For \(m\ge64\), \(1\le h\le\lfloor m/64\rfloor\), take \(r=\lfloor m/32\rfloor>h\). Apply the preceding random-row distance calculation to an \(r\)-by-\(m\) generator matrix. Its failure probability is at most \((2^r-1)e^{-m/16}<1\), so a rank-\(r\) code with \(d=\lceil m/4\rceil\) exists; fix one. This gives an unbounded family of source-admissible affine tuples, of polynomial description length in \(m,h\). Embed the \(z_i\)'s in the free old cube as above. Selecting \(g_1\) in one row and zero-padding makes \(W=g_1\) a surviving degree-one weight. Each weighted companion contains \(g_1(1+g_1)\) and has a Boolean certificate through degree three. Original replay still costs \(1+B\le D\), \(B\ge\max(u,2h+1)\). Thus the all-scalar width obstruction does not make this source hard for weighted removal.

Degenerate cases for the corollary. If \(r\le h\), a scalar row basis can make every companion zero, so that hypothesis is essential. A zero row assignment is covered: some nonzero input itself has the claimed projection-based width. For \(d=1\), the width lower bound says only that the nonconstant support needs a nonempty clause. A code of dimension zero and a non-surviving embedding do not meet the relevant hypotheses.
