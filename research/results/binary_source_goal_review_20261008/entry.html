Compression can create a large commutator

Proposition (compressed character commutator). In the charged character quotient, assume additionally that the graph has edge connectivity at least four. Write \(m=|E|\). The degree-one space \(Q_1\) has basis \(1,U_e,V_e\). Let \(P:Q_2\to Q_1\) retain these cut-code coordinates and kill the others. For \(g=\sum_e x_{e,0}\), \(h=\sum_e x_{e,1}\), set \(T_g=P M_g|_{Q_1}\), \(T_h=P M_h|_{Q_1}\), using the affine Fourier map. Their commutator has rank \(2m\) for even \(m\) and \(2m-2\) for odd \(m\), over both the descended binary space and its scalar extension.

Proof. A nonzero cut-code vector cannot have support at most three: a nonconstant vertex potential has a nonempty proper level set whose boundary is contained in that support, contradicting edge connectivity. Thus the displayed degree-one characters have distinct coordinates, and every product on two different edges has a coordinate outside \(Q_1\). This proves that \(P\) is well defined by the class dictionary. Products on a single edge obey \(U_e^2=V_e\), \(V_e^2=U_e\), \(U_eV_e=1\); all used products have degree at most two, inside the recorded window.

In \(F_4\), discard the scalar \(mI\) from both operators. The remaining multipliers are \(\sum(U_e+V_e)\) and \(\sum(\omega^2U_e+\omega V_e)\). The compressed actions are \[ T'_g1=\sum_e(U_e+V_e),\quad T'_gU_e=1+V_e,\quad T'_gV_e=1+U_e, \] \[ T'_h1=\sum_e(\omega^2U_e+\omega V_e),\quad T'_hU_e=\omega+\omega^2V_e,\quad T'_hV_e=\omega^2+\omega U_e. \] Using \(\omega+\omega^2=1\), the commutator kills one and maps \(U_e\) to \(\sum_{f\ne e}U_f\), and \(V_e\) to \(\sum_{f\ne e}V_f\). Each block is the all-ones matrix plus the identity. Its kernel is zero when \(m\) is even and the span of the all-ones vector when \(m\) is odd. This gives the ranks. The involution conjugating coefficients and exchanging \(U,V\) preserves the retained coordinates and their complement. Both original multipliers are binary, so the projection and operators descend under binary descent, with the same ranks. \(\square\)

The normalized ranks tend to one, not zero. The hypotheses are nonvacuous: for \(K_n\), take \(\Delta=n-1\), \(\varepsilon=\lceil n/2\rceil\), and \(D=2(n-1)\) for sufficiently large \(n\). This is not an affordable bounded-degree encoding for the goal. The argument also applies to any bounded-degree family meeting its hypotheses. It is a degree-one compression countercontrol, not a theorem about every cutoff, every projection, or actual optimized error ranks. Nonzero total charge and the older congruence on \(n\) are unnecessary here; the full lower cutoff is inherited for the encoding interface, while the calculation uses degree two. Buffered multiplication into larger degree spaces still commutes where its products fit.

Other transfer controls

Corollary (generic encoding cutoff). Let \(0\le\ell\le m\), \(1\le d\le m\), and let the disjoint nonempty \(d\)-set families in Razborov's Definition 4.4 be \(\ell\)-generic, with \(M>n\ge1\). His Theorem 4.5 certifies refutation degree strictly greater than \(B=\min(\ell+1,d/2)(n-1)+d\), for \(mn\) Boolean variables. Then \[B/(mn)\le 1/3+1/(3m)+1/n.\] Proof. Genericity supplies a \(d\)-set disjoint from each \(\ell\)-set, so \(d+\ell\le m\). For \(a=\min(\ell+1,d/2)\), \(2a\le d\), \(a-1\le\ell\), hence \(3a\le m+1\). Divide \(B\le an+d\) by \(mn\), using \(d\le m\). Disjoint nonempty families imply \(M\le\binom md\le2^m\); thus \(m\to\infty\) when \(n\to\infty\). The certified fraction is at most \(1/3+o(1)\). This bounds the theorem's certificate, not the actual refutation degree. Uniqueness in the genericity definition is unused by this numerical inequality; it remains part of the imported degree theorem. \(\square\)

Lemma (localization support cost). In the Boolean function algebra over any field, a nonzero \(w\) satisfying \(x_iw=w\) for \(i=1,\ldots,q\) has multilinear degree at least \(q\). Indeed it is supported where these coordinates are all one, hence its unique multilinear representative is \((\prod_{i=1}^q x_i)v\), with \(v\ne0\) in the other coordinates. Thus degree equals \(q+\deg v\). Rational cancellation does not avoid the cost of supporting a common nonzero weight on those denominators. Already \(x(1/x)=1\) in a fraction field does not put one in the polynomial ideal \((x)\).

Example (star marginal degree). For any \(m\ge1\), over \(F_4\) let a binary hidden variable \(z\) have weights \(1+\omega,\omega\), whose sum is one, and factors \(1+zx_i\). Exact elimination of \(z\) gives \[(1+\omega)+\omega\prod_{i=1}^m(1+x_i)=1+\omega\bigl(\prod_i(1+x_i)-1\bigr). \] Its multilinear old-variable degree is exactly \(m\), since the coefficient of \(\prod_i x_i\) is \(\omega\ne0\). All factors have degree two and the factor graph is a star. Efficient exact contraction therefore does not by itself control old polynomial degree. These are algebraic weights, not probabilities.

Lemma (prime-field deformation). If \(\beta\in F_p((t))\) obeys the legal coefficient equation \(\beta^p-\beta=0\), then \(\beta\in F_p\). The polynomial factors as \(\prod_{a\in F_p}(X-a)\), and a field has no zero divisors. Thus no nonconstant Laurent-series deformation of a legal scalar coefficient preserves that domain. This does not forbid using valuations as an external analysis tool. Separately, in characteristic two, \((1+x)+(1+x)=0\) shows why positive-coefficient support propagation cannot be applied without a cancellation check.
