1. Degree-explicit Fourier transfer

Let \(G\) be a simple graph of maximum degree \(\Delta\ge1\), orient each edge once, and put \(F=\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2(\omega)\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). At an edge use three Boolean indicators \(x_{e,a}\), \(a\in\mathbb F_3\), with row sum one, pair exclusions and Booleanity. At a vertex \(v\), with \(\sigma_{ue}=+1,\sigma_{ve}=-1\) for an edge oriented \(u\to v\), include the forbidden-tuple monomial \(E_a=\prod_{e\ni v}x_{e,a_e}\) whenever \(\sum\sigma_{ve}a_e\ne c_v\) modulo three. This is the one-hot Tseitin polynomial encoding. The associated CNF uses exactly-one clauses and these forbidden-tuple clauses. Directed reverse indicators can first be eliminated by the affine identification \(x_{\bar e,a}=x_{e,-a}\).

Introduce two variables per unoriented edge and maps \[U_e=\sum_a\omega^a x_{e,a},\quad V_e=\sum_a\omega^{-a}x_{e,a},\qquad x_{e,a}=\ell_{e,a}:=1+\omega^{-a}U_e+\omega^{-2a}V_e.\] The Fourier system consists of \(U_e^2+V_e,V_e^2+U_e,U_eV_e+1\), and \[B_v=\prod_{\sigma_{ve}=1}U_e\prod_{\sigma_{ve}=-1}V_e+\omega^{c_v}.\] Lemma (ternary Fourier transfer). A degree-\(D\) PC refutation of either polynomial encoding over \(F\) transfers to the other through degree \(\max\{D,2\Delta,2\}\). Replacing the quadratic local domain by \(U_e^3+1,V_e^3+1,U_eV_e+1\) costs at most \(\max\{D,4\}\). Using the indicated CNF instead of row sums adds at most degree three. None of these maxima compounds over edges or vertices.

Proof. The two affine maps compose to the identity on \(U,V\); on \(x_a\), their composition is \(x_a+1+\sum_bx_b\). Thus it is the identity modulo the row equation, through degree one. The Fourier quadratics map to degree-two consequences of Booleanity, exclusions and the row equation. Conversely the local relations reduce every monomial to \(1,U,V\), without increasing degree: replace \(U^2\) by \(V\), \(V^2\) by \(U\), and \(UV\) by one. These three functions are independent at \((U,V)=(\omega^a,\omega^{-a})\), \(a=0,1,2\), by the invertible three-point Fourier matrix. Hence a vanishing polynomial has a local-domain certificate through its own degree. In particular the images of Booleanity and pair exclusions have certificates through two; the row image is identically zero.

For a vertex of degree \(b\), expanding its Fourier product in one-hot coordinates gives \[B_v=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_3^b}(\omega^{\sum\sigma a}-\omega^{c_v})E_a \quad\bmod\text{row equations},\] through degree \(b\): the only difference is a multiple of \(\prod_{e\ni v}\sum_a x_{e,a}-1\), telescoped through the row equations. The satisfying tuples have coefficient zero. In the other direction, set \(L_a=\prod_e\ell_{e,a_e}\). On each edge, \(U\ell_a=\omega^a\ell_a\) and \(V\ell_a=\omega^{-a}\ell_a\) modulo the quadratic domain, with degree-two certificates. Telescoping over the \(b\) edges gives \[B_vL_a=(\omega^{\sum\sigma a}-\omega^{c_v})L_a\quad\bmod\text{local domain}\] through degree \(2b\). The scalar is nonzero for a forbidden tuple, so its indicator follows from \(B_v\) and the domain through \(2b\). Affine substitution does not increase degree. To replay a source multiplication by a variable mapped to \(c+\sum_jc_jy_j\), derive each \(y_j\phi(p)\) and combine with \(c\phi(p)\). For a nonzero source multiplication, \(\deg p\le D-1\), so all these intermediate lines fit \(D\). This proves the first maximum.

For the cubic comparison, \[U^2(UV+1)+V(U^3+1)=U^2+V,\qquad V^2(UV+1)+U(V^3+1)=V^2+U.\] The reverse cubic identity is \(U(U^2+V)+(UV+1)=U^3+1\), and likewise for \(V\). Finally, the positive exactly-one CNF clause expands to \(1+\sum_a x_a\) modulo pair exclusions through three; conversely this expansion derives the clause from the row equation. All assertions are literal polynomial identities or bounded-degree consequences, not merely evaluations. \(\square\)

This is a degree audit of the familiar Fourier representation in Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi, Sections 2–3, not a claim to a new encoding. Both reduction directions are supplied explicitly rather than inferred from the direction convention of its Lemma 2. The existing row-state normal form is the same local interpolation mechanism; the proof above also works over the extension field. For fixed \(\Delta\), variables, clauses and encoding length are all \(\Theta(|V(G)|)\) on regular graphs.

2. A containing space is enough, if used consistently

Fix \(n\equiv1\pmod3\), all charges one, and edge expansion \(|\delta S|\ge\varepsilon|S|\) for \(0<|S|\le n/2\), with \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \[\max\{4,2\Delta\}\le D<\varepsilon n/8.\] For each \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_3^{E(G)}\), let \(m_\alpha\) use \(U_e\) when \(\alpha_e=1\), \(V_e\) when \(\alpha_e=-1\), and neither otherwise. Its degree is \(|\operatorname{supp}\alpha|\). Local reduction gives precisely this filtered basis.

Let \((\partial s)_e=s_u-s_v\) on an edge oriented from \(u\) to \(v\). Among terms \(\omega^a m_\alpha\) of degree at most \(D\), declare \[\omega^a m_\alpha\sim\omega^b m_\beta \iff \alpha-\beta=\partial s,\quad |\operatorname{supp}s|<n/2,\quad \sum_v s_v=b-a\pmod3.\] Theorem (ternary character pseudo-ideal). This relation defines a quotient map \(R_D\) with one \(F\)-basis vector per monomial class, with the specified relative phases. Its kernel, pulled back by local reduction to ordinary degree-\(D\) polynomials, is a degree-\(D\) pseudo-ideal \(J_D\): it is linear and closed under multiplication by a variable on inputs of degree at most \(D-1\). It contains all Fourier-system axioms, excludes one, and therefore contains their degree-\(D\) PC consequences without containing a refutation. Equality with the actual consequence space is not asserted.

Proof. A witness \(s\) to a relation has \(|\operatorname{supp}\partial s|\le2D\). Every edge leaving its support contributes a nonzero boundary coordinate, so expansion gives \(|\operatorname{supp}s|\le2D/\varepsilon<n/4\). Reflexivity and symmetry are immediate. For transitivity, the sum of two witnesses has support less than \(n/2\) and the required boundary and charge, hence is another witness. A relation between two scalar multiples of the same monomial has \(\partial s=0\). Expansion implies the graph is connected, so \(s\) is constant; its support condition forces \(s=0\). Thus distinct phases of a monomial are not identified, and choosing one representative in each class defines the claimed nonzero quotient basis. In particular \(R_D(1)\ne0\).

A polynomial in the kernel can be expressed as a sum of phase-adjusted differences using only monomials already in its support: in each class choose one monomial occurring in that polynomial and cancel all the others against it. The remaining coefficient is zero by the kernel condition. If the polynomial has degree at most \(D-1\), all these differences have that degree bound. Multiplying a difference by \(U_e\) or \(V_e\), then locally reducing, leaves the exponent difference \(\partial s\) and its phase unchanged; both resulting terms have degree at most \(D\). Hence its image is zero. Local reduction commutes with multiplication as a map of the local-domain algebra, proving pseudo-ideal closure also for ordinary polynomials. Domain axioms reduce to zero. Each vertex axiom is a relation with witness the unit vector at that vertex, so it lies in the kernel. Induction on PC lines gives containment. \(\square\)

The congruence \(n\equiv1\pmod3\) ensures global inconsistency for the selected all-one-charge family; it is not needed for the local pseudo-ideal construction. The lower bound \(D\ge4\) retains the optional cubic comparison in the common window. This reconstructs the Laurent-relation mechanism of the same source, Sections 5–7, using edge-support degree and the explicit quadratic domain. It is not an assertion that a full ideal quotient is nonzero: the unrestricted Tseitin ideal is the unit ideal. Here \(D\) is essential. Pulling \(J_D\) back along the affine indicator map gives a pseudo-ideal containing the one-hot axioms, since their certificates fit \(D\). A short proof over \(\mathbb F_2\) is also one over \(F\), so the resulting old degree barrier applies in characteristic two. This is an old PC barrier, not the target Frege bound.

3. Relative errors can be measured here

Lemma (pseudo-ideal error kernel). Let \(J\subseteq F[y]_{\le D}\) be any degree-\(D\) pseudo-ideal, \(w\notin J\), \(\deg w\le b\), and let \(V_K=F[y]_{\le K}\). For finitely many tuples \((a_{\nu,i})_i\), suppose \(\deg a_{\nu,i}\le e\) and \(b+K+e\le D\). Write \[h=\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_K\mapsto wf\bmod J),\qquad E_\nu=\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_K\mapsto(wa_{\nu,i}f\bmod J)_i).\] If \(\sum_\nu E_\nu<h\), there is \(f\in V_K\) with \(wf\notin J\) and all \(wa_{\nu,i}f\in J\). No derivations from a smaller space \(C_D\subseteq J\) are required for this conclusion.

Proof. Successive variable multiplication shows that \(q\in J\) implies \(rq\in J\) whenever \(\deg r+\deg q\le D\). Consequently every error map kills the kernel of the weight map. On its quotient, of dimension \(h>0\), the stacked error map has rank at most \(\sum E_\nu<h\). Choose a nonzero kernel class. \(\square\)

For a complete ordinary NS identity \(1=\sum_j A_jF_j\) and a substitution \(\phi\), the same pseudo-ideal supplies a contradiction if \(W\notin J\), every \(W\phi(F_j)\in J\), and every original cofactor cost obeys \(\deg W+\deg\phi(A_j)+\deg\phi(F_j)\le D\). Absorb each cofactor inside \(J\) and sum to get \(W\in J\). This recovers the design-dependent form of colon multipliers and the common-kernel step of weighted approximation in one setting. It does not import Pang's prime-field contraction theorem into arbitrary extension-field matrices, and it does not supply the NS identity or a low-cost map for every Frege proof.

For the constructed space, use columns indexed by old monomials of degree at most \(K\), and replace each product by \(R_D(wm)\) or \((R_D(wP_\nu g_{\nu,i}m))_i\). Their matrix ranks are exactly \(h\) and \(E_\nu\). All ranks here are over \(F\), consistently. The construction therefore makes the relative test concrete while leaving its quantitative estimate open.
