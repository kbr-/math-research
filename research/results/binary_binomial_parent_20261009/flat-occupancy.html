Count the input quotient, not an output moment space

Let \(\mathbb K\) be any field, \(m\ge1\), and let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing \[ x_e^2-x_e,\quad y_e^2-y_e,\quad x_ey_e\qquad(e\in[m]). \] Thus each local coordinate has states \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\). Write \(V_K=\mathbb K[x,y]_{\le K}\), and define \[ B_m(K)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(K,m)}2^j\binom mj,\qquad B_{m,r}(K)=\sum_{j=0}^K[z^j]\, (1+2z)^{(m-r)_+}(1+z)^{\min(r,\,2m-r)} \tag{1} \] for \(0\le r\le2m\). These are integer dimension counts, not field-valued coefficients.

Lemma (three-state flat image). Let \(K\ge1\), let \(F_1,\ldots,F_q\) have degrees at most \(a\), and assume \(a+K\le D\). Let \(L\) be a space of affine polynomials such that \(\ell F_i\in J_D\) for every \(\ell\in L\) and every \(i\). If \(1\in L\), the tuple multiplication map is zero. Otherwise, with \(r=\dim L\), its rank satisfies \[ \operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\in V_K\mapsto([F_if])_i\bigr) \le \dim\frac{V_K}{(J_D\cap V_K)+L V_{K-1}} \le B_{m,r}(K). \tag{2} \] If \(F_i=wA_i\) are literal polynomial products, the map \([wf]\mapsto([A_iwf])_i\) on the weighted domain has the sharper bound \[ \operatorname{rank}\le \dim\frac{[wV_K]}{[wL V_{K-1}]}. \tag{3} \] Degree additivity for nonzero literal products supplies the absorption ceiling from \(a+K\le D\). If supplied charges or reduced representatives are used instead, require explicitly \(b+K+\max_i\deg A_i\le D\), where \(\deg w\le b\). All products in these formulas are within the supplied degree ceiling. For \(1\le K\le m/3\), \[ B_{m,r}(K)\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\min(r,m)K}{2m}\right) B_m(K). \tag{4} \]

Proof: the factorization. If \(1\in L\), each \(F_i\in J_D\), so its allowed multiples vanish. In the proper case, \(J_D\cap V_K\) is killed by multiplication by \(F_i\). Each \(\ell u\), \(\deg u\le K-1\), is also killed: reuse \(\ell F_i\in J_D\) and multiply by \(u\), through degree at most \(a+1+K-1\le D\). This proves the first inequality. For (3), the actual map factors through \([wV_K]\), and kills its subspace \([wL V_{K-1}]\), by the same argument. No pairing, invariant subspace or additional \(2K\) reserve is used.

Proof: the local count. Since \(1\notin L\), its \(r\) linear parts are independent. Gaussian elimination expresses \(r\) pivot variables affinely in the remaining \(2m-r\) variables. Substituting them in any polynomial through degree \(K\) preserves degree; its difference from the original is a sum of affine generators with cofactors through \(K-1\). Among the \(m\) pairs, let \(p_j\) have exactly \(j\) pivot variables. The untouched pairs retain their Boolean and exclusion reductions, and a pair with one free variable retains Boolean reduction. Ignoring other constraints only enlarges the quotient. It is therefore spanned by the monomials counted by \[ \sum_{d\le K}[z^d](1+2z)^{p_0}(1+z)^{p_1}, \qquad p_1+2p_2=r. \] Replacing one untouched pair and one double-pivot pair by two single-pivot pairs increases this polynomial coefficientwise, because \((1+z)^2-(1+2z)=z^2\). Repeat until either \(p_0=0\) or \(p_2=0\). This gives (1), proving (2). It refines the ordinary flat restriction count by keeping the local pair exclusions.

Proof of (4). First suppose \(r\le m\). Let \(a_j\) be the coefficient of \(z^j\) in the corresponding product of \(m\) factors \(1+\lambda_i z\), with \(r\) values \(\lambda_i=1\) and the others two. Counting extensions gives \(j a_j\ge(m-j+1)a_{j-1}\), so \(K\le m/3\) implies \(\sum_{j\le K}a_j\le2a_K\). Also \[ \frac{a_K}{2^K\binom mK}\le \left(1-\frac{r}{2m}\right)^K. \] Indeed the left numerator is an elementary symmetric sum of weights \(1/2\) or one; at fixed sum, pairwise averaging of two nonnegative weights increases each elementary symmetric sum, so its maximum is at equal weights. Combining this with \(B_m(K)\ge2^K\binom mK\) gives (4). For \(r>m\), \(B_{m,r}(K)\le B_{m,m}(K)\), giving the stated bound with \(\min(r,m)\). \(\square\)

The bound is uniform in the number \(q\) of tuple components. Coordinate flats in the free three-state algebra attain the count: set one coordinate to zero in distinct pairs until \(r=m\), then set second coordinates to zero. Multiplication by their zero-set indicator has image equal to the polynomial restrictions on that Cartesian state set. This is a dimension calibration, not an unsatisfiable-family lower bound.

Apply the factorization to the actual parent tuple

Now work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(w\) have degree at most \(b\), with \(H_w=\dim[wV_K]>0\). Fix coherent old Boolean feature polynomials \(P_c\), of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), and old affine spaces \(L_c\) with \[ wP_c\ell\in J_D\quad(\ell\in L_c). \] Actual affine-bottom products satisfying their weighted companions provide such features. Parent \(a\) has inputs \(g_{a,i}=c_{a,i}+\sum_{c\in C_a}\alpha_{a,i,c}P_c\), a fixed prefix \(Q_a\) of degree at most \(p_0\), and \(h\ge1\) remaining legal binary coefficient rows. Assume \[ b+p_0+K+(h+2)\delta\le D. \tag{6} \] For \(S\subseteq C_a\), put \(L_S=\sum_{c\in S}L_c\). Define its contribution as zero if \(1\in L_S\), and otherwise as \(B_{m,\dim L_S}(K)\). Let \(Z_a\) be the sum of these contributions over \(|S|\le h\), including \(S=\varnothing\).

Proof. That existing theorem bounds the expected rank by \(2^{-h}d_a\), where \(d_a\) is the tuple-image dimension on the coefficient-independent domain generated by at most \(h\) input words times \(V_K\). Expand those words using the displayed feature expressions. Repeated features can be replaced by single copies inside the tuple image: each binary polynomial satisfies \(P_c^2-P_c\) by old Boolean reduction through \(2\delta\), and (6) covers the weighted products. Thus the image lies in the sum, over \(|S|\le h\), of the tuple images \[ f\longmapsto([wQ_ag_{a,i}P_S f])_i,\qquad P_S=\prod_{c\in S}P_c. \] Every component of this tuple is annihilated by \(L_S\). To verify it, write a form as a sum of forms from the \(L_c\)'s and multiply the corresponding given \(wP_c\ell\) relation by the other features, \(Q_a\) and \(g_{a,i}\). The degree is at most \(b+p_0+(|S|+1)\delta+1\), and all subsequent degree-\(K-1\) multiples fit (6). Unit spans give zero tuples. Otherwise (2) bounds the whole tuple image by \(B_{m,\dim L_S}(K)\), without a factor for its number of coordinates. Rank subadditivity proves \(d_a\le Z_a\), hence (7). Choose the parent row matrices jointly and independently; an outcome with sum of ranks below \(H_w\) has a surviving common kernel. It supplies the same rows at every occurrence. \(\square\)

Old-affine parent parts can be included as additional Boolean features \(P_c=\ell_c\) with \(L_c=\operatorname{span}(1-\ell_c)\), since \(\ell_c(1-\ell_c)\) is a Boolean consequence. This makes (7) applicable to the stated affine-plus-bottom grammar, but many such rank-one features can make its profile bound poor. When the numerical profile is useful, and when it is not

For example suppose every proper feature union of size \(j\le h\) has rank at least \(r_0j\), with \(r_0h\le m\), \(K\le m/3\), and each parent uses at most \(M\) features. Put \(z=\exp(-r_0K/(2m))\). Equation (4) gives \[ Z_a\le B_m(K)\bigl[1+2\sum_{j=1}^h\binom Mjz^j\bigr] \le 2B_m(K)e^{Mz}. \] If \(H_w\ge\kappa B_m(K)\) and there are \(N_{\rm par}\) parents, it suffices that \(2^{1-h}N_{\rm par}e^{Mz}<\kappa\), together with (6) and the full replay ledger. These inequalities have nonempty parameter ranges; (5) supplies an actual unweighted charged denominator in its explicit window. A prescribed later weight still needs its own rank-retention evidence.

This differs from the flat-indicator replacement, which pays the union rank in the polynomial certificate degree. Here only existing annihilation is used. The stratum theorem instead constructs a complete map using a bound on all relevant low-rank spans and boundary joins. The present profile only uses feature sets through \(h\), but has its own denominator and profile-sum requirements. No general dominance or new unrestricted endpoint is asserted.

The normalization warning has a sharp charged control. For a nonbridge edge use \(w=x_{e,0}\), and \(L=\operatorname{span}(x_{e,1},x_{e,2})\). These two affine directions annihilate \(w\), but the tuple with sole component \(F=w\) is the identity map on \([wV_K]\). It has rank \(H_w\), not a strict fraction of it. The conjunction rank theorem makes this same-quotient statement explicit. Formula (3) correctly sees \([wL V_{K-1}]=0\). Counting annihilators already imposed by the weight cannot create a gain.
