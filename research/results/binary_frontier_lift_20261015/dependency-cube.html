A weighted restriction cube

The near-half thread proved the truncated-kernel dimension theorem at pinned commit caf21f9d4afe3086fc6039d809abda99f3ffa7e8. Its complete source record is preserved here with attribution and its original verification gate. The theorem applies over every field: a proper Boolean degree-\(R\) subspace closed under all legal variable multiplications and containing an unsatisfiable active input has a free coordinate set of size \(R-1\). Its monomials give the degree-layer binomial lower bounds. No closure under unbudgeted outside multiplication is asserted.

For this corollary, write \(B=F[x]/(x_i^2-x_i)\), identify the Boolean pseudoideal with its multilinear image in \(B_{\le D}\), and use multilinear representatives of \(\Gamma,w\). Ordinary PC proof degrees and all unreduced product ceilings in the replay are still charged. Define \(h_w(k)\) as the dimension of the image of \(f\mapsto\operatorname{ML}(wf)\) from \(B_{\le k}\) into \(B_{\le D}/J_D\).

Corollary (weighted restriction cube). Over any field \(F\), let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) Boolean pseudoideal containing all members of an unsatisfiable old family \(\Gamma\). Let \(w\notin J_D\), \(\deg w\le b\), and put \(R=D-b\). Assume \[ R\ge u:=\max(2,\max_{g\in\Gamma}\deg g). \] Then there is \(S\subseteq[n]\), \(|S|=R-1\), such that all \(2^{R-1}\) old monomials on \(S\) have linearly independent weighted images modulo \(J_D\). For \(0\le k\le R-1\), \[ h_w(k)\ge\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{R-1}{j}. \tag{1} \] For every \(A\subseteq S\), \(|A|=t\), and every binary pin \(\alpha\in\{0,1\}^A\), the weight \[ w\chi_{A,\alpha},\qquad \chi_{A,\alpha}=\prod_{\alpha_i=1}x_i\prod_{\alpha_i=0}(1-x_i), \] survives. If \(t\le R-u\), the restricted old family \(\Gamma|_{A=\alpha}\) has no ordinary PC refutation through degree \(R-t\). The selected set \(S\) may depend on \(w,J_D\); no uniform random coordinate set or source simplification is asserted.

Proof: weighted kernel and free coordinates. Work in the Boolean ring \(B=F[x]/(x_i^2-x_i)\), with multilinear representatives. Define \[ L_R=\{f\in B_{\le R}:\operatorname{ML}(wf)\in J_D\}. \] It is proper because \(w\) survives. If \(f\in L_R\cap B_{\le R-1}\), multiplication by a variable and Boolean reduction fit \(b+R\le D\); hence \(x_if\in L_R\). Every input \(g\in\Gamma\) lies in \(L_R\), by multiplying its old membership by \(w\) within \(b+\deg g\le D\). Thus the source-owned theorem applies.

For completeness, its mechanism is short. If a proper quotient containing an unsatisfiable family were spanned through degree \(R-2\), low-degree representatives would define commuting idempotent variable operators: comparing two successive multiplications uses only predecessors through \(R-1\). Acting on the nonzero class of one, their joint Boolean projectors would produce a satisfying assignment of the input, a contradiction. A degree-compatible standard-monomial basis therefore has a monomial of degree at least \(R-1\). Legal closure makes that basis closed under divisors. A degree-\(R-1\) divisor and all of its divisors give the claimed free coordinate set and (1). The complete operator and leading-term argument is in the preserved source proof; this is its weighted application, not new ownership of that theorem.

Proof: every pin and its degree cost. Each \(\chi_{A,\alpha}\) is a nonzero polynomial on \(S\), so free-coordinate injectivity gives \(w\chi_{A,\alpha}\notin J_D\). Suppose the restricted family had a degree-\(B\) refutation, where \(u\le B\le R-t\). Multiply every line by \(w\chi_{A,\alpha}\) and view it in the old variables. For each original \(g\), \(\chi_{A,\alpha}(g-g|_{A=\alpha})\) vanishes on the old Boolean cube and has a Boolean certificate through \(t+\deg g\). Its weighted certificate fits \(b+t+u\le D\). Thus the weighted restricted input belongs to \(J_D\); the original weighted \(g\) has the same bound. Restricted Boolean equations and every inference fit \(b+t+B\le D\). The final line would put \(w\chi_{A,\alpha}\) in \(J_D\), a contradiction. Taking \(B=R-t\) proves the assertion. Ordinary NS refutations in that degree range are excluded by their same-degree PC simulation. \(\square\)
