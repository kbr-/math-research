The subfield-offset ceiling

Corollary (subfield offset ceiling). Let \(E\) be a finite field of characteristic two and \(K=\mathbb F_{2^k}\subsetneq E\), with \(k\ge1\). Let \(\beta\in E\setminus K\) and let \(g\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) be multilinear of degree at most \(r\), where \(r\ge1\). The system consisting of \(g-\beta=0\) and all Boolean equations \(x_i^2-x_i=0\) has an ordinary PC refutation over \(E\) through degree \(2r\). No bound on \(k\), the number of monomials, or \([E:K]\) is required.

Proof. Write \(g=\sum_S a_Sx_S\), including a possible constant term, and define coefficientwise Frobenius conjugates \[ f_j=\sum_S a_S^{2^j}x_S-\beta^{2^j},\qquad 0\le j\le k. \] Each has degree at most \(r\), and \(f_0\) is an input. From a derived final line \(f_j\), multiply separately by its monomials and take their scalar combination to derive \(f_j^2\). These ordinary PC steps have degree at most \(2r\); the earlier derivation of \(f_j\) is reused without multiplying its old lines. In characteristic two, \[ f_j^2-f_{j+1}=\sum_S a_S^{2^{j+1}}(x_S^2-x_S). \] For \(S=\{i_1,\ldots,i_t\}\), the literal identity \[ x_S^2-x_S=\sum_{h=1}^t(x_{i_h}^2-x_{i_h})\prod_{a<h}x_{i_a}^2\prod_{a>h}x_{i_a} \] has every summand of degree at most \(2t\le2r\). Thus the Boolean axioms derive the displayed difference within the same ceiling, and subtraction derives \(f_{j+1}\). Repeating does not add degree costs. Since all \(a_S\in K\), subtracting \(f_0\) from \(f_k\) gives the constant \(\beta-\beta^{2^k}\). It is nonzero: the roots of \(T^{2^k}-T\) in \(E\) are exactly the \(2^k\) elements of \(K\), by the polynomial root bound. Scalar division yields one. Constant \(g\) already gives a nonzero constant input; this also satisfies the bound. \(\square\)

This is the existing bounded Frobenius argument with multiplier one. Alternatively expand the coefficients in an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis of their span together with \(\beta\); the companion lemma recovers the constant coordinate. Its hypotheses allow an extension-valued input system, so the argument does not require the input itself to be defined over \(\mathbb F_2\). The proof above supplies the complete specialization and its ordinary-degree ledger. Finiteness of \(E\) is retained to match that lemma and the tested source, although the direct argument needs only the finite subfield; strictness follows from the outside-target assumption.

Application and encoding boundary

The source's affine instance has PC degree at most two; its degree-five instance has PC degree at most ten over its stated extension field. Both bounds hold for every coefficient choice in the source's subfield regime, even when the inverse polynomial has maximal degree. Increasing the coefficient-field size cannot change these upper bounds. Hence neither instance provides a PC ceiling growing near \(v/2\).

These are extension-coefficient systems, not yet CNFs over \(\mathbb F_2\). There is no automatic scalar descent for arbitrary extension-valued input axioms. The direct coordinate encoding is even easier: choose an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis of \(E\) extending a basis of \(K\) and including \(\beta\). The \(\beta\)-coordinate equation of \(g-\beta=0\) is the nonzero constant equation \(1=0\). Thus this specific coordinate translation has degree-zero refutation. This observation does not assert that every circuit or CNF encoding inherits a degree-zero proof; any different encoding needs its own degree-transfer analysis.

The static inverse lower bound remains compatible with the dynamic upper bound. Iterated squaring and Boolean reduction keep the final consequences at degree \(r\), while flattening them into a certificate in the original axiom can create large cofactors. The previously recorded compression gap is an explicit control against identifying those two measures. The source itself also gives a non-multilinear IPS upper bound in Theorem 8; the present application concerns the separate ordinary-PC degree ledger.

8 October 2026 — Random intermediate sums bound modular PC degree beyond graphs

Working PC upper bound; non-star-local modular family test. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Question. On near-half-family, develop the queued binomial/code criterion check. Can replacing graph incidence by a general sparse matrix avoid the graph-star upper bounds and supply the required near-half Boolean PC barrier? The test below distinguishes inconsistency over the entire modular field from inconsistency only after restricting variables to Boolean values.

New mathematics. A two-stage path through a uniformly random row combination keeps every intermediate support near \((1-1/q)v\). Combining it with the recorded balanced-character argument gives PC degree at most \((1/2-1/(2q))v+O(w\sqrt{v\log v}+w)\) for Boolean encodings of linear systems already inconsistent over \(\mathbb F_q\), with row width \(w\), prime \(q\), and proof-field characteristic different from \(q\). No graph locality or occurrence bound is needed.

Goal progress. Unchanged. This eliminates a larger candidate class at fixed odd modulus and bounded width, but supplies no near-half hard family or Frege transfer. Modular systems that have field solutions but no Boolean solution remain outside this theorem.

General statement. thm:random-modular-degree-ceiling is an all-parameter ordinary-PC upper bound. It adapts the Gaussian-width viewpoint of Ben-Sasson–Impagliazzo, 2010, Section 5 and the recorded balanced modular proof. It is not an assertion of independent novelty for Gaussian-width methods.

Source comparison and exact setting

Ben-Sasson–Impagliazzo's journal Theorem 5.1 bounds Gaussian width for bounded-width mod-2 systems; Corollary 5.4 gives a quarter-variable PC upper bound in odd proof characteristic. Its proof orders a minimally inconsistent family at random. The earlier Buss–Grigoriev–Impagliazzo–Pitassi Theorem 6 instead provides the general Laurent-relation lower-bound interface. Neither checked statement directly supplies the binary-characteristic odd-modulus window needed here. We use a random intermediate row combination to treat arbitrary prime modulus, and write out the Boolean PC transfer. The source proof’s displayed small-subsystem and sampling bookkeeping is not used: in general the subsystem has at most \(v+1\) rows, and sampling a family of \(m\) rows to have mean size \(j\) would require probability \(j/m\).

Let \(q\) be prime, \(F\) a field with \(\operatorname{char}F\ne q\), and \(a_i\in\mathbb F_q^v\), \(b_i\in\mathbb F_q\). The modular equations \(a_i\cdot x=b_i\) are assumed inconsistent over \(\mathbb F_q^v\) itself. Each row has support at most \(w\), where \(w\ge1\). The polynomial input over \(F\) consists of all Boolean equations and, for each row, the falsity indicator of each Boolean assignment to its support violating the modular equation. The modular coefficients are evaluated in \(\mathbb F_q\), not reduced into the proof field. This is a literal CNF encoding with at most \(\sum_i2^{|\operatorname{supp}a_i|}\) row clauses.

The degree bound

Theorem (random modular ceiling). In the setting above, select an inconsistent subsystem of \(m\le v+1\) rows. Put \[ C=\min\left\{v,\left\lceil (1-1/q)v+w\sqrt{2m\log(4m+2)}\right\rceil\right\}. \] There is an ordinary PC refutation of the Boolean encoding over \(F\) of degree at most \[ \left\lceil C/2\right\rceil+w+1. \] In particular, for fixed \(q,w\) the degree is at most \((1/2-1/(2q))v+O_w(\sqrt{v\log v})\). A contradictory zero row instead gives the constant axiom immediately.

Small subsystem. Choose a basis of the row space of the coefficient matrix. The corresponding equations are consistent since those rows are independent. Since the full system is not, one additional equation is incompatible with them. This yields at most \(v+1\) rows and scalars \(\lambda_i\in\mathbb F_q\) with \[ \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i a_i=0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i b_i\ne0. \] Refuting this subsystem refutes the original input. No assumption \(m\le v\) is needed.

Random path. Draw independent uniform \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\in\mathbb F_q\). Beginning at the zero combination, set row coefficients successively to \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\). Then set them successively to \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\). This is a path of \(2m\) single-row changes from zero to an inconsistency witness. Its intermediate coefficient vectors are \[ (\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_j,0,\ldots,0),\qquad (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j,\rho_{j+1},\ldots,\rho_m). \] At a first-stage position, a coordinate of the resulting row sum is either zero or uniform in \(\mathbb F_q\). At a second-stage position it is uniform if some remaining random row has a nonzero coefficient there; otherwise it is zero, because the final row sum is zero. Primality of \(q\) makes every nonzero coefficient invertible. Thus at each of the \(2m+1\) states, the expected support is at most \((1-1/q)v\).

Changing one \(\rho_i\) alters the support size by at most \(w\). Exposing the independent coefficients gives a Doob martingale whose increments have absolute value at most \(w\). The bounded-difference estimate therefore gives \[ \Pr[W-\mathbb EW\ge t]\le \exp[-t^2/(2mw^2)]. \] For clarity, this estimate follows by applying the conditional bound \(\mathbb E e^{\theta Z}\le e^{\theta^2w^2/2}\) to each mean-zero increment \(Z\in[-w,w]\), multiplying these bounds, using Markov's inequality and minimizing over \(\theta=t/(mw^2)\). The conditional bound follows from convexity between the two endpoints and \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\). With \(t=w\sqrt{2m\log(4m+2)}\), a union bound over the states has failure probability at most one half. Hence some path has support at most \(C\) at every state. The cap by \(v\) is automatic. Reusing rows in this path is allowed in a proof.

Character realization. Adjoin a primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\) in a finite algebraic extension \(E/F\). Define \[ u_j(t)=1+(\zeta^t-1)x_j,\qquad X(a)=\prod_j u_j(a_j). \] The literal identity \[ u_j(s)u_j(t)-u_j(s+t)=(\zeta^s-1)(\zeta^t-1)(x_j^2-x_j) \] gives multiplication and inverse reduction through degree two. For every scalar \(t\in\mathbb F_q\), \(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}\) is a scalar linear combination of the row's forbidden Boolean indicators through degree at most \(w\): multilinear interpolation assigns zero coefficient to every allowed tuple. This also covers \(t=0\), whose polynomial is zero.

For a current row sum \(a\) and charge \(b\), retain a derived relation \[ A-\zeta^bB, \] where \(A,B\) are character products on disjoint supports, their exponent difference is \(a\), and each support has size at most \(\lceil|\operatorname{supp}a|/2\rceil\). The zero starting relation is available by subtraction. When the path changes row \(i\) by scalar \(t\), derive \[ (A-\zeta^bB)X(ta_i)+\zeta^bB(X(ta_i)-\zeta^{tb_i}) =AX(ta_i)-\zeta^{b+tb_i}B. \] Multiplication by an affine factor is simulated by variable multiplication and a scalar combination; multiplication by a product uses those steps in sequence. Both displayed summands fit degree \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w\). Repeated factors on a variable reduce by the literal identity above without increasing the current degree.

When both sides have a factor on the same variable, multiply the entire relation by the inverse of the second-side factor, and reduce. This removes that second-side factor and keeps on the first side only the exponent difference, possibly zero. Each operation increases temporary degree by at most one and neither reduced support increases. After removing common supports, their union is the support of the new row sum. If one side has at least two more factors, multiply by the inverse of a factor on that larger side and reduce; this transfers that factor, inverted, to the smaller side. The larger support does not increase. These operations balance the new relation while keeping all temporary degrees at most \(\lceil C/2\rceil+w+1\). Every Boolean correction is a multiple of the explicit degree-two identity by the other factors at the same ceiling. Thus these are PC inferences, not divisions or unbounded semantic consequences.

At the endpoint both character supports are empty and the charge is nonzero, giving \(1-\zeta^{\sum_i\lambda_i b_i}\ne0\). Scale to obtain one. Finally choose an \(F\)-basis of \(E\) containing one and take coefficient components of every proof line. Extension-scalar operations become \(F\)-linear combinations of components, and variable multiplication commutes with taking components. Since the original indicator and Boolean axioms lie in \(F[x]\), this yields the claimed refutation over \(F\) at the same degree. This descent does not assert a useful expanded proof-size bound. \(\square\)

What the theorem rules out

For the notebook's field \(F=\mathbb F_2\) and fixed approximation depth \(\ell\ge2\), take any fixed odd prime \(q\). At bounded row width the fixed gap \(v/(2q)\) eventually dominates both the error term and every permitted deficit \(s=o(v/(\log v)^{\ell-2})\). Thus these encodings cannot supply the near-half barrier, even when a variable occurs in three or more constraints and the graph-star model does not apply. For \(q=3\) the leading upper coefficient is one third. The previous graph bound gives \(v/4+o(v)\) on its fixed-degree graph families, stronger there; no stronger bound for that class is claimed.

The hypothesis of full-field inconsistency is essential to this proof. For example \(x=2\pmod3\) has no Boolean solution but has a field solution, so there is no linear inconsistency witness. This example is itself easy, and is only a scope control. More generally a field-consistent affine coset disjoint from \(\{0,1\}^v\) is not settled by the random path. Such systems are the remaining code-based formulation to examine. Nor does the theorem rule out growing moduli without comparing the shrinking gap with the target deficit and all other costs.
