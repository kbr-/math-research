Use the free carrier without extending it silently

Bravyi–Kitaev's Section 2, equations (1)–(2) defines ordered occupation-basis creation and annihilation matrices. Their signed entries can be reduced to characteristic two as algebraic matrices. The paper's complex unitarity, norms and physical parity restrictions are not thereby imported. In particular an arbitrary sum of an even and an odd operator is not automatically a physical gate in that model.

On a free coordinate carrier with basis \(e_A=[w x_A]\), \(A\subseteq S\), define creation \(c_i e_A=e_{A\cup\{i\}}\) when \(i\notin A\), and zero otherwise; annihilation \(a_i e_A=e_{A\setminus\{i\}}\) when \(i\in A\), and zero otherwise. Put \(n_i=c_i a_i\). Linear independence of the basis makes these definitions legitimate. Old multiplication by \(x_i\) is \(n_i+c_i\); its transpose is \(n_i+a_i\), the already recorded combined dual action. At the maximal cube size \(s=D-b-1\), multiplication and Boolean reduction on this carrier fit \(b+s+1=D\).

This is a representation of the selected carrier, not an action of arbitrary old variables on it. Separate ladder operators are not old polynomial multipliers. On the entire quotient, the earlier one-bit annihilator control still shows that the separated pieces need not descend even when their sum does. The new free basis removes that ambiguity inside the chosen carrier only. It does not make every outside error an endomorphism of that carrier.

An actual outside variable on one ternary edge

Work in the recorded charged binary one-hot quotient, inside its proper degree window, and choose an edge \(e\) lying on a cycle. The three local indicators \(x_{e,0},x_{e,1},x_{e,2}\) satisfy the one-hot relations through degree two. Their image has dimension three: after scalar extension to \(\mathbb F_4\), the characters \(1,U_e,V_e\) have distinct cut-code classes. A nonzero cut supported only on \(e\) would require a nonconstant potential on \(G-e\), which is connected. The invertible local Fourier transform and binary descent give independence of the three indicators.

Thus \(\langle1,x_{e,0}\rangle\) is a free Boolean-bit carrier, but multiplication by \(x_{e,1}\) sends its unit to a new direction outside that carrier. On \(f=a+b x_{e,0}\), the product is \(a x_{e,1}\). This is a real local outside-support map in the containing quotient. It is not a hard error: its kernel and the corresponding local coefficient maps are already tractable.

A full single creation/annihilation pair cannot act on the whole three-dimensional carrier with \(ac+ca=I\) in characteristic two: taking trace gives zero on the left and one on the right. This rejects only that representation. It does not reject occupation encodings of the three states.

The one-particle sector repairs the representation

Use three binary modes and restrict their eight-dimensional occupation space to the three basis states with exactly one occupied mode. On that sector, \(c_i a_j\) acts as the matrix unit \(E_{ij}\). In particular \(n_i=E_{ii}\), the \(n_i\)'s are orthogonal idempotents, and their sum is the identity. Identify the three local label indicators with these diagonal units. This faithfully represents the one-edge multiplication algebra and handles both indicators in the preceding outside-variable test.

The repair does not give new coefficient multipliers. Multiplication by any polynomial function on the three states is diagonal in their indicator basis. For \(i\ne j\), \(E_{ij}\) cannot be such a multiplication operator: it changes one state basis vector into another. Off-diagonal operators might serve an independent analysis, but need their own quotient compatibility and a quantitative estimate. A local state hop does not automatically preserve the charged relations of the full graph. The already studied circulation automorphisms and matrix methods retain their own hypotheses.

For the diagonal algebra, the functional state-reduction proof and newer profile transfer were read in full at their stated scopes. A tuple depending on one edge can select a nonzero input separately at each of its three states and interpolate those choices by affine one-hot indicators. Its first coefficient row normalizes the tuple, with remaining rows zero. Old local one-hot reduction supplies companion certificates through their original image degrees and coefficient-field certificates through degree two. This is exactly the existing state-normalizer mechanism; the graph's local one-hot relations supply its local ideal. It does not assert degree completeness of the entire inconsistent graph ideal.

As a concrete legal check, take inputs \(g_1=x_{e,0}+x_{e,2}\) and \(g_2=x_{e,1}+x_{e,2}\). Their three state patterns are \(10,01,11\), so no single constant coefficient row normalizes them all. The existing affine choice \(\beta_1=1,\ \beta_2=x_{e,1}\) makes the row sum one modulo the one-hot ideal. Companions fit original degree three, and the nonzero coefficient-field image has its degree-two Boolean certificate. This is also covered by the older affine pair normalizer; it is not a new lower-bound result.
