Question. On general-step, the binary goal still needs affordable error elimination through fixed depth. Can the character quotient control loss from a surviving weight and give an exact error-image quantity, rather than another ambient estimate? This continues the queue's binomial lead.
New mathematics. The quotient classes are the ternary cut-code cosets meeting a Hamming ball. A weight with \(s\) nonzero character classes preserves at least a \(1/s\) fraction of the unweighted domain dimension. Multiplication by an edge-label conjunction has an exact rank given by a punctured coset-ball count, or zero when its labels violate a local cut relation. A puncturing recurrence bounds that rank relative to the same quotient and gives simultaneous removal of sparse families of long conjunctions, without a disjointness assumption.
Goal progress. Unchanged. The weight estimate removes an uncontrolled extra loss when the weight has small character support. The new unweighted contraction covers sparse families of long conjunctions and their mixtures, not arbitrary gate polynomials. It does not show that later common multipliers keep small support or provide a complete depth recurrence.
General statement. lem:cut-code-classes, lem:sparse-weight-survival and thm:conjunction-quotient-rank and thm:punctured-quotient-contraction hold throughout the preceding expander/degree window, for all permitted cutoffs, weights and edge sets. No finite pattern extrapolation is used.
1. Low-support cut representatives
The inherited congruence on \(n\) and lower bound on \(D\) retain the inconsistent-family and encoding conventions; the rank proofs use expansion and the upper cutoff. Keep the exact graph, orientation, field and ceiling of the previous theorem. Put \(m=|E(G)|\), \(\mathcal B=\operatorname{im}(\partial:\mathbb F_3^{V(G)}\to\mathbb F_3^{E(G)})\), and let \(\mathcal H_K\) be the ternary Hamming ball of support at most \(K\). Write \(R_D\) for the phase-adjusted quotient map and \[h(K)=\dim R_D(F[U,V]_{\le K}).\] Lemma (cut-code classes). For \(0\le K\le D\), \[h(K)=|\{\alpha+\mathcal B:\alpha\in\mathcal H_K\}|.\] Two characters of degree at most \(D\) have the same quotient coordinate exactly when their exponents differ by \(\mathcal B\); the relative scalar remains the one in the previous theorem.
Proof. Only the reverse implication needs checking. Suppose \(\alpha-\beta=\partial s\) with both supports at most \(D\). If each of the three level sets of \(s\) has size at most \(n/2\), expansion and summing their boundaries give \[\varepsilon n\le\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_3}|\delta(s^{-1}(a))|=2|\operatorname{supp}\partial s|\le4D<\varepsilon n/2,\] a contradiction. Thus one level set has more than half the vertices. Subtract its value from \(s\), leaving the boundary unchanged and support less than \(n/2\). This is a witness to the quotient relation, with its specified phase. It is unique: two such small representatives have support at most \(2D/\varepsilon<n/4\) each; their difference cannot be a nonzero constant. The previously proved absence of scalar cycles gives one independent coordinate per coset encountered. \(\square\)
This is a truncated identification, not a globally consistent choice of charged phases on the full cut space. Global inconsistency remains; no evaluation map on satisfying assignments of the charged system is inferred.
2. A sparse weight cannot erase too much dimension
Lemma (sparse-weight survival). Let \(\deg w\le b\), \(w\notin J_D\), \(b+K\le D\), and let \(s\ge1\) be the number of nonzero quotient coordinates in \(R_D(w)\). Then \[h_w(K):=\operatorname{rank}(f\in F[U,V]_{\le K}\mapsto R_D(wf))\ \ge\ \frac{h(K)}s.\] In particular \(s\le h(b)\le\sum_{j\le b}\binom mj2^j\); for \(b\ge1\), \(\log_2s\le b\log_2(2m+1)\).
Proof. Collect \(w\) by quotient coordinates using representatives of degree at most \(b\), obtaining \(w'=\sum_{j=1}^s c_jm_{\beta_j}\) with \(c_j\ne0\) and distinct cut-code cosets. Since \(w-w'\in J_D\) and its degree is at most \(b\), bounded-degree absorption shows that \(w,w'\) give the same map on degree at most \(K\). Choose one monomial \(m_\alpha\) of degree at most \(K\) for each of the \(h(K)\) domain coordinates. These suffice: the weight map kills the domain kernel, again by absorption.
Each column has exactly \(s\) nonzero entries. Indeed the translated exponents \(\alpha+\beta_j\) stay in degree at most \(K+b\le D\), and belong to distinct cut-code cosets. Their phases are nonzero, so no two terms cancel. Each row has at most \(s\) nonzero entries: for a fixed \(j\), translation by \(\beta_j\) sends distinct domain cosets to distinct output cosets. Choose a basis of the matrix's row space. Every nonzero column must occur in the support of some basis row, since otherwise every row would vanish in that column. Thus \(h(K)\le s\operatorname{rank}M\), proving the bound. The support estimate counts ternary characters through degree \(b\); encoding their signed supports by length-\(b\) padded lists gives the final elementary bound. \(\square\)
Conditional use. Suppose unweighted tuple errors \(a_{\nu,i}\) have degree at most \(e\), and \(b+K+e\le D\). Multiplying them by \(w\) cannot increase their image rank modulo \(J_D\): a zero image can be multiplied by \(w\) inside the same window. Hence an unweighted bound \(\sum_\nu E^1_\nu<h(K)/s\) implies the weighted common-kernel criterion. The normalization is now entirely in this quotient, not in the full polynomial space.
The distinction matters. If \(b=n^\alpha\) for fixed \(\alpha<1\), the logarithmic support loss is sublinear. But the common-kernel lemma may return a multiplier of degree \(K=\Theta(n)\). Its character support need not stay small at the next level. This result does not justify treating all later weights as sparse, nor does it remove the loss between ambient dimension and \(h(K)\). The basic sparse-row rank argument is elementary; the contribution here is verifying that the charged, truncated quotient admits the required nonzero translated columns.
3. Exact rank of a label conjunction
Let \(T\subseteq E(G)\), \(r=|T|\), and \(K+r\le D\). Denote by \(W_T\) the coordinate subspace \(\mathbb F_3^T\subseteq\mathbb F_3^E\), and by \(\mathcal B_T=\mathcal B\cap W_T\) its local cut subspace. For \(\delta\in\mathcal B_T\), let \(s_\delta\) be its unique small vertex representative from the previous lemma and put \(\chi_T(\delta)=\sum_v(s_\delta)_v\). This is a linear functional: each representative has support at most \(r/\varepsilon\), so sums of two remain below \(n/2\) and agree with the unique small representative; scalar multiplication is similar.
For labels \(a\in\mathbb F_3^T\), set \[L_{T,a}=\prod_{e\in T}(1+\omega^{-a_e}U_e+\omega^{a_e}V_e),\qquad q_T(K)=|\{\alpha+(\mathcal B+W_T):\alpha\in\mathcal H_K\}|.\] Theorem (conjunction quotient rank). If some \(\delta\in\mathcal B_T\) satisfies \(a\cdot\delta\ne\chi_T(\delta)\), then multiplication by \(L_{T,a}\) on degree at most \(K\), followed by \(R_D\), has rank zero. Otherwise its rank is exactly \(q_T(K)\).
Proof. In the local-domain algebra, \[L_{T,a}=\sum_{\beta\in W_T}\omega^{-a\cdot\beta}m_\beta.\] There is no missing normalization: \(3=1\) in \(F\). For any domain character \(m_\alpha\), collect its product by cut-code cosets. Inside a fixed coset the terms differ by \(\delta\in\mathcal B_T\), and their relative coefficient is \(\omega^{\chi_T(\delta)-a\cdot\delta}\). The sum over this subspace is zero for a nontrivial character and equals \(|\mathcal B_T|=1\) in \(F\) for the trivial character. Thus every column is zero in the incompatible case. In the compatible case it is nonzero, with one nonzero entry for every coset in the orbit \(\alpha+W_T+\mathcal B\) modulo \(\mathcal B\).
Columns from different such orbits have disjoint supports. Columns within one orbit are proportional. To check the latter without an unrestricted multiplication assumption, write \(\alpha'=\alpha+\beta_0+\delta\), with \(\beta_0\in W_T\), \(\delta\in\mathcal B\). The exponents \(\alpha'\) and \(\alpha+\beta_0\) have support at most \(K+r\le D\), so their relative phase has a small witness. For every \(\gamma\in W_T\), that same witness relates \(m_{\alpha'+\gamma}\) and \(m_{\alpha+\beta_0+\gamma}\), both still within support \(K+r\); translation cancels in the exponent difference. Reindexing \(\gamma\mapsto\beta_0+\gamma\) in the Fourier sum changes it by one nonzero scalar. Hence each encountered orbit contributes rank one, exactly the claimed count. This argument does not multiply a degree-\(K+r\) relation by another degree-\(r\) polynomial outside the window. \(\square\)
Analytic controls cover the endpoints: \(T=\varnothing\) gives the identity map and \(q_T(K)=h(K)\). For \(K=0\), a compatible conjunction has rank one. If \(T\) contains all incident edges of a vertex and the labels violate its charge, its unit-vertex cut witnesses incompatibility, recovering its forbidden-tuple axiom. A one-character weight has \(s=1\) and exactly full domain rank. These controls test the formulas directly; no new numerical sweep or encoding change is needed.
4. Puncturing gives a relative contraction bound
Theorem (punctured quotient contraction). For \(K\ge1\), put \(\lambda=(K+2m)/(3K+2m)<1\) and \(a(T)=|T|-\dim\mathcal B_T\). Then \[q_T(K)/h(K)\le\lambda^{a(T)}.\] If additionally \(\varepsilon>1\) and \(|T|\le D\), then \(a(T)\ge(1-1/\varepsilon)|T|\). Consequently, for \(L\) label conjunctions with support sizes in \([r,d]\), a surviving degree-\(b\) weight with \(s\) nonzero character coordinates, and \(b+K+d\le D\), the inequality \[Ls\lambda^{(1-1/\varepsilon)r}<1\] produces one degree-at-most-\(K\) multiplier \(f\) with \(wf\notin J_D\) annihilating all the conjunctions, and their constant linear combinations, modulo \(J_D\). Arbitrary overlap is allowed.
Proof. For any ternary linear code \(B\subseteq\mathbb F_3^d\), let \(H_B(k)\) count cosets meeting its radius-\(k\) Hamming ball. Delete one coordinate, projecting \(B\) to \(B'\). If its unit vector belongs to \(B\), deletion leaves the count unchanged. Otherwise every projected coset has three original cosets above it. A projected coset reached in degree \(k\) has a lift reached in degree \(k\), by inserting a zero coordinate in a representative. One reached in degree \(k-1\) has all three lifts reached in degree \(k\). Thus \[H_B(k)\ge H_{B'}(k)+2H_{B'}(k-1).\]
Realize the coset count as the filtered dimension of the group algebra \(F[\mathbb F_3^{d-1}/B']\). This auxiliary algebra has consistent zero phases and is used only for counting, not as the charged proof space. In its polynomial presentation with \(U_i,V_i\), the local quadratic relations have leading monomials \(U_i^2,V_i^2,U_iV_i\) for a degree-compatible order. A standard-monomial basis is closed under divisors and uses at most one of \(U_i,V_i\) per coordinate. If \(b_j\) counts its degree-\(j\) monomials, counting extensions gives \((j+1)b_{j+1}\le2(d-1-j)b_j\). Therefore \(b_j/(2^j\binom{d-1}{j})\) is nonincreasing, and its cumulative-average inequality yields \[\frac{H_{B'}(k-1)}{H_{B'}(k)}\ge\frac{N_{d-1}(k-1)}{N_{d-1}(k)},\qquad N_t(k)=\sum_{j\le\min(k,t)}2^j\binom tj.\] When \(k\le d-1\le m\), the newest summand is at most \(2m/k\) times the previous cumulative sum, since consecutive summands have ratio \(2(d-k)/k\). When \(k>d-1\), the cumulative ratio is one. Hence it is always at least \(k/(k+2m)\). Each nontrivial deletion reduces the count by a factor at most \((k+2m)/(3k+2m)\). Deleting \(T\) performs exactly \(a(T)\) nontrivial steps, the dimension of its coordinate images modulo \(\mathcal B\), proving the ratio bound.
For the expansion estimate, cut vectors supported in \(T\) correspond to vertex potentials constant on components of \(G\setminus T\), modulo global constants. Thus \(\dim\mathcal B_T=c(G\setminus T)-1\). One component has more than \(n/2\) vertices: otherwise summing expansion gives \(\varepsilon n\le2|T|\le2D<\varepsilon n/4\). The union \(S\) of the other components has size below \(n/2\) and boundary contained in \(T\), so \(c-1\le|S|\le|T|/\varepsilon\). This proves the bound on \(a(T)\). Each conjunction's image rank is at most \(\lambda^{(1-1/\varepsilon)r}h(K)\). Weighting cannot increase it in the stated window. Their summed rank is strictly below \(h(K)/s\le h_w(K)\), so the common-kernel lemma supplies \(f\). \(\square\)
With fixed \(\varepsilon>1\) and \(K/m\) bounded below, \(\lambda\) stays below one. Polynomial inventory and polynomial character support are therefore covered by a sufficiently large constant times \(\log n\) minimum conjunction length, with constants depending on those exponents. The graph family is unchanged. This uses ternary-code fibers and a coordinate-deletion recurrence, not the invalid universal base-Hilbert substitution; that counterexample lacks these hypotheses. General gates need not have sparse expansions using only long conjunctions. In particular, all label conjunctions on one edge set sum to one and cannot satisfy the strict inequality.
