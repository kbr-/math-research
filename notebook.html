Pigeonhole principle lower bounds

Our goal: superpolynomial proof-size lower bounds for ordinary PHP in \(\mathrm{AC}^{0}[p]\)-Frege, for every fixed prime \(p\) and fixed depth.

Where we stand

The goal is still open. We have working proofs for eliminating extension blocks and batching blocks with nested spans or suitable nested cores. These produce genuine polynomial-calculus (PC) refutations with explicit degree bounds.

We have also optimized our static affine batching criterion completely. Some admissible families still exceed the available degree budget: a better ordering cannot fix that method. Those families are extension data, not refutations, so they leave room for an approach that uses the actual certificate.

All four references were imported during the source audit, and the base/residual PC lower-bound match and simulation parameters have been checked. A focused audit found no gap in the central elimination arguments; this is not a formal verification of the entire manuscript. Unused certificate blocks can be pruned for free, but we do not yet know how to handle all the essential blocks affordably.

The missing bridge: turn each relevant translated degree-\(D\) Nullstellensatz refutation into a base PC refutation with

\[ B\le \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor, \]

contradicting the base lower bound \(\deg_{\mathrm{PC}}(\mathcal F_n)\ge n/2+1\). Constructing a suitable joint degree-\(D\) design would be an alternative.

The remaining route

Highest risk first
  1. Prove affordable elimination or joint-design existence. Exploit the actual refutation and PHP structure to stay below the linear base-degree threshold. This is the main missing theorem.
  2. Cover the actual Frege simulation outputs. Handle nonlinear inputs, all companions and cofactors, earlier-level dependencies, and field equations. The balanced proof tree alone does not establish a cheap elimination bound.
  3. Complete the proof-level audit. Check the remaining quotient variants and any new composition rule, preserving original axiom degrees, freshness, bounded field reductions, and reuse of derived PC polynomials.
  4. Formalize the ordinary-PHP transfer. Convert a hypothetical short ordinary-PHP refutation to our exact Boolean linear-row base with bounded-depth and polynomial-size overhead. Do not silently add same-row exclusions.
  5. Close the parameters. For every fixed \(p,\ell,K\), show that a size-\(n^K\), depth-\(\ell\) proof would yield the forbidden base refutation for all sufficiently large \(n\), retaining every accuracy and depth factor.

Proposed next step

Write the exact certificate-composition rule for one modus-ponens inference in the balanced BIKPRS translation, retaining its cofactor products. After pruning unused blocks, attempt a degree bound that composes through the proof tree using PC reuse.

Check the proposed bound against the prefix-pebbling chain and irrelevant spread blocks before drawing a global conclusion. The task is to justify the composition bound, not assume that logarithmic tree height makes it affordable.

Goal and exact objects

The target is, for every fixed prime \(p\),

\[ \forall\ell,K\;\exists n_0\;\forall n\ge n_0: \operatorname{Size}_{\ell,p}(\mathrm{PHP}_n)>n^K. \]

Over \(\mathbb F_p\), use \(n+1\) pigeons, \(n\) holes, and the base

\[ \mathcal F_n=\{\rho_i-1\}_i\cup \{x_{ij}x_{i'j}:i\ne i'\}\cup\{x_{ij}^2-x_{ij}\}_{i,j}, \qquad \rho_i=\sum_jx_{ij}. \]

There are no same-row exclusions. A Boolean row summing to one modulo \(p\) can contain \(p+1\) ones. Do not substitute a functional or chessboard encoding.

An ordinary degree-\(D\) design is a normalized linear functional annihilating \(\mathcal I_D=\operatorname{span}\{qf:f\in\mathcal F_n,\ \deg(qf)\le D\}\). Polynomial calculus permits linear combinations and multiplication of an earlier polynomial by one variable, with ordinary collected line degree bounded by \(D\). Its consequence space \(\mathcal C_D\) can exceed \(\mathcal I_D\); neither is the unrestricted ideal. A design is not multiplicative. PC reuse gives

\[ f\in\mathcal C_c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad qf\in\mathcal C_{\max\{c,\deg q+\deg f\}}. \]

Multiply the final derived polynomial, not its entire derivation.

ENS block \(a\) includes all companions

\[ E_{a,i}=g_{a,i}\prod_{u=1}^{h_a} \left(1-\sum_jr_{a,u,j}g_{a,j}\right) \]

and field axioms \(r^p-r\). Fresh variables are shared within a block and disjoint across same-level blocks. Inputs may be nonlinear and use earlier levels. With nonzero inputs, set \(\delta_a=\max_i\deg g_{a,i}\) and retain the original companion degree \(e_{a,i}=\deg g_{a,i}+h_a(\delta_a+1)\). Activity and cofactor spaces use this original joint-variable degree; later specialization or Boolean reduction does not retroactively enlarge them.

Reusable working bounds and controls

A focused local audit found no gap in these central tools; this is not formal verification. Stable labels locate exact hypotheses and proofs in the historical claim index and selected records of claims.json.

  • Preliminaries, Chapter 1: lem:reuse, lem:substitution, lem:fieldreduction, and lem:selectors. Field-ideal membership admits degree-nonincreasing univariate reduction.
  • One-block and additive elimination, Chapter 7: thm:one-elimination and thm:additive. One block costs \((p-1)\delta\); reverse-level elimination has degree bound \(D+(p-1)\sum_a\delta_a\).
  • Nested spans, Chapter 8: thm:nested. An entire nested input-span chain costs \(D+(p-1)\delta\), by replaying the original proof and reusing learned inputs.
  • Nested cores and residuals, Chapter 8: thm:cores. Nested cores of degree \(\gamma\), residual ranks \(r_a\), and representatives of degree \(\eta_a\) give bound \(TD+(p-1)\gamma\), where
\[ T=\max_a\max\left\{1,\eta_a\left(\left\lceil \frac{(p-1)r_a}{h_a}\right\rceil-1\right)_+\right\}. \]

Quotient variants require supplied bounded-degree base derivations. No proof-size control for these local PC eliminations has been claimed or needed.

Static affine obstruction, Chapter 9. The static criterion is optimized over both order and batch partition; ordering by dimension is optimal. The \(n\) graph spaces \(V_\alpha=\{(u,\alpha u)\}\subset K^q\oplus K^q\) have pairwise zero intersection even modulo row equations, and criterion optimum \(\min\{D+(p-1)n,T(r)D\}\), eventually exceeding \(n/2\). Here \(K\) is the field used in the spread construction and \(T(r)\) is the static rank-dependent factor of Chapter 9. These are admissible extension data, not a refutation. A better static ordering cannot help; this does not rule out every elimination method.

Prefix-pebbling control, Chapter 7: lem:prefixcertificate is a genuine NS refutation of degree \(4h+2\), with adjacent-block interactions along a long chain. Its base can have high NS degree but has PC degree at most three. Sparse local interactions and high ordinary-design degree alone therefore do not give universal cheap batching; large antichains alone are not an obstruction.

Unused-block pruning: the first local research-log entry proves an elementary zero-cofactor lemma. Remove a block unused in the actual NS identity by setting its fresh variables to zero everywhere, including later inputs and cofactors, then clean up zero input coordinates and blocks. Degree does not increase. This avoids charging irrelevant spread blocks but does not bound essential surviving support; it is a clarification, not a breakthrough.

Separate non-Boolean branch: the corrected range is \(2d-1\le n\), not \(d<n\). Generic survival there does not imply Boolean ENS survival. Consult Chapter 2 only if this branch becomes relevant.

Theorem 3.1, printed p. 297 (PDF p. 7), gives PC degree at least \(n/2+1\) for every \(m>n\) and every field. Our ordinary-PC derivations map degree-nonincreasingly into his calculus; deleting row exclusions gives the base and residual PC lower bounds. This discharges the imported hypothesis in thm:rigidity, not the final payoff.
  • BIKPRS, user-supplied author-layout copy: Definitions 6.1, 6.4, and 6.5 distinguish Boolean originals from field extension variables.   • Pebbling, arXiv:2001.02481v1: equations (2.5)–(2.6) and Theorem 3.1 match the all-field NS/reversible-pebbling correspondence. The family \(\Gamma(1,r)\), from Definition 4.7 and Lemma 4.9, has a single sink, size \(\Theta(r^3)\), and price \(\Omega(r)\).

Degree target and unresolved transfer

The affordable elimination theorem must cover every relevant translated NS certificate and yield base PC degree at most \(b_n=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). A polynomial bound in \(D,h,\log M\), with \(M\) the number of companions, would suffice in the intended fixed-depth, fixed-prime parameter regime, but is unproved. A joint-design construction is an alternative.

The next argument must exploit actual balanced simulation certificates, local derivations and cofactors, and richer PHP consequences. Read the exact relevant Chapter 7–8 proofs and BIKPRS pp. 28–31 when needed. The ordinary-PHP transfer to our exact polynomial base remains to be formalized: Krajíček Lemma 5.1 includes functionality and does not automatically supply that transfer. Final closure must cover every fixed \(p,\ell,K\).
