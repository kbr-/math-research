Boolean invariance is a concrete but restricted mechanism

Lemma (Boolean pair invariants). Let \(n\ge2\), \(x\in\mathbb F_2^n\), \(y\in(\mathbb F_2^n)^*\), and \(u=yx\). If a polynomial \(f(x,y)\) of degree \(d<n\) defines a Boolean function invariant under \((x,y)\mapsto(Tx,yT^{-1})\) for every \(T\in\mathrm{GL}_n(2)\), then modulo the Boolean axioms it equals \(a+b u\). If \(d<2\), then \(b=0\). The equality has an ordinary Boolean-axiom certificate through degree \(d\). The strict threshold cannot be increased to include degree \(n\).

Proof. There are five orbits: both zero; just \(x\) nonzero; just \(y\) nonzero; both nonzero with \(yx=0\); and \(yx=1\). For the fourth orbit choose a basis starting with \(x\), then a vector on which \(y\) is one, and complete in its kernel. For the fifth choose a basis starting with \(x\) and complete in the kernel. This proves transitivity, and the other orbits are immediate. Put \(N_x=1+\prod_i(1+x_i)\), and similarly \(N_y\). The functions \(1,N_x,N_y,N_xN_y,u\) have independent values on these five orbits and span all invariant functions.

The unique multilinear representative is consequently \(a+b_1N_x+b_2N_y+cN_xN_y+e u\). Its degree is at most \(d\), because Boolean reduction never increases degree. The coefficient of the full \(x,y\) monomial first forces \(c=0\), and the separate full \(x\) and full \(y\) monomials force \(b_1=b_2=0\). If \(d<2\), the quadratic monomials force \(e=0\). Reducing each power with \(z^k-z=(z^2-z)(1+z+\cdots+z^{k-2})\) gives the degree-\(d\) certificate. Finally \(N_x\) is invariant of degree \(n\), but on \(y=0\) varies while \(u=0\) stays fixed. \(\square\)

This concerns Boolean-function invariance, not formal polynomial invariance. It assumes invariance; actual compiler inputs have not been shown invariant. The Chen–Wehlau vector/covector invariant theorem motivates the object, but the elementary proof above does not depend on its formal invariant-ring generators. Version 2 was withdrawn; the source here is version 3. Multi-row/covector extensions and a degree-paid way to obtain source invariance remain open.

Finite jets do not repair the selector shortcut

Lemma (finite jet selectors). Let \(f_1,\ldots,f_s\) be the nonconstant clause polynomials of an unsatisfiable CNF over \(\mathbb F_2\), with no empty clause. For every \(q=2^e\), set \[ P=\prod_i(1-f_i),\qquad A_i=f_i^{q-1}\prod_{j<i}(1-f_j^q). \] Then \(\sum_i A_if_i=1-P^q\ne1\) as a formal polynomial, but its difference from one has every Hasse derivative of total order less than \(q\) zero at every Boolean point. The Hilbert-like candidate \(C(x,Y,Z)=\sum_iA_iY_i\), with zero Boolean-placeholder coefficients, satisfies \(C(x,0,0)=0\) and has constant arithmetic depth and size polynomial in \(q\) and the written CNF length. It is not an IPS certificate.

Proof. The usual telescoping identity applied to \(f_i^q\) gives \(\sum_iA_if_i=1-\prod_i(1-f_i^q)=1-P^q\). Each \(1-f_i\) is nonzero, so their product is nonzero in the polynomial ring. Unsatisfiability implies \(P(a)=0\) on the cube: some clause is false, so its \(f_i(a)=1\). In the expansion \(P(a+z)\) there is no constant term. Frobenius raises every surviving monomial's degree by \(q\); hence \(P(a+z)^q\) has no terms below degree \(q\). These coefficients are the Hasse derivatives. Each clause is a product of literals; build its powers by repeated factors, then the displayed unbounded-fanin products and sum. This has a constant number of arithmetic layers and polynomial size in \(q\) and input length. Its formal degree is \(O(qL)\) for total literal length \(L\). \(\square\)

For a prescribed fixed jet order choose a larger \(q\). This does not refute an identity test whose jet order is tied to and exceeds the candidate's formal degree, or a sufficiently strong size-dependent order. It constructs no low-complexity Boolean correction coefficients. Dvir–Kopparty–Saraf–Sudan, Lemma 2.7, bounds summed multiplicities by total degree times the grid factor; the construction is consistent with that bound. The multiplicity estimate already appears in the imported rank engine and is not a new lower-bound import here.

Two controls for paired restrictions

Proposition (paired restriction controls). First take \(N\) pairs \((x_i,y_i)\) and the fixed width-two DNF \(\Phi=\bigvee_i(x_i\mathbin\oplus y_i)\). Keep \(b\) pairs live and assign equal bits within every fixed pair. The residual has exact parity-decision-tree depth \(b\), irrespective of the fixed bits. Second, take a raw binary matrix and the conjunction of \(r\) constraints \((A_{\rm old})_{i1}=0\) on distinct rows. Choose uniform \(T\in\mathrm{GL}_N(2)\), substitute \(A_{\rm old}=AT\), and retain a fixed set of \(N'\) live columns, chosen independently of \(T\). Except with probability \[ \frac{2^{N-N'}-1}{2^N-1}, \] the residual affine constraints have rank \(r\), are jointly consistent on the raw live cube, and their conjunction has Boolean degree \(r\). Fixed-column values may contribute arbitrary constant offsets.

Proof. In the first statement the residual is \(\bigvee_{i\text{ live}}(x_i+y_i)\), so \(b\) parity queries suffice. Set the live \(y_i=0\). Along the zero path of a parity decision tree with fewer than \(b\) queries, the queried homogeneous equations have a nonzero common kernel vector. That vector follows the same path as zero but has OR value one, a contradiction. For the second, the first column of \(T\) is uniform among the \(2^N-1\) nonzero vectors. Precisely \(2^{N-N'}-1\) have zero live projection. Otherwise the residual forms are \(c_i+\sum_{k\text{ live}}T_{k1}A_{ik}\). They are nonzero and use disjoint row variables. Each can independently be set to zero, and selecting one nonzero coefficient in each row gives a noncancelling degree-\(r\) monomial in their complement product. \(\square\)

The first law differs from independent coordinate restrictions. Beame's switching primer, Lemma 1, uses uniformly chosen unset coordinates and uniformly assigned remaining bits; its \((7pr)^s\) bound cannot be transferred to paired assignments. The second control fixes the formula before sampling the basis and defeats a blanket vanish-or-small-rank statement with the correct quantifier order. Neither control supplies an actual defect of a valid short refutation.

There is also a positive control: if the first example's live pairs represent columns of one pigeon row after the actual unary strengthening, the old equation is \(\sum_i u_i=1\), \(u_i=x_i+y_i\). The single legal row \(q=\sum_i u_i\) has \(P=1-q\) equal to that old axiom up to sign; every companion \(u_iP\) has an ordinary certificate through degree two. Thus raw switching failure must not be called source hardness. Nor does the second control assert independence after using all hard-family consequences.

A common binary basis cannot make copying Gaussian

Proposition (copy basis obstruction). Over any field of characteristic two, no common invertible two-state basis on the four legs turns the equality tensor \(E=e_0^{\otimes4}+e_1^{\otimes4}\) into a matchgate signature obeying the standard Pfaffian identities.

Proof. Write the columns of the basis matrix as \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\). The transformed symmetric signature has weight-\(k\) coordinate \(f_k=a^{4-k}b^k+c^{4-k}d^k\). The four-leg matchgate identity is \[ f_{0000}f_{1111}-f_{1100}f_{0011}+f_{1010}f_{0101}-f_{1001}f_{0110}=0. \] In characteristic two and for a symmetric signature this requires \(f_0f_4+f_2^2=0\). Instead direct expansion gives \(f_0f_4+f_2^2=a^4d^4+c^4b^4=(ad+bc)^4=\det(T)^4\ne0\). This necessary identity already gives the contradiction. \(\square\)

The identity is a polynomial Pfaffian identity with integer coefficients, so this necessary condition reduces to characteristic two; no classification theorem over \(\mathbb F_2\) is assumed. See Cai–Gorenstein, Theorem 2.1 and Section 3. The control excludes only one common two-state basis without auxiliary states. It says nothing decisive about leg-dependent bases, higher bond spaces, non-Gaussian constructions or a different proof encoding.
