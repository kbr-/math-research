# Boolean order of the rank residual

Proposed lemma rank residual order. Work over F2, m>n>=1, r=m-n,
A an m by n variable matrix and B an n by m variable matrix. Put
F_ij=delta_ij+(AB)_ij, P=prod_(i,j)(1+F_ij), and let I be the ideal
generated by x^2+x for every entry variable x. Then
P lies in I^(r+1) but not in I^(r+2).
Equivalently its minimum ordinary local vanishing order on the full
Boolean cube is r+1. This concerns the fixed normalized residual, not
the degree of an original PC proof or circuit size of a certificate.

Proof, lower bound. At a Boolean assignment a, set C=A(a)B(a), E=I_m+C.
rank E >= m-rank C >=r, so the number k of nonzero entries of E is >=r.
Each such entry makes a factor of P vanish. If k>=r+1, local order is
at least r+1. If k=r, rank E=r and its r nonzero entries lie in distinct
rows and columns. The fixed space ker(I+E)=ker C has dimension at least r,
is contained in im E, and im E has dimension r. Thus im E=ker(I+E),
so E^2=E. An idempotent partial permutation is diagonal: an off-diagonal
nonzero E_ij has the unique row-i position j, and E_jj must be zero
because column j is already occupied at i; hence (E^2)_ij=0, impossible.
All diagonal nonzero entries are one. Hence C is diagonal with n ones
and r zeros, so rank C=n and both A and B have full inner rank n.
For a deleted diagonal index i, row C_i and column C_i vanish.
Full row rank of B forces row A_i=0; full column rank of A forces
column B_i=0. Its vanishing factor (AB)_ii therefore has local order two.
There are r such factors, giving order 2r>=r+1.

Sharpness. At one Boolean point set rows A_i=e_i for i<n and A_i=e_n
for i>=n. Set columns B_j=e_j for j<n and B_j=0 for j>=n.
Then C=diag(I_(n-1),0_(r+1)). The only vanishing factors are diagonals
i=n,...,m, whose linear parts in perturbations are distinct variables
dB_(n,i). All other factors have constant one. Thus the leading form is
prod_(i=n)^m dB_(n,i), nonzero of degree r+1.

Global ideal statement. Boolean division gives I=intersection_a m_a,
where the finite Boolean-point maximal ideals are pairwise comaximal.
Their powers remain comaximal: expand 1=(u+v)^(2q-1) when u+v=1,
u in m_a,v in m_b. The Chinese remainder product/intersection identity
then gives I^q=intersection_a m_a^q. Apply the local order bounds.
P is nonzero formally since every factor is nonzero and the ring is a
domain. QED.

Nonvacuity: m=n+1 for every n>=1 gives exact order two. Taking m=2n
gives order n+1 at unbounded size. The displayed Boolean point supplies
an exact witness in each case. n=0 is excluded: a diagonal factor is
then identically zero. For m<=n the rank system is satisfiable and P
can evaluate to one, so the assertion is not extended there.

Controls. At ANY fixed Boolean point the leading form of P is a product
of leading forms of its factors. Each factor has degree <=2, hence its
leading form is constant, linear or quadratic. Its explicit circuit has
polynomial size O(m^2 n), and depth bounded by a constant, using expanded
quadratic factors and one outer product. Thus a single-point leading
residual cannot itself be the needed hard circuit polynomial, even though
its vanishing order is large. No generic extraction of a high component
from a supplied circuit is assumed.

Nor do arbitrary residual certificate coefficients inherit that depth
of ideal membership. Write b_j=x_j^2+x_j for the Boolean generators,
separate from the matrix B. For Q(x,Z)=sum_j q_j(x)Z_j and P=sum_j q_j b_j,
the syzygy b_2 Z_1+b_1 Z_2 replaces (q_1,q_2) by (q_1+b_2,q_2+b_1),
and preserves the formal certificate at small constant-depth
cost. At r>=2, at most one of Q and Q+that syzygy can have every coefficient
in I^r: their coefficient difference b_2 is not in I^2 and I^r subset I^2.
b_2 has local order one at every Boolean point. This is a full-coset scope
control, not an impossibility of all invariants.
