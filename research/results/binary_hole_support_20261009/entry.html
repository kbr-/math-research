Quadratic hole support

Proposition. Let \(n=2^\ell,\ell\ge2\), fix a pigeon \(i\), and take \(B\subsetneq[n]\). For the tuple of all \(\binom{\ell}{2}\) functions \(b_{is}b_{it}\), \(s<t\), suppose that on \(\Omega_B\) each function equals an arbitrary globally old-affine baseline plus a function of the selected holes' occupancy states. Then \[ |B|\ge n-\ell-1. \] This bound is attained for a suitable \(B\). It is a statement about agreement on the whole stated domain, not only on a chosen weight support.

Proof. Put \(S=[n]\setminus B\), identified with its bit labels, and fix all other pigeons at a label \(j_0\in S\). Let pigeon \(i\) vary over \(S\). These assignments lie in \(\Omega_B\) and have the identical all-empty occupancy state on \(B\). Each claimed baseline restricts to an affine function of the \(\ell\) bits of row \(i\). Therefore every squarefree quadratic coordinate monomial is affine as a function on \(S\).

Let \(A\subseteq\mathbb F_2^S\) be the span of the constant and restricted coordinate functions. Their squares equal themselves, and their pairwise products belong to \(A\) by the preceding condition. Thus \(A\) is an algebra. It contains every point indicator \(\prod_t(x_t-a_t+1)\) for \(a\in S\), since these are products of members of \(A\); restricted to \(S\), they form the delta-function basis. Consequently \(A=\mathbb F_2^S\), and \(|S|=\dim A\le\ell+1\). More precisely the quadratic-affinity condition is equivalent to affine independence of \(S\): the forward argument gives full rank of affine evaluation; conversely full rank makes every function on \(S\) affine.

For sharpness choose \(S=\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_\ell\}\). All displayed quadratics vanish on \(S\). Set every baseline to zero. If row \(i\) occupies a hole in \(B\), its identity is visible in the occupancy data and specifies every quadratic value. If it occupies no selected hole, its label is in \(S\) and all values are zero. This defines the required offset functions on every domain point. Hence \(|B|=n-\ell-1\) suffices. \(\square\)

Actual source control. For every row \(i\) and pair \(s<t\), use a complete affine bottom tuple \((1+b_{is},1+b_{it})\). Its induced product is \(b_{is}b_{it}\). For each row add a parent reading all those genuine products. Accuracies may all equal \(3\ell\). Inventory is \(O(n\ell^2)\), and original bottom and parent degrees are polylogarithmic; every companion and coefficient field is retained. Each parent independently requires the linear-sized bag above even when its proposed baseline mixes all old rows. Yet the entire polynomial family is already covered by the rank-two-bottom source corollary: shared bottom maps preserve the complete original degree, leaving arbitrary quadratic old parent tuples. Therefore this is a failure of a proposed universal representation, not source hardness. We do not need a new quadratic theorem or a single-parent example.

Affine probes with hole domains

Keep the complete binary product-only DAG, nested proper hole sets \(B_a\), accuracy at least \(3\ell\), and compact old base from the preceding theorem. Allow each block its own list of \(u_a\) old-affine probes \(F_{a,1},\ldots,F_{a,u_a}\). On its local domain assume \[ \overline g_a=Q_a+ v_a(F_{a,1},\ldots,F_{a,u_a},\operatorname{occ}_{B_a}), \qquad \deg Q_{a,i}\le1. \] Probe spaces need not be nested or independent. Only the domains require the earlier nesting. Set \[ R=\max_a\{u_a+|B_a|\log_2(n+2)\}. \]

Corollary (probe-hole source exclusion). If block inventory is polynomial, \(R\le n^\eta\operatorname{polylog}n\), and original proof degree \(D\le n^\alpha\operatorname{polylog}n\), with fixed \(\alpha,\eta\ge0\) and \(2\alpha+\eta<1\), the complete source has no such ordinary NS or PC refutation for sufficiently large \(n\). There is no separate depth restriction.

Proof. Multiply each hole-state selector by the affine-probe cell selector \[ \prod_{j\le u_a}(1+F_{a,j}+\sigma_j),\qquad \sigma\in\mathbb F_2^{u_a}. \] Together they form a zero-one partition on \(\Omega_{B_a}\). Keep only realizable joint cells; on each, the offset vector is constant and the reference tuple is affine. Their selector degree is at most \[ t=\max_a(u_a+\ell|B_a|)\le R, \] and their total number is at most \(M_0\le M2^R\). The reference family is chosen before the common old weight. The finite affine construction gives \(T_0=k=\lceil\sqrt{(n+1)\ln(4M_0)}\rceil\). Every weighted reference companion vanishes on the full old cube, so these same maps satisfy all local domains.

Use proper-hole reduction and nested interpolation once, with \(\mu=\ell,\lambda=0\). Their original-degree ceiling is \[ B_*=\ell[(k+t)D+k] =O(n^{\alpha+(1+\eta)/2}\operatorname{polylog}n)=o(n). \] The compact row-linear separation conditions hold exactly as before. It excludes the transferred nonzero \(f\), proving the claim. Probe-cell enumeration is only existential, possibly exponential. The theorem claims degree, not an efficient or size-preserving transformation. \(\square\)

For the quadratic control take every bag empty and give each row's parent its \(\ell\) row bits as probes; bottoms have zero offsets and need no probes. Then \(R=\ell\), and the whole source is covered. This is the tested alternative representation required by the obstruction. In this case it recovers elementary row-feature normalization, not a stronger quadratic result. The older two-level feature theorem already charges the same affine-feature resource in its setting; the current corollary merely composes it with nested local domains and the previously proved direct map.
