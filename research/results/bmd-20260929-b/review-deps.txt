Transfer to the actual prefix
Lemma. In the deflated core, with \(\ell_1=2N-3\) and \(Q_i=\sum_{j\le i}SD_F^jv\), there are graded isomorphisms \(F/Q_i\cong V/\langle b_1,\ldots,D^{\ell_1+i}b_1\rangle\) for every \(i\ge0\). In particular \(C_N^+\cong V/\langle b_1,\ldots,D^{R+1}b_1\rangle\), and the boundary maximal-minor ideal \(J_N\) is the zeroth Fitting ideal of \(V/\langle b_1,\ldots,D^Rb_1\rangle\).
Proof. By the block presentation, \(B_1=\bigoplus_{j<\ell_1}SD^jb_1\) is a free summand of \(V\) with complement the remaining strings, identified with \(F\); let \(\pi:V\to F\) be the projection. Each feedback term \(C_rb_1\) has order at most \(w_r-4<\ell_1\), so it lies in \(B_1\). Hence, for \(x\in V\) with coefficient \(x_{\ell_1-1}\) on \(D^{\ell_1-1}b_1\), \(\pi(Dx)=D_F\pi(x)+x_{\ell_1-1}v\), because \(D^{\ell_1}b_1\equiv v\) modulo \(B_1\) by \(L_1b_1=v\). Induction gives \(\pi(D^{\ell_1+j}b_1)\in D_F^jv+\sum_{i<j}SD_F^iv\). Since the prefix contains \(B_1\), \(V/\langle b_1,\ldots,D^{\ell_1+i}b_1\rangle=F/\pi(\ldots)=F/Q_i\). With \(i=q+1\), \(\ell_1+q+1=R+1\). The Fitting statement follows because \(J_N=I_q(v_0,\ldots,v_q)\) is the zeroth Fitting ideal of \(F/Q_q\). \(\square\)
Pair evaluation. Three recorded facts identify the prefix with the matrices \(A_L\) as column spans. First, the centred Ore recurrence takes as pair columns the coefficients \(g_j\) of \((1+ZT)^{-1/2}\wedge Z(1+ZT)^{-3/2}\). Since \(Y(1+XT)-X(1+YT)=Y-X\), this series is \(q\bigl(((1+XT)(1+YT))^{-3/2}\bigr)\) with \(q(h)=(Y-X)h\), so \(g_j=q(H_j)\). The normalization-quotient theorem records the same identification. Second, that lemma gives \(g_j=P_j(\nabla_0)(1\wedge Z)\), with \(P_j\) of order \(j\), leading coefficient \(1/j!\), and polynomial unitriangular conversion to divided powers in every prefix. Hence \(\langle b_1,\ldots,D^Lb_1\rangle=\langle q(H_0),\ldots,q(H_L)\rangle\) as \(S\)-submodules for every \(L\), with \(H_j\) in centred roots. Third, the translation identity \(((1+(X+c)T)(1+(Y+c)T))^{-3/2}=(1+cT)^{-3}((1+XT')(1+YT'))^{-3/2}\), \(T'=T/(1+cT)\), gives \(H_k(X+c,Y+c)=H_k(X,Y)+\sum_{j<k}\gamma_{kj}(c)H_j(X,Y)\), again unitriangular. So the uncentred columns \(H_0,\ldots,H_L\) have the same span; bmd_cube_wronskian_covariance.gp checks the resulting invariance \(F(b+c)=F(b)\) at \(N=6\). Pull back along \(\phi:U\to\operatorname{Spec}S\), \(a\mapsto\) the centred coefficients of \(\prod_i(Z-a_i)\). On the separated locus, evaluation at the ordered root pairs identifies \(\bigwedge^2(S[Z]/p_c)\otimes\mathcal O_U\) with \(\mathcal O_U^R\), sending \(q(h)\) to \(((a_j-a_i)h(a_i,a_j))_{i<j}\), and the factors \(a_j-a_i\) are units. Since Fitting ideals and supports of a cokernel depend only on the column span, \(\phi^*C_N^+\cong\operatorname{coker}A_{R+1}\) and \(J_N\mathcal O_U=I_R(A_R)\) exactly, not only up to radical. The map \(\phi\) is smooth: it is the quotient by translation followed by the finite étale root cover off the discriminant. It is therefore faithfully flat onto its image, so codimensions of supports are preserved and reducedness of \(J_N\mathcal O_U\) at a prime descends to \(J_N\) at its image. With the proposition, \(\operatorname{Supp}\phi^*C_N^+=\{\operatorname{rank}A_{R+1}<R\}\) is the locus of a section with contact at least \(R+4\). Such contact forces \(n_{R+1}\ge R+4\), hence \(\operatorname{wt}_P\ge3\).
The actual cube has no extra invariant codimension-two component
Theorem. Work in characteristic zero with \(N\ge5\), \(S=k[c_2,\ldots,c_N]\), the centered derivation \(\delta_0\), and the exact core \(F,v\) of rank \(q=\binom{N-2}{2}\). Write \[ \Gamma_j=\sum_{i=0}^j S D^iv,\quad \Gamma=\sum_{i\ge0}S D^iv,\quad C=F/\Gamma,\quad C^+=F/\Gamma_{q+1}. \] Let \(P_{22}\) be the prime of the double-pair closure, and \(L_N=\ker(C^+\to D_{22}(-4))\). Then:
At \(P_{22}\), already \((\Gamma_q)_{P_{22}}=\Gamma_{P_{22}}\). Thus the boundary prefix, and hence the first post-boundary prefix, has the full local quotient.
For every other height-two prime \(P\) with \(\delta_0P\subseteq P\), already \((\Gamma_{q-1})_P=F_P\).
Consequently \((L_N)_P=0\) at every invariant height-two prime. Any codimension-two component of \(L_N\) is non-invariant.
Proof. The normalization quotient and its lower-dimensional kernel show \[ C_{P_{22}}\cong (D_{22})_{P_{22}}\cong\kappa(P_{22}). \] The last equality uses birationality: at the generic polynomial with exactly two double roots, its unique squared monic quadratic factor is their product. Localization of this finite normalization at the generic prime is its function field. The shift \(-4\) does not affect this ungraded local assertion. The earlier explicit transverse calculation independently gives the same reduced quotient in the full centered coefficient ring; ramified fixed-root lengths are not substituted here.
The prime \(P_{22}\) is invariant. In centered labeled roots the connection has \(\delta_0a_i=-a_i^2-2c_2/N\), so \(\delta_0(a_i-a_j)=-(a_i+a_j)(a_i-a_j)\). Each double-pair diagonal, and hence its contraction to the coefficient ring, is preserved. The regular local ring \(S_{P_{22}}\) has dimension and embedding dimension two. Applying (LP) with \(r=q,c=2\) gives generation by \(q+1\) iterates, through order \(q\), as asserted.
The support theorem gives \(\operatorname{Supp}C=V(P_{22})\) in the full centered coefficient space. It is one irreducible locus: the factorization parameter space for a squared quadratic and its residual monic factor is irreducible and dominates it. Thus, at any other height-two prime, \(C_P=0\). If that prime is invariant, the first assertion of the local lemma makes the first \(q\) iterates span \(F_P\). Finally the normalization quotient has no lower-dimensional kernel at height two. These facts prove the assertion about \(L_N\). \(\square\)
In particular the invariant triple-cluster stratum causes no extra prefix component. This theorem does not classify all height-two primes lying inside the collision divisor: a special divisor inside the open one-double-root stratum need not be invariant. Both such loci and collision-free loci remain possible locations of extra contributions. Localization here bounds order but allows coefficient denominators; it does not prove the required global Ext initial-degree inequality.
A sufficient stronger condition, with the missing hypothesis explicit. Let \(J_N\) be the maximal-minor ideal of the boundary core matrix \((v_0,\ldots,v_q)\). If \[ (J_N)_P=P S_P \quad\text{at every height-two prime }P\supset J_N \text{ other than }P_{22}, \tag{GR} \] then \(L_N\) has codimension at least three, and the conditional equality of the duality theorem holds in every \(N\) satisfying (GR).
To prove this, let \(J_N^+\) be the maximal-minor ideal after adding \(v_{q+1}\). In a free frame, differentiate each determinant and use \(\delta_0 v_i=v_{i+1}-Bv_i\), where \(B\) is the connection matrix. The sum of the \(B\)-terms is minus its trace times the old determinant. Every other term is a maximal minor using the enlarged column set. Hence \[ \delta_0J_N\subseteq J_N^+,\qquad J_N\subseteq J_N^+. \] If \(P\) supports \(L_N\) at height two, it also supports \(C^+\), so \(J_N^+\subseteq P\). The preceding theorem excludes \(P_{22}\) and every invariant \(P\). But (GR), together with these containments after localization (using the derivation quotient rule on units), gives \(\delta_0(P S_P)\subseteq P S_P\). Contraction gives \(\delta_0P\subseteq P\), a contradiction. Thus no such \(P\) exists. The duality theorem now applies. The known squarefreeness of the single boundary determinant \(F_N\) is not (GR); no generic reducedness is assumed proved.
An exact degree formula from the actual prefix cokernel
Theorem. Let \(k\) have characteristic zero, \(N\ge5\), \(S=k[c_2,\ldots,c_N]\), with \(\deg c_j=j\). Put \[ q=\binom{N-2}{2},\quad w=2N-1,\quad p=w+q=\binom N2+2,\quad \lambda=3\binom N4. \] In the free marked core \(F\), let \(v_i=D^iv\), of weight \(w+i\), and define the actual finite prefix \[ C^+=\operatorname{coker}\left[ E=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{q+1}S(-w-i)\xrightarrow{(v_0,\ldots,v_{q+1})}F\right]. \] Write \(e_N=\operatorname{indeg}\operatorname{Ext}^2_S(C^+,S)\), with initial degree \(+\infty\) for the zero module. Then the original first-step degree is \[ \boxed{\ \alpha_N:=\ell_0(3,p+1) =\min\{\lambda,\ \lambda+p+e_N\}\ }. \tag{ED} \] In particular \[ 2\le\alpha_N\le \min\left\{\lambda,\lambda+\binom N2-4N+7\right\}. \tag{UB} \] If the kernel of the natural surjection \(C^+\to D_{22}(-4)\) has codimension at least three, then \[ \alpha_N=\min\left\{\lambda,\lambda+\binom N2-4N+7\right\}. \tag{CE} \] The condition is sufficient, and is not assumed proved. It predicts twelve at \(N=5\), forty-three at \(N=6\), and \(\lambda_N\) for every \(N\ge7\).
Rank-two grading and determinant. Let \(K=\ker(E\to F)\), and use the original excess grading \[ K_{\mathrm{ex}}=K(p+1),\quad E_{\mathrm{ex}}=E(p+1),\quad F_{\mathrm{ex}}=F(p+1),\quad C^+_{\mathrm{ex}}=C^+(p+1). \] The exact leading-coordinate sequence is \[ 0\longrightarrow S(-\lambda+1)\xrightarrow{G}K_{\mathrm{ex}} \longrightarrow I_N^c\longrightarrow0. \tag{KS} \] Here \(G\) is the primitive core boundary relation with a zero last coordinate; its original boundary excess \(\lambda\) decreases by one when regarded at the next order. The centered threshold dictionary identifies \(\operatorname{indeg}I_N^c=\alpha_N\). The proved first-step theorem gives height two for this proper ideal. This is the all-dimensional instance of the earlier rank-two solution-module construction, over the weighted coefficient ring.
The kernel \(K_{\mathrm{ex}}\) is reflexive: it is torsion-free, and the depth lemma applied to its torsion-free image in a free target gives depth at least \(\min(2,\dim S_{\mathfrak p})\) at every prime. Since \(I_N^c\) is the unit ideal in codimension one, (KS) gives \(\det K_{\mathrm{ex}}=S(-\lambda+1)\). The wedge pairing of a rank-two free module gives, in codimension one, and then by reflexive extension, \[ K_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\cong K_{\mathrm{ex}}(\lambda-1). \tag{SD} \] No freeness of this rank-two module is asserted.
Dualizing without replacing the prefix. The boundary prefix is onto in codimension one: the boundary theorem removes every common factor of its maximal minors after passing to the explicit free hull, and the constant-pivot deflation is polynomial. Adding a column preserves this property, so \(C^+\) is supported in codimension at least two. For a finite module over the regular ring \(S\) supported in codimension at least \(c\), \(\operatorname{Ext}^i(-,S)=0\) for \(i<c\): its annihilator contains a regular sequence of length \(c\), and successive change of rings shifts the first possible Ext by \(c\). Apply this with \(c=2\), and dualize the two short exact sequences through the prefix image. They give \[ 0\longrightarrow F_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\longrightarrow E_{\mathrm{ex}}^* \longrightarrow K_{\mathrm{ex}}^* \longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^2_S(C^+_{\mathrm{ex}},S) \longrightarrow0. \tag{DX} \] In particular the prefix image has dual \(F_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\), since both \(\operatorname{Hom}(C^+,S)\) and \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(C^+,S)\) vanish.
The generator weights of \(E_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\) are \(q+1-i\), \(0\le i\le q+1\). They are nonnegative, with exactly one weight zero. The retained block weights in \(F\) are at most \(2N-4\), so every generator weight of \(F_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\) is at least \(p+1-(2N-4)=q+4>0\). The degree-zero image from \(E_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\) is therefore one-dimensional. Its functional is the highest-coordinate projection. It kills \(G\); under (SD), the vector corresponding to it is a rational multiple of \(G\). Primitivity of \(G\) forces that scalar to be polynomial: a denominator prime would divide every coordinate of \(G\). Both vectors have the same degree, so the scalar is a nonzero constant. Thus the sole degree-zero image in (DX) corresponds exactly to the old boundary relation, not a first-step witness.
Set \(\tau=\operatorname{indeg}\operatorname{Ext}^2(C^+_{\mathrm{ex}},S)=p+1+e_N\). At negative degrees (DX) identifies \(K_{\mathrm{ex}}^*\) with this Ext module. At degree zero its quotient removes precisely the old boundary line. By (SD) and (KS), if \(\tau<0\) the first new highest coefficient occurs in degree \(\lambda-1+\tau\); if \(\tau=0\) it occurs in degree \(\lambda-1\). If \(\tau>0\), none occurs at or below \(\lambda-1\), whereas transport of the boundary supplies the coefficient \(F\) in degree \(\lambda\). These statements also cover zero Ext by the convention above. Consequently \(\alpha_N=\lambda-1+\min(1,\tau)\), which is (ED). This counts actual polynomial sections, not Euler characteristic.
The normalization supplies an unconditional upper bound. There is a surjection from this prefix to the full cyclic defect and then to its top normalization quotient \(D_{22}(-4)\). Let its kernel be \(L\). Since \(L\subset C^+\), it too is supported in codimension at least two. Hence \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(L,S)=0\), and the long exact sequence gives an injection \[ \operatorname{Ext}^2_S(D_{22}(-4),S) \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^2_S(C^+,S). \tag{NI} \] The minimal normalization resolution has basis polynomial \[ b_{22}(t)=\frac{(1-t^{N-3})(1-t^{N-2})}{(1-t)(1-t^2)} \] of highest weight \(2N-8\), and Koszul relation weights \(N-1,N\). The largest last-free-module shift for \(D_{22}(-4)\) is therefore \((2N-8)+4+(N-1)+N=4N-5\). Dualizing, all generator degrees are at least \(-(4N-5)\), and the lowest one survives: minimality makes every incoming coefficient positive-degree, so no nonzero constant basis vector can be in its image. Thus its Ext initial degree is exactly \(-(4N-5)\). Injection (NI) gives \(e_N\le-(4N-5)\); (ED) proves (UB). Properness supplies its lower bound.
If \(L\) instead has codimension at least three, both \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(L,S)\) and \(\operatorname{Ext}^2(L,S)\) vanish. Injection (NI) is then an isomorphism, proving (CE). The weaker degree condition \[ e_N\ge-\max\{p,4N-5\} \tag{WG} \] already suffices for (CE), and, together with (NI), is equivalent to its numerical conclusion. Thus codimension-two stabilization is a useful possible mechanism, not an extra obligation imposed on the goal. No equality of the full prefix and full cyclic defect is needed. \(\square\)
Wronskian discriminant criterion
Definition. Clearing the denominators of \(F_{\rm sep}(b(z))\) by a power of \(\prod_i(a_i-z)\) and removing every factor \(z-a_i\) of the result in \(k[a][z]\) gives \(W\in k[a][z]\). Let \(\Delta_W\in k[a]\) be the discriminant of \(W_a=W(a;\cdot)\) as a binary form of the formal degree \(\deg_zW\), and \(\Delta_W^{\rm nc}\) its part prime to the collision forms \(a_i-a_j\); on \(U\) the two differ by a unit. By part (3), at every separated \(a\) and every finite \(z_0\notin\{a_i\}\), the multiplicity of \(z_0\) as a root of \(W_a\) equals the ramification weight over the corresponding fibre, because the cleared factors do not vanish there. Only such finite non-branch roots are used below. The formal degree and the behaviour at \(z=\infty\) and at the branch values are not needed. \(W_a\ne0\) for every separated \(a\): in characteristic zero the Wronskian of a linear series is not identically zero. At \(N=5,6,7\) the computation below finds \(\deg_zW=2\lambda_N\) on the tested line.
Theorem. Let \(N\ge3\). Suppose \(\Delta_W^{\rm nc}\) is nonzero and squarefree. Then on the separated locus \(U\):
the locus \(\operatorname{wt}_P\ge3\) has codimension at least three; in particular so does \(\{\operatorname{rank}A_{R+1}<R\}\), and \(C_N^+\) has no codimension-two support off the discriminant divisor;
every irreducible component of \(V(F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\cap U\) has codimension two and weight exactly two at its generic point, and \((F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\) is reduced there;
\(J_N=I_R(A_R)\) is reduced at every height-two prime meeting \(U\). Equivalently, by the normal-map criterion, every height-two component of the boundary off the discriminant divisor has generic corank one and normal rank two.
Proof. Write \(a(s)=e^{s\delta}a\). By the proof of part (2), \(\mu_s\) lifts to an isomorphism \(X_a\cong X_{a(s)}\) carrying \(V_a\) onto \(V_{a(s)}\). It carries the mark \(P\) of \(X_a\) to a point of \(X_{a(s)}\) over the base value \(\mu_s(\infty)=-1/s\). For small \(s\ne0\) this value is finite and not a branch value. Weights are preserved by the isomorphism, so, by the Definition, \(W_{a(s)}\) has a root of multiplicity exactly \(\operatorname{wt}_P(a)\) at the corresponding finite non-branch position \(\zeta(s)\). No flow line lies in \(\{\operatorname{wt}_P\ge1\}\): otherwise every point over every nearby base value would have positive weight, and \(W_a=0\).
(1) Let \(Y\) be an irreducible component of \(\{\operatorname{wt}_P\ge3\}\cap U\), and let \(\Gamma\) be the closure of its flow-out. \(Y\) is not flow-invariant, so \(\dim\Gamma=\dim Y+1\). For \(a\in Y\) and small \(s\ne0\), \(W_{a(s)}\) has a finite non-branch root of multiplicity at least three, so \(\Delta_W\) vanishes on a dense subset of \(\Gamma\), hence on \(\Gamma\). As \(\Delta_W\ne0\), \(\Gamma\) lies in a divisor and \(\operatorname{codim}Y\ge2\). Suppose \(\operatorname{codim}Y=2\). Then \(\Gamma\) is an irreducible divisor meeting \(U\), hence not a collision divisor. At a generic point \(a'=a(s_0)\) of \(\Gamma\), with \(a\in Y\) generic and \(s_0\ne0\) small, \(W_{a'}\) has a root of multiplicity \(m=\operatorname{wt}_P(a)\ge3\) at a finite non-branch \(\zeta_0\). Take a curve germ \(y(\varepsilon)\) meeting \(\Gamma\) transversally at \(a'\). Hensel's lemma factors \(W_{y(\varepsilon)}=g_\varepsilon h_\varepsilon\) near \(\zeta_0\), with \(g_\varepsilon\) monic of degree \(m\), \(g_0=(z-\zeta_0)^m\) and \(h_0(\zeta_0)\ne0\). Then \(\operatorname{Disc}(W)=\operatorname{Disc}(g)\operatorname{Disc}(h)\operatorname{Res}(g,h)^2\) for the binary forms, so \(\operatorname{Disc}(g_\varepsilon)\) divides \(\Delta_W(y(\varepsilon))\). After translating \(z\) by \(\zeta_0\), every nonleading coefficient of \(g_\varepsilon\) is \(O(\varepsilon)\). The discriminant of a monic degree-\(m\) polynomial is isobaric of weight \(m(m-1)\) with coefficient weights at most \(m\), so each monomial has degree at least \(m-1\). Hence \(\operatorname{ord}_\Gamma\Delta_W\ge m-1\ge2\), contradicting squarefreeness of \(\Delta_W^{\rm nc}\). The contact locus and the support of \(\phi^*C_N^+\) lie in \(\{\operatorname{wt}_P\ge3\}\) by the pair-evaluation paragraph.
(2) By part (2) of the proposition, \(X=V(F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\cap U=\{\operatorname{wt}_P\ge2\}\). A codimension-one component would not be flow-invariant. Its flow-out would be open, and on it \(W\) would have a double finite root, forcing \(\Delta_W=0\). Every component \(Z\) is cut out by two equations, so it has codimension exactly two, and by (1) weight exactly two at its generic point \(a_0\). Reducedness at \(a_0\) implies reducedness at the generic point, and may be checked in the completion \(\widehat{\mathcal O}\), since the local rings are excellent. Choose a smooth slice \(y\mapsto a(y)\) through \(a_0\) transverse to \(\delta\), and flow-box coordinates \(a=e^{s\delta}a(y)\). Then \(F_{\rm sep}(a_0)=\partial_sF_{\rm sep}(a_0)=0\ne\partial_s^2F_{\rm sep}(a_0)\). Weierstrass preparation gives \(F_{\rm sep}=u\,(s^2+bs+c)\) with \(u\) a unit and \(b,c\in k[[y]]\). Hence \[ (F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\widehat{\mathcal O}=(s+b/2,\ \Delta),\qquad \Delta=b^2-4c,\qquad \widehat{\mathcal O}/(F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\cong k[[y]]/(\Delta). \] Fix a small \(s_0\ne0\) and put \(a'(y)=e^{s_0\delta}a(y)\). Since \(e^{s\delta}a(y)=e^{(s-s_0)\delta}a'(y)\), the preamble identifies the mark of \(e^{s\delta}a(y)\) with a point of \(X_{a'(y)}\) at a finite non-branch position \(\zeta=\varphi(s)\) near \(\zeta_0=\varphi(0)\), where \(\varphi\) is a Möbius function of \(s\) with nonzero derivative. By parts (2) and (3), the order of \(F_{\rm sep}(e^{s\delta}a(y))\) at \(s_k\) equals the multiplicity of \(W_{a'(y)}\) at \(\varphi(s_k)\). So the two roots \(s_1,s_2\) of \(s^2+bs+c\) correspond to the two roots near \(\zeta_0\) of the Hensel factor \(g_y\) of \(W_{a'(y)}\), and \(\operatorname{Disc}(g_y)=(\varphi(s_1)-\varphi(s_2))^2=(\text{unit})\cdot\Delta(y)\). The projection of \(Z\) to the slice is \(V(\Delta)\), a hypersurface. Let \(\gamma\in k[[y]]\) be its reduced local equation at the generic point \(y_0\), which is smooth there, so \(\gamma\) is irreducible. The flow-out \(\Gamma_Z\) is a divisor meeting \(U\), so squarefreeness of \(\Delta_W^{\rm nc}\) gives \(\Delta_W=(\text{unit})\cdot(\text{reduced equation of }\Gamma_Z)\) at its generic point \(a'(y_0)\). In the flow box that equation restricts to \(\gamma\) on the transverse slice \(a'\). Thus \(\Delta(y)\cdot(\text{unit})\cdot\operatorname{Disc}(h_y)\operatorname{Res}(g_y,h_y)^2=(\text{unit})\cdot\gamma(y)\). Since \(\Delta\) vanishes on \(V(\gamma)\) and \(\gamma\) is irreducible, \(\Delta=(\text{unit})\cdot\gamma\), and \(k[[y]]/(\Delta)\) is reduced.
(3) The locus \(V(J_N\mathcal O_U)=\{\operatorname{rank}A_R<R\}\) has all components of codimension at most two, by the Eagon–Northcott bound for maximal minors. It lies in \(X\), by part (1) of the proposition, since contact at least \(R+3\) gives weight at least two. So its height-two components are components \(Z\) of \(X\). At the generic point the weight is two, and the vanishing sequence containing an order at least \(R+3\) is \((0,\ldots,R,R+3)\). Only one section has order at least \(R+1\), so \(\operatorname{rank}A_{R-2}=R-1\), and part (4) gives \(J_N\mathcal O=(F_{\rm sep},\delta F_{\rm sep})\), reduced by (2). By faithfully flat descent along the smooth map \(\phi\) (pair-evaluation paragraph), \(J_N\) is reduced at the image prime over \(S\). \(\square\)
The proof also gives the converse direction used in the certificates: a codimension-two component of \(\{\operatorname{wt}_P\ge3\}\) forces \(\Delta_W\) to vanish at least twice along its flow-out. Squarefreeness is sufficient, not necessary, for (1). A repeated factor can instead come from weight-three points of type \((0,\ldots,R-1,R+1,R+3)\), where the contact is only \(R+3\) and \(C_N^+\) still vanishes; the sixteen tested six-root points below are of this type.
