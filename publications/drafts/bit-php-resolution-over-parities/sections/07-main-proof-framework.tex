\section{Closing the proof-size parameters}\label{sec:main-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix \(K>0\) and suppose that an arbitrarily large \(n=2^\ell\) has a
refutation with \(S\le n^K\) nodes.
Choose \(h=3\ell\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:clause-transfer} gives a complete one-level affine
family with a PC refutation through
\[
 D=\max\{7\ell,12\ell+1\}=12\ell+1.
\]
After scalar cleanup let \(M\ge1\) bound its number of proper blocks.
We may choose
\[
 M\le3S+\binom{n+1}{2}+1\le3n^K+n^2+1.
\]
Put
\[
 k=\left\lceil\sqrt{(n+1)\ln(4M)}\right\rceil,\qquad
 B=k(D+1)=k(12\ell+2).
\]
Since \(K\) is fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-asymptotics}
 k=O_K(\sqrt{n\log n}),\qquad
 B=O_K\bigl(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}\bigr)=o(n).
\end{equation}
For all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over the alleged source
proofs, this implies
\[
 D\ge2h+1,\quad
 1\le k\le B\le n/2,\quad
 k\le n+1,\quad n\ge2B-1,\quad 4(k-1)<n.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} excludes exactly the PC refutation supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:clause-transfer}, a contradiction.
The threshold can depend on \(K\) but not on the chosen proof.
As \(K\) was arbitrary, the asserted superpolynomial lower bound follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What the conclusion uses}
The source clauses may have any affine rank. The argument partitions
the actual complete block family by rank, uses one common multiplier
on the high side, and packs every block on the low side.
The final contradiction is the impossibility of an old consequence
\(f\ne0\) in a separated row-linear space. It is not an application
of a degree lower bound for one alone.

Only the old functional system uses PC/NS equality, established by
prescribed-moment extension. The augmented system is handled by a
primitive PC replay. Similarly, only the number of source blocks and
their compact input descriptions need be polynomial. Expanded PC
polynomials and their derivations need not have polynomial monomial
count: the contradiction uses degree.

The value \(B\) in \eqref{eq:main-asymptotics} is sufficient for the
claimed qualitative theorem. This draft does not assert a sharper
explicit exponential size bound.

\subsection{Relationship to the broader PHP project}
This result arose within a broader mathematical research project led by
Kamil Braun, whose main objective is a superpolynomial lower
bound for ordinary PHP in every fixed-depth
\(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege system. That objective remains open.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns a specific subsystem over \(\F\).
The project's research record, supporting code, and autonomous-assistant
workflow are publicly available in the \texttt{math-research} repository,
a versioned archive hosted on GitHub \cite{Repo}:
\begin{center}
 \url{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research}
\end{center}
Section~\ref{sec:framework} describes the archive and framework.

The standard substitution from unary PHP into bit PHP replaces a unary
variable by the conjunction describing one bit label; see, for example,
\cite{BB21}. For bounded-depth Frege it has polynomial size overhead
and a constant formula-depth increase. But an affine expression in
unary variables becomes a polynomial in bit conjunctions, which is
generally nonlinear. This direct substitution therefore does not
transfer the present theorem to unary \(\Res\), and a subsystem lower
bound does not by itself establish a Frege lower bound.

\subsection{An inspectable workflow for autonomous research}
The project also develops an open framework for autonomous mathematical
research: a collection of instructions, tools, and record-keeping conventions
for AI assistants, distributed in the same repository.
Its purpose is to let an autonomous assistant continue a mathematical
investigation across finite context windows and session boundaries
while retaining inspectable evidence \cite{Repo}.
Its main mechanisms are:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Persistent context.}
 A stable restart guide, a concise living overview, and a claim index
 recover the relevant definitions and proofs without reloading the
 full conversation or depending on a particular machine.
 \item \textbf{Recorded successes and failures.}
 Dated, append-only entries retain arguments, assumptions,
 obstructions, and unsuccessful attempts with explicit claim status.
 Corrections receive new entries instead of silently replacing history.
 \item \textbf{Reproducible experiments.}
 Protected execution, complete outputs, provenance hashes, and
 timing records preserve what was actually computed under bounded
 resources.
 \item \textbf{Repeated investigation and process review.}
 Each cycle targets a named obligation, checks its outcome, and
 assesses wasted work or missing tools. Bounded framework
 improvements are recorded separately from mathematical results.
 \item \textbf{A live, versioned notebook.}
 Saved versions in Git, a version-control system, and the public notebook let
 readers inspect the development locally or online.
\end{itemize}
The durable record uses ordinary source, HTML, Markdown, and data files.
These mechanisms make work recoverable and reviewable; they do not
make a recorded proof automatically correct or formally verified.

\subsection{Contributions and development history}\label{sec:model-assistance}
The principal models used were Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Fable 5.1,
Claude Opus 5, and GPT-6 Astra. GPT-6 Astra was the main contributor
to the mathematical development and drafting of this result.
The following account describes the author's experience; assessments
of model performance are his impressions from this investigation.

The author, Kamil Braun, began in a Claude conversation on the
\$20 Pro plan, initially attempting to attack \(P\ne NP\).
He used Sonnet 5; Fable models were unavailable to him on that plan.
As the conversation progressed, the objective narrowed to the less
ambitious, though still ambitious, problem of superpolynomial lower
bounds for ordinary PHP in fixed-depth \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege.
Repeatedly reaching the five-hour usage limit, and dissatisfied with
Sonnet 5's capabilities for this task, he upgraded to the Max 20x plan.

He then attempted research with Fable 5.1. Hypotheses and proposed
lemma proofs accumulated, but no clear route to the goal emerged.
During the conversation, Claude began classifying his requests as
unsafe cybersecurity research; the author reports that the interface
repeatedly switched him to Opus 4.8. He also attempted to continue
with Opus 5, but remained dissatisfied with its performance.
Increasingly frustrated, he purchased OpenAI's ChatGPT Pro x20 plan
to try GPT-6 Astra.

In the browser-based ChatGPT interface, GPT-6 Astra appeared much more
confident and optimistic than Opus 5 and produced dozens of proposed
lemmas and theorems. Two obstacles became apparent. First, it was
unclear whether this growing body of mathematics advanced the main
goal or merely supplied additional results without a sufficient
application. Second, context compaction caused important information,
including previously derived results, to be lost from the model's
working context. The author therefore sought an explicit context
management strategy outside the chat interface. He asked ChatGPT to
prepare a handoff package for Codex, an AI coding-agent environment,
collecting the results and arguments developed in the conversation
so that work could continue from files.

The author created a Git repository and specified requirements for
the research framework described above. Using Codex CLI with
GPT-6 Astra at Max thinking effort, he refined the framework through
several rounds of interaction. Its first version was preserved in
the initial commit of the public \texttt{math-research} repository,
\href{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/commit/2f9568f}
{\texttt{2f9568f}}.

Initially, the author manually requested individual research turns,
each followed by a request to identify friction in the framework and
implement improvements. After several such turns, the need to automate
this cycle became clear, particularly when he needed to step away
from the laptop. The resulting \emph{Spin} prompt, introduced in commit
\href{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/commit/5570c3e}
{\texttt{5570c3e}}, instructs the agent to repeat research, record its
results, assess the process, and improve the framework when warranted.
The author assigned this continuing task through Codex's \texttt{/goal}
feature. At the time this manuscript version was prepared, the agent
was continuing these cycles autonomously under that assignment.

The author's main contribution is the design, direction, and iterative
refinement of the framework, rather than the mathematical derivations
themselves. Explicit resume protocols restore context, durable records
preserve results and failed attempts, and computation tools support
mathematical reasoning with software. The author continues to provide
strategic questions and steering prompts when he notices weaknesses
in the process. For example, he questioned whether successive lemmas
were approaching the main goal or merely orbiting it. This concern is
recorded in the notebook entry
\href{https://kbr.is-a.dev/math-research/\#entry-2026-09-12-column-literal-selector-profiles}
{``Classify column-literal selectors and realize arbitrary joint profiles
within literal OR syntax.''}
The resulting reassessment and route audit appear in commits
\href{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/commit/432269f}{\texttt{432269f}}
and \href{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/commit/603a99d}{\texttt{603a99d}}.
Commit \href{https://github.com/kbr-/math-research/commit/e25983e}
{\texttt{e25983e}} revised the workflow to require each research cycle
to address a named missing implication, explain how its task could
test or discharge that obligation, and state a concrete stopping point.
The Git history and notebook research record preserve these framework
changes and the strategic questions that motivated them.
