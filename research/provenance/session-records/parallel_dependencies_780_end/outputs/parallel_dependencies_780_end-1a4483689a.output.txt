
###wide-rounds-exposed-tree
1. The reordered node
Definition. The wide-round tree \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) is that of the previous entry (the setting with split rows, the wide graph \(G\) and its canonical minimum vertex cover \(C\), the statuses, the preference, the leaves) with the order inside a node changed. At the current term \(T\), chosen at the start of the node: first its uncovered wide rows are handled in the term's order, a row of \(C_{\rm rows}\) by a wide pigeon query over the unused holes of \(Q\) and a row outside \(C_{\rm rows}\) by hole queries at the unresolved holes of its cube in \(Q\) in increasing order until found or exhausted; then, if \(T\) is still neither falsified nor counting as falsified and its pin is alive, the round of the complete-term rule; then its uncovered light rows are moved in the term's order, whatever the term's status. The order of the previous entry, the round first and the tail rows in the term's order, is called round-first below; from here on \(\mathcal T_w\) is the reordered tree. On a reader without wide rows the node has no wide phase and the round is made exactly when the pin is alive at the start, so \(\mathcal T_w\) coincides with the complete-term tree \(\mathcal T_c\).
Lemma R0 (the previous entry survives). Lemmas W0 to W5, Theorem W and Corollaries 1 and 2 of the previous entry hold for the reordered tree with the same proofs. Proof. Lemma W0 and Lemma W2 do not mention the order inside a node. Lemma W1 counts the wide queries of a path by the rows of \(C_{\rm rows}\) and the holes of \(C_{\rm holes}\) they consume, in any order. Lemma W3 argues at the start of the round's node, where the preference is applied, and uses only that assignments and resolutions are never undone; the trigger pair is alive at the round, as before. Lemma W4's decoder processes the node in the reordered order: after the wide phase, read from \(\delta\) as before, it makes the round exactly when \(T\) is alive with an alive pin in \(\alpha\), which agrees with reality, because the rows assigned by the wide phase and the resolved holes agree, the status of the pin agrees, its row lying in \(A\) for a role-separated reader and, for a two-role reader, being assigned by the round when alive and hence never a row moved later, the two-role form's point, and the light rows of \(T\) moved later are consistent in \(\alpha\) and unassigned in reality, so they falsify nothing in either; claims (i) to (iii) are unchanged. Lemma W5 counts nodes, moves, rounds and wide queries, not their order, and Theorem W and the corollaries use only these lemmas and the multi-row transfer. \(\square\)


###wide-rounds-exposed-facts
2. The two facts of the exposed-row proofs on the reordered tree
Setting. A reader of the previous entry's setting, role-separated or two-role, with rows split into \(B_{\rm l}\) and \(B_{\rm w}\). Let \(B_{\rm e}\subseteq B\) be any set of exposed tail rows fixed by the reader and \(B_0=B\setminus B_{\rm e}\); the exposed-row theorems take \(B_{\rm e}=B\) (the few-light-rows and two-role few-rows theorems), the crowded rows (the combined theorem) or an exposed set containing them (the two-role exposed theorem). A host \(h\) is a tail, rows \(J_h\subseteq B\) with patterns \(p_{h,j}\); its reduced tail is its restriction to \(B_0\). Write \(\zeta=\mu'|_{B_{\rm e}}\) and \(R_{\rm e}=R'\cap B_{\rm e}\). For a path \(P\) of \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) with at least one round let \(x_{\rm last}\) be the trigger hole of its last round, unresolved at the start of that round's node, and define the labeling \(y_P\) of \(R_{\rm e}\): a row assigned on \(P\) gets its label; if \(P\) is cut inside its last node at or after that node's round, an unassigned tail row of that node's current term gets a label of the term's pattern on it, and such a row is light, the term's wide rows having been assigned before the round; every other unassigned row gets \(x_{\rm last}\). Call a host consistent (with \(\zeta_{y_P}=\zeta\cup y_P\)) if \(\zeta_{y_P}(j)\in C(p_{h,j})\) for every \(j\in J_h\cap B_{\rm e}\). Every label a path assigns is a hole of \(Q\), a pigeon query ranging over unused holes of \(Q\) and a hole query assigning its hole.
Lemma X (exposed-row facts). Let \(P\) be a path of \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) before any 1-leaf with at least one round. (a) The host of every round of \(P\) is consistent, except for rounds at whose node a light row of \(B_{\rm e}\) in the host is assigned, by its move or by the round's hole answer, to a label outside its pattern's cube; these rounds number at most \(|R_{\rm e}|\). (b) Let \(h_1\) be a consistent host with a matched pair \((i',x')\), so that \(T=[i'\to x']\wedge h_1\) is a term of the reader with \(\mu'(i')=x'\). Then no round of \(P\) has a host with the same reduced tail as \(h_1\); and if every row of \(h_1\) in \(B_0\) is matched by \(\rho'\) to a label of its pattern's cube, \(P\) has no round at all.
Proof. (a) Let the round be at node \(m\) with current term \(T''\) and host \(h\), and let \(j\in J_h\cap B_{\rm e}\). If \(j\) is matched by \(\rho'\), its label is consistent, since \(T''\) is not falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi\). If \(j\) is residual and assigned before \(m\), or during the wide phase of \(m\) as the answer of a query, its label is consistent, since \(T''\) is neither falsified nor counting as falsified at the round, which comes after the wide phase; and this label is \(y_P(j)\). If \(j\) is wide and uncovered at the start of \(m\), the wide phase assigns it, at a hole of its cube when found by a hole query, or by a wide pigeon query or as a bystander with a label that is consistent because \(T''\) is alive at the round; so it falls under the previous case. If \(j\) is light and uncovered at the round, it is assigned at \(m\) by the round's hole query, whose answer ranges over the unassigned rows of \(R'\), or by its move after the round: to a consistent label, or to an inconsistent one, in which case the round is charged to \(j\); a residual row is assigned at one node only, so at most \(|R_{\rm e}|\) rounds are charged; if \(P\) is cut before \(j\) is assigned, \(y_P(j)\) is consistent by the convention. No other case arises: after the wide phase the node assigns only the round's pigeon, the round's hole answer and moved light rows, a wide row of \(h\) is assigned before the round, and every row of \(h\) is assigned by the end of \(m\) unless \(P\) is cut there. (b) Suppose a round of \(P\) at node \(m\) has current term \(T''\) whose host has the reduced tail of \(h_1\). At the start of \(m\), \(T''\) is neither falsified nor counting as falsified, and \(T\)'s pinned part holds under \(\rho'\). The rows of \(T\) in \(B_0\) are those of \(T''\) with the same patterns, so they contribute the same status. A row \(j\) of \(h_1\) in \(B_{\rm e}\) matched by \(\rho'\) is consistent, since \(h_1\) is consistent; one assigned before \(m\) carries \(y_P(j)\in C(p_{h_1,j})\); one unassigned at the start of \(m\) can make \(T\) count as falsified only if it is wide and every hole of \(C(p_{h_1,j})\cap Q\) is resolved before \(m\), but \(y_P(j)\in C(p_{h_1,j})\) is a hole of \(Q\) unresolved at the start of \(m\): the label \(j\) receives later, a hole unused until then, or \(x_{\rm last}\), unresolved at the start of the last round's node, which is at or after \(m\); the third convention concerns light rows, which never make a term count as falsified. So \(T\) is neither falsified nor counting as falsified at the start of \(m\), and it is pin-matched; the preference then selects a pin-matched term, whose pinned row is matched and not residual, and no round follows at \(m\), a contradiction. If every row of \(h_1\) in \(B_0\) is matched consistently, the status of \(T\) at the start of any node of \(P\) up to the last round's depends on its rows in \(B_{\rm e}\) alone, which by the same argument never falsify it; so \(T\) is alive and pin-matched at the start of every round's node, and there is no round. \(\square\)
On the round-first tree fact (a) fails: the pinned reader with the terms \([a\to x]\wedge[j\in p]\) for the pins \((a,x)\), \(x\in C(p)\cap Q\), and the rows \(j\in B\) makes a round at a term whose wide row \(j\) then has its cube exhausted by the round's own hole query, and \(j\) is later assigned outside the cube; the reordered node makes no round there, because the hole query at \(x\) for \(j\) comes first and either satisfies the tail or kills the pin (Section 6).


###wide-rounds-exposed-transfer
3. Every recorded round bound holds on the wide-round tree
Theorem T (transfer). The following hold on \(\mathcal T_w\) with the probabilities recorded: the few-light-rows theorem (\(4n^{-c-2}\)), the combined theorem with its size corollary (\(6n^{-c-2}\)), the two-role few-rows theorem (\(4n^{-c-2}\)), the two-role exposed theorem with its Corollary 2 (\(6n^{-c-2}\)), and the two-role Corollary 1 for sizes below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\) (\(3n^{-c-1}\)); with the multi-row size corollary of the previous entry, every round bound cited in clauses (a) to (g) of the current-term corollary holds on \(\mathcal T_w\). Proof. Each proof was re-read for this entry. The four exposed-row proofs use of the tree exactly the two facts about a bad path stated in their settings, the disjointness of the trigger pairs (Lemma 1 of the complete-term rule, on \(\mathcal T_w\) by Lemma W3 and Lemma R0), and the 1-leaf rule, which the reordering keeps; their probabilistic steps ((K1) and (K2), (H1) to (H4c), (E1) and (E2), the residual-row count) concern \(\rho'\) and the family of hosts consistent with a fixed labeling, not the tree. Lemma X supplies the two facts: its (a) is their fact (a) with the charging of Lemma 2 of the complete-term rule, fewer than \(r_0\) charged rounds outside the excluded event \(|R_{\rm e}|\ge r_0\), with \(R_{\rm e}\) the residual light rows, crowded rows or exposed rows respectively; its (b) is their fact (b), including the strengthened form the combined and two-role exposed proofs use in (H4b) and (H4c), and the form of the two-role few-rows proof, in which a host of \(\mathcal H_y\) with a matched pair has a term never falsified on the path. The labelings \(y_P\) are labelings of \(R_{\rm e}\) by labels, fewer than \(n^{r_0}\) of them, as the union bounds count. The charged rounds are absorbed as in Lemma 2: the final counts \(N/192+2^d+r_0\) for the few-light-rows and combined theorems and \(N/192+2^d+2r_0\) for the two-role theorems stay below \(N/48\), because \(N\ge2^{d+7}c'\log_2n\ge256r_0\). The two-role Corollary 1 rests on steps (H1) to (H3) of the multi-row theorem, role-independent, and on Lemma 1 with no exposed row, which Lemma W3 gives on \(\mathcal T_w\). \(\square\)
Scope. With wide rows, clauses (b), (e) and (g) add nothing to (a) and (d): their hypothesis \(J_0\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) caps every tail's total width below \((1-\varepsilon)\log_2n\), so their wide patterns satisfy the wide mass condition of the previous entry only when \(r(N+1)n^{c-1+2\varepsilon}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\), that is for \(c<2-3\varepsilon\), where the multi-row bound applies; and Lemma W2's mass condition itself fails for a reader of size \(n^c\), \(c\ge2-2\varepsilon\), whose every term has a wide row. What the transfer adds is the few-rows theorems, clauses (c) and (f), which bound the rows, not the widths or the size, and the two-role Corollary 1, clause (d), which Corollary B's two-role form uses.


###wide-rounds-exposed-corollaries
4. Few tail rows, or few wide rows: switching at every width
Lemma Y (few wide rows). \(\nu(G)\le|R'\cap B_{\rm w}|\), and for \(b_{\rm w}=|B_{\rm w}|\) and \(r\ge1\), \(\Pr[|R'\cap B_{\rm w}|\ge r]\le\binom{b_{\rm w}}r\bigl(\tfrac{N+1}{n+1}\bigr)^r\le\bigl(\tfrac{eb_{\rm w}(N+1)}{r(n+1)}\bigr)^r\); for \(b_{\rm w}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) this is at most \(n^{-\varepsilon r}\). Proof. A matching of \(G\) uses distinct rows of \(R'\cap B_{\rm w}\). A fixed set of \(r\) rows is residual with probability \(\prod_{k<r}(N+1-k)/(n+1-k)\), and \(e(N+1)\le N^4\), as in the few-light-rows setting. \(\square\) So a reader with at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) wide rows has \(\nu(G)<r_0=\lceil(c+2)/\varepsilon\rceil+1\) except with probability \(n^{-c-2}\), whatever its wide patterns and its size; by Lemma W1 its wide queries number fewer than \(r_0\) on every path, and Theorem W applies with \(p_W\le n^{-c-2}\) as soon as \(\nu_1=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\ge r_0-1\), which \(N\ge48r_0\) gives.
Corollary A (few tail rows, every size and width). Let \(F\) be a role-separated reader of the previous entry's setting with at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) tail rows, light and wide together, at most \(M\le n^c\) terms for any \(c>0\), light patterns with \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(32rN^4(N+1))\), wide patterns of any width; let \(r_0,c',d\) be as in the few-light-rows theorem, \(N\ge2^{d+7}c'\log_2n\) with \((Nn^{-\varepsilon})^{d+1}\le n^{-c'}/2\), \(N\ge48(2r+1)\), \(N\ge8(c+1)\log_2n+(2r+2)(16+24\log_2N)\), \(s\ge(2c+1)\ln n+\ln r\), and \(n\) large enough for the cited theorems' own conditions. Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) switches \(F\) within the height budget except with probability at most \(4n^{-c-1}\); the bound \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\) of the setting is automatic. The same holds for a two-role reader with those rows under sparse pinning on its light rows. Proof. The few-light-rows theorem on \(\mathcal T_w\) (Theorem T) gives at most \(\nu_0-1\) rounds on every path before a 1-leaf except with probability \(4n^{-c-2}\), its count being below \(\lfloor N/48\rfloor-1\) with the slack recorded; Lemma Y with \(b_{\rm w}\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) gives \(p_W\le n^{-c-2}\), as \(N\ge256r_0\); Theorem W (Lemma R0) with \(Mr\,e^{-s}\le n^{-c-1}\) and \(2^{-N/8+(2r+2)(2+3\log_2N)}\le n^{-c-1}\) gives the total \(5n^{-c-2}+\tfrac{14}5n^{-c-1}\le4n^{-c-1}\) for \(n\ge5\). The two-role form uses the two-role few-rows theorem on \(\mathcal T_w\), \(4n^{-c-2}\), and Theorem W's two-role form; \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\) is automatic. \(\square\)
Corollary B (few wide rows, size below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\)). Let \(F\) be a role-separated reader of the previous entry's setting with at most \(n^c\) terms, \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\) as the multi-row corollary states, at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) wide rows and any number of light rows, light patterns in the range above and wide patterns of any width, under the multi-row theorem's conditions on \(N\), \(800\,\mathrm e\,(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), \(N\ge48r_0\) with \(r_0=\lceil(c+2)/\varepsilon\rceil+1\), and the conditions on \(N\), \(s\) and \(n\) of Corollary A's last three clauses. Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) switches \(F\) within the height budget except with probability at most \(7n^{-c-1}\); the same holds for a two-role reader under sparse pinning. Proof. The multi-row size corollary on \(\mathcal T_w\), or its two-role Corollary 1 by Theorem T, gives \(p_R=3n^{-c-1}\); Lemma Y gives \(p_W\le n^{-c-2}\); Theorem W gives \(3n^{-c-1}+n^{-c-2}+\tfrac{14}5n^{-c-1}\le7n^{-c-1}\). \(\square\)
Against Corollary 2 of the previous entry, Corollary B reaches \(c<2-\varepsilon\) instead of \(2-2\varepsilon\), needs no wide mass condition and covers every wide width, at the price of few wide rows; Corollary A is the first switching statement of the window at every polynomial size with tail rows of every width, for readers whose tails live on \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) rows. Both keep the light range for the light rows, the range intrinsic to extension encodings (code obstruction).


###wide-mass-rows
1. Lemma W2 by rows
For a wide row \(j\in B_{\rm w}\) let \(S_j=\bigcup_{T\ni j}C(p_{T,j})\) be the union of the cubes of its patterns over the terms on it; the wide graph \(G\) of the wide-rounds entry has the edges \((j,x)\), \(j\in R'\cap B_{\rm w}\), \(x\in Q\cap S_j\). For a label \(x\) let the row degree \(d_{\rm r}(x)=|\{j\in B_{\rm w}:\ x\in S_j\}|\) and the row mass \(m_{\rm r}=\sum_xd_{\rm r}(x)=\sum_j|S_j|\); \(d_{\rm r}\le d_{\rm w}\) and \(m_{\rm r}\le m_{\rm w}\), with equality when no two patterns on a row overlap. Lemma W2 (rows). Lemma W2 and its polynomial form hold with \(d_{\rm r}\), \(\mathrm{Heavy}=\{x:\ d_{\rm r}(x)>D\}\) and \(m_{\rm r}\) in place of \(d_{\rm w}\), \(\mathrm{Heavy}\) and \(m_{\rm w}\). Proof. The recorded proof uses the wide degree once, to bound the number of rows adjacent to a hole \(x_i\) of a matching; that number is \(d_{\rm r}(x_i)\). \(\square\)


###wide-mass-theorem
2. Single-row pin-free readers switch at every size
Setting. A reader \(F\) whose terms are single tail literals \([j\in p]\), \(j\in B\), \(p\) a pattern of any width from \(0\) to \(\ell\), any number of terms and of patterns per row; every row of \(B\) is wide, \(B_{\rm w}=B\), \(B_{\rm l}=\varnothing\), and there are no pins. The wide-round tree \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) of the previous entry makes neither rounds nor light moves, so every query on a path is a wide query. A term \([j\in p]\) is satisfied by \(\rho'\) when \(j\) is matched and \(\mu'(j)\in C(p)\).
Theorem V (row-mass dichotomy). Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac12)\) and \(c'>0\); put \(d=\lceil c'/\varepsilon\rceil+2\) and \(t=\lceil c'/\varepsilon\rceil+1\). Let \(N\ge8(2^d+t-1)\), and let \(n\) be large enough that \(4N(N+1)(n+1)\le n^{2-2\varepsilon}\), \(N^{d+1}\le n^{3\varepsilon}/2\) and \((eN)^t\le n^{\varepsilon}/2\). Then for every reader of the setting and a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\), except with probability at most \(e^{-N}+n^{-c'}\), the tree \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) has height at most \(2^d+t-2\le N/8-1\), and \(F\) switches under \(\rho'\) within the height budget. With \(N\ge(c+1)\ln n\) and \(c'=c+1\) the failure is at most \(2n^{-c-1}\), whatever the number of terms.
Proof. Two cases by the row mass \(m_{\rm r}=\sum_{j\in B}|S_j|\), fixed by the reader. (D1) \(m_{\rm r}\ge4N(n+1)\). Given \(Q\) and \(R'\), \(\mu'\) is a uniform bijection of the rows outside \(R'\) onto the labels outside \(Q\), and for a matched row \(j\) the event \(E_j\) that \(\mu'(j)\in S_j\), which satisfies a term on \(j\), has probability \(|S_j\setminus Q|/(n-N)\ge(|S_j|-N)/n\). The events \(E_j\) are increasing and depend on pairwise disjoint rows of the bijection, so negative association gives \(\Pr[\text{no }E_j]\le\prod_{j\notin R'}(1-\Pr[E_j])\le\exp\bigl(-\sum_{j\notin R'}(|S_j|-N)/n\bigr)\), as in step (H4c) of the multi-row theorem. The residual rows carry at most \((N+1)n\) of \(m_{\rm r}\) and the subtracted \(N\) per row totals at most \((n+1)N\), so the exponent is at least \((4N(n+1)-(N+1)n-N(n+1))/n=(2Nn+3N-n)/n\ge N\) for \(N\ge1\). So some term is satisfied by \(\rho'\) except with probability \(e^{-N}\), and then the root of \(\mathcal T_w(F,\rho')\) is a 1-leaf. (D2) \(m_{\rm r}<4N(n+1)\le n^{2-2\varepsilon}/(N+1)\). The polynomial form of Lemma W2 (rows) with the row mass in place of the wide mass gives \(\Pr[\nu(G)\ge2^d+t-1]\le n^{-c'}\). Outside this event every path has at most \(2^d+t-2\) wide queries by Lemma W1, hence that height, and \(2^d+t-2\le N/8-1\) by the bound on \(N\). In both cases the tree is a matching decision tree for \(\rho'\) with pigeon queries over unused holes and hole-query nodes whose 1-leaves contain a satisfied term and whose 0-leaves falsify or annihilate every term (Lemma W0), of height at most \(N/8-r\) with \(r=1\), which is what the height budget asks of a switched reader; no pair space enters, the reader having no light rows (the scope of Theorem W). The last clause: \(e^{-N}\le n^{-c-1}\) for \(N\ge(c+1)\ln n\). \(\square\)


###wide-mass-pinned
4. The pinned and multi-row cases
The alive wide graph. Let \(G_{\rm a}\) be the wide graph restricted to the incidences of terms not falsified by \(\rho'\) at the root (pin matched or alive, matched tail rows consistent). Lemma W1 holds with a vertex cover of \(G_{\rm a}\): a term falsified at the root is never current, so every hole query is at an edge of \(G_{\rm a}\). The decoder of Lemma W4 cannot compute \(G_{\rm a}\) at the root, because the pins at the filling rows of undecoded slots are undecided there; but the cover can be written into the code. Remark (the cover in the code). A cover with at most \(\nu_1=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\) elements, each a row of \(R'\cap B_{\rm w}\) or a hole of \(Q\), takes at most \((2N+2)^{\nu_1}\le2^{(N/48)(2+\log_2N)}\) values, and adding this factor to the count of Lemma W5 changes the exponent of Theorem W to \(-(5N/48)\log_2N+(2r+1)(2+3\log_2N)+1\), which is at most \(-N/8+(2r+2)(2+3\log_2N)\) for every \(N\ge1\); so Theorem W holds with the cover of any decoder-independent graph containing the queried edges written into the code, at no cost to its bound. This is not implemented in the checker.
Single-wide-row pinned terms. Let the terms be \([a\to y]\wedge[j\in p]\), one wide row and no light row; the hosts are the pins \((a,y)\), with row mass \(\mu_{(a,y)}=\sum_j|S^{(a,y)}_j|\), \(S^{(a,y)}_j\) the union of the cubes of the patterns on \(j\) in the terms with that pin. Three steps are available and one is missing. (i) A heavy pin, \(\mu_{(a,y)}\ge4N(n+1)\), has a matched wide incidence except with probability \(e^{-N}\), by the argument of (D1) applied to its rows; if the pin is matched the term is satisfied, and if the pin is alive the term with the matched wide row is alive with no uncovered wide row. With the preference refined to prefer, among alive terms of the same pin status, those without an uncovered wide row, which the decoder can apply since wide rows are never placed, such a term is current before any term of its host with an uncovered wide row and resolves the host by its round, so the host contributes no wide query; hence the queried edges lie in \(G'_{\rm a}\), the alive incidences of hosts without an alive wide-satisfied term. (ii) Among light pins, the edges of \(G'_{\rm a}\) at alive pins, \(a\in R'\cap A\) and \(y\in Q\), need a count of their own: an edge is charged per pin, with the incidence degree over pins at \((j,x)\) rather than the row degree, the pinned rows' residual events cost \((N+1)/(n+1)\) each only for distinct pinned rows, and the events \(y\in Q\) of the pins are events of the flat, the rank lemma's territory, not residual events; this count is to be written out. (iii) The edges at matched light pins are the obstacle. Counting them as in Lemma W2 with the matched status costing \(1/(n-N)\) per pin bounds the heavy holes by \(\bigl(\sum_{\text{light pins}}\mu\bigr)(N+1)/n^{2-\varepsilon}\), which is at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}\) only when the light pins' total row mass is at most \(n^{3-2\varepsilon}/(N+1)\), that is for \(c<2-2\varepsilon\) when there are \(n^c\) light pins of mass up to \(4N(n+1)\); the count charges every light pin although only one pin per pinned row is matched. The repair is a dichotomy on the matched row mass \(M_{\rm m}=\sum_{a\notin R'}\mu_{(a,\mu'(a))}\): given \(Q\) and \(R'\) its expectation is \(\sum_{\text{pins}}\mu/(n-N)\) up to the residual pinned rows, above \(n^{2-2\varepsilon}/(N+1)\) exactly when the count fails, and when \(M_{\rm m}\) is large the wide bijection, given the pinned images, satisfies a term by the argument of (D1) with the unions \(S^{\rm m}_j=\bigcup_aS^{(a,\mu'(a))}_j\) in place of \(S_j\), except that the probabilities are \(|S^{\rm m}_j\cap\mathrm{Rem}|/|\mathrm{Rem}|\), with \(\mathrm{Rem}\) the labels outside \(Q\) not taken by pinned rows, while the edges use \(S^{\rm m}_j\cap Q\). What is missing is the concentration of \(M_{\rm m}\), and of its part in \(\mathrm{Rem}\), over the pinned images: both are sums over the pinned rows of terms bounded by \(4N(n+1)\) that depend on a uniform injection of \(A\setminus R'\) into the labels outside \(Q\), and the rank lemma does not apply to them, their label sets depending on the images. The natural tool is the Azuma inequality for the sequential exposure of the images, whose martingale differences are bounded by twice the largest term through the transposition coupling, giving failure \(\exp(-\Omega(n^{1-4\varepsilon}/N^4))\) for \(|B|\) of order \(n\), useful only for \(\varepsilon<\tfrac14\), where Theorem V and the barrier are stated for \(\varepsilon<\tfrac12\); this concentration, together with the alive-pin count of (ii), is the named obligation of the pinned case.
Multi-row wide terms. The wide graph of all incidences is the wrong graph for terms with several wide rows: for the terms \([j\in p]\wedge[j'=z]\) with \(|C(p)|=n/2\) on disjoint pairs of rows, \(G\) joins every residual row \(j\) to half of \(Q\), so \(\nu(G)\) is of order \(N\), while a term is alive only if \(j'\) is residual or matched at \(z\), which leaves few alive incidences. The extension therefore starts from \(G_{\rm a}\) with the cover in the code, and the satisfaction step needs the greedy row-disjoint families of steps (H4b) and (H4c) of the multi-row theorem, because the satisfaction events of terms sharing rows are positively correlated.


###route-review-conjecture
2. The general claim of the literal line, stated once
Conjecture L (literal frontier on the compact family). Fix \(c\ge1\) and let \(r\) be polylogarithmic in \(n\). There is a polylogarithmic \(N=N(n,c,r)\) such that for every reader \(F\) on the PHP board with \(n\) labels, a disjunction of at most \(n^c\) terms, each a conjunction of at most \(r\) single-row conditions that are pins \([a\to y]\) or pattern literals \([j\in p]\) of any width, role-separated or two-role, a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) leaves \(F\) computed by a tree of pigeon and hole queries of height at most \(N/8-r\) that is admissible for the image lemma, except with probability \(O(n^{-c-1})\).
Proved cases. Every class listed in Section 1, with the exact hypotheses in the window theorem paragraph of the working context and its links; in particular every source of total size below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\) (lemma), every reader of unary terms (theorem), every role-separated reader below size \(n^{3-2\varepsilon}\) with tails in the light range on boundedly many rows of bounded total width (corollary, composition), readers with split rows below size \(n^{2-2\varepsilon}\), with few tail rows at every size, with few wide rows below \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\), in each case with the light rows in the light range and, for two-role readers, sparse pinning (corollaries, corollaries), and pin-free readers of single-row terms at every size (theorem).
Open cases. Pinned terms with a wide row among many wide rows of large mass from size \(n^{2-2\varepsilon}\) on, and terms with several wide rows (remaining); two-role rows pinned to more than a quarter of their own pattern cube; more than \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) crowded rows at sizes at least \(n^{3-2\varepsilon}\); many heavy two-role rows.
Falsification attempt. The recorded negative results on these objects were re-read in the claim index: the pigeon-only refutation at size \(n^2\) (entry), the refuted skipping of dead terms, the counterexample to the adaptive rule, the failure of the hole-only rule for sparse pins, the shared-cube lemma, the code obstruction for extension encodings, the tightness of the dichotomy's failure \(n^{c-2}\), the dense-pinning and dense-cube obstructions, and the (H4c) obstruction. Each refutes a class of trees, an encoding, or a proof method. The one recorded result of the form "this reader has no shallow tree under most restrictions" is the pinned-row refutation for the fixed subcube; it does not bear on Conjecture L, because its family is the fixed-subcube family and not the random-flat \(\Phi_0\), its threshold \(N/(2\log_2N+35)\) lies below \(N/8-r\), and on the random-flat family its reader is unary and switches. The lower bound of the alive-pair matching obligation, height at least the matching number when no term is satisfied, is consistent with the conjecture. Conjecture L generalizes the earlier general column claim. On the random-flat family, none of the recorded results exhibits a reader for which every admissible tree of height \(N/8-r\) fails with probability above the conjectured order, and the shared-cube reader, the recorded hard instance for wide rows, switches at every size by the wide-mass theorem. No recorded instance contradicts Conjecture L. It stands as a plausible conjecture with a long list of proved cases and no general proof. The line is parked at this statement.


###route-review-need
3. What the main goal needs from the literal frontier
The frontier block is a wide disjunction of narrow terms, each the conjunction of at most \(r_{\rm high}\) affine conditions (frontier). In the bit encoding a form is a sum over rows of an affine functional of the row's label, \(L=\sum_j\lambda_j(\text{label}_j)\). A literal condition is a form supported on one row. Conjecture L, proved in full, would give an old-only image for exactly those readers whose terms consist of one-row conditions. For proof systems this is the situation of bounded-depth Frege without counting gates, where PHP lower bounds are classical (cited as context in cycle 174, not used). It is not sufficient for \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege: no proved source restriction excludes low blocks on forms with large row support, the low-block decision shows that restriction does not remove them, and a restriction \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) turns such a form into the form \(\sum_{j\in R'}\lambda_j|_Q+\beta\), \(\beta=\sum_{j\notin R'}\lambda_j(\mu'(j))\), which still involves every residual row on which \(\lambda_j\) is not constant on \(Q\); a form supported on at least half the rows has at least \((N+1)/4\) residual rows except with probability \(e^{-\Omega(N)}\), and a given linear part is constant on \(Q\) with probability about \(1/N\) (simultaneously for all rows that share it), so with probability \(1-O(1/N)\) the restricted form involves a constant fraction of the residual rows, above the height budget; this is far from the failure \(O(n^{-c-1})\) that switching needs. This is the recorded statement that terms containing parities do not switch. Nor is the literal machinery known to be necessary: a mechanism for dense forms would contain the literal case, but nothing recorded indicates that it would take the form of the pinned trees, rounds and covers built in cycles 175 to 214.
Estimate. The chance that completing Conjecture L advances the main goal is put at 10 to 15 percent: it matters only if the dense-form core is solved by a method that composes with these trees on \(\Phi_0\). The chance that the program as a whole reaches the main goal is below 5 percent; a superpolynomial lower bound in \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege is a long-standing open problem, and the route's own record contains a linear degree barrier against universal forms of its general step. These numbers are judgments, recorded so that later reviews can revise them.


###route-review-alternatives
4. Alternatives for the first obligation, and the selection
(A) Continue the case list with the pinned single-wide-row dichotomy. Rejected: it does not bear on the obligation, by Section 3.
(B) Sparse parities. A form with row support \(T\), \(|T|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/(N+1)\), has at least \(d\) residual rows with probability at most \(\binom{N+1}{d}(|T|/(n+1-d))^d\le(2n^{-\varepsilon})^d\) for \(d\le n/2\), so for \(d=\lceil(c+2)/\varepsilon\rceil\) every such form of a source of size \(n^c\) becomes a condition on fewer than \(d\) residual rows except with probability \(r2^dn^{-2}\), and sparse forms reduce to conditions on boundedly many rows. Not selected: proofs may use dense forms, and connectives of bounded fan-in are absorbed in the classical framework by composing decision trees, so this case does not meet the obstruction. It is recorded so that it is not rediscovered as progress.
(C) The alternatives reviewed in cycle 171 (active-window reduction, density coordinates, PHP-relative interfaces, virtual clause source). Their placement is unchanged: none addresses second-level inputs that are sums of low flat indicators.
(D) Re-decide the low-block restriction decision on the compact family. Selected. The decision of cycle 173 was negative for a residual board of polynomial size and asked only whether a restriction makes dense forms constant. Since cycle 179, after the polylogarithmic high threshold of cycle 174, the residual is polylogarithmic (the height budget \(N/8-r\) was fixed in cycle 186); cycles 173 and 174 both argued on a polynomial residual. After \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) every form is an affine form on the residual bit space \(V=Q^{R'}\), of dimension \(v=(N+1)\ell_2\), polylogarithmic; its linear part depends only on \((Q,R')\) and its constant \(\beta\) on \(\mu'\). A narrow term of rank at most \(r\) becomes the indicator of a flat of \(V\) of codimension at most \(r\) (or zero), and a reader of \(a\le n^c\) such terms becomes a disjunction of flat indicators on a space with only \(2^v\) points. Three things change relative to cycle 173 and have not been examined. First, the constants: \(\beta\) is random through \(\mu'\) only when the linear parts of the functionals \(\lambda_j\) vary across the matched rows (functionals that differ by constants give a \(\beta\) determined by \((Q,R')\)); for a form with the same functional on every row, \(\beta=\sum_{x\notin Q}\lambda(x)\), because \(\mu'\) is a bijection onto the labels outside \(Q\), and this is 0 whenever \(\ell_2\ge2\), since an affine functional sums to zero over \(\mathbb F_2^\ell\) and over a flat of dimension at least two. Second, coverage: if the constants of \(a\) terms of rank \(r\) were jointly close to uniform, each point of \(V\) would lie in a given flat with probability \(2^{-r}\), and for \(a\) well above \(2^rv\) the reader would be identically 1 on \(V\), the analogue for dense terms of the satisfied case of the heavy-term dichotomy. Third, the certificate: a reader that is 1 on \(V\) without any single term being 1 has no tree certificate within the budget, and its image needs a Nullstellensatz certificate that the flats cover \(V\), of degree within the budget; for rank one this is linear algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\) (an inconsistent linear system has a degree-one refutation), and for higher rank a linearization count suggests, heuristically and without proof, a certificate of degree about \(r\) when \(a\) exceeds the number \(\binom{v}{\le r}\) of monomials. None of the three is proved, and the second and third are stated for independent constants, which structured sources need not have.
Question D (next step). For a reader of at most \(n^c\) narrow terms of rank at most \(r\) on forms of large row support, under a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\): (1) prove or refute a distribution lemma for the constants \(\beta\) under the uniform bijection \(\mu'\), with the hypothesis on the variation of \(\lambda_j\) across rows that it needs (the transposition coupling of the matched-mass analysis is the available tool); (2) decide whether, for terms with independent constants, the restricted reader is identically 1 on \(V\) with a covering certificate of degree within \(N/8\), starting with rank one and rank two; (3) before either, try to defeat the hypothesis with structured families: forms with the same functional on every row, whose constants are deterministic; disjoint rank-two terms on \(V\) (a tribes function, of full degree \(v\)); and the fresh-block sources of cycles 167 to 170. The universal source-map obstruction gives degree above \(v/3\), more than \(N/8\), for universal Booleanity-valid maps on \(v\) bits, so a certificate within the budget can exist only for restricted or PHP-relative classes of readers, and part (2) must say which. The stopping point is a proved distribution lemma together with a decision on the certificate degree in ranks one and two, or an explicit reader that defeats the hypothesis, recorded as an obstruction for the compact family. Either outcome is about terms containing parities, which no entry since cycle 174 has been.
Estimate for the selected line. About 10 to 15 percent that it yields a mechanism that moves the general step; about 70 percent that within three cycles it yields a recorded lemma or obstruction about dense-form terms on the compact family. The estimate of advancing the goal is low for every available line. The selection criterion is that (D) tests the binding obstruction directly and cheaply, and the next review, now forced by the tool after six research entries, will revisit it.
