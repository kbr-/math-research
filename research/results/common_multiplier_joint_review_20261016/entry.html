Pigeonhole joint savings are matching-domain savings

16 October 2026 · Working proof, refutation and finite checks · Joint-savings goal review.

Question. Ten research entries have worked on joint-error-savings since the architecture review of 15 October, six of them since the last route review, all with goal progress unchanged and the last seven with no bearing at the goal's own parameters (the article tags of the record and the turn guidance of compute.sh start). The open-item count has not dropped, so this review is goal-level. It asks what the line has established, why every saving found so far is support counting or domination, whether the user's proposal of 16 October (prefer a natural map or an exactly described kernel to a dimension count) gives a different mechanism, and whether the next cycles stay on this statement or switch.

General statement. The line's general claim is that the joint action of actual errors can be bounded below the sum of their ranks by their structure, in the protected quotient, with a gain in a sufficient multiplier budget or admissible range. Established on the line: thm:overlapping-curve-recovery, lem:exact-common-kernel-selection, lem:restriction-rank-overlap, thm:shared-span-affine-exclusion, thm:increment-count-exclusion, prop:common-kernel-ceiling, prop:coordinate-count-window, lem:harris-kernel-certificate, lem:greedy-transversal-certificate, thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion, cor:all-prime-small-inventory-exclusion, lem:domain-model-stacked-rank and lem:two-input-kernel-enlargement. This review adds lem:factor-ideal-domination: when factors have a principal generator inside the window, the common kernel of their multiplication maps is that generator's annihilator; check:clique-factor-kernels shows that the pigeonhole joint savings computed so far are of this kind; and ex:support-saving-not-inherited corrects a transfer clause of lem:domain-model-stacked-rank. The reviewer's inertness argument, checked here, shows that the line's pigeonhole evidence lies in a domain model on hole-respecting assignments.

Obstacle

Every joint saving the line has produced has one of three forms: a union of supports in an evaluation model (curve recovery, the domain-only model of lem:domain-model-stacked-rank), domination by one coarser map (shared spans, the shared input of the truncated-quotient check), or a product of killers of individual factors (the transversal of thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion, whose degree grows with the number of distinct factors). The non-domination wording of the obligation was met, on its letter and in a weak form, by the trivial transversal for \(M\le k\) blocks. And the one mechanism found in what the line took for a proper truncated quotient, the single-input killers of lem:two-input-kernel-enlargement, gives a joint saving that the checks below show to be a domain-model saving on hole-respecting assignments, also dominated by one annihilator: below degree \(n\) the product pigeonhole closure is the hole ideal, and these systems are never proper above degree \(n\). What the line is stuck on is a joint saving of none of the three kinds, and it has not yet computed one kernel in a window where the closure is not the degree part of a vanishing ideal.

Factor ideal domination

Statement. Over a field \(F\), let \(C\) be a degree-\(D\) PC closure (members of degree below \(D\) multiplied by variables), \(V_K\) the polynomials of degree at most \(K\), and for a polynomial \(w\) put \(\operatorname{Ann}_K(w)=\{u\in V_K:wu\in C\}\). Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_r\) have degrees \(e_i\le e\), and suppose \(s=\sum_ia_iw_i\) with \(\deg a_i+e_i\le K'\), \(\deg s\le\sigma\), \(sw_i-w_i\in C\) for every \(i\), \(K+K'\le D\) and \(K+\sigma+e\le D\). Then (a) \(\bigcap_i\operatorname{Ann}_K(w_i)=\operatorname{Ann}_K(s)\): the common kernel of the factor maps is one annihilator. (b) If errors satisfy \(E_\nu=h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}+c_\nu\) with \(c_\nu\in C\), \(\deg h_\nu\le g\), \(\deg c_\nu+K\le D\) and \(K+e+g\le D\), their common kernel contains \(\bigcap_i\operatorname{Ann}_K(w_i)\) directly, with no \(s\); by (a) it contains \(\operatorname{Ann}_K(s)\). Under \(sw_i\equiv w_i\) alone and \(K+\sigma+e+g\le D\), every map \([u]\mapsto[E_\nu u]\) also factors through \([u]\mapsto[su]\); since \(s=1\) always qualifies for this, the factorization says nothing by itself, and the content of the lemma is the principality in (a). (c) In a domain model, where \(C\) contains every polynomial of degree at most \(D\) vanishing on a finite set \(X\) and each \(w_i\) is \(0/1\)-valued on \(X\), \(s=1-\prod_i(1-w_i)\) qualifies whenever \(K+(r+1)e\le D\). (d) For the pigeonhole hole axioms \(x_{ij}x_{kj}\), the factors \(w_i=x_{ij}\), \(i\in I\), of one hole \(j\) and \(s=\sum_{i\in I}x_{ij}\) qualify whenever \(K+2\le D\).

Proof. (a) If \(w_iu\in C\) for all \(i\), then \(su=\sum_ia_i(w_iu)\), and each \(a_i(w_iu)\) is a member multiplied variable by variable through intermediate degrees below \(K+\deg a_i+e_i\le K+K'\le D\), so \(su\in C\). Conversely, if \(su\in C\), then \(w_iu=w_i(su)-(sw_i-w_i)u\); the first term multiplies a member of degree at most \(K+\sigma\) by \(w_i\) through degrees below \(K+\sigma+e\le D\), and the second multiplies the member \(sw_i-w_i\), of degree at most \(\sigma+e\), by \(u\) through degrees below \(K+\sigma+e\). (b) For \(u\in\bigcap_i\operatorname{Ann}_K(w_i)\), \(w_{i(\nu)}u\in C\) has degree at most \(K+e\); multiplying by \(h_\nu\) through degrees below \(K+e+g\le D\) and adding \(c_\nu u\in C\) gives \(E_\nu u\in C\). For the factorization, \(E_\nu u-h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}su=c_\nu u+h_\nu(w_{i(\nu)}-sw_{i(\nu)})u\), a sum of members by the same degree count; so \([E_\nu u]=[h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}\cdot su]\), and multiplication of a member of degree at most \(K+\sigma\) by \(h_\nu w_{i(\nu)}\) stays in \(C\), so the right side depends only on \([su]\). (c) On \(X\), \(s\) is the indicator of the union of the supports, so \(sw_i-w_i\) vanishes on \(X\) and has degree at most \((r+1)e\le D\); the inclusion–exclusion form \(s=\sum_iw_i\prod_{j<i}(1-w_j)\) gives \(K'=re\), and \(K+re\le D\). (d) \(sx_{ij}-x_{ij}=x_{ij}^2-x_{ij}+\sum_{k\in I,k\ne i}x_{kj}x_{ij}\) is a sum of Boolean and hole axioms of degree \(2\), with \(K'=\sigma=e=1\).

Relations. Part (c) recovers the union-of-supports kernel of lem:domain-model-stacked-rank as one annihilator: in a domain model every factor family is dominated, by the indicator of the union of its supports, at the degree cost \((r+1)e\). Part (d) is the column structure of historical lem:columnconditioning, whose indicator \(1-\sum_ix_{ij}\) of an empty column is \(1-s\). The degree of a principal generator of two disjoint monomials \(x_A,x_B\) is given by prop:disjoint-product-map-degree (near-half-pc-degree): coefficient maps of degree \(\min(|A|,|B|)\) are necessary and suffice as Boolean functions, so for two variables in different holes \(\sigma=2\).

Consequence for the route. A joint saving among errors with shared factors that is not domination needs the truncation to block every affordable principal generator: no \(s\) of degree at most \(D-K-e\) with \(sw_i\equiv w_i\). In a domain model the indicator of the union of supports is such a generator up to degree \((r+1)e\), so below that degree truncation is the only possible source.

Degenerate cases. \(r=0\): \(s=0\) and both sides of (a) are \(V_K\). \(r=1\): \(s=w_1\) qualifies when \(w_1^2-w_1\in C\), and (a) is trivial. A duplicated factor changes neither side. \(s=1\) satisfies the factorization in (b) for every family, which is why (b)'s factorization carries no content of its own. When \(K+\sigma+e>D\) the lemma says nothing.

Nonvacuity. In Pang's matched ledger with \(p=2\), \(d=t=1\): for (a), the pigeonhole system with \(5\) pigeons and \(4\) holes at \(D=3\), \(K=1\), \(w_i=x_{i0}\) for \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), \(s\) their sum, \(K'=\sigma=e=1\): \(K+K'=2\le3\) and \(K+\sigma+e=3\le3\), with \(C_3\) proper. For (b), the same factors with \(E_\nu=y\,x_{i0}\) (\(g=1\)) at \(D=4\), where the closure is proper (php_degrees.json: dimension \(4905\), refutation degree \(5\)): \(K+\sigma+e+g=4\le4\).

Reading. In each case the computed stacked kernel equals \(\operatorname{Ann}_1(s)\) for one linear \(s\) (check:clique-factor-kernels); lem:factor-ideal-domination (a), (b) and (d) prove that it contains \(\operatorname{Ann}_1(s)\), and the equality is computed, not proved. The joint saving of these families, large against the summed count, is domination by one annihilator.

Inertness. The second-stage reviewer gave a reason that goes further, and the author checked it. Below the degree \(n\) of the product pigeon axioms they cannot enter a member of degree below \(D\le n\): a member \(h+\sum_i\lambda_iP_i\) of degree below \(D\), with \(h\) in the span \(H\) of monomials with two variables in one hole, would need the top monomials \(x_{i0}\cdots x_{i,n-1}\), which are standard, to cancel, so \(\lambda=0\). Hence \(C_D\cap V_{D-1}=H\cap V_{D-1}\), the degree part of the vanishing ideal of the hole-respecting assignments \(Y\). And the systems are never proper above degree \(n\): the committed check php_degrees.py gives refutation degree \(n+1\) at \(n=2,3,4\) holes (php_degrees.json). So every pigeonhole kernel computed on this line, in checks A to D and in check:truncated-kernel-beyond-support, is a domain-model kernel on \(Y\) (where \(\deg(g_1g_2u)=D=n\), as in A, a pigeon axiom still cannot enter: a nonzero combination of them keeps standard linear terms \(x_{ij}\), which no member of \(H\) and no multiple of \(g_1g_2\) contains): its savings are union-of-supports counts on \(Y\), by lem:domain-model-stacked-rank with \(X=Y\). The single-input killers of lem:two-input-kernel-enlargement are beyond supports on the cube, but they are supports on \(Y\). The next entry registers this inertness in general form.

A correction to the contraction count

Correction. The contraction-count entry stated in lem:domain-model-stacked-rank that domain-only savings “carry into Pang's proper quotient since \(B_D\) is inside \(C_D\)”. The inclusion gives only that each rank and the stacked rank can drop in the proper quotient; the saving, the sum of ranks minus the stacked rank, has no monotone relation between the two quotients, and the record already refutes the transfer. In check:truncated-kernel-beyond-support (\(4\) pigeons, \(3\) holes, \(D=3\), \(K=1\), where \(C_3\cap V_1=0\), so the support kernels are the domain-model kernels), the support ranks sum to \(66\) against a stacked support rank of \(12\), a saving of \(54\), while the proper-quotient ranks sum to \(20\) against a stacked rank of \(9\), a saving of \(11\). What survives is the inequality: the stacked proper rank is at most \(\dim U|_S\). ex:support-saving-not-inherited registers this and corrects the clause; the rest of the lemma is unaffected. The stage-one reviewer found the gap from the statement alone.

Architecture review

Necessary target. For joint-error-savings: on a nontrivial unbounded family of actual errors, an all-parameter bound on their stacked rank in one protected quotient, strictly below the summed count, from hypotheses checkable on the errors and the system, giving a better sufficient multiplier budget or admissible range after costs; at Pang's parameters, an effective count \(c<S\) in \(S(1-\eta)^t\) that survives the adaptive choices. Downstream. Through lem:exact-common-kernel-selection any such certificate feeds thm:certified-repair-composition; through cor:pang-observation-calibration an effective count \(c\) would cut \(t\), and the degree \(\Delta=(p-1)dt\), by the factor \(\ln S/\ln c\). That is weaker than the goal's general repair theorem but is the explicit consequence this statement asks for.

Extra requirements. The approach has demanded non-domination. Restated with lem:factor-ideal-domination, the demand is that the certified common kernel is not \(\operatorname{Ann}_K(s)\) for one element \(s\) of the window through which every error factors, not a union of supports, and not spanned by products of killers of the individual factors, whose degree grows with the number of distinct factors. The first two are the open statement's own exclusions. The third is a change of target, flagged as such: the trivial \(M\le k\) transversal of thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion met the old wording on its letter, in a weak form, and widens Braun's admissible range at polynomial degree; this review keeps that result as an elementary saving and excludes its kind from the endpoint because a product of per-factor killers pays in degree what the summed count pays in rank, one factor per error class, so it cannot give the effective count \(c\ll S\) the target asks for at Pang's parameters. What can be weakened is the requirement that the saving appear on arbitrary families; a saving on the actual error families of one application is enough.

Independent alternative. Before seeing the route, the medium reviewer received the goal, the open statement and the registered result statements only, with no open conjecture registered on the route (review record). It found no architecture that survives the exclusions. Its nearest candidate is an inverse-system Bonferroni bound: with \(\Lambda\) the functionals vanishing on \(C_D\), each kernel is the annihilator of \(E_j\Lambda\), and the functionals \(u\mapsto\mu(E_iE_ju)\) lie in \(E_i\Lambda\cap E_j\Lambda\) when \(2e+K\le D\), so the joint rank is at most \(\sum_jr_j-\sum_{j\ge2}\operatorname{rank}_C(E_jE_{b(j)})\) for any parents \(b(j)<j\). It is the shape of lem:restriction-rank-overlap in the dual, reduces to inclusion–exclusion of supports in the domain model, and near Pang's threshold, with nearly independent supports, its product ranks are second order and change \(t\) by \(O(1)\); an effective count \(c=S^{o(1)}\) needs nearly nested errors. Its second suggestion changes the specification: couple Pang's adaptive choices so that error supports nest; this is untested. Compared with the route: the Bonferroni bound is a transfer of a listed result, and lem:factor-ideal-domination explains where its product terms are large, namely when factors share a cheap generator; coupled choices are the one proposal that targets \(c\ll S\) at Pang's parameters, and they are recorded below for the next review rather than adopted, since nothing yet shows that nesting is compatible with Pang's per-family contraction.

Rank normalization. All ranks here are measured in one quotient, \(V_K/(C\cap V_K)\), and the lemma compares kernels, not dimensions, in that quotient; relative contraction is the form Pang's recurrence already uses, and an effective count is a relative statement of the same kind.

Outside leads

  • Every finitely generated ideal of a Boolean ring is principal, \((a,b)=(a+b+ab)\) (Stone, 1936). It targets joint-error-savings: errors with shared factors have the factor ideal's annihilator as common kernel. Answers. It says when a joint saving is domination: exactly when the principal generator fits the window. Test. Passed: lem:factor-ideal-domination (c) and (d) above, checked on four pigeonhole family sets. Break point. The generator's degree: \(r\) factors of degree \(e\) need degree about \(re\), so in a truncated closure the theorem breaks once \(K+(r+1)e>D\), unless axioms supply a cheaper generator, as the hole axioms do; the test found the break does not occur within one hole. The test ran in a domain model on \(Y\), where it was bound to pass; whether the theorem's conclusion survives where the closure is not a vanishing ideal is the decisive test above.
