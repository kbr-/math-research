                                                   Abstract
          Bishnoi, Boyadzhiyska, Das and Mészáros ask whether a polynomial over F2 that vanishes
      with multiplicity at least k at every nonzero point of Fn2 , and with multiplicity at most k − 1 at
      the origin, has degree at least 2k − ⌊k/2n−1 ⌋, when k ≥ 2n−2 . We observe that the requested
      inequality is an immediate consequence of the multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel lemma of Dvir,
      Kopparty, Saraf and Sudan. The deduction works for all positive integers n, k, without the stated
      restriction on k.


1    The question and the standard estimate
All degrees below are ordinary total degrees of formal polynomials. For a nonzero polynomial
P ∈ F[x1 , . . . , xn ] and a ∈ Fn , write

Its multiplicity at a is

                        multa (P ) = min{|α| : cα ̸= 0},            |α| = α1 + · · · + αn .

This is Hasse multiplicity: all Hasse derivatives of total order less than multa (P ) vanish at a. We
set multa (0) = ∞. In particular, a nonvanishing value has multiplicity zero. We do not reduce
polynomials modulo the field equations, since that operation need not preserve multiplicities.

2    The deduction
Proposition 1. For every pair of positive integers n, k, every polynomial P ∈ F2 [x1 , . . . , xn ]
satisfying (1) satisfies (2).

    Thus the polynomial inequality in the published Question 4.3 has an affirmative answer, without
the assumption k ≥ 2n−2 . The upper bound on the origin’s multiplicity is used here only to ensure
that P ≠ 0. In fact, the same argument applies to any nonzero polynomial with the required
multiplicities at the other points.
    The purpose of this note is to record this direct implication of the existing multiplicity Schwartz–
Zippel lemma. It does not supply a new proof of that lemma or a new method for lower bounds,
and it does not address the other covering questions in [1].
