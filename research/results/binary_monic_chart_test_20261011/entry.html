Spare-row coordinate fibers

Proposition. Work over a field \(F\) and its algebraic closure \(E\). Let \(J\subset F[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, extended to \(J_E\). Consider an acyclic ENS source whose inputs \(g_{a,i}\) are polynomials in old variables and earlier whole products, never in raw coefficient variables. Put \(\delta_a=\max_i\delta_{a,i}\), where \(\delta_{a,i}\) is the structural weighted input degree, and \(s_a=h_a(\delta_a+1)\). Old variables and raw coefficients have weight one, and product \(P_a\) has weight \(s_a\). Every coefficient map ranges independently over the same fixed old monomial basis of degree at most \(T\ge1\) in each row of its block. Fix \(B\ge\max_a(s_a+\delta_a)\) and \(b+TB\le D\).

Parameterize nonzero weights in a chosen complement of \(J_E\cap E[x]_{\le b}\), and fix one marked coordinate to one. The companion chart consists of the scalar equations \(W\Phi(g_{a,i}P_a)\in J_E\). It imposes no coefficient-field equations or additional coefficient gauges. Suppose it has a point with at most one nonzero coefficient row in each block, and every block with coefficient coordinates has \(h_a\ge2\). Then for each original scalar coefficient coordinate \(z_j\) there is an affine line of chart points on which only \(z_j\) varies. The base points may differ between coordinates; the weight is fixed along each line. Consequently the chart ideal contains no polynomial monic of positive degree in any single original \(z_j\), with coefficients in all the other coordinates. In particular it contains no proposed pivot \(z_j+Q\) with \(Q\) independent of \(z_j\).

Proof. Fix a coordinate in row \(u\), slot \(i\), of block \(a\). Permute that block's rows so its sole possibly nonzero row is some \(v\ne u\). This preserves its product literally and therefore every later input and product; all row coefficient domains are identical. Write its original mapped inputs as \(G_i\) and its active factor as \(f=1-\sum_i A_iG_i\). The other factors are one, hence \(\Phi(P_a)=f\), and \(WG_if\in J_E\). Vary only the chosen zero-row coordinate by \(\lambda\), giving that slot the map \(\lambda m(x)\) for its basis monomial \(m\), \(\deg m\le T\). The new product is \[P_{a,\lambda}=f(1-\lambda mG_i),\qquad W(P_{a,\lambda}-f)=-\lambda mWG_if\in J_E.\] The last multiplication costs at most \(b+T(2\delta_a+2)\le b+Ts_a\), since \(\deg f\le T(\delta_a+1)\) and \(h_a\ge2\). Multiplication by another input proves all the new companions through \(b+T(s_a+\delta_a)\le D\).

Rebuild descendants in source order, holding their original raw coefficient maps fixed. For any expression of structural weight \(q\), monomial telescoping replaces earlier product atoms one at a time. An atom of weight \(s_c\) has an already proved weighted difference through \(b+Ts_c\); the remaining factors cost at most \(T(q-s_c)\). Thus its weighted expression difference is in \(J_E\) through \(b+Tq\). Apply this first to each descendant product, then to each companion. All costs fit \(b+TB\); all descendants remain successful. No multiplication closure above \(D\) is asserted. The marked weight is unchanged, and this construction works for every \(\lambda\in E\). A monic polynomial of positive degree in \(z_j\) belonging to the chart ideal would restrict to a monic univariate polynomial vanishing on this whole line. Since \(E\) is infinite, that is impossible. \(\square\)

Scope. This is an obstruction to a particular elimination rule, conditional on a supplied successful map; it is not an obstruction to propriety or to Gröbner methods. It also does not require every successful point to have a spare row. The proof works blockwise whenever that block has a spare row after a row permutation. Raw-coefficient readers, unequal row domains, extra coefficient gauges, and fresh-field equations are excluded. In characteristic two, the scalar change \(u=a+b, v=a\) changes \((1+a)(1+b)\) into \(v^2+uv+u+1\), monic in \(v\). Thus allowing new scalar coordinates and nonlinear monic relations escapes the stated coordinate obstruction; it does not by itself solve a coupled source.

Nonvacuity. Over binary old variables, take arbitrarily many affine-bottom two-input blocks, and parents whose two inputs are arbitrary binary affine mixtures of old coordinates and those shared bottom products. Give a bottom tuple \((g_1,g_2)\) one active row \((1,1+g_1)\), all other rows zero. Its product is \((1+g_1)(1+g_2)\). Rebuild a parent's tuple \((G_1,G_2)\), and use one active row \((1,1+G_1)\). Bottom product degree is at most two, so parent coefficient maps have degree at most two. All companions vanish on the old Boolean cube; termwise Boolean reduction proves their membership in any old Boolean pseudoideal through their actual degree. With \(T=2,W=1,h_a\ge2\), \(D\ge2B\) suffices, and any proper old Boolean pseudoideal at that ceiling keeps the weight. This is a construction at unbounded variable and block counts, with genuine shared mixtures, not a unit-input hypothesis. If all accuracies equal \(h\), bottom weights are \(2h\), parent weights \(h(2h+1)\), and the parent companion ceiling is at most \(2h^2+3h\). Retain these original costs despite cancellations. Two-input tuples are a control, not coverage of arbitrary compiler tuples.

Degenerate cases. With no coefficient coordinates the assertion is vacuous; an all-zero tuple has product one. Accuracy one fails the spare-row hypothesis: the constant input one gives the ideal \((1-z)\), which has a monic pivot. If fresh fields are included, a nonbinary constant in the spare row need not satisfy \(z^2-z=0\); the asserted line is no longer a line of allowed points. A later input equal to a raw spare coefficient likewise changes under this variation even when every earlier product stays unchanged. These are excluded inputs, not exceptions to the conclusion.

Full-grid source ideal

Theorem. Let an arbitrary finite acyclic binary ENS source depend on \(r\ge1\) old Boolean coordinates and earlier whole products only. Each block has \(h_a\ge1\) rows. Fix weight one. Parameterize each coefficient map by all multilinear monomials on those \(r\) coordinates; omit fresh-field equations. Let \(I\) be the scalar parameter ideal of all coefficients of the multilinear remainders of all companions. There is an explicit invertible affine change of scalar parameters taking \(I\) to a squarefree monomial ideal whose nontrivial generators have pairwise disjoint variable sets. In particular \(I\) is proper and radical, and the transformed generators are a complete Gröbner basis in every monomial order. The construction uses the full degree-\(r\) function space, not a smaller prescribed map degree.

Proof. Replace monomial coefficients by values \(c_{a,u,i,t}\) at each \(t\in\{0,1\}^r\). Boolean evaluation is invertible: its inverse is the multilinear interpolation basis \(\prod_{j:t_j=1}x_j\prod_{j:t_j=0}(1+x_j)\). Applying this invertible linear map to output remainders as well shows that \(I\) is equally the ideal generated by all companion evaluations at all points. The parameters for distinct points are independent.

At one fixed point \(t\), define the canonical products recursively. Substitute old coordinates \(t\) and earlier canonical products into each input tuple, obtaining binary constants \(v_{a,i,t}\). Set \(\kappa_{a,t}=1\) if they are all zero, and \(\kappa_{a,t}=0\) otherwise. We prove by induction in source order that the prefix companion ideal equals the ideal of \[Q_{a,t}=\prod_{u=1}^{h_a}\left(1-\sum_i v_{a,i,t}c_{a,u,i,t}\right)\] for its active pairs \((a,t)\), where active means some \(v_{a,i,t}=1\), and that \(P_a(t)-\kappa_{a,t}\) belongs to that prefix ideal. Modulo the preceding ideal, all earlier products equal their canonical constants, so the current inputs reduce to \(v_{a,i,t}\). If all are zero, all companions reduce to zero and the product reduces to one. Otherwise a companion with \(v_{a,i,t}=1\) reduces to \(Q_{a,t}\), every other companion reduces either to it or to zero, and the product reduces to \(Q_{a,t}\). This proves both assertions as equalities of ideals, not merely equalities of zero sets or radical ideals. Shared parents cause no difficulty because the same earlier product is reduced identically in every occurrence.

For each active pair and each row, choose an input with \(v_{a,i,t}=1\), replace its scalar coordinate by \[q_{a,u,t}=1-\sum_i v_{a,i,t}c_{a,u,i,t},\] and retain the other coordinates of that row. This is invertible affine over \(\mathbb F_2\). The variable sets for different rows, blocks and points are disjoint. Thus the ideal becomes \[\bigl(\,\prod_{u=1}^{h_a}q_{a,u,t}: (a,t)\text{ active}\,\bigr).\] These nonconstant squarefree monomials are a Gröbner basis: every S-polynomial is identically zero. This accounts for all pairs, including shared-parent pairs after the proved ideal substitution. The ideal is proper since setting one \(q\) per active pair to zero annihilates every generator. For radicality, it is the intersection of coordinate primes obtained by choosing one variable from each generating monomial. Indeed a monomial divisible by no generator omits at least one variable from each, so choosing those omitted variables gives a prime not containing that monomial; monomial membership proves the equality. Hence the ideal, and its inverse affine image, is radical. Inactive pairs and retained row coordinates remain free. \(\square\)

Degree and quotient accounting. Independent evaluation coordinates require the full \(2^r\)-dimensional old function space; the inverse interpolation maps have degree at most \(r\). No efficient explicit table representation is claimed. In a degree-\(D\) old Boolean pseudoideal the constructed pointwise-zero companions have paid certificates through \(rB\). With an incoming surviving weight of degree \(b\), the sufficient ceiling is \(b+rB\le D\); for use of companion-only extension-field descent keep the recorded stronger combined ceiling \(b+r\max(B,3L)\le D\). Full-grid binary solutions already have Boolean coefficient maps. Exact identification of a weighted chart with the pointwise ideal additionally needs the map \(f\mapsto[Wf]\) to be injective on all multilinear functions of these old coordinates, with those products and reductions inside the paid window. Without that assumption a hard old quotient can kill nonzero functions, and its chart need not be the pointwise chart studied here.

For degree \(T<r\), coefficient values lie in the evaluation image of degree-at-most-\(T\) multilinear polynomials. Those are homogeneous linear relations on the value coordinates, becoming affine linear relations after the row change. Thus, in the pointwise model, the exact low-degree scalar ideal is the corresponding affine linear section of the above monomial presentation. This follows by quotienting the parameter ring by the linear domain equations before evaluating the same companion polynomials. Propriety of the ambient ideal says nothing about propriety of that section. In a paid weighted application one must separately justify its comparison with the old quotient; the full function-space identification requires at least \(b+\max(r,TB)\le D\) and the injectivity just stated.

Empty-section control. For one row, inputs \((x,y)\), weight one, and constant coefficient maps \(a,b\), the companions at \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) require \(1+a=1+b=1+a+b=0\). Their sum is one. Hence the constant-map section is the unit ideal although the full-grid ideal is proper. This is a complete-observation control, not a hard unsatisfiable base formula. For two rows the constant maps \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\) do succeed; the negative control is not asserted there. The example isolates the interpolation price instead of calling every nonlinear remainder an obstruction.

Nonvacuity. Any positive number of blocks and old coordinates is allowed. For example, one input \(x\), one row and one old bit has the active equation \(1-c_{1}=0\) at \(x=1\), no equation at \(x=0\), and a proper ideal. The shared-parent computation below has two old bits, three blocks and two rows per block. The proof works without a limit on source depth or shared occurrences; its degree cost grows with the old support and the retained original source ceiling.

Degenerate cases. An empty tuple is inactive and has product one. With no active pairs the ideal is zero. Zero rows are excluded: a nonzero input with empty product one can make the ideal the unit ideal. With zero old coordinates the same proof uses a single point and constant maps. The theorem assumes binary source inputs; a general extension-field-valued input can have a nonzero canonical constant other than one and needs the corresponding rescaling, which is not used here.
