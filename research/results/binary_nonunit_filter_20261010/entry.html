The inactive component and its paid consequence

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with the local three-state algebra on \(m\) coordinates: \[ A=\mathbb F_2[x_e,y_e:e\in[m]]/ (x_e^2-x_e,\ y_e^2-y_e,\ x_ey_e:e\in[m]). \] Its ordinary degree-\(\le K\) dimension is \(B_m(K)=\sum_{j\le K}2^j\binom mj\). Let \(m\ge3\), \(1\le K

Let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing the local equations, \(D\ge2\). Let \(\deg w\le b\), and let an actual old polynomial \(g\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge0\), with \[ wg\in J_D,\qquad [g]_A=G(z),\qquad G(0)=1,\qquad b+K+\delta\le D. \] No assumption that \(G\)'s written feature expression has small degree is made. Write \(h_w(K)=\dim[wV_K]\) modulo \(J_D\). Properness and weight survival are not needed for the bound itself.

Proposition (inactive feature rank loss). With \(\rho=K/m\) and \[ \alpha_i=(1-\rho)^{r_i}+(\rho/2)^{r_i}, \] one has \[ h_w(K)\le B_m(K)- \sum_{j=0}^{K}[v^j]\prod_{i=1}^{q} \bigl((1+2v)^{r_i}-1-v^{r_i}\bigr) \le (m+1)\Bigl(\sum_i\alpha_i\Bigr)B_m(K) \le(m+1)q\bigl((1-\rho)^r+(\rho/2)^r\bigr)B_m(K). \tag{1} \] The bound may exceed \(B_m(K)\); the trivial bound remains available in that range.

Proof: which vectors must disappear. Use the degree-compatible block basis from the coded theorem, whose proof was reread. First relabel the two independent indicators on each coordinate by an invertible affine change so the selected local label is one of them. This preserves the ordinary filtration and local algebra; every chosen complete-assignment indicator \(z_i\) is then a normal monomial. On block \(i\), keep \(1,z_i\); replace every other normal monomial \(u\) by \(u-u(a_i)\), where \(a_i\) is the indicated local assignment. This triangular change only adds constants and preserves the filtration. Let \(H_i\) be the span of those other vectors. Then \(z_i H_i=0\), and the degree polynomial of \(H_i\) is \[ (1+2v)^{r_i}-1-v^{r_i}. \] The tensor component \(T_0=\bigotimes_i H_i\) is annihilated by every \(z_i\). Consequently multiplication by \(G(z)\) acts as \(G(0)=1\) on \(T_0\). This uses a tensor decomposition in the local algebra before imposing the old equations; no component projector is claimed to descend to the charged quotient.

For a degree-\(\le K\) representative \(f\in T_0\), the polynomial \(gf-f\) vanishes in \(A\) and has actual degree at most \(K+\delta\). Normal local reduction does not increase degree, so it has a certificate from the local equations through that degree. Multiply by \(w\), paying \(b+K+\delta\le D\). Also \(wgf\in J_D\) by absorption from \(wg\in J_D\). Subtracting proves \(wf\in J_D\). Thus the entire degree-\(\le K\) part of \(T_0\) lies in the kernel of the weight map. That map descends from \(A_{\le K}\), since the local equations and their weighted multiples fit the same window. Subtracting the dimension of \(T_0\cap A_{\le K}\) proves the first inequality of (1).

Proof: count the complement. Set \(\theta=K/[2(m-K)]\), so \(0<\theta<1\). In the product degree-generating function evaluated at \(\theta\), block \(i\)'s complementary active space \(\langle1,z_i\rangle\) has probability \[ \frac{1+\theta^{r_i}}{(1+2\theta)^{r_i}}=\alpha_i. \] The total generating weight of components other than \(T_0\) is \[ (1+2\theta)^m\bigl(1-\prod_i(1-\alpha_i)\bigr) \le (1+2\theta)^m\sum_i\alpha_i. \] All coefficients count dimensions and are nonnegative real numbers. Their cumulative count through \(K\) is at most \(\theta^{-K}\) times this weight. Since \(K\) is a mode of \(\mathrm{Bin}(m,K/m)\), \[ B_m(K)\ge2^K\binom mK\ge \frac{\theta^{-K}(1+2\theta)^m}{m+1}. \] Combine these bounds. Both bases \(1-\rho,\rho/2\) lie in \((0,1)\), giving the last inequality when \(r_i\ge r\). This completes the proof. \(\square\)

Why a whole feature-only base loses the denominator

Suppose every non-domain axiom of a locally unsatisfiable old system has local class \(G_a(z)\), and all its actual degrees are at most \(\delta\), with \(wG_a(z)\) represented by annihilated actual axiom polynomials in the paid window. At least one \(G_a(0)\) is one: otherwise a local assignment realizing all \(z_i=0\) would satisfy every axiom. Apply (1) to that axiom. In particular, if \[ h_w(K)\ge\gamma B_m(K),\qquad (m+1)q\bigl((1-\rho)^r+(\rho/2)^r\bigr)<\gamma, \tag{2} \] these hypotheses are incompatible.

This is a nonempty asymptotic test. Fix \(0<\rho_0<1/2\), let \(K=\lfloor\rho_0m\rfloor\), and retain the actual \(\rho=K/m\). If \[ r\ge \frac{4\log m}{-\log(1-\rho)}, \] then \((1-\rho)^r\le m^{-4}\) and \((\rho/2)^r\le m^{-4}\). Since \(q\le m\), the last ratio in (1) is at most \(2(m+1)/m^3\), tending to zero. Partitions with such logarithmic block lengths exist for large \(m\); round the minimum up and distribute leftover coordinates. Thus any fixed \(\gamma>0\) is eventually contradicted. The paid \(b+K+\delta\le D\) assumption remains necessary; the asymptotic count does not grant it.

In any application with a verified half-ambient guarantee at the chosen cutoff, take \(\gamma=1/2\) in (2). Whenever its block-length and paid-degree margins hold, the entire unsatisfiable base cannot be functions of that one common long-feature dictionary while retaining that guarantee. This application is explicitly conditional on the dimension premise at the same cutoff; no new linear-cutoff estimate is inferred from the older sublinear-cutoff coded theorem. This does not contradict coded cleanup of selected parent tuples: those theorems leave the old axioms outside the special input class. Different dictionaries per axiom, raw-coordinate terms, small surviving dimension and a different quotient are not ruled out. The result explains a specific source-coverage failure, not the impossibility of coding methods or of a different lower-bound family.
