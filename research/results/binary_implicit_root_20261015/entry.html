Coupled pivot chart

Lemma. Let \(A=\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\), \(v\ge2\), \(B_i=x_i^2+x_i\), \(k_1,k_2\ge1\), and \(h\in A\). In \(R=A[Y]\), put \[ T=Y-h,\qquad J=(B_1^{k_1},B_2^{k_2})R,\qquad I=(B_1^{k_1}T,B_2^{k_2}T)=TJ . \] If \(0\ne P\in I\) and \(P=T^m Q\), where \(m\ge1\) is its exact \(T\)-multiplicity, then \(Q\in J\). Thus \(Q(\beta,h(\beta))=0\) at every \(\beta\in\mathbb F_2^v\). No such coupled polynomial has a unit residual cofactor at a binary point of the target graph. Moreover \[ I:J^\infty=(T), \] where saturation means the union of \(I:J^n=\{f:fJ^n\subseteq I\}\). Constructing its monic generator in \(Y\) as a small binary circuit is equivalent, up to constant added depth and linear added size, to constructing \(h\) itself. These statements do not bound the complexity of a saturation algorithm.

Proof. Since \(P\in TJ\), cancellation in the domain \(R\) gives \(T^{m-1}Q\in J\). In \((A/(B_1^{k_1},B_2^{k_2}))[Y]\), multiplication by the monic polynomial \(Y-h\) is injective: the highest nonzero coefficient of any nonzero polynomial remains the highest coefficient after multiplication. This works even though the coefficient ring has nilpotents. Repeated cancellation there gives \(Q\in J\). Both generators of \(J\) vanish at a binary old point, proving the cofactor assertion. Translating that point and \(Y\) by binary constants cannot change its zero value; allowing polynomial multipliers in \(Y\) was already included in \(I\).

For saturation, \(TJ\subseteq I\) gives \((T)\subseteq I:J\). Conversely, if \(fJ^n\subseteq I\), reduce \(fB_1^{k_1n}\in(T)\) modulo \(T\). The quotient \(R/(T)\cong A\) is a domain and \(B_1^{k_1n}\ne0\), hence \(f\bmod T=0\). This includes \(n=0\). Thus the saturation equals \((T)\). Its monic generator is \(Y-h\): units of \(R\) are the nonzero binary constants, and the generator is fixed by monicity. From its circuit obtain \(h\) by setting \(Y=0\); from a circuit for \(h\) add one sum gate and \(Y\). No cofactor construction is hidden in that equivalence. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. Use \(F=(z,1+z,z^2+z,w^2+w)\), with all four indexed generators and explicit certificate \((1,1,0,0)\). For \(H=(F\cdot Z)^M\), written using \(M\) ordinary wires, both literal dividends are \(B_i^M\), with \(h=1\) and \(k_i=M\). The lemma applies for every \(M\ge1\), and unused old variables with their Boolean fields give unbounded dimension. Its chart obstruction coexists with the already cheap quotient one. This is a control of the proposed operation, not a candidate hard family.

Degenerate cases. The lemma permits \(h=0\), when \(T=Y\), and every constant \(h\); the same monic argument applies. It excludes \(P=0\), which has no exact finite multiplicity. If a pivot exponent is zero, \(J=R\) and the cofactor obstruction is false: \(P=T\) is already available. Fewer than two old variables is outside the stated setup. A single Boolean point is not assumed to determine a formal quotient.

The actual source application

Retain the two numerator DAGs \(g_i=B_i^{k_i}h\), size polynomial in \(S\) and depth at most \(2d+4\), for a size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) binary invariant probe. Zero dividends and a zero exponent are already settled cases. In the remaining case set \(P_i=B_i^{k_i}Y-g_i\). Its multiplicity in \(Y-h\) is one, but its residual cofactor \(B_i^{k_i}\) vanishes at every binary old center. In Bhattacharjee et al., Closure under factorization from a result of Furstenberg, the small-characteristic power-series-root theorem requires \(\phi(0)=0\) and \(Q(0,0)\ne0\) for \(P=(Y-\phi)^{2^\ell e}Q\), \(e\) odd. The lemma shows that polynomial coupling of the two equations does not meet that unit-cofactor hypothesis at a binary graph point, even if the target-root multiplicity increases. Saturation removes the vertical components algebraically, but returning its monic generator is the original extraction task.

The source's valid preprocessing is \(x_j\mapsto t x_j+a_jY+b_j\), requiring a nonzero top \(Y\)-coefficient and squarefreeness of the specialized squarefree part. These are actual hypotheses, not consequences of coprimality of the two pivots. A binary coefficient choice can still select an extension-valued root at \(t=0\). We make no claim that all such preprocessing fails: if the chosen center in the original variables is nonbinary, the lemma's binary-center obstruction no longer applies. Nonzero scalar normalization and invertible coordinate changes at a fixed binary graph point cannot turn its zero residual cofactor into a unit. This is a local cofactor statement, not a prohibition on other charts or preprocessing algorithms.

A valid extension chart. Choose \(\omega\in\mathbb F_4\) with \(\omega^2+\omega=1\), and \(a=(\omega,0,\ldots,0)\). For the first actual dividend, \(B_1(a)=1\), so the scalar \(c=g_1(a)=h(a)\) lies in \(\mathbb F_4\). With new old-variable increments \(\xi\), set \[ q(\xi)=B_1(a_1+\xi_1)^{k_1}=(1+\xi_1+\xi_1^2)^{k_1},\quad r(\xi)=g_1(a+\xi)+c\,q(\xi). \] The explicit circuit \(qY+r=q(Y-\phi)\), \(\phi(\xi)=h(a+\xi)+c\), has \(q(0)=1\), \(r(0)=\phi(0)=0\), and target-root multiplicity one. Here \(\ell=0,e=1\); no Frobenius root is owed. This chart uses one actual numerator rather than assuming that coupling is necessary. The second numerator remains a compatible presentation, but contributes no automatic binary descent.

Truncation and constants. A conservative degree ceiling for the dividend and \(h\) is \(\nu=3(S+1)^{d+1}\). Indeed the original syntactic total-degree bound is at most \((S+1)^d\); after denominator clearing, each original monomial has old degree plus at most twice that bound from powers of \(B_1\), so the displayed ceiling suffices. Substitution, the \(k_1\) ordinary power wires and the scalar \(c\) keep the chart polynomial-size and fixed-depth over \(\mathbb F_4\). Apply the source's multiplicity-one diagonal expression after \(\xi\mapsto t\xi\), retaining \(t\)-degree at most \(\nu\). Its finite sum uses \(m\le2\nu\), coefficients of \(Y^{m-1}\), and final homogeneous truncation. These coefficients have polynomial degree: the \(Y\)-degree is at most \(2\nu\), and total \(t\)-degree is \(O(\nu^2)\). The interpolation lemma explicitly requires more field elements than these degree ceilings. An extension \(E=\mathbb F_{2^r}\supseteq\mathbb F_4\) with even \(r=O(\log(\nu+2))\) suffices. All evaluation points and Vandermonde weights are constants in \(E\), not claimed binary constants. The resulting source bound is polynomial size and constant added depth over an extension, with no power loss in this chart. Since \(\deg\phi\le\nu\), the truncation is the full polynomial; undoing the shift gives \(h(x)=\phi(x-a)+c\). That final polynomial has binary coefficients, but its supplied circuit still has extension constants.

For this linear-in-\(Y\) application the finite-sum cutoff can also be checked directly. Write \(\widehat P=q(t\xi)Y+r(t\xi)\), with \(1-q(t\xi)\) and \(r(t\xi)\) divisible by \(t\). Then every term of \((Y-\widehat P)^m\) is divisible by \(t^m\). Consequently terms \(m>\nu\) cannot affect the required truncation. This avoids relying on the earlier record's unverified concern about the general higher-multiplicity appendix estimate. The present argument does not certify that general estimate or the whole factorization theorem.

The full binary-coordinate control already exhibits a binary-coefficient polynomial with a linear-size depth-four circuit over \(\mathbb F_4\) but no polynomial-size fixed-depth binary circuit. Thus even fixed extension degree and an already binary output do not justify a generic constant-depth descent. The homogeneous-component control likewise forbids replacing interpolation by free generic binary component extraction. Neither control proves that this particular final quotient or its structured interpolation sum is hard. It may have a binary simplification; none is constructed here. We do not ask for every subgate's coefficients, which the trivial-invariant Taylor control already rules out as an implementation requirement.
