 Formula depth, source dependency height, selector rank, cyclic length, and auxiliary-field dimension are different quantities. The recent cyclic and bounded-probe theorems concern restricted two-level sources. Their rank or field-dimension parameters do not establish Frege lower bounds at successive formula depths. 
 The source majorant does control dependency height: along a nonzero selector dependency, weighted reflection gives \(w_a\ge h w_c\), and every nonconstant first product has weight at least \(h\). A path of \(H\) products therefore satisfies \(h^H\le L_0\), so \(H\le d+\lfloor\log_h(d+1)\rfloor\). For fixed \(d\) and sufficiently large \(n\), \(h&gt;d+1\) and \(H\le d\). This controls the number of stages, not their ranks. 
 Fixed source height does not bound \(r_a\). For example, \(r\) distinct rank-two bottoms on inputs \((1-a_i,1-b_i)\), followed by a parent with inputs \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_r\), have parent selector rank \(r\) at two levels. The corresponding Boolean syntax uses negated bottom ORs, so flattening an adjacent positive OR is not the reason for that rank. This is an admissible local source family, not a claim that these blocks survive every cleanup or are essential in a short PHP proof. The  existing rank-two theorem  already handles this family by a different argument.

depth-uniform-route-verdict
  No finite list of depths justifies generalization.  A theorem with a closed recurrence, or a simultaneous construction for every fixed depth, is needed. We already have a depth-uniform source induction under a rank condition, and Section 2 extends its construction to every rank with an explicit inflation factor. The unresolved part is a sufficiently small budget on the actual general source, not the ability to write an induction symbol. 
 The ordinary-to-bit reduction and original old-degree barrier are available. The recent special-family bounds are useful tests and remove particular hypotheses, but they do not prove the required quantitative source closure. Moreover the exact full-source compiler/profile audit here is binary. The all-prime old base and affine endpoint do not by themselves establish the corresponding odd-prime source application. 
  Next test.  Investigate a local OR-profile step for arbitrary admissible earlier profiles in one common affine system. Can its cost be bounded polynomially in the maximum child cost, \(h\), and the logarithm of input inventory, while keeping the enlarged affine inventory quasipolynomial and retaining every original-input prefix and companion? Such a bound, with a fixed recurrence through the source, would remain polylogarithmic at every fixed depth and close the binary endpoint above. It is an unproved construction target. Test the unrestricted local interface, or exhibit a precise obstruction forcing a different endpoint; do not assume cyclic representation, bounded probe dimension, or bounded selector rank.
