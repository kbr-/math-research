8562: \[ (\ell\mathbin{\lrcorner}\psi)(u) =\psi(\operatorname{ml}(\ell u)), \qquad u\in M_{d-1}. \]
8567: Working contraction bound. The map from \(\ell\) to this functional has rank at least \(d\). A uniform linear form therefore annihilates it with probability at most \(p^{-d}\); every nonzero contraction has least nonzero degree \(d-1\).
8568: Proof. Choose a \(d\)-set \(A\) with \(\psi(X^A)=c\ne0\). For columns \(\ell=X_j\), \(j\in A\), and tests \(u=X^{A\setminus\{i\}}\), \(i\in A\), the contraction matrix is \(cI_d\). The diagonal product is \(X^A\). Off the diagonal, Boolean reduction gives \(X^{A\setminus\{i\}}\), whose value is zero by the least-degree hypothesis. This proves rank at least \(d\). A contraction vanishes below degree \(d-1\) because multiplication by \(\ell\) increases degree by at most one.
8575: where \(\chi\) is a nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb F_p\). Conditional on the first \(d-1\) forms, averaging the last form gives zero for a surviving degree-one functional and one for the zero functional. Iterating the contraction bound gives
8576: \[ 0\le\beta_d(\psi) \le1-\prod_{j=2}^{d}(1-p^{-j}) \le\sum_{j=2}^{d}p^{-j} \le\frac1{p(p-1)} \le\frac12. \tag{SHALLOW-contraction-bias} \]
8584: The empty product for \(d=1\) gives zero bias. This proof works over every prime field. Boolean reduction and the least-degree hypothesis are essential, as the raw-power control below illustrates.
8585: Retained preliminary tensor argument. The least-degree functional also defines a symmetric \(d\)-linear form that vanishes when \(p\) slots are equal, because \(\ell^p=\ell\) in the Boolean quotient. More generally, any nonzero symmetric form with that property has radical codimension at least \(\lceil d/(p-1)\rceil\): in a smaller quotient basis, every basis tuple repeats some vector at least \(p\) times. Contraction preserves the property, so random evaluation is nonzero with probability at least \(\prod_{j=1}^d(1-p^{-\lceil j/(p-1)\rceil})\ge(1-1/p)^p\). The last inequality follows by retaining the first \(p-1\) factors and bounding the remaining product by one minus the sum of its deficits.
