Item 3, the gluing lemma is false. X = {1..n}, Y = X+h on a line, thresholds r_k = e^(k-1),
G_k = {|x-y| <= r_k}, weights e^k. For r_k < h, m_k = h - floor(r_k) <= h; m_k = 0 for r_k >= h;
so sum_k w_k m_k = O(h^2). An edge of length d >= 1 costs at least d, and every perfect matching has
sum (y-x) = nh, so layered cost >= nh. With h = sqrt n the per-level sum is O(n) while every matching
costs at least n^(3/2). Why: a Hall deficiency counts how many edges leave G_k, not how far they go;
the true dual is a potential whose level sets each carry a deficiency, an integral over level sets,
by coarea a Lipschitz-type class supremum, the route's own formulation. Open: a per-level condition
with completion lengths ("local completion", unmatched vertices at level k matchable within
G_(k+1)); the counterexample fails it; it may hold for random samples; test on random 1D samples.
