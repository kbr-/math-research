Affine chart descent

Corollary. Use the source, pseudoideal and structural degrees of companion-only map descent: \(J_2\subset\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) contains old Booleanity, \(s_a=h_a(\delta_a+1)\le L\), \(L,T\ge1\), and \(b+3TL\le D\). Choose a binary linear complement \(Q\) to \(J_2\cap\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le b}\). Parameterize \(W\in E\otimes Q\) and all coefficient maps in fixed binary old-polynomial spaces of degree at most \(T\), with scalar coordinates \(w,z\). Impose a marked coordinate \(w_k=1\) and any finite list of affine linear equations over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the combined coordinates \((w,z)\). The chart of all weighted companion memberships has an algebraic-closure point if and only if it has a binary point in this same affine chart. No coefficient-field equations need be imposed in the premise; the constructed binary maps satisfy them. Original source replay still needs all fixed old generators in the quotient and \(b+T\max(B,3L)\le D\).

Proof. All coordinates of a geometric point lie in some finite field \(E=\mathbb F_{2^e}\). Choose the coefficientwise unital projection \(\pi(a)=\operatorname{Tr}(ca)\), \(\operatorname{Tr}(c)=1\), used by the cited theorem. The marked coordinate and the complement guarantee \(W\notin J_E\). The theorem maps every original coefficient polynomial to its \(\pi\)-projection, rebuilds all products coherently, and proves \(W\Phi_0(g_{a,i}P_a)\in J_E\). These rebuilt images are binary polynomials. Applying \(\pi\) to the coefficients of the weighted membership therefore gives \[\pi(W)\Phi_0(g_{a,i}P_a)\in J_2.\] Here multiplication commutes with \(\pi\) only because the second factor is binary; no multiplicativity of trace is used. The scalar coordinates of \(\pi(W)\) and \(\Phi_0\) are precisely \(\pi(w),\pi(z)\). Every binary affine equation is preserved by linearity and \(\pi(1)=1\), even if it mixes weight and coefficient coordinates. In particular the marked coordinate remains one, so \(\pi(W)\notin J_2\). Degrees do not increase. The theorem already supplies weighted fresh-field memberships. The converse is inclusion of binary points in geometric points. \(\square\)

This extracts more information from the theorem's explicit projection than its earlier conclusion “some binary component survives”: in a marked binary complement chart, the same unital trace preserves the mark. It is an existence reduction, not an isomorphism of schemes or an efficient procedure for finding a geometric point. Affine equations with nonbinary constants and nonlinear gauges are not covered.

Nonvacuity. Take a proper Boolean-only quotient, one old bit \(x\), two rows and one input \(x\), with coefficient maps \(1,\alpha\) over \(\mathbb F_4\). The product \((1+x)(1+\alpha x)\) has zero companion on the cube. With \(W=1,T=1,L=4,D=12\), fix the first coefficient and weight to one by affine equations. Descent preserves those equations and produces a binary second row. Arbitrarily many unused old variables preserve the example. Imposing additionally \(z_2^2+z_2+1=0\) keeps the geometric point but removes every binary point: the nonlinear-gauge exclusion is substantive.

Degenerate cases. With no blocks, this is unital projection of a nonempty binary affine space with a fixed mark. A zero-dimensional complement has no marked chart and is excluded. Empty or inconsistent affine constraints give respectively the original chart or no point on either side. The result does not preserve a nonbinary prescribed row sum. It does preserve every prescribed binary row sum coefficientwise.

The paired-row test

Fix binary old inputs \(G_i\) of degree at most \(\delta\), a surviving binary \(W\) of degree at most \(b\), coefficient degree bound \(T\ge1\), and \(h\ge2\). Fix all rows except a pair and prescribe that pair's sum \(u\) as a binary polynomial vector of degree at most \(T\). Write the pair as \(v,u+v\), put \(U=\sum_i u_iG_i\), \(V=\sum_i v_iG_i\), and let \(R_0\) be the product of the other fixed binary row factors. At \[ b+\delta+(h+1)(T+\delta)\le D, \] the one-block projection argument in the descent proof maps any successful extension-field pair to a successful binary pair while preserving its sum and the other rows. Its projection ceiling is exactly the displayed one; no reconstruction of earlier blocks is needed because these inputs are fixed.

For a binary \(v\), the product identity becomes \[ R_0(1+V)(1+U+V)-R_0(1+U)(1+V)=R_0(V^2+V). \] The right side is an old Boolean consequence through \(h(T+\delta)\): Boolean reduction of \(V^2+V\) costs \(2(T+\delta)\), and the other \(h-2\) factors supply the remaining degree. Multiplying by \(WG_i\) fits the preceding reserve. Write \(v_j=\sum_t z_{jt}m_t\) in its permitted binary basis. With \(K=WR_0(1+U)\), all local companions vanish exactly when the binary coordinates solve \[ [KG_i]=\sum_{j,t}z_{jt}[KG_im_tG_j]\quad\text{for every }i. \] This is one shared linear system in the actual paid quotient, of the same form as the recorded affine-feasibility criterion. The multiplier \(K\) may vanish; \(W\), not \(K\), is the surviving weight for the original source. No new rank estimate or efficient compressed solver follows. Selecting \(u\) remains unresolved. With shared descendants, choices solving this local system can still change every later input, so independent local solvability is not a complete source map.

Binary source row gauges

Lemma. For an acyclic binary product-only source, fix binary coefficient maps of degree at most \(T\), an old Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, and a weight of degree at most \(b\). Each block's rows use the same binary old-polynomial basis. Replace each block's coefficient-row matrix by any invertible binary constant row operation, rebuilding all products in source order. If \(B\ge\max(2,\max_a(s_a+\delta_a))\) and \(b+TB\le D\), every weighted product difference and weighted companion difference lies in the old pseudoideal. In particular successful maps remain successful, with the same weight, coefficient degree bound and original source costs.

Proof. Binary invertible row operations are generated by swaps and adding one row to another. Swaps preserve a product literally. For one addition, write the two old row values as \(A,C\); changing \(C\) to \(C+A\) changes the two complementary factors by \[ (1+A)(1+C+A)-(1+A)(1+C)=A+A^2. \] Mapped inputs and coefficients are binary old polynomials. Each row value has degree at most \(T(\delta_a+1)\), so the Boolean certificate for \(A^2+A\), times the other \(h_a-2\) factors, stays through \(Ts_a\). It follows that the weighted product difference is in \(J\) through \(b+Ts_a\); multiplying by an input pays at most \(b+T(s_a+\delta_a)\). Multiple row operations reuse this ceiling, rather than adding it for each operation, since every intermediate row is still a binary linear combination of the same degree-bounded maps.

For later blocks first rebuild their inputs, keeping their raw coefficient maps fixed, and telescope earlier product differences exactly as in the source reconstruction proof: replacing a product atom of weight \(s_c\) in an expression of weight \(q\) costs \(b+Tq\). Then perform that block's row operations. This proves the claim inductively for every shared occurrence and every companion. Fresh coefficient fields also follow from old Booleanity through \(b+2T\le D\). Raw-coefficient input readers are excluded. \(\square\)

Chart consequence. List a block's coefficient maps in its common old basis, as an \(h_a\)-by-\(s\) binary scalar matrix. Binary row reduction puts it into reduced row echelon form without changing the basis or degree. For each rank and pivot-column pattern, being in that reduced form is specified by affine binary equations: pivot columns are the prescribed unit vectors, entries left of each pivot are zero, and rows below the rank are zero. All other entries remain free. Consequently, after companion-only descent and these paid row operations, any successful geometric source point has a successful point in one of the finitely many such gauges, with its marked weight preserved. Within any specified gauge, the affine-chart corollary descends a geometric point to a binary point in that same gauge. This is a cover for existence, not an assertion that nonbinary row operations act on the geometric success scheme.

The geometric-point covering assertion uses the combined ceiling \(b+T\max(B,3L)\le D\): descent pays \(3L\), and the subsequent row operations pay \(B\). The binary row-invariance lemma by itself requires only \(b+TB\le D\).

Nonvacuity. Two identical binary coefficient rows have rank one and reduce to one nonzero row and one zero row. Their product changes from \((1+A)^2\) to \(1+A\), with precisely the displayed Boolean certificate. For a successful example take input \(x\) and both coefficient maps one. Distinct independent constant coefficient rows show rank two patterns, and adjoining arbitrary independent coordinates gives unbounded ambient size. Rank zero has product one; it is successful only where the weighted input companions already vanish.

Degenerate cases. A one-row binary matrix has no nonidentity invertible row operation. Empty coefficient matrices and empty tuples are covered by the zero-rank pattern. Over \(\mathbb F_4\), input one and rows \(\alpha,0\) change product from \(1+\alpha\) to \((1+\alpha)^2\) after adding the first row to the second; these differ since \(\alpha^2+\alpha=1\). Thus the assertion needs binary coefficient maps, not just characteristic two. Row reduction with unequal permitted row spaces can leave those spaces and is not allowed. Accuracy and original product weights are never replaced by the reduced matrix rank.
