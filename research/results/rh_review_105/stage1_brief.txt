prop:rightmost-point-window-criterion
  Statement: W >= 0 integrable on [0, l], not almost everywhere zero, a(z) = int W(v) e^(zv) dv; Z a multiset of points rho = beta + i gamma with multiplicities m_rho >= 1, tau real, sum over Z of m_rho |a(rho - i tau)| e^(beta_rho u) finite at the two ends of each window used; P(u) = sum m_rho a(rho - i tau) e^((rho - i tau) u). (1) If rho_0 = beta_0 + i tau is in Z with multiplicity m_0, every point has beta_rho <= beta_0, U1 real, R > 0 and |P(u)| <= E(u) on [U1, U1 + R], then m_0 <= sup over the window of E(u) e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) + sum over the other points of m_rho e^(-(beta_0 - beta_rho) U1) min(1, 16/(R^2 (gamma_rho - tau)^2)). (2) If moreover U1 >= 0 and, for some eta > 0 and g > 0, the other points with beta_rho > beta_0 - eta have multiplicities at most m' and heights gamma with |gamma - tau| >= g and pairwise distances at least g, and the remaining points have at most kappa log(|t| + 3) of them, with multiplicity, in each |gamma - t| <= 1, then m_0 <= sup E(u) e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) + (16 pi^2/3) m'/(R g)^2 + kappa e^(-eta U1) [log(|tau| + 3) + (32/R^2)(1.645 log(|tau| + 4) + 1.9)]. (3) For W continuous on the line, supported in [0, l], and the points beta_0 + i(tau + j g), j an integer, unit multiplicities, with a(beta_0 + i lambda) = O(1/lambda^2): P(u) = (2 pi/g) sum over integers n of W(2 pi n/g - u) e^(2 pi n beta_0/g), which is 0 on every window [U1, U1 + R] with no multiple of 2 pi/g in [U1, U1 + R + l]. (4) Under the premise of the Riesz mean formula from one premise, for W the hat function on [0, l] and tau real with i tau not a zero: sum over n of Lambda(n) n^(-i tau) W(log n - u) = a(1 - i tau) e^((1 - i tau) u) - P(u) - sum over q >= 1 of a(-2q - i tau) e^(-(2q + i tau) u) for u > 0, P taken over the nontrivial zeros.
  Assessment: Working elementary proof of (1) to (3): P is averaged against a triangle on the window after division by the rightmost point's exponential, whose transform at a point with real part at most 0 is at most min(1, 16/(R^2 lambda^2)) in modulus, and |a(beta + i lambda)| <= a(beta) <= a(beta_0) for a nonnegative weight on v >= 0; (2) is (1) with a sum over separated heights and a shell count; (3) is Poisson summation, the transport to a continuous weight of the progression named in the fourteenth route review for the sharp cutoff. The mechanism is that of the short resonance-free range, a recorded theorem in the Gram model, carried to prime sums at one height with a pointwise premise. Part (4) is the second difference in the length of the Riesz mean formula and is conditional on that formula's premise. Numerical control: (3) on a truncated progression; (1) in 200 random configurations, a weak check of the direction only, the constants never binding; no instance computed for (2). For exact hiding at equal real parts and unit multiplicities, (2) excludes R g > 7.26 and (3) realises R g < 2 pi - l g.

prop:gaussian-comb-hiding
  Statement: W >= 0 integrable on [0, l], not almost everywhere zero, a(z) = int W(v) e^(zv) dv; beta_0, tau real, g > 0, c > 0, U > 0; points rho_j = beta_0 - c j^2/U + i(tau + j g), j an integer, unit multiplicities, so that rho_0 is strictly rightmost; P(u) = sum over all j and P_J(u) = sum over |j| <= J of a(rho_j - i tau) e^((rho_j - i tau) u). (1) For u > 0: P(u) = int W(v) e^(beta_0 x) Theta(x) dv with x = u + v and Theta(x) = sqrt(pi U/(c x)) sum over integers n of exp(-U (g x - 2 pi n)^2/(4 c x)) > 0. (2) If 0 < d <= pi, R >= 0, g x is at distance at least d from every multiple of 2 pi for U <= x <= U + R + l, and A = c (U + R + l)/U <= d^2/2, then 0 < P(u) e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) <= 4.2 A^(-1/2) e^(-d^2/(4A)) for U <= u <= U + R. (3) For J >= 1 and u >= U: |P(u) - P_J(u)| e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) <= e^(-c J^2)/(c J). (4) Hence for Lam >= 1/2, with A = d^2/(4 Lam), c = A U/(U + R + l), r = (R + l)/U and J the least integer at least sqrt(Lam/c): the 2J + 1 <= (4 Lam/d) sqrt(1 + r) + 3 points with |j| <= J, of heights within J g of tau, have |P_J(u)| e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) <= (8.4 sqrt(Lam) + 2 sqrt(1 + r)) e^(-Lam)/d on [U, U + R]. A spacing g meeting the hypothesis of (2) exists if and only if d (2 + r) <= 2 pi, and then g = d/U is one.
  Assessment: Working elementary proof: Poisson summation for a Gaussian turns the sum over the comb into a theta function whose peaks sit where g x is a multiple of 2 pi and whose width is governed by the fall of the real parts; between the peaks it is exponentially small; the points far along the comb are exponentially lower and are cut off. The construction was suggested by the reviewer of the rightmost point window criterion and is verified here with constants. Numerical control at one instance (21 points): the supremum on the window against the bound, and the direct sum against the closed form by an independent quadrature, on the window and near a peak. It is a statement about a model configuration, not about zeros of zeta: it shows that in the model a count of near-tied points of the order of the logarithm of the required accuracy suffices to hide a strictly rightmost point, so an exclusion from a bound on such sums must assume fewer; whether as many are necessary is not proved here.

prop:window-power-sum-count
  Statement: W >= 0 integrable on [0, l], not almost everywhere zero, a(z) = int W(v) e^(zv) dv; a finite set of n points z_j = beta_j + i gamma_j with multiplicities m_j > 0 and beta_j <= beta_0, one of them rho_0 = beta_0 + i tau with multiplicity m_0; P(u) = sum m_j a(z_j - i tau) e^((z_j - i tau) u); U >= 0, R > 0. Hypothesis F: the points with |gamma_j - tau| > pi/(2l) have sum of m_j |a(z_j - i tau)| at most phi a(beta_0) m_0 with 0 <= phi < 1. (1) Then sup over [U, U + R] of |P(u)| e^(-beta_0 u)/a(beta_0) >= 2 (1 - phi) m_0 (R/(8e(U + R)))^n. (2) If the points are split into Z1, with n1 points containing rho_0 and satisfying F among themselves, and Z2 with beta_j <= beta_0 - eta for some eta >= 0 and S2 = sum over Z2 of m_j |a(z_j - i tau)|, then that supremum is at least 2 (1 - phi) m_0 (R/(8e(U + R)))^n1 - (S2/a(beta_0)) e^(-eta U). (3) Hence if the supremum is at most eps m_0 and (S2/a(beta_0)) e^(-eta U) <= eps m_0 with 0 < eps < (1 - phi), then n1 >= log((1 - phi)/eps)/log(8e(U + R)/R); n1 counts every point of Z1, at near and far heights. (4) Without F: for n pairs gamma_j +- i delta_j, delta_1 >= delta_j > 0, m_j > 0, S(s) = sum m_j (e^(-is(gamma_j - i delta_j)) + e^(-is(gamma_j + i delta_j))), every h > 0 and integer m >= 0, some s = nu h with m < nu <= m + 2n has |S(s)| >= 2 m_1 e^(delta_1 s) (2n/(8e(m + 2n)))^(2n).
  Assessment: Working proof from one published theorem, Turan's second main theorem (Theorem 8.1 of his 1984 book, read as page images in an earlier cycle, quoted in the notebook's working context, not reread here): the sum is sampled at equally spaced lengths, where it is a power sum with nodes of modulus at most one, and the partial sums of the coefficients are bounded below, in (1) because the weight's transform has nonnegative real part at heights within pi/(2l) and the far points are assumed light, in (4) because the coefficients are positive; coincident nodes are merged. Part (2) is the triangle inequality. An earlier entry of this notebook outlined this use of the theorem, with the positivity at near heights and the obstruction from far heights, and left it unverified; this carries it out with the obstruction assumed away by F. F is itself an assumption of sparsity: many near-tied points at far heights violate it. With the Gaussian comb the number of points needed to hide to e^(-Lam) is of order Lam in the model from both sides, under the condition that the comb's heights are near and up to its prefactor; the constants differ by about 7.5 at r = 1/4 in the limit of a short weight.

prop:twist-averaged-extraction

(1) If for each w in a set W0 there are a number A^(w), a set P_w of v with (v, w) in R, and E_w >= 0 with |A_(v,w) - A^(w)| <= E_w for v in P_w, then sum over w in W0 of |P_w| (|A^(w)| - E_w)_+^2 <= M. (2) If 1 denotes one value of w, B_v = A_(v,1) for v in V = {v : (v,1) in R}, and for each v in V the rows (v, w) with w != 1 have sum of |A_(v,w)|^2 at most S_v, rows with v outside V being absent, then sum over V of |B_v|^2 <= M <= sum over V of |B_v|^2 + sum over V of S_v; and if |B_v - A| <= E on V then |V| (|A| - E)_+^2 <= M <= 2 |V| (|A|^2 + E^2) + sum of S_v. (3) Exponents: with D >= 2, H = D^beta, k >= 1, |P_w| >= c (H/N(w))^(1/k) for the w in W0, all of norm N(w) <= D^gamma, and M <= C D^(1+eps) H, part (1) gives sum over W0 of (|A^(w)| - E_w)_+^2 <= (C/c) D^(1 + eps + beta - (beta - gamma)/k), so for |W0| >= c' D^gamma a mean value at most (C/(c c')) D^(1 + eps + (beta - gamma)(1 - 1/k)); with S_v = C D^(1+eps)(H N(v)^(-k) + D) the upper bound of (2) is 2|V|(|A|^2 + E^2) + C D^(1+eps)(H sum N(v)^(-k) + D |V|). (4) Heights: for complex a_n, D < n <= 2D, A(t) = sum a_n n^(-it), S = sum |a_n| and 0 < E: |A(t) - A(0)| <= E for |t| <= delta = E/(S log(2D)); so if the integral of |A|^2 over [-T, T], T >= delta, is at most B, then |A(0)| <= (B S log(2D)/(2E))^(1/2) + E; with |a_n| = 1 for all D values of n and B >= D^2 this bound is at least 0.83 D for every E.

prop:moment-extraction-exponents
  Statement: Let k, m >= 1, beta > 0, H = D^beta, and complex A, A_u (u in P) with |P| >= H^(1/k) D^-eps / C, |A - A_u| <= E and sum over P of |A_u|^(2m) <= C D^(m+eps) H. Then |A| <= C^(1/m) D^(1/2 + beta(1-1/k)/(2m) + eps/m) + E. With E <= D^(1-beta/k) the best exponent over beta is (k-1+m)/(k-1+2m); for beta >= m the first exponent is at least 1 - 1/(2k) for every m.
  Assessment: Working elementary proof with exponent arithmetic. Applied to the imported sextic family (k = 6, m = 1, beta slightly above 1) it reproduces the source's 11/12. For beta >= m the exponent is at best 1 - 1/(2k). For beta < m the moment hypothesis alone, from one row, gives every member the exponent 1/2 + beta/(2m), so the extraction only improves a saving the hypothesis already contains. For Moebius coefficients the defect unfolds into sums of the same kind with the same excluded set, so an induction over scales removes it.
