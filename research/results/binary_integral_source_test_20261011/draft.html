Matching nuclear dominance

Lemma. Let \(\mathcal O\) be \(\mathbb Z\) or the Eisenstein integers with the complex embedding and residue fields used in the arithmetic-rank lemma. Let \(M\) be a finite rectangular matrix over its residue field and \(A\) any matrix over \(\mathcal O\) reducing to \(M\). Let \(\nu(M)\) be the maximum size of a matching in the bipartite graph whose edges are the nonzero entries of \(M\). Then \[\operatorname{rank}M\le\nu(M)\le\|A\|_* .\] Consequently any strict rank upper bound obtained from \(\|A\|_*\) is already implied by the stronger support-matching upper bound, in that same basis. This statement also applies after any specified basis change, with \(M\) and its lift both changed consistently.

Proof. If an \(r\)-minor is nonzero, its determinant expansion has a nonzero product selecting entries in distinct rows and columns, so \(r\le\nu(M)\). This proves the first inequality over every field, without any assertion that a matching gives a nonzero determinant.

Choose a maximum matching \((i_a,j_a)\), \(1\le a\le\nu\). Each \(A_{i_a j_a}\) is a nonzero lattice element, hence has complex modulus at least one. Form a rectangular partial permutation matrix \(P\) supported at these positions, with entries \(P_{i_a j_a}=A_{i_a j_a}/|A_{i_a j_a}|\). Its operator norm is one when \(\nu>0\), and \[\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(P^*A)=\sum_a|A_{i_a j_a}|\ge\nu.\] For a singular-value decomposition \(A=\sum_b\sigma_b u_bv_b^*\), \[|\operatorname{tr}(P^*A)|\le\sum_b\sigma_b|v_b^*P^*u_b|\le\sum_b\sigma_b=\|A\|_* .\] The zero matching case is immediate. No arithmetic property of \(P\) is required: it is only a dual witness for the real/complex norm. Extra entries of the lift reducing to zero cannot invalidate this witness. Odd denominator \(q\) gives \(\nu(M)\le\|qA\|_*=q\|A\|_*\); an explicit divided integral lift of the deflation matrix has exactly the same comparison. Thus denominator clearing and precision do not evade the result. \(\square\)

This does not say that a norm calculation can never help prove a combinatorial statement about an implicit matrix. It says that the norm threshold supplies no rank-deficit criterion beyond its zero-pattern matching. In particular it cannot reveal finite-field cancellation in a support pattern that already has a full column matching. This is stronger than the previous square-root support floor: an identity matrix has only \(H\) nonzero entries but still forces norm at least \(H\).

Charged matching saturation

Corollary. Work in the proper charged character pseudoideal over \(\mathbb F_4\) with arbitrary charge vector, \(\max(4,2\Delta)\le D<\varepsilon n/8\), its fixed cut-code class coordinates and bounded multiplication. Let \(w\) have degree at most \(b\), and let \(E_1,\ldots,E_t\) be any old error polynomials of degree at most \(e\), with \[b+K+e\le D,\qquad H=\dim[wV_K]>0.\] Choose a basis of \([wV_K]\) from its character translates \([w m_\alpha]\), \(|\alpha|\le K\), and express the error map on those columns in the fixed output character-class basis. If any \([wE_i]\ne0\), that coordinate error map, hence the complete stacked map, has a matching of size \(H\). Every arithmetic lift of this matrix has nuclear norm at least \(H\). If every \([wE_i]=0\), the map is zero by bounded absorption.

Proof. Characters of degree at most \(K\) span the domain, so their weighted images contain a basis of size \(H\). The selected characters must have distinct cut-code cosets: characters in one coset are proportional in \(Q_K\), and multiplying by \(w\) preserves that relation through \(b+K\le D\). Fix an error with \([wE_i]\ne0\), and collect \(F=wE_i\) by output class, using representatives of degree at most \(b+e\): \[[F]=\sum_{j=1}^s c_j[m_{\beta_j}],\qquad c_j\ne0,\] with distinct cut-code labels. The difference from this representative has degree at most \(b+e\) and lies in the pseudoideal, so multiplying by each selected character is legal through \(b+e+K\le D\). In column \(\alpha\), the summands have labels \(\alpha+\beta_j\); they are distinct, and their charged phases are nonzero. Fix one \(j_0\). For every selected column, the row labelled \(\alpha+\beta_{j_0}\) has a nonzero entry. These rows are distinct across the columns because translation of distinct cut-code cosets is injective. They form the required matching.

This is precisely the support calculation in the full proof of lem:sparse-weight-survival, now applied to \(F=wE_i\) and a selected subset of domain characters. The cut-code dictionary and arbitrary-charge extension were reread; all translated characters stay within the ceiling, so no global charged algebra or satisfying-point model is used. Apply the preceding lemma for the norm consequence. If every \(wE_i\) vanishes, absorption gives all columns zero. \(\square\)
