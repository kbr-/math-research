\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Polynomials that vanish to high order on a grid are a basic tool of the
polynomial method. Over the Boolean cube a natural extremal question asks how
small the degree of such a polynomial can be when it is required to vanish to
order \(k\) everywhere except at one point. Over \(\mathbb R\) this question was
answered by Sauermann and Wigderson~\cite{SW20}: the least degree of
\(P\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with \(P(\zero)\ne0\) and zeros of multiplicity
at least \(k\ge2\) at all other points of \(\{0,1\}^n\) is \(n+2k-3\) when
\(n\ge2k-3\) \cite[Theorem~1.3]{SW20}. They observed
that the answer depends on the field and that characteristic two behaves
differently. Menezes~\cite{Men26} determined the least degree over every field
when \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge k-1\), also prescribing the order of vanishing at the
origin.

This paper determines the binary case in every dimension. Throughout,
multiplicity means Hasse multiplicity of formal polynomials
(Definition~\ref{def:mult}); in particular no polynomial is reduced modulo
\(x_i^2-x_i\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:admissible}
Let \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\). For \(0\le\ell<k\), let \(\delta(n,k,\ell)\) be the
least total degree of a polynomial \(P\in\Ft[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) with
\(\mult_a(P)\ge k\) for every \(a\in\Ft^n\setminus\{\zero\}\) and
\(\mult_{\zero}(P)=\ell\); we call \(\mult_{\zero}(P)\) the \emph{origin order} of
\(P\). A polynomial is \emph{\(k\)-admissible} if
\(\mult_a(P)\ge k\) for every nonzero \(a\) and \(\mult_{\zero}(P)\le k-1\)
\lean{claims/BinaryMultiplicityDegreeFormula.lean}, and \(D(n,k)\) is the least
total degree of a \(k\)-admissible polynomial. Thus
\(D(n,k)=\min_{0\le\ell<k}\delta(n,k,\ell)\).
\end{definition}

The condition at the origin excludes \(P=0\)
\lean{claims/DegreeFormulaArithmetic.lean} and allows any origin order below
\(k\), not only a nonzero value at the origin.
Bishnoi, Boyadzhiyska, Das and M\'esz\'aros~\cite[Question~4.3]{BBDM23} asked,
in the study of subspace coverings with multiplicities, whether
\(D(n,k)\ge2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) when \(k\ge2^{n-2}\). This bound
follows for all \(n\) and \(k\) from the multiplicity Schwartz--Zippel lemma of Dvir,
Kopparty, Saraf and Sudan~\cite{DKSS13}; see Proposition~\ref{prop:cover-bound},
which was the content of an earlier note~\cite{Note}. Bishnoi et al.\ also
construct multisets of \(2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) affine hyperplanes that
cover every nonzero point at least \(k\) times and the origin at most \(k-1\)
times, for \(k\ge2^{n-2}\) \cite[Theorem~1.2(a) of arXiv v1]{BBDM23}, and the
bound is attained for \(k\ge2^{n-2}\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:main}).

\subsection{Main results}

For integers \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\) put
\[
 \Phi(n,k,\ell)=n+2\ell+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{k-\ell-1}{2^j}\Bigr\rfloor .
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For all integers \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le\ell<k\),
\(\delta(n,k,\ell)=\Phi(n,k,\ell)\): some polynomial with multiplicity at least
\(k\) at every nonzero point of \(\Ft^n\) and exactly \(\ell\) at the origin has
degree \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)\), and every such polynomial has degree at least
\(\Phi(n,k,\ell)\) \lean{claims/PerOrderValue.lean}.
\end{theorem}

Write \(k-\ell-1=q2^n+r\) with \(0\le r<2^n\), and let \(s_2\) be the binary
digit sum. By Legendre's formula (Lemma~\ref{lem:legendre}),
\[
 \Phi(n,k,\ell)=n+2k-2-2q-s_2(r)
\]
\lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}.
In particular \(\Phi(n,k,\ell)=n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)\) when \(k-\ell-1<2^n\)
\lean{claims/PerOrderLegendre.lean}. When
\(k-\ell-1\ge2^n\), that is \(q\ge1\), \(\Phi\) is strictly smaller, since
\(s_2(k-\ell-1)=s_2(q)+s_2(r)\le q+s_2(r)<2q+s_2(r)\)
\lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}.
A polynomial of degree \(\Phi\) is \(y_1^\ell F^{2q}g_{r+1}\), where
\(y_1=x_1^2+x_1\), \(F\) is the product of the \(2^n-1\) affine forms
\(1+u\cdot x\) with \(u\ne\zero\), and \(g_{r+1}\) is a truncated product
(Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}); its degree is exactly \(\Phi\). The simplest
example with \(k-\ell-1\ge2^n\) is \(n=1\), \(k=3\), \(\ell=0\): there
\(\delta=\Phi=3\), attained by \((1+x)^3\), while \(n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)=4\). The same
example appears in Menezes' discussion of dimensions below his range
\cite[Section~8]{Men26}. For
\(n=2\), \(k=5\), \(\ell=0\) the two values are \(8\) and \(9\)
\lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}.

Minimizing over \(\ell\) gives \(D(n,k)\).

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:main}
For all integers \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\),
\[
 D(n,k)=\min_{0\le\ell<k}\Phi(n,k,\ell)=\begin{cases}
 2k+n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor, & k<2^{n-1},\\
 2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor, & k\ge2^{n-1},
 \end{cases}
\]
and some \(k\)-admissible polynomial has degree exactly
\(2k-\lfloor k/2^{n-1}\rfloor\) if and only if \(k\ge2^{n-2}\)
\textnormal{\small\leanref{claims/PerOrderMinimum.lean}{[Lean: minimum over \(\ell\)]}\,\leanref{claims/BinaryMultiplicityDegreeComplete.lean}{[Lean: value and attainment]}}.
\end{corollary}

Three features of these formulas stand out.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Blocks of \(2^n\).} As a function of \(k-\ell\), the excess
 \(\Phi-n-2\ell\) grows by \(2^{n+1}-2\) with each block of \(2^n\)
 \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}. The product \(F^2\) has degree at most
 \(2^{n+1}-2\) and multiplicity at least \(2^n\) at every nonzero point
 (Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperplane-product}) \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}, and the
 construction pays each block with
 one factor \(F^2\) and the remainder \(r\) with the truncated product
 \(g_{r+1}\).
 \item \textbf{Where the Schwartz--Zippel bound is exact.} It is attained if
 and only if \(k\ge2^{n-2}\). For \(k<2^{n-2}\), \(D(n,k)\) exceeds it by
 \(n-2-\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor\ge1\), and for fixed \(k\) each further dimension
 costs exactly one degree. For example \(D(n,1)=n\), \(D(n,4)=n+4\),
 \(D(n,8)=n+11\) and \(D(n,15)=n+25\) for \(n\ge\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\)
 \lean{claims/DegreeFormulaArithmetic.lean}.
 \item \textbf{Comparison with the reals.} Over \(\mathbb R\), for \(k\ge2\) and
 \(n\ge2k-3\), the least degree with origin order \(\ell\) is \(n+2k-3\) for every
 \(\ell\le k-2\), and \(n+2k-2\) for \(\ell=k-1\) \cite[Theorems~1.4 and~1.5]{SW20};
 Huang, Wang and Wang extend the first case to \(n\ge k-1\)
 \cite[Theorem~1.8 of arXiv v3]{HWW26}. In the range \(n\ge2k-3\) \(k-\ell-1<2^n\), so over \(\Ft\) it is
 \(n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)\): lower by \(s_2(k-\ell-1)-1\ge0\) for \(\ell\le k-2\),
 and the same for \(\ell=k-1\) \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Menezes~\cite{Men26}.} In a recent preprint, Menezes determines,
 over every field \(\F\), the least degree \(\delta_\F(n,k,\ell)\) of a polynomial
 with order at least \(k\) at the nonzero vertices of \(\{0,1\}^n\) and order
 exactly \(\ell\) at the origin, for \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge k-1\). In characteristic
 two his formula is \(n+2k-2-s_2(k-\ell-1)\) \cite[Corollary~1.3]{Men26}. For
 \(n\ge k-1\) we have \(k-\ell-1<2^n\), so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} agrees with
 it there \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean} and extends it, over \(\Ft\), to every \(n\ge1\) and every \(k\ge1\).
 The new range is \(2\le n<k-1\) (Menezes treats \(n=1\); see below), and there the formula changes form as soon as
 \(k-\ell-1\ge2^n\). Our upper-bound construction multiplies Menezes'
 Catalan truncation, specialized to \(\Ft\), by powers of \(F\). The telescoping
 identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} gives a short self-contained proof that
 the truncation works in every dimension. His degree formula is stated for
 \(n\ge k-1\), the range of his theorem. He notes that this hypothesis is
 substantive, gives \(\delta_\F(1,k,\ell)=k+\ell\) over every field, and names
 smaller dimensions as a further direction, where a formula must account for
 cancellation between his basis elements \cite[Section~8]{Men26}. For \(n=1\)
 our formula also gives \(k+\ell\) \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}. We checked on 25 September 2026 that arXiv
 lists only version~1 of~\cite{Men26}.
 Our lower bound is proved independently of~\cite{Men26}.
 \item \textbf{Bishnoi, Boyadzhiyska, Das and M\'esz\'aros~\cite{BBDM23}.} They
 determine the least size of a multiset of affine hyperplanes (and, more
 generally, subspaces of codimension \(d\)) covering the nonzero points of
 \(\Ft^n\) at least \(k\) times and the origin at most \(k-1\) times, for
 \(k\ge2^{n-2}\). With Proposition~\ref{prop:cover-bound}, which answers their
 question on the polynomial version, their covers determine \(D(n,k)\) for
 \(k\ge2^{n-2}\). Corollary~\ref{cor:main} completes the range \(k<2^{n-2}\), and
 Theorem~\ref{thm:main} refines every value by the origin order. They refine
 their covers in the same way, by the number of times the origin is covered
 \cite[Section~2 of arXiv v1]{BBDM23}. The factor \(F\) is the product of the
 linear forms of the \(2^n-1\) hyperplanes of their covers that avoid the
 origin.
 \item \textbf{Sauermann and Wigderson~\cite{SW20}.} Their theorems are the real
 case described above. The case \(\ell=k-1\), with value \(n+2k-2\) for every
 \(n\ge1\), is their Theorem~1.5; they state it over \(\mathbb R\), and Bishnoi
 et al.\ note that its proof is valid over every field \cite[Section~1.3 of
 arXiv v1]{BBDM23}. So the case
 \(\ell=k-1\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, where \(\Phi(n,k,k-1)=n+2k-2\), is not
 new \lean{claims/PerOrderArithmetic.lean}. In characteristic two they give a polynomial of degree \(n+4\) for
 \(k=4\), below \(n+2k-3=n+5\), showing that the real formula fails there.
 \item \textbf{Dvir, Kopparty, Saraf and Sudan~\cite{DKSS13}.} Their
 multiplicity Schwartz--Zippel lemma is used only in the second proof of
 Corollary~\ref{cor:main} (Appendix~\ref{sec:second-proof}) and for
 Proposition~\ref{prop:cover-bound}.
 \item \textbf{Alon~\cite{Alo99} and Alon and F\"uredi~\cite{AF93}.} The lower
 bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the grid-vanishing lemma of the
 combinatorial Nullstellensatz~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{Alo99}, for a grid of \(2^n\)
 polynomials. For \(k=1\), Corollary~\ref{cor:main} says \(D(n,1)=n\) for
 \(n\ge2\) \lean{claims/DegreeFormulaArithmetic.lean}; the lower bound is the case \(\F=\Ft\) of~\cite[Theorem~5]{AF93}.
\end{itemize}

On 24 and 25 September 2026 we searched the web for high-order vanishing on the
hypercube in positive characteristic and for punctured multiplicity
Nullstellens\"atze, and read the works that Semantic Scholar lists as citing
\cite{SW20}, \cite{BBDM23} or~\cite{Men26}. None determines \(\delta\) over
\(\Ft\) for \(n<k-1\) and \(n\ge2\); the citing works treat the reals, hyperplane
covers or simple vanishing. A bounded search does not establish that no such
result exists.

\subsection{Notation}

Throughout, \(n\) is the number of variables, \(k\) the multiplicity required at
nonzero points and \(\ell<k\) the origin order. We write
\(m=k-\ell-1=q2^n+r\) with \(0\le r<2^n\), and \(h=2q+s_2(r)\), so that
\(\Phi=n+2k-2-h\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:legendre}). Further,
\(y_i=x_i^2+x_i\) (in Section~\ref{sec:lower}, \(y_i\) is an independent
variable, and substituting \(y_i\mapsto x_i^2+x_i\) recovers this meaning);
\(F=\prod_{u\ne\zero}(1+u\cdot x)\) is the hyperplane product
(Section~\ref{sec:upper}); \(g_s\) is the truncated product
(Definition~\ref{def:truncated}); \(\hat x_K(Y)=\sum_{s<K}Y^{2^s}\) is the local
inverse of \(x\mapsto x^2+x\), and \(w=P(\hat x_K(y_1),\ldots,\hat x_K(y_n))\)
(Section~\ref{sec:lower}). For a polynomial \(f\), \(f_d\) is its homogeneous
component of degree \(d\).

\subsection{Proof outline}

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Upper bound (Section~\ref{sec:upper}).} With
 \(y=x^2+x\) we have \((1+x)+\sum_{j<K}y^{2^j}=(1+x)^{2^K}\) over \(\Ft\).
 Expanding \(\prod_i(1+x_i)^{2^K}\) this way and discarding the terms of
 cost at least \(s\) leaves a polynomial \(g_s\). It still vanishes to order
 \(s\) off the origin, takes the value \(1\) at the origin, and has small
 degree (Theorem~\ref{thm:truncated-product}). The product \(F\) has
 multiplicity at least \(2^{n-1}\) at every nonzero point. Multiplying \(F^{2q}g_{r+1}\)
 by \(y_1^\ell\) sets the origin order (Theorem~\ref{thm:construction}).
 \item \textbf{Lower bound (Section~\ref{sec:lower}).} Write
 \(P=\sum_\varepsilon x^\varepsilon A_\varepsilon(x^2+x)\) with squarefree
 \(x^\varepsilon\). A nonzero coefficient of \(y^e\) in \(A_\varepsilon\) forces
 \(\deg P\ge|\varepsilon|+2|e|\). The multiplicity conditions determine the
 low-degree parts of every \(A_\varepsilon\) from one power series \(w\), the
 composition of \(P\) with the additive local inverse
 \(\hat x(y)=\sum_sy^{2^s}\) of \(x^2+x\). So a polynomial of degree below
 \(\Phi\) makes certain parts of \(w\,\prod_{i\in T}\hat x(y_i)\) vanish. These
 parts are values of additive forms, and a recursion for repeated arguments
 shows that the forms vanish on the whole span \(V\) of \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\).
 Reducing modulo \(\prod_{v\in V}(Y-v)\) and applying the grid-vanishing lemma
 then isolates the lowest part \(w_\ell\), which is nonzero.
 \item \textbf{Second proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:main}
 (Appendix~\ref{sec:second-proof}).} After an invertible linear change of
 coordinates, \(S=P(x',0)+P(x',1)\) is admissible in \(n-1\) variables, has the
 same origin order, and has degree at least one lower
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:step}). Iterating down to \(n_0=\lfloor\log_2k\rfloor+2\),
 where the multiplicity Schwartz--Zippel lemma already gives degree \(2k\),
 yields \(D(n,k)\ge2k+n-n_0\). This was the proof of version~1 of this paper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formal verification}

Every mathematical claim of this paper is proved in Lean~4 with Mathlib: the
theorems and lemmas, the remarks, the examples and the comparisons made in the
text, together with the multiplicity Schwartz--Zippel lemma, the hyperplane
covers and the parity of Catalan numbers. The proofs use only Lean's standard
axioms. Each claim carries a [Lean] link to its formal proof at a fixed
repository commit, except the grid-vanishing lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:grid-vanishing}), which the Lean proof takes from Mathlib.
Appendix~\ref{sec:verification} maps statements to declarations and gives the
commands to check them. Not formalized are statements about the content of
other papers, which we cite rather than prove: what their theorems say over
other fields or in other ranges, and how their constructions relate to ours.
Formal verification covers the statements under the linked definitions. It does
not cover the prose, the comparison with the literature, or the fidelity of the
definitions to the informal notions, although the definitions are short
(Definitions~\ref{def:mult} and~\ref{def:admissible}).
