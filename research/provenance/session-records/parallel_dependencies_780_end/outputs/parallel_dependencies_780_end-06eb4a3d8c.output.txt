
###dense-forms-invariance
2. Invariance of random dense forms
Lemma I. Let \(L_1,\dots,L_m\) be independent uniform forms: all \(a^{(i)}_j\in\mathbb F_2^\ell\) and \(c_i\in\mathbb F_2\) independent and uniform. For every fixed \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) the restrictions \(L_1|_{\rho'},\dots,L_m|_{\rho'}\) are independent uniform affine forms on \(V\). The proof uses of \(\Phi_0\) only that residual labels are \(q_0+By\) with \(B\) of full column rank and that the other rows receive constants; the size of \(R'\), the bijection, the uniformity of the flat and the PHP structure are not used, so the invariance holds for every restriction of this shape. Consequently, for any Boolean template applied to the forms, the restricted reader has, for every \(\rho'\), exactly the distribution of the same template applied to independent uniform affine forms on \(\mathbb F_2^v\).
Proof. In (DF-restrict) the linear part is \((B^{\mathsf T}a_j)_{j\in R'}\). The map \(a\mapsto B^{\mathsf T}a\) is linear and onto \(\mathbb F_2^{\ell_2}\) because \(B\) has rank \(\ell_2\), so each \(B^{\mathsf T}a_j\) is uniform, and they are independent over \(j\in R'\) and over the forms. The constant contains the summand \(c_i\), which is uniform and independent of all \(a^{(i)}_j\); hence the constant is uniform and independent of the linear part and of the other forms. \(\square\)
Remark (one-bit parities). The same proof applies to the subfamily \(a_j\in\{0,e\}\) for a fixed \(e\) with \(B^{\mathsf T}e\ne0\) (parities of one bit combination over a uniform set of rows): the restrictions are independent uniform affine forms in the \(N+1\) variables \(z_j=\langle B^{\mathsf T}e,y_j\rangle\), so everything below holds with \(v\) replaced by \(N+1\). For a uniform flat, \(B^{\mathsf T}e=0\) has probability \((2^{\ell-\ell_2}-1)/(2^\ell-1)\), about \(1/N\).


###dense-forms-structured
3. The two structured extremes are benign
Lemma P (packing by restricted rank). Let the linear parts of all restricted forms of a reader span a space of dimension \(m\) of linear forms on \(V\), with basis \(Z_1,\dots,Z_m\). Then every restricted term, the reader, and \(P\) are functions of \(Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_m)\in\mathbb F_2^m\). Put \(\hat P=P(Z)\) as a multilinear polynomial in \(Z\), and \(U_i(Z)\) the indicator that \(i\) is the first term equal to 1 at \(Z\). Then \(1-\hat P-\sum_iU_it_i\), \(t_i\hat P\) and \(\hat P^2-\hat P\) vanish as functions of \(Z\), so they are combinations of the polynomials \(Z_k^2-Z_k\) at their own degrees: the profile holds with \(s=m+w\) (first certificate and \(\deg U_i+\deg t_i\) at most \(m+w\), companions at most \(m+2w\), Booleanity at most \(2m\le2s\)). In characteristic two \(Z_k^2-Z_k\) is exactly a sum of Booleanity axioms of the cell variables, the cross terms cancelling, which makes "from Booleanity alone" literal; for an odd prime the same step needs the row exclusions at degree two, and this entry is binary while the main goal is for all primes. \(\square\)
Relation to recorded results. Lemma P is an application of the recorded exact packing of a function of \(m\) Boolean linear forms (cycle 174, binary affine-span packing); what is new is the choice of the basis after the restriction. The last step of Lemma C below, that a span containing 1 gives the image 0 with constant coefficients, is the recorded removal of input spans containing one; what is new there is the probabilistic spanning bound.
Symmetric forms. If every form of the reader has the same functional on every row, \(a_j=a\), then by (DF-restrict) and \(\sum_{x\notin Q}x=0\) for \(\ell_2\ge2\), \(L|_{\rho'}=\langle B^{\mathsf T}a,Y\rangle+\langle a,q_0\rangle+c\) with \(Y=\sum_{j\in R'}y_j\), because \(N+1\) is odd. The whole reader is a function of \(Y\in\mathbb F_2^{\ell_2}\): \(m\le\ell_2=\log_2N\), and Lemma P gives an exact image with \(s=\log_2N+w\), for every \(\rho'\) and any number of terms. More generally, forms that are symmetric within each of \(g\) classes of rows have \(m\le\ell_2\min(g,N+1)\); before any restriction such a reader is already a function of \(g\ell\) forms and Lemma P packs it without a restriction at \(s=g\ell+w\), polylogarithmic for polylogarithmic \(g\), although it may sit in the syntactic frontier zone; so the symmetric case shows no gain of the restriction either. Sparse forms reduce to conditions on boundedly many residual rows (alternative (B) of the review). The first falsification target of Question D therefore fails to defeat anything: symmetric and class-symmetric forms are the easy case, not a hard one.


###dense-forms-certificate
4. Low rank: the restricted reader is exactly 1, with a certificate of degree the rank
Lemma C. Let \(t_1,\dots,t_a\) be independent random terms of rank \(w\) on \(\mathbb F_2^v\), each \(t_i=\prod_{k\le w}(1+L_{ik})\) with independent uniform affine forms. Let \(D_w=\sum_{k\le w}\binom vk\) be the dimension of the space \(\mathcal P_w\) of multilinear polynomials of degree at most \(w\). If \(a\ge2^w(D_w\ln2+\ln(1/\eta))\), then with probability at least \(1-\eta\) the \(t_i\) span \(\mathcal P_w\). In that event \(1=\sum_ic_it_i\) with \(c_i\in\mathbb F_2\), the reader is identically 1 on \(\mathbb F_2^v\), and \(\hat P=0\), \(U_i=c_i\) satisfy all three certificates through degree \(w\) from Booleanity alone.
Proof. The products \(\prod_{k\le w}(1+L_k)\) over all choices of affine forms span \(\mathcal P_w\): the choice \(L_k=1+u_k\) for \(k\in S\) and \(L_k=0\) otherwise gives the monomial \(u_S\), for every \(|S|\le w\). Let \(\varphi\) be a nonzero linear functional on \(\mathcal P_w\). As a function of the coefficient vectors of \(L_1,\dots,L_w\), \(\varphi(\prod_k(1+L_k))\) is a polynomial that is affine in each coefficient vector, since multilinear reduction is linear; it has degree at most \(w\), is multilinear, and is not identically zero by the spanning property. A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most \(w\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) is nonzero at a uniform point with probability at least \(2^{-w}\). So a random term lies in \(\ker\varphi\) with probability at most \(1-2^{-w}\), all \(a\) terms with probability at most \(e^{-a2^{-w}}\), and the union over the fewer than \(2^{D_w}\) functionals gives failure at most \(2^{D_w}e^{-a2^{-w}}\le\eta\). If the terms span, 1 is a combination of them; then \(1-\hat P-\sum_ic_it_i=0\) identically after multilinear reduction, which costs Booleanity multiples of degree at most \(w\), and the other two certificates are trivial for \(\hat P=0\). \(\square\)
Corollary (generic dense readers of low rank). Let a source reader have \(a=n^c\) terms of rank \(w\) on independent uniform forms, and let \(w\le w_1\), where \(w_1\) is the largest \(w\) with \(2^w(D_w\ln2+(2c+2)\ln n)\le n^c\); since \(D_w\le(v+1)^w\) gives \(2^wD_w\le(2v+2)^w\), one has \(w_1\ge c\log_2n/\log_2(2v+2)-2\) as soon as \((2v+2)^2\ge(2c+3)\ln n\), \(v\ge3\) and the resulting rank is at least 2, so \(w_1\) is of order \(\log n/\log\log n\). By Lemma I and Lemma C, for every \(\rho'\) the restricted reader is identically 1 with certificates of degree \(w\), except with probability \(n^{-2c-2}\) over the source; by Markov's inequality, for all but an \(n^{-c-1}\) fraction of sources this holds except with probability \(n^{-c-1}\) over \(\rho'\). Before the restriction the same linearization needs \(a\ge2^wD_w\) with \(D_w\) counted in \((n+1)\ell\) variables, that is \(w<c\). This is what the polylogarithmic residual buys for generic dense readers: a covering certificate of degree the rank, for ranks up to \(w_1\) instead of ranks below \(c\).


###dense-forms-balanced
5. The balanced rank: the coverage hypothesis fails and the restriction gains nothing
Lemma B. (Lemmas C and B use no PHP structure, only the independence of the forms within and across terms.) In the setting of Lemma C, a fixed point \(u\) lies in the flat of a term with probability exactly \(2^{-w}\), and for \(u\ne u'\) the events are independent (the difference \(L(u)-L(u')\) is a uniform bit independent of \(L(u)\)). So the number of points of \(\mathbb F_2^v\) not covered by the reader has mean \(2^v(1-2^{-w})^a\) and variance at most its mean. For \(a\le2^w\) the mean uncovered share is at least \(1/4\), and the covered share is \(1-(1-2^{-w})^a\ge1-e^{-a2^{-w}}\); by Chebyshev both shares are within \(2^{-v/3}\) of their means except with probability \(2^{-v/3+2}\).
Hence the coverage hypothesis of Question D is false at the balanced rank \(w\approx\log_2a\), which lies inside the frontier zone (ranks below \(r_{\rm high}\), more than polylogarithmically many terms): there, for each fixed \(\rho'\), the restricted reader is a nonconstant function on \(V\) with both values on a constant share of the points except with probability \(2^{-v/3+2}\) over the source, so by Markov's inequality, for all but a \(2^{-v/6+1}\) fraction of sources this holds for all but a \(2^{-v/6+1}\) fraction of the restrictions; and by Lemma I the restricted reader is distributed exactly as a random reader of the same shape on a cube of dimension \(v\). The restriction replaces the dimension \((n+1)\ell\) by \(v\) and changes nothing else: the balanced rank is untouched by it. No tree or image within the budget is known for such a reader. Each restricted form involves every residual row with a nonzero \(B^{\mathsf T}a_j\), all but about one of the \(N+1\) rows on average; an exact image certified by Booleanity alone must equal \(P\) on \(V\), so its degree is the \(\mathbb F_2\)-degree of the complement of a random union of about \(2^w\) flats, for which no bound is proved here; and the universal source-map obstruction, which concerns its own three-level source, names certificates from genuine PHP consequences as the escape. For generic sources of size \(n^c\) the ranks therefore split as follows.

###php-relative-images-method
2. Method
Board: \(N+1\) rows, \(N\) holes, cell variables over \(\mathbb F_2\). System A has Booleanity, the row exclusions and the row axioms \(Q_j=\sum_xX_{jx}-1\); its models are the labelings. System B adds the hole exclusions and is the functional PHP of the image lemma. \(T_d\) is the span of the monomials of degree at most \(d\) that survive the exclusions: partial labelings for A, partial matchings for B. \(V_d\subseteq T_d\) is the degree-\(d\) polynomial-calculus closure of the row axioms: the span of \(tQ_j\), \(|t|\le d-1\), closed under multiplying elements of degree at most \(d-1\) by variables, the low-degree elements being read off an echelon basis whose pivots prefer high degree. For system A the quotient dimensions equal \(\sum_{k\le d}\binom{N+1}k(N-1)^k\), the functions of row-degree at most \(d\), in every case computed (16, 106, 376 for \(N=4\)), which validates the closure. In every nonvacuous case the closure equals the span of the \(tQ_j\); the closure rounds add elements only at the refutation degree (\(N=4\), \(d=3\): 325 to 381, all of \(T_3\)).
A block reading inputs \(g_1,\dots,g_a\) of degree \(w\) has product \(P=\prod_i(1-g_i)\). For a parameter \(s\) the linear image conditions are: \(\hat P\in T_s\), \(U_i\in T_{s-w}\), \(1-\hat P-\sum_iU_ig_i\in V_s\), and \(g_i\hat P\in V_{s+w}\). The Booleanity condition on \(\hat P\) is not imposed, so an infeasible system is a definitive answer and a feasible one is a necessary condition only. Without the hole axioms the conditions force \(\hat P=P\) as functions on labelings, so an image exists exactly when \(P\) has row-degree at most \(s\) and the \(U_i\) exist. The test covers unweighted images only (no weight as in the barrier of the universal obstruction) and \(s\le2\). Dense forms are \(L=\sum_j[x_j\in H_j]+c\) with \(H_j\) a uniform half of the labels for each row, or, on the board with eight holes, an affine half-space \(\langle a_j,x\rangle=1\) of \(\mathbb F_2^3\) with uniform \(a_j\).


###php-relative-images-results
3. Results
Rank-one inputs \(g_i=L_i\), so \(P\) is the indicator of the flat \(L_1=\dots=L_\rho=0\). On the boards with six holes (three random readers for each \(\rho\)) and eight holes (two readers with uniform halves, three with affine half-spaces), with and without the hole axioms: \(\rho=2\) is infeasible at \(s=1\) and feasible at \(s=2\); \(\rho=3\) is infeasible at \(s=1\) and at \(s=2\); three forms that are symmetric across rows (on uniform halves also in the affine run) behave like \(\rho=3\). The answers under A and B agree in all 38 reader and parameter pairs. Feasibility without the hole axioms implies feasibility with them, since \(V^A\) maps into \(V^B\); the informative part is that all 30 pairs that are infeasible without the hole axioms stay infeasible with them. (In a separate run with \(d\le4\) on six holes, which confirms \(V_4=T_4\) there, the sampled symmetric reader is linearly inconsistent and has the image 0 at \(s=1\) in both systems, and the flat of codimension three has the image \(P\) itself at \(s=3\) without the hole axioms, as it must.) Positive control: the reader with \(1+L_1=[x_0=0]\), \(1+L_2=[x_1=0]\) and a dense \(L_3\), whose flat is empty exactly because of a hole exclusion, is infeasible at \(s=2\) without the hole axioms and feasible with them, on both boards. The control's gain sits in the exclusion ideal itself: its product is zero in the quotient of system B, with certificate \(U_1=1\), \(U_2=X_{00}\). It shows that the pipeline separates the two systems; it does not show that a gain needing the row axioms together with the hole exclusions would be detected. The records add that below the refutation degree \(V_d^B\) equals the Nullstellensatz span of the \(tQ_j\), and that \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) have the same dimensions in both systems (5, 75; 7, 238; 9, 549 for four, six, eight holes). With that caveat, the computation sees no gain for dense forms.
Rank-two inputs at the balanced rank (eight holes, \(s=w=2\), hole axioms, \(V_4\)): six readers with \(a=2,4,8\) independent terms \(t_k=(1+L_k)(1+L'_k)\) on dense forms with uniform half-sets, two readers for each \(a\); \(a=2^w\) is the analogue, at the smallest scale, of the balanced rank of the previous entry, and \(a=2,8\) lie on either side of it. Unknowns are \(\hat P\in T_2\) and constants \(U_k\); the conditions are \(1-\hat P-\sum_kU_kt_k\in V_2\) and \(t_k\hat P\in V_4\). All six readers are infeasible with the hole axioms. Without them an image must equal \(P\) on labelings, and for all six a nonzero three-row mixed difference of \(P\) shows row-degree at least three, so they are infeasible there as well. The control with \(t_1=[x_0=0](1+L)\), \(t_2=[x_1=0](1+L')\), where \(t_1t_2\) vanishes by a hole exclusion and \(\hat P=1+t_1+t_2\), is feasible with the hole axioms and excluded without them by the same mixed-difference test.


###php-relative-images-meaning
4. What this says and what it does not
On these boards the hole axioms do not lower the image degree of any block on dense forms that was tried: a flat of codimension three on dense forms has no image of degree two even modulo the functional PHP, and a balanced reader of rank-two terms has none at \(s=2\), where the unknown coefficients are constants, so that the statement is that no \(1+\sum_kc_kt_k\) modulo \(V_2\) annihilates every \(t_k\) modulo \(V_4\); \(s=3\) cannot be tested on this board, since \(V_5=T_5\). The intuition is that below the refutation degree the hole axioms only distinguish sampling with and without replacement on at most \(N/2\) rows, and a dense form, which sums a half-set indicator over all rows, is insensitive to that difference; the control differs because its inputs pin two rows to one hole. This is a finite check on tiny boards and with few random readers, not a theorem: a proof would need the normal form of the closure \(V_d\) (Razborov's pigeon dance), which was not used here. It also concerns images, that is substitutions of old polynomials for the block's variables. By duality a design extension, a functional with value 1 at 1 that vanishes on the degree-\(d\) closure of the augmented system (the PHP together with the block's axioms in its new variables), exists exactly when 1 lies outside that closure; for Nullstellensatz degree the same holds with the span in place of the closure. An image yields such an extension by composition and not conversely, so this formulation covers extensions that are not substitutions; it is the general step in miniature. It is computable on the same boards with the same closure code, the new variables enlarging \(T_d\) only slightly.


###augmented-php-setup
1. Setting and what substitution can certify
Base: the functional PHP over \(\mathbb F_2\) on \(N+1\) rows and \(N\) holes, of refutation degree \(D_0=N/2+1\) (bridge; attained for \(N=4,6\) by the computed closures). Block (the ENS format of the working context with accuracy one): inputs \(L_1,\dots,L_\rho\), dense forms \(L=\sum_j[x_j\in H_j]+c\); fresh Boolean coefficient variables \(r_1,\dots,r_\rho\); product \(P=1-\sum_jr_jL_j\); axioms \(L_iP=0\) for every \(i\). For every assignment of the old variables the block is satisfiable, so the augmented system is refuted only through the PHP; the question is at which degree, counting old and new variables alike.
Lemma (the recorded substitution, in polynomial-calculus form). Let \(\sigma\) substitute \(r_1=1\) and \(r_j=\prod_{i<j}(1+L_i)\) for \(j\ge2\). Then \(\sigma(P)=\prod_j(1+L_j)\), \(\sigma(L_iP)\) is zero modulo Booleanity and the row exclusions, and a derivation of degree \(d\) from the augmented system maps to a derivation from the base of degree at most \(\max(d,d+\rho-2)\) of the image of its last line; \(\sigma\) fixes old polynomials. Hence an old consequence at degree \(d\) is a base consequence at degree \(d+\rho-2\): two inputs are fully conservative at every degree (the recorded corollary), three inputs cannot be refuted through degree \(D_0-2\) and four through \(D_0-3\), and nothing follows about old consequences at the same degree for \(\rho\ge3\). The lemma uses of the inputs only \(g^2=g\) modulo the base and of the base only Booleanity and the row exclusions; density, the hole exclusions and the PHP play no part.
Proof. \(1-\sum_j\sigma(r_j)L_j=1-\sum_jL_j\prod_{i<j}(1+L_i)\) telescopes to \(\prod_j(1+L_j)\) because \(L\prod_{i<j}(1+L_i)=\prod_{i<j}(1+L_i)-\prod_{i\le j}(1+L_i)\) in characteristic two. Then \(L_i\prod_j(1+L_j)\) contains the factor \(L_i(1+L_i)=L_i+L_i^2\), and \(L^2=L\) modulo Booleanity and the row exclusions in characteristic two (the mixed products of one row vanish and those of two rows cancel in pairs). Keep every line multilinear in the new variables. Since \(\sigma(r_i)\sigma(r_j)=\sigma(r_{\max(i,j)})\) modulo Booleanity and the row exclusions, a monomial of degree \(d\) carrying \(l\ge1\) new variables with largest index \(j\) has image of degree at most \(d-l+(j-1)\le d+\rho-2\). A derivation maps line by line: multiplication by an old variable commutes with \(\sigma\); multiplication by \(r_j\) is mapped to multiplication by the factors \(1+L_i\), \(i<j\), one at a time, reducing \((1+L_i)^2\) to \(1+L_i\) after each factor, and the intermediate lines stay within \(d+\rho-2\) because a line that may be multiplied has degree at most \(d-1\). \(\square\)

3. Reading
For three and four inputs the substitution certifies only that no refutation exists through degree three and two, and nothing about old consequences at the same degree. A substitution with coefficient images of degree at most one would give an image with \(s=2\); the image test of the previous entry, run on the exact forms of these blocks (three inputs with both seeds and with affine half-spaces, four inputs), is infeasible at \(s=1,2\) with and without the hole axioms, and on eight holes the part of \(V_4\) of degree at most three equals \(V_3\) (12729), so the weaker condition that a degree-four certificate would need coincides with the image condition. So the extension that the computation proves to exist at degree four is not a substitution of degree one, in agreement with what cycle 170 records over Boolean bases. This is an instance, on a base outside its hypothesis, of the recorded level-one conservativity conjecture: no loss at ranks three and four.
The test has low power, and the limits matter more than the result. With companions of degree three and closure degree four, the coefficient criterion leaves only multipliers of degree at most one; the recorded loss at rank three appeared at degree seven and needed a second-level block in the base. ###level-two-feasibility-count

On the functional PHP with \(N\) holes a closure test is nonvacuous only below the refutation degree \(D_0=N/2+1\) (bridge). A second-level block whose inputs are all first-level products has companions of degree five; a mixed block, reading a form and a first-level product, has the companion of degree four on its form input and degree five on the other. What the count does not cover. It does not use the symmetry of the board or of symmetric blocks, and it does not work in the quotient of the monomial space by the base closure (on eight holes the quotient saves only a factor 2.5). It also says nothing about other unsatisfiable bases: a base with few variables and refutation degree above seven, for instance a small random CNF over \(\mathbb F_2\), could carry a second-level block at the degree of the recorded loss within a few million monomials. Whether such a base exists at a computable size was not checked. That test would show whether the recorded rank-dependent loss persists over an unsatisfiable base in general; it would not test the mechanism that cycle 168 asked about, the PHP-relative clamping of the one-affine-level theorem, which is a property of the board. It is recorded here as the remaining exact test and is not proposed as the next step.


###level-two-literature
2. The known lower bounds for polynomial calculus with extension variables
Two papers were retrieved and read in part (locators in the source audit); their statements are imported as context and nothing in the notebook depends on them.
Impagliazzo, Mouli, Pitassi, CCC 2023. For every prime \(p\) and \(\kappa=O(\log n)\) there is an unsatisfiable system over \(O(n\log n)\) variables of degree \(O(\log n)\) such that every polynomial-calculus refutation over \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(M\) extension variables, each depending on at most \(\kappa\) original variables, has size \(\exp(\Omega(n^2)/(10^\kappa(M+n\log n)))\) (abstract). The two parameter choices stated as theorems: \(M=n\,\mathrm{polylog}\,n\) extension variables of arity \(\kappa=O(\log\log n)\) force size \(2^{\Omega(n/\mathrm{polylog}\,n)}\) (Theorem 1), and \(M=n^{1+\alpha}\) of arity \(\beta\log n\) force size \(2^{\Omega(n^\gamma)}\) for some constants \(0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma<1\) (Theorem 2). The first paragraph of their introduction states that superpolynomial lower bounds for \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege are open. After Theorem 2 they remark: "our extension variables are only allowed to depend on the original variables, and not on previously defined extension variables. (In the more general case where extension variables are defined recursively, the proof system corresponds to \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege, where the level of recursion corresponds to the \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\) circuit depth.)" They describe the result as a lower bound for a restricted class of depth-2.5 refutations. Their Section 3 sets aside \(p=2\): over \(\mathbb F_2\) every extension variable is zero-one valued, so the standard size-degree tradeoffs apply with the extension variables counted as variables, and \(\kappa\)-local extension variables change the degree by at most a factor \(\kappa\); since the tradeoffs need a degree of at least the square root of the number of variables, this tolerates close to \(n^2/\kappa^2\) such extension variables.
Dahiya, Mahajan, Mouli, ECCC TR23-132 (SAT 2024), Theorem 1.2. For large \(N\), any \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1\), constant \(c\) and prime \(p\) there is a tautology over \(N\) variables such that every refutation over \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(N^{1+\varepsilon(1-\delta)}\) extension variables, each depending on \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) variables and of size at most \(N^c\), has size \(\exp(\Omega(N^{\varepsilon\delta}/\mathrm{polylog}\,N))\). The formula is an XOR-ification of Razborov's generalized \(\mathrm{PHP}^{m,r}_n\). The method: a random restriction that fixes one variable of each XOR gadget reduces every extension axiom of polynomial size to logarithmic degree; extension variables of bounded degree are then removed one at a time by restrictions whose Hamming weight is bounded by that degree, with cleanup restrictions that preserve the degree hardness of the formula; the measure is Sokolov's quadratic degree. They restrict to \(p>2\) and refer to the remark above for \(p=2\). Their extension axioms also define each new variable by a polynomial in the original variables only. Their introduction recalls that constant-depth polynomial calculus (Grigoriev and Hirsch) simulates \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege at proportional depth, and adds a caveat: by Raz and Tzameret and by Impagliazzo, Mouli and Pitassi the same system simulates Cutting Planes with bounded coefficients, sum-of-squares, \(\mathrm{AC}^0[q]\)-Frege for every prime \(q\) at a fixed depth, and \(\mathrm{TC}^0\)-Frege at proportional depth, so that "general lower bounds for this system are much harder than lower bounds for \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege".
Position of the general step. In these terms the notebook's source is a system of extension variables of constant nesting depth, polynomial in number, whose defining polynomials depend on all original variables. The published bounds stop at arity \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) with a tradeoff against the number of extension variables, and both papers say explicitly that extension variables depend on original variables only; the first identifies the recursive case with \(\mathrm{AC}^0[p]\)-Frege, which is the mapping used here. That boundary is the one the notebook found from its own side: forms of row support at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/(N+1)\) reduce to boundedly many residual rows under the compact restriction (alternative (B) of the route review), an analogue of a restriction shrinking an extension axiom of arity \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) (there the reduction goes through the XOR gadget of the lifted formula and the size bound of the axiom, here through sparse row support, so the mechanisms are analogous and not the same), while for dense forms the restriction changes nothing at the balanced rank (Lemmas I and B). The remark on \(p=2\) reduces their question to the degree of the augmented system only in their parameter range, fewer than about \(n^2/\kappa^2\) extension variables; for the polynomially many dense extension variables of a compiled source the size-degree tradeoff is void. The notebook's general step is also a degree statement for the augmented system, but for a different reason (a contradiction below the old degree barrier for a compiled source, with the inventory entering through the weight). Their factor-\(\kappa\) bound is the analogue of the recorded substitution cost, which is additive in the rank at one level and multiplicative along chains; both are void for dense inputs. The caveat of the second paper bears on the form of the step: a method that handled arbitrary nested dense extension variables would prove more than the main goal, so the step should use the block format of the compiled source, in agreement with the universal obstruction and with the route item as stated. So the obligation is not covered by a known technique with different parameters; it sits beyond the published frontier in the dense and nested direction. One difference in method is worth keeping: both papers restrict the original variables so as to simplify the extension axioms and then repair the hard formula, whereas the notebook restricts to a residual PHP and asks the source to have an image there; their cleanup step, which restores a hard sub-instance after each restriction, has no counterpart in the notebook, and their hard formula (the generalized PHP, which maps \(r\)-subsets of pigeons and tolerates such cleanups) is not the ordinary PHP of the main goal.


###collapse-induction-composition
1. The induction is a composition of recorded results
Cycle 174 already records the alternatives at every level on the support of the weight: a block whose purely affine part has rank at least \(r_{\rm high}=\lceil v\ln(16M)/k\rceil\) is clamped, a block reading at most polylogarithmically many low indicators is packed exactly, and what is left at every level is a wide disjunction of narrow terms, on whose union, that entry asserts without proof, no weight vanishes. Cycle 173 states the same zone for the row-linear weight. The collapse of the previous entries fits into this list as one more removable case, and it is the recorded removal of an input span that contains one, applied after the images of the lower level:
Collapse step. Let a block \(R\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) have inputs whose images, after the substitutions of the lower levels, are old polynomials \(q_1,\dots,q_a\) of degree at most \(w\), and suppose \(1+\sum_ic_iq_i\) reduces to zero by Booleanity for some constants \(c_i\in\mathbb F_2\). Assign the first coefficient row of \(R\) the constants \(c_i\) and the other rows zero. The product of \(R\) has the image \(1+\sum_ic_iq_i\), which is zero with a Boolean certificate of degree at most \(w\); all companions and field equations of \(R\) have zero images. No degree is spent beyond the images of the inputs. For a generic reader of \(a\ge2^w(D_w\ln2+\ln(1/\eta))\) terms of rank \(w\) on the residual bit space, Lemma C supplies the hypothesis with \(q_i\) the flat indicators, whose coefficient images have degree at most \(w-1\) by the one-bin telescoping map. A slightly more general form costs nothing either: if constants \(c_i\) exist with \(q_j(1+\sum_ic_iq_i)\equiv0\) for every \(j\), then \(\hat P=1+\sum_ic_iq_i\) is an exact image of the product. Example: terms that all contain one common form \(L_0\) and are otherwise generic never cover the space, yet the reader has the exact image \(L_0\).
This is a restatement, not a new lemma: the normal form removes a block whose literal input span contains one, and the step applies it to the images of the inputs instead of the literal inputs.


###collapse-induction-survivors
2. Attempts to break the induction, and what survives
Mixed blocks. A block one level up that reads a collapsed reader and a surviving one sees a constant in place of the first, so it is the same block on the surviving inputs only. Constants propagate upwards and never help with the surviving inputs.
Shared forms. Lemma C needs independent forms. Terms sharing forms are handled exactly when the shared part factors out as in the example above; a family of terms whose forms are shared in a less regular way has a smaller span and no covering certificate with constant coefficients, and the step does not apply.
Survivors. For a generic reader of \(a=n^c\) terms of rank \(w\) on the residual bit space of dimension \(v\), the step applies for \(w\le w_1\approx c\log_2n/\log_2(2v+2)\) and the weight clamps the terms for \(w\ge r_{\rm high}\). The expected number of uncovered points is \(2^v(1-2^{-w})^a\), so the covering threshold is \(a\ln(1/(1-2^{-w}))=v\ln2\) (for \(w=1\) this is \(a=v\); for large \(w\) it is \(a2^{-w}\approx v\ln2\)). Two zones survive. Between \(w_1\) and the threshold the reader is constant with high probability, but only a covering certificate with constant coefficients is excluded and the cost of a certificate with polynomial coefficients is open (the second row of the table of the dense-forms entry). Beyond the threshold, with a margin (by the variance bound of Lemma B the probability of no uncovered point is at most the reciprocal of the mean), the reader is not constant as a function; this zone contains the balanced rank. In both zones exact packing costs about \(aw\), and the count of cycle 174, which is what produces a weight, needs \(k\ge v\ln(16a)/w\) for a weight of degree \(k\) vanishing on all \(a\) flats, more than \(0.69\,v\) at the balanced rank against \(k\le v/4\) (that no weight exists is expected for generic flats but is not proved, here or in cycle 174). These survivors are the frontier recorded in cycles 173 and 174. A block reading a surviving reader has no input image to which the step could apply, so the induction stops at its first step for every source that contains one such reader, and the question of dependence between levels is not reached. The proposal of the previous entry is withdrawn as a route: it handles a generic source only if the source has no reader in these zones.
