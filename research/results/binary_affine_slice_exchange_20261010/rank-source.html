Setting

\(\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\) contains the Boolean axioms \(x_i^2+x_i\). \(C_D\) is the set of polynomials derivable from \(\mathcal Q\) by polynomial calculus within degree \(D\): the closure of the elements of \(\mathcal Q\) of degree at most \(D\) under sums and under multiplication of elements of degree at most \(D-1\) by a variable. \(V_k\) is the space of polynomials of degree at most \(k\). Two standard facts. (M) If \(g\in C_D\) and \(\deg g+\deg u\le D\), then \(gu\in C_D\): multiply by the variables of each monomial of \(u\) one at a time. (B) For \(g\) of degree at most \(e\le D/2\), \(g^2+g\in C_D\): it vanishes on the cube and has degree at most \(2e\), and a polynomial of degree at most \(D\) vanishing on the cube is a combination of multiples \(m\,(x_i^2+x_i)\) of degree at most its own. For a field \(E\supseteq\mathbb F_2\) put \(C_D^E=E\otimes C_D\subseteq E[x]_{\le D}\); then \(C_D^E\cap E[x]_{\le j}=E\otimes(C_D\cap V_j)\), since extension of scalars commutes with intersections of subspaces, and hence (M) holds over \(E\): if \(G\in C_D^E\), \(u\in E[x]\) and \(\deg G+\deg u\le D\), then \(Gu\in C_D^E\).

Fix a set of affine elements of \(\mathcal Q\), possibly empty, consistent as a linear system, and let \(\varrho\) be the rank of their linear parts; put \(n=v-\varrho\), \(v\) the number of variables of the ring. \(N_n(k)=\sum_{j\le k}\binom nj\).

Rank retention

Lemma (binary rank retention). Let \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) have degree at most \(a\), \(h_1,\ldots,h_r\) degree at most \(b\), and \(b+k+2a\le D\). Let \(M_i:V_k\to(V_D/C_D)^r\), \(M_if=([fg_ih_j])_j\), and \(\overline M=(M_1,\ldots,M_s)\) the stacked map. Then some \(c\in\mathbb F_2^s\) has \(\operatorname{rank}\sum_ic_iM_i\ge\tfrac12\operatorname{rank}\overline M\).

Proof. Step 1, multiplicities. For a nonzero polynomial \(H\) in \(s\) variables over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(e\in\mathbb F_2^s\) let \(\operatorname{mult}(H,e)\) be the least degree of a term of \(H(e+z)\). Then \(\sum_e\operatorname{mult}(H,e)\le\deg H\cdot2^{s-1}\). For \(s=1\) this is the count of roots with multiplicity. For \(s>1\) write \(H(y,z)=\sum_{j\le t}H_j(y)z^j\), \(H_t\ne0\). Fix \(e'\in\mathbb F_2^{s-1}\), let \(\mu=\operatorname{mult}(H_t,e')\) and \(y^\alpha\) a monomial of degree \(\mu\) in \(H_t(e'+y)\). The coefficient \(h_\alpha(z)\) of \(y^\alpha\) in \(H(e'+y,z)\) has degree exactly \(t\). For \(e_s\in\mathbb F_2\), \(H(e'+y,e_s+z)\) contains \(y^\alpha\) times the lowest term of \(h_\alpha(e_s+z)\), and different monomials in \(y\) do not cancel, so \(\operatorname{mult}(H,(e',e_s))\le\mu+\operatorname{mult}(h_\alpha,e_s)\). Summing over \(e_s\) gives at most \(2\mu+t\); summing over \(e'\) and using induction for \(H_t\) gives at most \(2\deg H_t\,2^{s-2}+t2^{s-1}\le\deg H\cdot2^{s-1}\).

Step 2, specialization. Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_s\) be matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) of one shape, \(A(\lambda)=\sum\lambda_iA_i\) with indeterminates \(\lambda_i\), and \(R\) its rank over \(E=\mathbb F_2(\lambda)\). Then the average of \(\operatorname{rank}A(e)\) over \(e\in\mathbb F_2^s\) is at least \(R/2\). If \(R>0\), take an \(R\times R\) submatrix \(B(\lambda)\) with \(H=\det B\ne0\), of degree at most \(R\). For \(e\) let \(\rho_e\le\operatorname{rank}A(e)\) be the rank of \(B(e)\). Row operations over \(\mathbb F_2\) make \(R-\rho_e\) rows of \(B(e)\) vanish without changing the determinant; as \(B(e+z)=B(e)+B(z)\), those rows of \(B(e+z)\) are linear forms in \(z\), so every term of \(H(e+z)\) has degree at least \(R-\rho_e\). Thus \(R-\operatorname{rank}A(e)\le\operatorname{mult}(H,e)\), and Step 1 gives the average.

Step 3, descent. Let \(W\subseteq E^m\) be an \(E\)-subspace, \(E\) of characteristic two, closed under squaring of all coordinates. Then \(W\) has a basis in \(\mathbb F_2^m\): if \(T\) is the reduced row echelon basis matrix of \(W\), the matrix of squares of its entries has rows in \(W\), the same pivots and the same echelon form, so its rows are independent elements of \(W\), as many as its dimension, and it is a reduced echelon basis matrix of \(W\); it equals \(T\) by uniqueness, and the entries of \(T\) satisfy \(c^2=c\).

Step 4, kernels. Let \(A_i:U\to W'\) be linear maps of finite-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-spaces, in matrix form. The entries of \(A(\lambda^2)=\sum\lambda_i^2A_i\) are the squares of those of \(A(\lambda)\), so its minors are the squares of the minors and the ranks over \(E\) agree. Suppose \(\ker_EA(\lambda)\subseteq\ker_EA(\lambda^2)\). Then the kernels are equal. For \(u\in\ker_EA(\lambda)\), squaring the coordinates of \(A(\lambda)u=0\) gives \(A(\lambda^2)u^{[2]}=0\), so the kernel is closed under squaring of coordinates and has a basis in \(U\) by Step 3. For \(u\in U\), \(\sum\lambda_iA_iu=0\) forces every \(A_iu=0\), the \(\lambda_i\) being independent over \(\mathbb F_2\). So \(\ker_EA(\lambda)=E\otimes\bigcap_i\ker A_i\) and \(\operatorname{rank}_EA(\lambda)=\operatorname{rank}\overline A\).

Step 5, multiplication maps. Extend the \(M_i\) over \(E\); \(E\otimes(V_D/C_D)=E[x]_{\le D}/C_D^E\). Let \(f\in\ker_EM(\lambda)\), so with \(q_\lambda=\sum\lambda_ig_i\), \(h_jq_\lambda f\in C_D^E\) for all \(j\), of degree at most \(b+a+k\). Multiplying by \(q_\lambda\), of degree at most \(a\), \(h_jq_\lambda^2f\in C_D^E\) by (M) over \(E\), as \(b+k+2a\le D\). Now \(q_\lambda^2=\sum\lambda_i^2g_i^2\), and \(h_j(g_i^2+g_i)f\in C_D^E\) by (B) and (M) over \(E\), degree \(2a+b+k\le D\). Hence \(\sum\lambda_i^2h_jg_if\in C_D^E\), that is \(f\in\ker_EM(\lambda^2)\). By Step 4 the rank of \(M(\lambda)\) over \(E\) is \(\operatorname{rank}\overline M\), and by Step 2 some specialization has rank at least half of it. \(\square\)

Normalized rank and gain

Fix \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) of degree at most \(d\ge1\) and \(P\in V_a\). For \(k\) with \(a+k+d\le D\) let \(Z_P(k)=\{f\in V_k:Pg_if\in C_D\text{ for all }i\}\) and \(c_P(k)=\dim V_k/Z_P(k)\).

Lemma (normalized rank monotone). Let \(a+K+d\le D\) and \(1\le K\le n\). Then \(c_P(k)/N_n(k)\) is nonincreasing for \(0\le k\le K\); in particular \(c_P(K-d)\ge\frac{N_n(K-d)}{N_n(K)}c_P(K)\) when \(K\ge d\).

Proof. For \(k\le K\): \(Z_P(k)=Z_P(K)\cap V_k\); \(C_D\cap V_k\subseteq Z_P(k)\) by (M); and \(x\,Z_P(k)\subseteq Z_P(k+1)\) for \(k<K\), since \(Pg_if\in C_D\) has degree at most \(a+d+k\le D-1\). Take a degree-respecting monomial order in which the pivot variables of the reduced affine equations of \(\mathcal Q\), if any, are the largest variables. Let \(\mathcal L\) be the set of leading monomials of nonzero elements of \(Z_P(K)\) and \(\mathcal B\) the monomials of degree at most \(K\) outside \(\mathcal L\); \(\mathcal B\) is a basis of \(V_K/Z_P(K)\), and its elements of degree at most \(k\) are a basis of \(V_k/Z_P(k)\), because an element of \(Z_P(K)\) with leading monomial of degree at most \(k\) lies in \(V_k\). \(\mathcal L\) is closed under multiplication by variables within degree \(K\): if \(f\in Z_P(K)\) has leading monomial of degree at most \(K-1\), then \(\deg f\le K-1\), so \(f\in Z_P(K-1)\), \(xf\in Z_P(K)\), and the leading monomial of \(xf\) is \(x\) times that of \(f\). So \(\mathcal B\) is closed under taking divisors. \(\mathcal L\) contains every \(x_i^2\) when \(K\ge2\), and every pivot variable: sums of the affine axioms lie in \(C_D\) for \(D\ge1\), each row of the reduced system has one pivot variable as its leading monomial, consistency excluding the row \(1\), and \(C_D\cap V_1\subseteq Z_P(K)\) as \(K\ge1\). So \(\mathcal B\) consists of squarefree monomials in the \(n\) non-pivot variables: a family of subsets of an \(n\)-set closed under taking subsets. With \(b_j\) its number of \(j\)-sets, counting pairs of a \((j+1)\)-set of the family and a \(j\)-subset gives \((j+1)b_{j+1}\le(n-j)b_j\), so \(b_j/\binom nj\) is nonincreasing. \(c_P(k)/N_n(k)\) is the average of these ratios for \(j\le k\) with weights \(\binom nj\), and adding a term not above the earlier ones does not raise an average. \(\square\)

Gain. Let \(K\ge d\) and \(a+K+2d\le D\). Then some \(q\in\operatorname{span}\{g_i\}\) has \(c_{P(1+q)}(K)\le c_P(K)-\tfrac12c_P(K-d)\).

Proof. On \(V_{K-d}\) let \(M_if=([Pg_\alpha g_if])_\alpha\). The stacked kernel is \(Z_P(K-d)\): if \(f\in Z_P(K-d)\), \(Pg_\alpha f\in C_D\) has degree at most \(a+K\) and (M) applies with \(g_i\); conversely \(Pg_i^2f\in C_D\) and \(P(g_i^2+g_i)f\in C_D\) by (B) and (M), degree \(a+K+d\), give \(Pg_if\in C_D\). So \(\operatorname{rank}\overline M=c_P(K-d)\). Apply rank retention with \(h_\alpha=Pg_\alpha\), \(b=a+d\), the \(g_i\) of degree at most \(d\), \(k=K-d\): \(b+k+2d=a+K+2d\le D\). It gives \(q=\sum c_ig_i\) with \(\operatorname{rank}(f\mapsto([Pg_\alpha qf])_\alpha)\ge\tfrac12c_P(K-d)\) on \(V_{K-d}\), that is \(\dim(qV_{K-d}+Z_P(K))/Z_P(K)\ge\tfrac12c_P(K-d)\). With \(P'=P(1+q)\): \(Z_P(K)\subseteq Z_{P'}(K)\) by (M), degree \(a+d+K+d\); and for \(f\in V_{K-d}\), \(P'g_iqf=Pg_if(q+q^2)\in C_D\) by (B) and (M), degree \(a+d+K-d+2d\). So \(\dim Z_{P'}(K)\ge\dim Z_P(K)+\tfrac12c_P(K-d)\). \(\square\)

Theorem (binary approximator rank). Let \(1\le d\le K\le n\), \(t\ge1\) and \(dt+K+d\le D\). For every family \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) of degree at most \(d\) there are \(q_1,\ldots,q_t\in\operatorname{span}\{g_i\}\) such that \(P=\prod_{j\le t}(1+q_j)\) satisfies

\[c_P(K)\ \le\ h_K\Bigl(1-\tfrac12\frac{N_n(K-d)}{N_n(K)}\Bigr)^{t},\qquad h_K=\dim V_K/(C_D\cap V_K).\]

Proof. For \(P=1\), \(C_D\cap V_K\subseteq Z_1(K)\) by (M), so \(c_1(K)\le h_K\). In round \(j\le t\) the current product has degree \(a\le(j-1)d\); the lemma applies, as \(a+K+d\le D\), and the gain, as \(a+K+2d\le(j+1)d+K\le dt+K+d\). Together \(c_{P(1+q)}(K)\le c_P(K)\bigl(1-\tfrac12N_n(K-d)/N_n(K)\bigr)\). \(\square\)

This is the statement imported in the bounded-depth transfer entry, with the free dimension included. The hypothesis \(1\notin C_D\) is not needed for it; it is used in the leveled lemma, where \(h_K>0\) is needed.

The transfer as one statement

Theorem (near-half Frege transfer). Fix a Frege system \(F\) in the DeMorgan language, a depth \(d\ge1\) and \(A>0\), and let \(c_F\) be the constant of the simulation, \(\ell=d+c_F\). For each \(n\) in an infinite set let \(F_n\) be a CNF of width \(w_n\) and \(\Lambda_n\) a set of affine equations over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(n=v_n-\operatorname{rank}\Lambda_n\), \(v_n\) the number of variables of \(F_n\) and \(\Lambda_n\) together, such that the polynomial system of the clause polynomials of \(F_n\), the Boolean axioms and \(\Lambda_n\) has no polynomial calculus refutation of degree \(\lfloor n/2-s_n\rfloor\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), where \(s_n\ge0\) and \((s_n+w_n)(\log n)^{\ell-2}=o(n)\); here \(\ell\ge2\), and \(\Lambda_n\) is consistent because the system has no refutation. Then for all large \(n\), \(F_n\) has no \(F_d(\mathrm{MOD}_2)\)-refutation with at most \(n^A\) symbols.

Proof. The Frege parity size bound, whose only condition was the theorem just proved. \(\square\)
