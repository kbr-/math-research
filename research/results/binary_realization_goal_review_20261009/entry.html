A useful near-half import: intrinsic shadow ratios

The user's standing request to exchange useful results prompted a fresh read of the near-half notebook. Its Kruskal–Katona growth lemma was read in full, together with its scope warnings. Here is its direct extension from binary PC closure to any field's Boolean pseudoideal, using the same proof. This is an attributed generalized import, not an independently new shadow method. The near-half notebook still reports no qualifying family.

Let \(J_D\subseteq\mathbb F[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Fix a degree-compatible monomial order and polynomials \(E_i\) of degree at most \(e\), with \(e+K\le D\). Set \[ Z_k=\{f\in V_k:E_if\in J_D\ \forall i\},\qquad c(k)=\dim V_k/Z_k,\quad 0\le k\le K. \] Let \(m_j\) be the number of degree-\(j\) standard monomials of \(J_D\). Suppose \(1\le\delta\le K\), \(X\ge K\) is real, and \(m_j\le\binom Xj\) for every \(K-\delta<j\le K\).

Corollary (intrinsic shadow ratio). If \(c(K)>0\), then \[ \frac{c(K-\delta)}{c(K)} \ge\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\rho^i\right)^{-1}, \qquad \rho=\frac{X-K+\delta}{K-\delta+1}. \] If \(c(K)=0\), the error tuple is already killed on \(V_K\), and no ratio is asserted. In particular \(\rho\le1+1/\delta\) gives at least \(1/(1+e_{\rm Euler}\delta)\). A weight or prefactor is included inside the \(E_i\), and its degree must be included in \(e\).

Proof. The spaces satisfy \(Z_k=Z_K\cap V_k\), \(J_D\cap V_k\subseteq Z_k\), and \(xZ_k\subseteq Z_{k+1}\) for \(k<K\), by bounded absorption. The standard monomials of \(Z_K\) consequently form a downward-closed family \(\mathcal M\) of squarefree monomials, contained in the old standard family. A degree-compatible order makes its cumulative level count exactly \(c(k)\). Indeed leading monomials multiply within the cutoff, and Booleanity removes repeated powers. This argument uses no characteristic-two identity.

For a nonempty degree-\(j\) level in the specified window, write its size as \(\binom{x_j}{j}\), with \(j\le x_j\le X\). The Lovász shadow bound and its iteration imply that level \(i_0=K-\delta\) has at least \(\binom{x_j}{i_0}\) elements. Thus its ratio to that lower level is at most \[ \frac{\binom{x_j}{j}}{\binom{x_j}{i_0}} =\prod_{t=i_0}^{j-1}\frac{x_j-t}{t+1} \le \rho^{j-i_0}. \] Sum over the window and bound that lower level by \(c(i_0)\). If every upper level is empty, the cumulative ratio is one. This proves the formula; \((1+1/\delta)^\delta\le e_{\rm Euler}\) gives its stated consequence. \(\square\)

This repairs no false base-Hilbert-ratio theorem. In the existing five-point control, take \(K=2,\delta=1,X=2\): the new bound is \(2/3\), below the actual error ratio \(3/4\), whereas the base cumulative ratio \(4/5\) is invalid. It uses upper bounds on individual top levels and controls every contained error family, rather than substituting one cumulative quotient ratio. Its level-cap hypothesis remains unproved for the relevant main source. The same proof also accepts a verified cap on the actual error standard family, instead of the larger old family; no such source-wide cap is assumed. The all-field extension concerns this shadow ratio only, not the binary gain theorem.

Projective cover cost

Let \(S_0\xrightarrow{a_0}S_1\to\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{D-1}}S_D\) be finite-dimensional vector spaces over any field. Put \[ \beta_0=\dim S_0,\qquad \beta_b=\dim S_b-\operatorname{rank}a_{b-1}\quad(b\ge1). \] A cover here is a diagram \(E_0\hookrightarrow E_1\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow E_D\) with injective structure maps and commuting surjections \(\pi_b:E_b\to S_b\). This is a projective cover construction for the finite equioriented path; “injective” describes its structure maps, not an injective object in the module category.

Lemma (projective cover cost). The smallest possible terminal dimension is \[ \min\dim E_D=\sum_{b=0}^D\beta_b =\dim S_D+\sum_{b=0}^{D-1}\dim\ker a_b. \] One construction simultaneously attains \(\dim E_j=\sum_{b\le j}\beta_b\) at every grade. It supplies only the structure maps and projections, not lifts of other operators or source relations.

Proof. In any such cover, the map from \(E_b\) onto \(S_b/\operatorname{im}a_{b-1}\) kills the image of \(E_{b-1}\), by commutativity. It is onto, so \[ \dim E_b-\dim E_{b-1}\ge\beta_b. \] Also \(\dim E_0\ge\beta_0\). Summing proves the lower bound at every grade. For attainment choose a complement \(C_b\) to \(\operatorname{im}a_{b-1}\) in \(S_b\), with \(C_0=S_0\). Let \(E_j=\bigoplus_{b\le j}C_b\), keep all earlier summands under inclusion, and send \(v\in C_b\) to \(a_{j-1}\cdots a_bv\), or to itself when \(b=j\). Induction from \(S_j=\operatorname{im}a_{j-1}+C_j\) proves that this projection is onto. Its squares commute by construction. The final equality is the telescoping rank-nullity identity. \(\square\)

For the normalized Boolean horizon control, every inclusion has rank one. The formula gives terminal cover cost \(2^n-1\), exactly its full continuation rank. Retaining only the terminal observed dimension one loses all the intervening births. The construction makes that cost explicit, but cannot turn a degree-two NS design of the path control into a proper PC representation: lifting all its source equations and legal multiplications would contradict its degree-two PC refutation.

An algebraic input family need not already be a CNF
