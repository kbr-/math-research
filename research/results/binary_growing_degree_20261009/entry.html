A hard graph and real mass on the same restriction

Lemma (hard restriction mass). Let \(G\) be a simple graph on \(n\ge2048\) vertices, of maximum degree at most \(2\Delta\), satisfying \[ |\partial_G S|\ge(\Delta/4)|S|\quad(0<|S|\le n/2). \] Retain edges independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\), put \(t=p\Delta\), and assume \(t\ge128\log(2n)\). Assign all other edges independent uniform labels in \(\mathbb F_3\). For any old charges of nonzero total, with probability at least \(1-\eta\), where \[ a=n e^{-t/32},\qquad \eta=\frac a{1-a}+n e^{-2t/3}<\frac14, \tag{1} \] the live graph \(H\) has maximum degree at most \(4t\) and edge expansion at least \(t/8\). On this event the restricted canonical one-hot Tseitin CNF has no ordinary PC or NS refutation over \(\mathbb F_2\) through \[ D_H=\lfloor tn/128\rfloor. \tag{2} \] The same hard event works for every assignment of the fixed labels.

Let \(F\) be any fixed categorical CNF on the edge labels, with clause width at most an integer \(\kappa\ge0\), and choose integer \(b\ge1\) with \[ \theta=\sum_{\ell=b+1}^{\kappa}\binom{\kappa}{\ell} \left(\frac{3p}{1-p}\right)^\ell<1. \] There exists a restriction on that hard event for which the full-live-cube real signed width-\(b\) expansion of \(F|_\rho\) has absolute mass at most \[ \frac1{(1-\theta)(1-\eta)}. \tag{3} \] For \(\theta\le1/2\) this is at most \(8/3\). For a fixed finite list of \(M\) such CNFs there is one hard restriction whose sum of expansion masses is at most \(M/((1-\theta)(1-\eta))\); no simultaneous constant bound for each member is asserted.

Cut concentration. For independent Bernoulli sums of mean \(\mu\), exponential Markov at \(2^{-X}\) gives \(\Pr(X\le\mu/2)\le\exp(-(1-\log2)\mu/2)\le e^{-\mu/8}\). Similarly exponential Markov at \(2^X\) gives \(\Pr(X\ge2\mu)\le\exp(-(2\log2-1)\mu)\le e^{-\mu/3}\). These follow from \(\mathbb E e^{sX}\le\exp(\mu(e^s-1))\), obtained by multiplying \(1+q_i(e^s-1)\le\exp(q_i(e^s-1))\).

For a fixed \(S\), \(s=|S|\le n/2\), its retained cut has mean at least \(ts/4\). The probability it has fewer than \(ts/8\) edges is at most \(e^{-ts/32}\). There are at most \(n^s\) such sets, so a union bound gives failure at most \(\sum_{s\ge1}a^s=a/(1-a)\). For a vertex, the retained degree has mean at most \(2t\). The same upper exponential bound, evaluated at threshold \(4t\), gives probability at most \(e^{-2t/3}\), even if the actual mean is smaller. A second union bound proves (1). Our assumption gives \(a\le1/(16n^3)\); the displayed second term is at most \(n(2n)^{-256/3}\), so \(\eta<1/4\).

The remaining edge-domain axioms are exactly those of \(H\). Thus the residual encoding is the ordinary charged encoding already audited, with \(\Delta_H\le4t\), \(\varepsilon_H\ge t/8\). Equation (2) satisfies \[ \max(4,2\Delta_H)\le D_H<\varepsilon_H n/8 \] under the stated numerical range. Apply the arbitrary-charge theorem and its Fourier/CNF degree ledger. Ordinary NS simulates in PC at its original degree. This constructs a new residual pseudoideal; it does not assert that the old one absorbs the indicator of all fixed edges. Constant restriction of an original proof preserves its degree, but the retained ENS generators still remain and require their own treatment.

Joint real mass. The categorical reverse-path theorem supplies a nonnegative bound \(W\) on absolute expansion mass with \(\mathbb EW\le(1-\theta)^{-1}\), on precisely this product law. Since \(\Pr(H\text{ is good})\ge1-\eta\), \[ \mathbb E[W\mid H\text{ is good}] \le\frac{\mathbb EW}{1-\eta}. \] A restriction no larger than that conditional average exists, proving (3). Sum the \(M\) bounds for the stated list version. This is no independence assumption about \(W\) and the hard event.

A concrete width choice is \[ b=\max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{6e\kappa p}{1-p}\right\rceil\right). \tag{4} \] If \(b\ge\kappa\), \(\theta=0\). Otherwise \(\binom{\kappa}{\ell}\le(e\kappa/\ell)^\ell\) makes every summand for \(\ell>b\) at most \(2^{-\ell}\), giving \(\theta\le2^{-b}\le1/2\). For \(p\to0\) and \(\kappa\) of order \(\Delta\), this has \(b=O(p\kappa+1)=o(\kappa)\). \(\square\)
