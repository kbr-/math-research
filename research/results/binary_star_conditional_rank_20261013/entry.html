Star mask slowdown

Proposition. Let \(G\) be a simple \(n\)-vertex graph of edge expansion \(\varepsilon>2\), minimum degree \(d\) and maximum degree \(R\). Use any nonzero-total ternary charge vector, the one-hot binary encoding and the descended charged pseudoideal \(J_{2,D}\), with \(2R+4\le D<\varepsilon n/8\). Fix an edge \(e\) and a label \(a\). Put \(g=x_{e,a}\), \(w=1\), \(K=0\), and initial prefix \(P=1\). For the review's one-input sampler, condition on its uniformly chosen vertex \(v\) of degree \(r_v\), and independently choose binary \(\xi_\tau\) for each charge-compatible pattern \(\tau\) of that star. Let \(\beta_v=\sum_\tau\chi_{v,\tau}\xi_\tau\). The initial error rank is one, and the new rank is zero with probability \[ \pi_v=\begin{cases}2^{-3^{r_v-2}},&v\text{ incident with }e,\\2^{-3^{r_v-1}},&v\text{ not incident with }e.\end{cases} \]

Consequently \(\mathbb E c(1+\beta_vg)=1-\pi\), where \(\pi=n^{-1}\sum_v\pi_v\le2^{-3^{d-2}}\). On any admissible growing-degree family with \(d\to\infty\), no fixed positive \(\gamma\) can make this at most \((1-\gamma)c(1)\). This law is nevertheless applied to an easy source: a deterministic scalar row \(\beta=1\) kills its only companion. A half-scalar, half-star mixture has expected rank \(3/4-\pi/2\le3/4\) on this same control. These are rank statements in the paid charged quotient, not point-density surrogates.

Faithful local patterns inside the charged space

Completed star slowdown

Corollary. In the same growing graph family, fix \(0<\alpha<\min(1/17,1/\ell)\), keep \(h=\lceil\Delta\log n\rceil\) and all original costs, and use an independent single-star sample in every one of the parent's \(h\) rows. At \(w=1,K=0\), the final companion rank has expectation tending to one. By contrast, the half-scalar, half-star law has expected final rank at most \((3/4)^h\) on this particular one-input source. This extension tests whether later rows could supply a fixed fractional gain missing from the first-row calculation. It does not refute a strictly positive but tiny chance of successful removal or a strict joint-rank estimate.

Proof: random centers keep a connected complement. Put \(q=h/n\), \(L=\lfloor\Delta/8\rfloor\) and \(r_* =\lceil\Delta/8\rceil\). The sampled vertices are distinct and avoid the endpoints of \(e\) except with probability at most \(h(h-1)/(2n)+2h/n=o(1)\). Conditional on distinctness their set \(U\) is uniform among the \(h\)-subsets. For any fixed set of \(t\) vertices, its containment probability is at most \(q^t\), by the falling-factorial ratio.

If \(G-U-e\) is disconnected, a smallest component \(S\) is a connected subset of \(G\) of size \(s\le n/2\). All its external neighbors must belong to \(U\), except possibly the outside endpoint of the fixed edge \(e\) when \(e\) crosses \(S\); otherwise none is exempt. The required neighbor subset is therefore fixed by \(S,e\), so its containment probability is at most \(q^{t-1}\), without an extra choice factor. Let \(t=|N_G(S)\setminus S|\). Simplicity and minimum degree give \(t\ge d-s+1\); expansion and maximum degree give \(t\ge\varepsilon s/R\ge s/8\). For \(s\le L\), therefore \(t-1\ge L\). For \(s>L\), \(t-1\ge s/8-1\). Each connected \(s\)-set can be encoded by a root and a depth-first walk of a spanning tree, of length \(2(s-1)\); its visited set recovers it. There are at most \(nR^{2(s-1)}\) such sets. A union bound consequently gives \[ \Pr(G-U-e\text{ disconnected}\mid\text{distinct}) \le nL(R^2q)^L+\frac nq\frac{\theta^{L+1}}{1-\theta}, \qquad \theta=R^2q^{1/8}, \]

for sufficiently large \(n\) with \(\theta<1\). Here \(R^2q=O(n^{3\alpha-1}\log n)\) and \(\theta=O(n^{(17\alpha-1)/8}(\log n)^{1/8})\). Both bases tend to zero and \(L\) grows as a positive power of \(n\), so the bound is \(o(1)\). Dropping the event \(S\cap U=\varnothing\) only enlarges the probability bound; no independence of components is assumed.

Also, for any vertex, the probability that \(U\) contains at least \(r_*\) of its neighbors is at most \(\binom R{r_*}q^{r_*}\le(16\exp(1)q)^{r_*}\). A union bound over vertices tends to zero. Thus with probability \(1-o(1)\), the centers are distinct, miss the edge endpoints, have a connected complement even after removing \(e\), and each has fewer than \(r_*\) neighbors among the centers. Each then has at least \(d-r_*+1\ge L\) edges to the complement.

Proof: a compatible surviving pattern. On this event fix the labels of edges joining two centers to zero. Each center still has at least \(L\) private star edges; the number of completions satisfying its charge is at least \(3^{L-1}\). Its random coefficient function has no zero on this completion set only when all these independent sampled bits are one, of probability at most \(2^{-3^{L-1}}\). Except with probability \(h2^{-3^{L-1}}=o(1)\), every center has a compatible private completion with coefficient zero. The private edge sets of different centers are disjoint. Combine those completions and give \(e\) its chosen label \(a\).

Let \(A=\bigcup_{v\in U}\operatorname{star}(v)\cup\{e\}\), of size at most \(hR+1\). Since \(G-U-e\) is connected, the local cut space on \(A\) is generated by the center cuts. Their vertex potentials have support in \(U\), which is smaller than \(n/2\). The combined pattern satisfies all those charge constraints, so its indicator \(Q\) has a nonzero class by the local character argument. The completed companion is \[ E=g\prod_{j=1}^h(1+\beta_jg). \]

At the selected pattern, \(g=1\) and each \(\beta_j=0\). The local-domain eigenidentities therefore give \(EQ=Q\) modulo the old domain equations, through degree at most \(2hR+h+2\). This fits \(RB\le D\), since \(B\ge2h^2+3h\), \(R\ge3\), and \(R(2h^2+3h)\ge2hR+h+2\). If \(E\) belonged to the old pseudoideal, bounded absorption by \(Q\) would put the nonzero class of \(Q\) in it, a contradiction. Hence the final constant-domain rank is one with probability \(1-o(1)\), proving the expected-rank limit.

For the mixed law, each row has probability one quarter of selecting the scalar coefficient one. Any such row puts the factor \(1+g\) in the completed product, so the companion vanishes by \(g(1+g)=0\), with all other factors absorbed inside the same original reserve. The probability no such row occurs is \((3/4)^h\), proving the upper bound. No average over actual global satisfying assignments of the inconsistent old system was used.
