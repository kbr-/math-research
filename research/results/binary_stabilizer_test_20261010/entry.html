A degree-paid algebra, with its actual limitations

Let \(F\) be any field, \(D\ge2\), and \(J\subseteq F[x]_{\le D}\) a proper degree-\(D\) pseudoideal: it is a linear subspace, does not contain one, and is closed under every variable multiplication whose result stays within degree \(D\). Put \[ Q_i=F[x]_{\le i}/(J\cap F[x]_{\le i}),\qquad \mathcal E_D=\bigoplus_{i=0}^D Q_i. \] The copies in different degrees are distinct. For homogeneous classes define \[ [f]_i[g]_j= \begin{cases}[fg]_{i+j},&i+j\le D,\\0,&i+j>D.\end{cases} \] Let \(z=[1]_1\), and distinguish the algebra unit \([1]_0\) from the nonzero marked class \([1]_D=z^D\).

Proposition (truncated stabilizer algebra). This is a finite-dimensional commutative unital graded algebra. Its positive-degree ideal \(\mathfrak m=\bigoplus_{i=1}^D Q_i\) has \(\mathfrak m^{D+1}=0\), and its only idempotents are zero and one. Inverting \(z\), or imposing \(z=1\), makes the algebra zero. If \(F\) is infinite and \(\dim Q_D\ge2\), it has infinitely many unital subalgebras. Already for the one-bit Boolean pseudoideal, the degree-at-most-one space times the degree-at-most-\(D-1\) space is the entire algebra, with product ratio \((2D+1)/(2D-1)\), whose stabilizer is the entire algebra but still has no nontrivial idempotent.

Proof: construction. If \(f-f'\in J\cap F[x]_{\le i}\) and \(g\in F[x]_{\le j}\), then \((f-f')g\in J\) whenever \(i+j\le D\). Expand \(g\) into monomials and use legal bounded multiplication at every step. Thus multiplication is well-defined. If three homogeneous degrees sum to at most \(D\), both associations give the same polynomial product. If their sum exceeds \(D\), both associations are zero; this also covers an intermediate product already beyond \(D\). Bilinearity proves associativity in general, and commutativity and the unit are immediate. This uses the same paid multiplication as filtered dual transport, not an uncharged multiplication on a single \(Q_D\).

Proof: nilpotence and idempotents. Since \(J\) is proper, \(Q_0=F\). Products of \(D+1\) positive-degree elements vanish. For an idempotent \(e\), its constant coordinate is either zero or one. In the first case \(e\in\mathfrak m\) is nilpotent and idempotent, hence zero. In the second case \(1-e\) has both properties, hence is zero. Also \(z^{D+1}=0\), while \(z^D=[1]_D\ne0\) by properness. Localizing at a nilpotent gives the zero algebra. Imposing \(z=1\) likewise sends \(z^{D+1}=0\) to \(1=0\). Thus dehomogenizing this already truncated algebra does not recover the original filtered spaces.

Proof: subalgebras. Choose independent \(u,v\in Q_D\) in the top summand. It has square zero. For every \(\alpha\in F\), the space \(F1\oplus F(u+\alpha v)\) is a unital subalgebra. Distinct parameters give distinct lines modulo the unit. If \(F\) is infinite, there are infinitely many such subalgebras. In particular extending a finite-field envelope to an infinite rational-function field does not preserve finiteness of its subalgebra inventory.

Proof: Boolean boundary control. Take exactly the minimal degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J=\{(x^2-x)g:\deg g\le D-2\}\) of the one-bit Boolean system. This is not an arbitrary larger proper pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Then each \(Q_i\), \(i\ge1\), has basis \(1,x\). The resulting algebra is \[ \mathcal E_D\cong F[z,X]/(X^2-zX,\ (z,X)^{D+1}), \] where \(X=[x]_1\). A basis is \(1\), together with \(z^i,z^{i-1}X\) for \(1\le i\le D\). Hence its dimension is \(2D+1\). Let \(A=\mathcal E_{\le1}\) and \(B=\mathcal E_{\le D-1}\) denote their vector spaces inside this algebra. They contain the unit, have dimensions three and \(2D-1\), and \(AB\) spans all of \(\mathcal E_D\): the lower grades are already in \(1B\), and multiplying grade \(D-1\) by \(z\) gives both top basis vectors. Its stabilizer is therefore the whole algebra. The product ratio means \(\dim\operatorname{span}(AB)/\dim B=1+2/(2D-1)\), tending to one, despite the absence of nontrivial idempotents. The algebra generated by \(A\) is the whole envelope, so over an infinite field it fails the finite-subalgebra hypothesis just proved. This is a satisfiable one-bit control, not a hard formula. \(\square\)

Why this matters for the actual interface

The construction respects every multiplication it represents, so the failure is not that no ambient algebra can be written down. Its algebraic properties are different from those of a Boolean function algebra. In \(\mathcal E_D\), a Boolean coordinate satisfies \(X^2=zX\), not \(X^2=X\). Every subalgebra still has only trivial idempotents. A theorem producing a stabilizer subalgebra without a semisimplicity guarantee therefore does not automatically produce a selector.

The marked-state and unit hypotheses also differ. A candidate space sitting in a positive homogeneous grade contains only nilpotent elements, hence no invertible element; its nonzero old-unit class \(z^K\) is not the ambient unit. Adding \([1]_0\) changes the space being measured. In the one-bit example the broad graded sum does contain a unit, but near-minimal relative product growth comes from reaching the imposed upper cutoff, not from a surviving source branch. These observations do not prove that every filtered adaptation fails; they identify the costs and hypotheses that such an adaptation must replace.

Over the untruncated Boolean algebra there is a different problem. It has many genuine idempotents, but imposing every unsatisfiable old axiom in a full algebra kills the unit. A source argument cannot replace the proper truncated old quotient by that full quotient. One may still use an algebra on a restricted represented action, provided its degree and source interpretation are proved.
