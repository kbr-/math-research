Coordinate line coefficients

Lemma. Over any field \(k\), write \(B_i=x_i^2-x_i\), and suppose \[ P(x)=\sum_{i=1}^v q_i(x)B_i. \] For each \(j\), put \(I_{-j}=(B_i:i\ne j)\). The class of \(q_j\) modulo \(I_{-j}\) is determined by \(P\), independently of the certificate. Let \(t=x_j\), and define the polynomial divided difference \[ \mathcal D_a f=\frac{f(t)-f(a)}{t-a}\qquad(a\in\{0,1\}), \quad R_j=\mathcal D_1\mathcal D_0P. \] These quotients are formal polynomial identities, not division gates. Then \(q_j\equiv R_j\pmod{I_{-j}}\). If a division-free arithmetic circuit of size \(S\) and depth \(d\), with unbounded fan-in and size counting gates and wires, computes \(P\), all \(v\) representatives have a joint circuit of size \(O(v(S+1)^4)\), depth at most \(4d+4\), over the same field. These are not necessarily a full correction tuple or multilinear normal forms in the other coordinates.

Class proof. Polynomial division gives the exact identity \[ P=t(t-1)R_j+(1-t)P|_{t=0}+tP|_{t=1}. \] Both endpoint polynomials belong to \(I_{-j}\), by evaluating the supplied certificate. Reducing it modulo \(I_{-j}\) therefore yields \(B_j(q_j-R_j)=0\). The quotient ring is \(A[t]\), where \(A=k[x_i:i\ne j]/I_{-j}\). Multiplication by the monic polynomial \(t^2-t\) is injective even though \(A\) has zero divisors: the highest coefficient of a nonzero product cannot disappear. Hence the classes agree. Applying the same argument to a syzygy proves independence under every coefficient change. Equivalently, fix all coordinates other than \(j\) to Boolean constants and leave \(x_j\) formal; this equivalence follows from the two-root Chinese remainder decomposition in the other variables.

Circuit proof. For one divided difference, retain each original gate value, its evaluation at \(t=a\), and its divided difference. Sums commute with \(\mathcal D_a\). At a product gate with inputs \(f_1,\ldots,f_s\), use \[ \mathcal D_a\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^s f_i\right) =\sum_{i=1}^s (\mathcal D_af_i) \prod_{u<i}f_u(t)\prod_{u>i}f_u(a). \] Telescoping proves this identity, including repeated inputs. At an input gate, the divided difference is one for \(t\) and zero for all other variables or constants. The products and final sum use at most two additional layers per original layer; their total new wire cost is bounded by a constant times \(\sum s^2\le S^2\), with shared original/evaluation gates. Thus one pass costs \(O((S+1)^2)\) and depth at most \(2d+1\); applying two passes costs at most \(4d+3\), below the stated conservative bound. Repeat for the \(v\) output representatives. Only zero, one and the original field constants are used. No interpolation grid or extraction of an arbitrary homogeneous component occurs. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For \(P=B_j\), the line coefficient is one. For \(P=B_j^s\), \(s\ge2\), its matching line class is \(B_j^{s-1}\), nonzero as a polynomial in the free coordinate although all Boolean-point values vanish. Thus the domain really extends cube traces, while its representative remains cheap.

Degenerate cases. Zero residuals have zero matching line classes. Constant and empty gates use the usual constant divided differences. Other coordinates are Boolean modulo \(I_{-j}\); this is not a statement about arbitrary affine lines moving several coordinates. No PC-degree or full-certificate construction is inferred.

The earlier cor:coefficient-cube-traces has a sharper size/depth bound for its smaller domain, so no blanket refinement of that circuit bound is claimed. The present representatives need not assemble: for \(P=B_1B_2\), all matching line classes are zero, while \(P\) is a nonzero formal polynomial. Also a Koszul change \((q_1,q_2)\mapsto(q_1+B_2,q_2-B_1)\) can change \(q_1\) on a line where coordinate two is free. The invariant for \(q_j\) uses its matching coordinate \(x_j\), not any freely chosen coordinate.

Rank coordinate freedom

Proposition. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix and \(B\) an \(n\times m\) matrix of independent entry variables, with \(m>n\ge1\), \(r=m-n\), and \[ P=\prod_{i,j=1}^m\bigl(1+\delta_{ij}+(AB)_{ij}\bigr). \] After any choice of at most \(r\) individual entry variables is left formal and every other entry is fixed to a Boolean constant, the resulting polynomial is identically zero. A restriction with \(r+1\) formal entries gives \[ \prod_{j=n}^m t_j(1+t_j)^r\ne0, \] so the threshold is sharp. If \(b_e=x_e^2+x_e\) ranges over all entry Boolean axioms and \(J_s=(\prod_{e\in S}b_e:|S|=s)\), with distinct indices in each generator, then \(P\in J_{r+1}\setminus J_{r+2}\). The same witness gives exact ordinary Boolean ideal order \(r+1\), as in the earlier result. For every formal correction \(P=\sum_e q_eb_e\), \[ q_e\in (b_f:f\ne e). \] This last statement concerns matching coordinate lines.

Vanishing proof. Let \(R\) be the row indices of \(A\) containing a free entry, and \(C\) the column indices of \(B\) containing a free entry. If at most \(r\) entries are free, \(|R\cup C|\le r\). Every \((AB)_{ij}\) with \(i\notin R,j\notin C\) is a fixed binary constant. If \(P\) is not identically zero, each corresponding constant factor equals one, so this rectangular block agrees with the corresponding identity entries.

Put \(S=[m]\setminus(R\cup C)\). Then \(A_SB_S=I_{|S|}\), where \(A_S\) means the selected rows and \(B_S\) the selected columns. If \(|S|>n\), rank makes this impossible. Otherwise \(|S|=n\) and all preceding cardinality inequalities are equalities. In particular \(|R\cup C|=r\) and \(R\cap C=\varnothing\). The fixed square matrices \(A_S,B_S\) are invertible. If \(i\in R\), then \(i\notin C\), and the unchanged off-diagonal entries give \(A_SB_i=0\), so the fixed column \(B_i=0\). Therefore \((AB)_{ii}=0\) even when row \(A_i\) varies, making its diagonal factor zero. If \(j\in C\), similarly \(A_jB_S=0\), so the fixed row \(A_j=0\) and its diagonal factor is zero. Since \(r\ge1\), at least one of these sets is nonempty. This contradicts nonzero \(P\) and proves vanishing.

Sharpness. Set \(A_i=e_i\) for \(i<n\), and \(A_i=e_n\) for \(i\ge n\). For columns \(j<n\), set \(B_j=e_j\); for \(j\ge n\), set \(B_j=t_je_n\). Exactly \(r+1\) individual entries are free. The upper \((n-1)\)-square is identity and the cross blocks are zero. In the lower \((r+1)\)-square every column is constant \(t_j\), giving the displayed nonzero product. This works also for \(n=1\). Its lowest term at all \(t_j=0\) is \(\prod_jt_j\), of degree \(r+1\).

Ideal interpretation. For a set \(T\) of free variables, vanishing on every such formal coordinate face is equivalent to membership in \((b_e:e\notin T)\): the quotient is the product, over Boolean assignments outside \(T\), of polynomial rings in \(x_T\). To intersect these ideals, regard \(\mathbb F_2[x]\) as a free module over \(\mathbb F_2[b_e:e]\), with basis \(x^\epsilon\), \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{2mn}\). This follows by adjoining independent symbols \(z_e\), imposing the separate monic equations \(x_e^2+x_e=z_e\), and eliminating either the \(z_e\) or the squares of the \(x_e\). The normal form is unique by successive monic division.

In that free-module representation, intersection over all \(|T|=s-1\) reduces coefficientwise to the coordinate ideals \((z_e:e\notin T)\). A monomial belongs to their intersection exactly when it uses at least \(s\) distinct \(z_e\). The intersection is therefore \(J_s\). The vanishing and sharpness restrictions give \(P\in J_{r+1}\setminus J_{r+2}\). Since \(J_{r+1}\subseteq I^{r+1}\), \(I=(b_e)\), ordinary order is at least \(r+1\). The sharpness restriction has local order exactly \(r+1\), so \(P\notin I^{r+2}\), recovering lem:rank-residual-order while strengthening its support statement.

Coefficient consequence. Since \(r+1\ge2\), every generator of \(J_{r+1}\) contains an axiom different from any prescribed \(b_e\). Thus \(P\) is zero modulo \(I_{-e}\). In the reduced correction identity only \(b_eq_e\) remains. The monic regularity argument of lem:coordinate-line-coefficients gives \(q_e=0\) there, for every correction tuple, with no choice of canonical representative. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. The sharp witness applies at every \(m>n\ge1\), with unbounded dimensions. For \(m=2n\), the threshold is \(n+1\); for \(m=n+1\), it is two. The residual has an explicit polynomial-size constant-depth product circuit, so this property is not itself circuit hardness.

Degenerate cases. At \(n=0\) a diagonal factor is identically zero; at \(m\le n\) a Boolean identity factorization exists. Both are excluded. The result counts free individual entry variables, not free linear parameters that move many entries. Even within the stated family, off-matching coefficient queries can be changed by cheap Koszul syzygies.

What the invariant does not supply

The squarefree Boolean support ideal is stronger than the corresponding ordinary power in general: repeated powers of one \(b_e\) have high local multiplicity but do not vanish on that coordinate line. Nevertheless \(\prod_{e\in S}b_e\) itself has large squarefree support threshold and a small constant-depth circuit. Neither support order nor the coordinate arrangement alone gives a lower bound. The full formal correction remains the circuit task; the zero matching line classes do not reconstruct it.

Minimal face corrections

Corollary. In the rank setting, fix all but exactly \(r+1\) entry variables to Boolean constants, and call the free set \(T\). The restricted residual \(P_T\) is either zero or has the factorization \[ P_T=\left(\prod_{e\in T}(t_e^2+t_e)\right)H_T, \] where \(H_T\) has a product-of-quadratic-factors circuit of size \(O(m^2n)\) and constant depth over \(\mathbb F_2\). For any chosen \(e\in T\), the quotient \(Q_e=P_T/(t_e^2+t_e)\) has such a circuit, giving the formal correction \(P_T=(t_e^2+t_e)Q_e\) with all other coefficients zero. This is a face-dependent construction, not a global certificate of the same cost.

Proof. Setting any \(t_e\) to zero or one leaves at most \(r\) free entries, so the preceding proposition makes the resulting polynomial zero. Thus both \(t_e\) and \(t_e+1\) divide \(P_T\); all these linear polynomials for distinct coordinates and values are pairwise nonassociate primes in \(\mathbb F_2[t_T]\). Their entire product divides \(P_T\). If it is nonzero, its original factors are nonzero polynomials of degree at most two. Each required linear divisor divides at least one such factor. Cancel them successively, allocating one occurrence of each prime divisor to a factor. The remaining factors still have degree at most two; none acquires a denominator.

Each original factor has at most \(O(n)\) monomials after restriction. A quotient of a quadratic by a variable or its shift is linear, with no new variable support, and a second division leaves a constant. Thus the total factor description remains \(O(m^2n)\); sum-of-monomials circuits for the factors followed by one product layer have constant depth. Cancelling only the two divisors for the chosen \(e\) proves the stated small \(Q_e\), and multiplying by its placeholder gives the correction circuit. If \(P_T=0\), use the zero correction. No claim about choosing these factors coherently as the face varies is used. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. The preceding sharp face is nonzero and gives \(H_T=\prod_j(1+t_j)^{r-1}\). Both zero and nonzero faces are included; no arbitrary small-dimensional satisfiable model is substituted for the rank residual.

Correction to the earlier coefficient control

The rank-order entry says that at most one tuple has all coefficients in \(I^r\). As a global uniqueness assertion this is false; the valid comparison is at most one of the two tuples related by its displayed unscaled Koszul change, for \(r\ge2\). Deep tuples exist because \(P\in I^{r+1}=\sum_e b_eI^r\). Given one, add the syzygy \(h(b_2Z_1+b_1Z_2)\) with nonzero \(h\in I^{r-1}\), for example \(h=b_1^{r-1}\). The tuple remains deep and changes nontrivially. The unscaled change still proves that arbitrary representatives need not be deep. This dated correction changes neither the exact ordinary-order theorem nor its proof, and the new coordinate-freedom proof does not rely on uniqueness.
