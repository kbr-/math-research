Charge a potential path using the existing balanced proof

Work over \(\mathbb F_4\) in the ternary Fourier system, with maximum graph degree \(\Delta\), and its affine one-hot encoding. For a vertex potential \(t\in\mathbb F_3^V\), write \(\alpha=\nabla t\), \(q=|\operatorname{supp}\alpha|\). Suppose its nonzero coordinates can be installed one at a time, with intermediate potentials \(t_j\), starting at zero and ending at \(t\), so that \[ |\operatorname{supp}\nabla t_j|\le C. \tag{1} \] The balanced-cut induction gives a derivation of a disjoint-support binomial \(A-\omega^{c\cdot t}B\), with exponent difference \(\alpha\), through \[ \max\{3,2\Delta,\lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta+1\}. \tag{2} \] Its final factors have degrees at most \(\lceil q/2\rceil\). This is an application of the earlier proof to ternary-valued updates, not an assumed change of its degree measure.

Here are the required details. An update of value one uses the original vertex character equation \(X_v-\omega^{c_v}\). Value two uses \(X_v^{-1}-\omega^{-c_v}\), where the inverse is the degree-\(\deg(v)\) monomial with every \(U,V\) exchanged. Multiply the original equation by that monomial, reduce \(U^2=V,V^2=U,UV=1\), and scale; all lines fit \(2\Delta\). If the current balanced relation is \(A-aB\), add its product with the update character \(X\) to \(aB(X-b)\), with the appropriate sign, obtaining \(AX-abB\). Each multiplication costs at most \(\lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta\). Cancel common edge factors and rebalance by multiplying the whole relation by one inverse edge character at a time. The temporary degree increases by at most one; local reductions do not increase it. These are exactly the prior cancellation and balancing operations.

Finally multiply by \(B^{-1}\) and reduce locally. The first term costs \(\deg A+\deg B=q\), the second at most \(2\deg B\le q+1\). The resulting unbalanced relation \[ m_\alpha-\omega^{c\cdot t} \tag{3} \] therefore has an ordinary PC derivation through \[ \max\{3,2\Delta,\lceil C/2\rceil+\Delta+1,q+1\}. \tag{4} \] Affine transfer to one-hot axioms retains the \(2\Delta\) ceiling and degree-three CNF row conversion already included here. All inverse operations are polynomial character multiplications, never formal division. The formula is an upper bound, not a claim of minimal degree.

Peeling makes the path support monotone

Call the required graph property: every nonempty \(S\subseteq V\) with \(|S|<n/4\) contains a vertex \(v\) with \[ 2\deg_{G[S]}(v)\le\deg_G(v). \tag{5} \] For a potential supported on \(S\), remove that vertex by setting its value to zero. Edges from \(v\) outside \(S\) cease to be nonzero in the gradient; edges to equal-valued vertices in \(S\) become nonzero; edges to different nonzero values stay nonzero. The support change is \[ \deg_{\rm same}(v)-\deg_{\rm outside}(v) \le2\deg_{G[S]}(v)-\deg_G(v)\le0. \] Repeated removal applies (5) to the remaining support. Reverse the order to obtain (1) with \(C=q\). Different nonzero potential values cause no extra update or degree cost.

Such expanding graphs exist in the growing-degree regime

Lemma (peelable expander family). For any sequence \(\delta=\delta(n)\) with \(\delta/\log n\to\infty\) and \(0<\delta\le n-1\), sufficiently large \(n\) admit simple graphs with degrees in \([0.9\delta,1.1\delta]\), edge expansion at least \(\delta/4\), and (5). A lexicographically first such graph is a well-defined family; no efficient graph generator is asserted.

Proof. Sample each edge independently with probability \(\delta/(n-1)\). Standard exponential-Markov bounds for a sum of independent Bernoulli variables give degree failure at most \(2n\exp(-\delta/300)\). These bounds follow by \(\mathbb E e^{uX}\le\exp(\mathbb EX(e^u-1))\), minimizing over \(u\); the upper and lower relative deviations \(0.1\) permit the displayed safe constant.

For a fixed set \(S\) of size \(s\le n/4\), the expected internal edge count is at most \(\delta s/8\). The same bound for the event \(e(S)\ge\delta s/5\) gives probability at most \(\exp(-\delta s/64)\). Indeed its exponent rate is at least \[ \delta s\left(\tfrac15\log(8/5)-\tfrac3{40}\right) \ge \tfrac9{520}\delta s>\tfrac1{64}\delta s, \] using \(\log(1+x)\ge2x/(2+x)\) at \(x=3/5\). Union-bound with \(\binom ns\le n^s\): the failure over all such nonempty sets is at most \(a/(1-a)\), \(a=n e^{-\delta/64}\), once \(a<1\). Every remaining set has average internal degree below \(0.4\delta\), whereas every vertex degree is at least \(0.9\delta\). Some vertex satisfies (5).

For any fixed \(S\) of size \(s\le n/2\), its expected cut size is at least \(\delta s/2\). A lower-tail exponential-Markov bound gives \[ \Pr(|\partial S|<\delta s/4)\le e^{-\delta s/16}. \] The union bound is at most \(b/(1-b)\), \(b=n e^{-\delta/16}\). The sum of these two bounds and the degree-failure bound tends to zero. Thus a graph with all properties exists. This proof charges the entire set inventory; a typical-set estimate alone would not prove peeling. \(\square\)

Joint restriction scope. Choose, for example, \(\delta=n^\alpha\) and \(p=n^{-\beta}\) with \(0<\beta<\alpha<1\). If an independently sampled graph above is thinned by independent \(p\)-live edges, its marginal live graph is exactly the same random-graph model with mean degree \(t=p\delta\). Since \(t/\log n\to\infty\), the lemma applies to that live graph too. Let \(e_n\to0\) bound its failure probability. Markov's inequality shows that all but at most \(\sqrt{e_n}\) of the original graphs have conditional live failure probability at most \(\sqrt{e_n}\). Original good graphs have probability tending to one, so one can fix an original graph satisfying both conditions, independently of any proof or its size exponent.

For that fixed family, live expansion, bounded degree and peeling hold jointly with probability \(1-o(1)\); every fixed label choice preserves total charge. The source's real full-cube mass estimate applies to every fixed eligible CNF under this same independent restriction law. If its expected mass is at most \(M_0\), conditioning on the good live event gives a restriction with mass at most \(M_0/(1-o(1))\). For a list, apply this only to its total mass and pay the list inventory. The canonical encoding still has logarithmic length \(\Theta(\delta)\); no succinct encoding or efficient conditional selection is inferred. This establishes an available family for the strengthened test, not peeling for every residual graph of every earlier fixed family.
