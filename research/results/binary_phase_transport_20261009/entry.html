Sequential phase growth

Work over \(E=\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2(\omega)\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), with old Boolean axioms. For binary polynomials \(g_i\) of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), put \(u_i=1+\omega g_i\), \(v_i=1+\omega^2g_i\). Then \[u_iv_i=1+g_i+g_i^2.\tag{1}\] The correction \(g_i^2-g_i\) has an ordinary Boolean certificate through degree \(2\delta\): square termwise in characteristic two, then replace squared variables one at a time in each squared monomial. The coefficient field is used for analysis; these phases are not legal replacements for the source's binary coefficient variables.

Lemma (sequential phase growth). Let \(f\) have degree at most \(e\), and let \(U=u_{i_1}\cdots u_{i_r}\), \(r\ge1\). From a PC derivation of \(Uf\) through degree \(B\), together with the old Boolean equations, one can derive \(f\) through \[\max\{B,e+(r+1)\delta\}.\tag{2}\] Hence multiplication by this phase word is injective on degree-\(e\) quotient classes in any Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal when \(e+(r+1)\delta\le D\).

In particular, for the binary-defined nonzero tuple space \(Y\) of input phase growth, with coordinate representatives through \(d_0\), \(\mathcal B=\operatorname{span}(1,g_i)\), and its canonical largest minimum-growth subspace \(X\) of ratio \(\rho\), one has \[\dim\mathcal B^\ell X\le\rho^\ell\dim X\qquad\text{if}\qquad d_0+(\ell+1)\delta\le D,\quad\ell\ge1.\tag{3}\] The minimizer is binary and has the same minimum over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(E\), as before.

Proof of cancellation. Given the completed line \(u_{i_1}\cdots u_{i_r}f\), multiply it by \(v_{i_r}\). Use (1) to subtract the Boolean consequence \[(g_{i_r}^2+g_{i_r})u_{i_1}\cdots u_{i_{r-1}}f.\] This leaves \(u_{i_1}\cdots u_{i_{r-1}}f\). Both multiplication and correction have degree at most \(e+(r+1)\delta\). Repeat on the resulting completed line. Subsequent peaks are no larger; earlier lines of the derivation are never multiplied by an accumulating inverse. Multiplication by a polynomial here means its ordinary sequence of monomial multiplications and a linear combination, all at the same ceiling. This proves (2). For quotient injectivity, perform the same membership argument inside \(J_D\); every multiplication and Boolean correction fits. The empty word is the identity and needs no inverse reserve.

Proof of magnification. For completeness, the dimension argument remains the same. Choose a minimizer \(X\subseteq Y\). For words \(c_j\) of at most \(\ell-1\) phases, set \(T_j=\sum_{i\le j}c_iX\) and \(Z_j=\{x\in X:c_jx\in T_{j-1}\}\). Injection on \(X\) gives \(\dim T_j-\dim T_{j-1}=\dim X-\dim Z_j\). On \(\mathcal BX\), the representative degree is at most \(d_0+\delta\); cancelling a word of length at most \(\ell-1\) needs at most \(d_0+(\ell+1)\delta\) by (2). Relation propagation by one input fits \(d_0+\ell\delta\). Therefore \[\dim\mathcal BT_j-\dim\mathcal BT_{j-1}\le \dim\mathcal BX-\dim\mathcal BZ_j\le\rho(\dim X-\dim Z_j).\] The last inequality is minimality, including \(Z_j=0\). Summing over a list spanning the words of each length gives \(\dim\mathcal B^jX\le\rho\dim\mathcal B^{j-1}X\) and then (3). The identity and the phases span the same \(\mathcal B\), so their words span its powers.

To retain binary scope, the submodular argument from the full predecessor proof applies unchanged: \(F(L)=\dim\mathcal BL\) satisfies \(F(L+N)+F(L\cap N)\le F(L)+F(N)\), so sums of minimizers minimize. Their largest sum is unique and fixed by coefficient Frobenius. If \(z=A+\omega C\) lies in it, then \(C=z+\sigma z\) and \(A=z+\omega C\) lie in it and are binary. These binary vectors span the minimizer, with unchanged ranks after extension. No additional reserve is used in this descent. \(\square\)

The imported character lemma itself uses products of one-variable characters and balanced support transport. We do not assert that nonlinear factors have disjoint support or inherit its support count. The transferred mechanism is reuse of a final line; (1) and the full new cancellation proof establish the nonlinear statement independently. The older doubled-reserve lemma remains valid. At \(\ell=1\), (3) has the same stated reserve as before; at larger \(\ell\), it is strictly weaker.

A fixed joint batch

Fix a binary degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, a binary weight \(w\) of degree at most \(b\), and an earlier-obligation domain \(U_0\subseteq V_K\) with \(\dim[wU_0]>0\). There are \(M\ge1\) fixed input tuples \(g_{a,i}\) of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), with fixed binary prefixes \(P_a\) charged at most \(p_0\). Each distinct block has \(h\ge1\) legal binary rows remaining. These inputs and prefixes are fixed before selecting those rows: a newly chosen row in this batch does not change another member's tuple. Every occurrence of the same block uses its one common coefficient choice.

Let \[Tf=([wP_ag_{a,i}f])_{a,i},\quad Y=T(U_0),\quad \mathcal B=\operatorname{span}(1,g_{a,i}:a,i),\quad d_0=b+p_0+K+\delta.\] If \(Y=0\), every selected error already vanishes on this domain. Otherwise let \(X\subseteq Y\) be its canonical largest \(\mathcal B\)-growth minimizer, with ratio \(\rho\), and put \(U_X=T^{-1}(X)\), \(c=\dim X\), \(H_X=\dim[wU_X]\).

Corollary (joint growth removal). Suppose \[b+p_0+K+(h+2)\delta\le D,\qquad M(\rho/2)^h<1.\tag{4}\] Then one coherent family of legal constant binary rows and some \(f\in U_X\) satisfy \(wf\notin J_D\) and \[wP_a\prod_{j=1}^h(1-\textstyle\sum_i C_{a,ji}g_{a,i})\,g_{a,i}f\in J_D \quad\text{for every }a,i.\tag{5}\] Earlier obligations encoded in \(U_0\) persist. Original source replay and remaining dependency levels are not included in (4); they retain their separate degree conditions.

Proof. Absorption gives \(\ker(w|_{U_X})\subseteq\ker(T|_{U_X})\), so \(H_X\ge c>0\). All these spaces and a preimage basis can be chosen over \(\mathbb F_2\), by (3)'s descent. Apply (3) at \(\ell=h\): its reserve \(d_0+(h+1)\delta\le D\) is precisely the first condition in (4), giving \(\dim\mathcal B^hX\le\rho^hc\).

Let \(\pi_a\) project a joint tuple onto block \(a\). For the existing input-word shortening theorem, its enlarged block image on the fixed domain \(U_X\) is exactly \[\mathcal B_a^h\pi_aX,\qquad \mathcal B_a=\operatorname{span}(1,g_{a,i}:i).\] Indeed \(T(U_X)=X\), and polynomial multiplication commutes with each tuple coordinate and projection within the displayed reserve. Its dimension is at most \(\dim\mathcal B^hX\le\rho^hc\). The shortening theorem, with the same \(w,P_a,U_X,\delta,h\), bounds the expected actual block error rank by \(2^{-h}\rho^hc\). Sum over the \(M\) blocks. Condition (4) makes this sum less than \(c\le H_X\). Choose a single family of block matrices attaining a strict sum deficit; stacking their actual maps still has rank below \(H_X\). Its kernel contains a nonzero weighted class with binary representative \(wf\), proving (5). Membership in \(U_X\subseteq U_0\) preserves the earlier constraints. No graded-rank summation or deletion of return terms occurs. \(\square\)

This is a conditional composition of the old arbitrary-domain shortening and minimum-growth mechanisms. At \(h=1\), using the generated enlargement already gives the threshold \(\rho<2/M\) directly; it is not a new one-row theorem. The new reserve matters for \(h>1\). With the older phase bound the same composition asked for \(b+p_0+K+(2h+1)\delta\le D\); now it asks only for the shortening buffer. It does not certify a small \(\rho\). For instance \(\rho=4/3\) permits \(M(2/3)^h<1\), but the preceding construction supplies that value only for its explicit easy-to-normalize inputs, not for general source batches.

Effect on the route and controls

The refined reserve also permits the kernel tuple floor at \(d_0+(\ell+1)s\le D\), by taking all monomials through \(s\) as the phase inputs and repeating its weighted-coordinate proof. All other hypotheses remain, including \(D-d_0\) covering the old input width and two. That remains a lower bound on full old-multiplier growth, not an upper estimate on the actual input action. The controlled returning-rank cost, original companion degrees and old weighted survival conditions all remain.

The constant-input case gives scalar phases and trivial cancellation. At a nonbinary value \(g=\omega\), (1)'s correction need not vanish, so the argument cannot be extended to arbitrary extension-field inputs while omitting their Boolean certificates. One phase uses the original \(e+2\delta\) reserve; a long word of \(r\) degree-\(\delta\) factors has the explicit peaks \(e+(r+1)\delta,e+r\delta,\ldots,e+2\delta\), instead of simultaneous inverse multiplication at \(e+2r\delta\). These are exact symbolic controls. There is no new proof rule or encoding: every step is ordinary PC multiplication plus a supplied Boolean consequence.

When \(M=n^A\) and a uniform \(\rho\le2-\epsilon\) with fixed \(\epsilon>0\) were proved on the coherent joint domain, logarithmically many rows would satisfy the second condition of (4). The missing implication is precisely such a source-derived growth or comparable actual-error bound, together with the next-level degree/weight accounting. The corollary does not assume it away or change the goal to proving low growth for arbitrary sources. A joint minimizer may have very small weighted preimage rank; (5) only promises one surviving class.
