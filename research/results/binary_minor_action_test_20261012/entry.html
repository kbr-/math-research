A consistent algebra in which to identify the image

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with \(A\) of shape \(m\times n\), \(B\) of shape \(n\times m\), \(m\ge k\), \(1\le k<n\). Fix \(k\) outer indices \(I\), and let \(G\) consist of all Boolean axioms and the partial rank equations \((AB)_{I,I}=I_k\). This system is consistent: use an identity frame for these indices and set all other entries to zero. Let \(R\) be the polynomial ring in all entries. For generators \(F\), write \(I_q(F)\) for ordinary ideal certificates whose individual generator–cofactor products have degree at most \(q\). This is not notation for degree-\(q\) PC closure.

Let \(H\) be the following finite set of squarefree degree-\(k+1\) monomials: \[H=\left\{\prod_{j\in J}A_{i_jj}: |J|=k+1,\ J\subseteq[n],\ i_j\in I\right\}.\tag{1}\] Their Boolean zero set says that the selected rows collectively use at most \(k\) columns. The partial rank equations force their row rank to be \(k\), so exactly \(k\) columns are used at each point of \(G+H\).

For each \(k\)-set \(S\) of inner indices, use the relabeled adjugate chart \(\sigma_S\) from the preceding entry, and put \[w_S=\det A_{I,S},\quad v_S=\det B_{S,I},\quad p_S=w_Sv_S,\quad \Lambda(f)=\sum_{|S|=k}p_S\sigma_S(f),\qquad T=k+1.\tag{2}\] Every chart fixes \(AB\) and has degree at most \(T\). In the selected rows, it fixes the columns in \(S\) and multiplies each outside entry by \(1+w_S\). Fresh spectator variables, if included, are fixed throughout.

Theorem (minor chart retraction). For \(\deg f\le d\), \[\Lambda(1)-1\in I_{2k}(G),\qquad f-\Lambda(f)\in I_{2k+Td}(G+H).\tag{3}\] For \(q\ge\max(2,k+1)\), \[\Lambda\bigl(I_q(G+H)\bigr)\subseteq I_{2k+Tq}(G).\tag{4}\] Consequently \(\Lambda\) descends to a unital idempotent linear map on the consistent algebra \(Q=R/(G)\), whose kernel is exactly the ideal generated by \(H\) in \(Q\). Its image, with product \(u*v=\Lambda(uv)\), is isomorphic to \(R/(G+H)\). In particular the induced actions \(T_x(u)=\Lambda(xu)\) satisfy \(T_x\Lambda(f)=\Lambda(xf)\). Explicitly, with \(c=2k(1+T)\), \[\Lambda^2(f)-\Lambda(f)\in I_{c+T^2d}(G),\qquad \Lambda(x\Lambda(f))-\Lambda(xf)\in I_{c+T^2d+T}(G).\tag{5}\] These are ordinary certificate identities. They do not by themselves establish kernel stability at one fixed PC cutoff after replacing \(G\) by the full inconsistent rank base.

Proof with the original degree costs

Unit and comparison. The preceding Cauchy–Binet identity gives \(\sum_Sp_S=\det((AB)_{I,I})\) and the first certificate in (3). For any selected-row entry \(A_{ij}\) with \(j\notin S\), expand \(w_S A_{ij}\). Each determinant monomial uses one entry in each of the \(k\) columns \(S\), so after multiplication it is one of the generators (1). Thus \(w_S A_{ij}\in I_{k+1}(H)\).

Every coordinate correction \(\sigma_S(x)-x\) is an entry of either \(A_{\cdot,S}\operatorname{adj}(A_{I,S})A_{I,S^c}\) or \(\operatorname{adj}(A_{I,S})A_{I,S^c}B_{S^c,\cdot}\), with zero corrections on the untouched positions. After multiplying by \(p_S\), the preceding \(H\)-certificate is multiplied by \(v_S\) of degree \(k\) and further factors of total degree at most \(k\). Therefore \[p_S(\sigma_S(x)-x)\in I_{3k+1}(H).\] Telescope a degree-\(d\) monomial one coordinate at a time. The other factors have total degree at most \(T(d-1)\), giving \(3k+1+T(d-1)=2k+Td\). For constants the difference is zero. Finally \[f-\Lambda(f)=(1-\sum_Sp_S)f+\sum_Sp_S(f-\sigma_S(f)),\] and the unit-certificate term costs \(2k+d\le2k+Td\). This proves (3) without assuming that the charts preserve ordinary products after they are summed.

Certificate transport. Fix one chart. A partial rank generator is fixed literally. The image of a Boolean generator has a Boolean certificate through \(2T\). For \(h\in H\), its \(k+1\) distinct column indices include at least one outside \(S\). The corresponding selected-row entry maps to \((1+w_S)A_{ij}\). Multiplying by \(p_S=w_Sv_S\), use the Boolean certificate \(w_S^2+w_S\) through \(2k\). The remaining factors cost at most \(k\) from \(v_S\), one from \(A_{ij}\), and \(kT\) from the other \(k\) transformed entries. The resulting ceiling is \[3k+1+kT=2k+T(k+1).\] For a term \(a h\) of an ordinary certificate, the substituted cofactor has degree at most \(T\deg a\); the same argument for each type of generator gives the uniform bound \(2k+Tq\). Sum over terms and charts. This proves (4) and also shows that \(\Lambda\) is well-defined modulo \((G)\).

Image and actions. Let \(J\) be the ideal of \(H\) inside \(Q\). Formula (4), with arbitrary finite \(q\), says that \(\Lambda\) kills \(J\). Formula (3) says \(f-\Lambda(f)\in J\). Hence \(\ker\Lambda=J\), and applying \(\Lambda\) to that difference proves idempotence. Since \(J\) is an ideal, \(\Lambda(x\Lambda(f))=\Lambda(xf)\) follows, and the image with the stated transported product is exactly \(Q/J\). Its variable actions commute and are Boolean because the ordinary variable actions on \(Q/J\) do. This algebra is consistent; no nonzero full quotient of the contradictory rank equations is invoked.

For the explicit ceilings, apply (4) to the certificate \(f-\Lambda(f)\) from (3), at \(q=2k+Td\). This \(q\) is at least \(\max(2,k+1)\), even for \(d=0\). The resulting ceiling is \(2k+T(2k+Td)=c+T^2d\). Multiply the certificate in (3) by \(x\) first and apply (4) at \(q+1\), giving the second ceiling in (5). These calculations prove paid identities, not an assertion that applying \(\Lambda\) to an arbitrary degree-\(D\) PC derivation stays at \(D\). \(\square\)
