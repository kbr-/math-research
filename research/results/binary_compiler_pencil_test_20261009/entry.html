Paid old-affine helper completion

Corollary (paid affine helper completion). Let \(J_D\) be a degree-\(D\) binary Boolean pseudoideal containing an old system \(\Gamma\) on \(v\) variables, and let \(w_0\notin J_D\), \(\deg w_0\le b\). Fix \(M\ge1\) finite old-affine tuples before choosing their rows, and integers \(1\le K\le v\), \(t\ge1\), with \[ b+K+t+1\le D,\qquad M(1-\eta)^t<1,\qquad \eta=\frac12\frac{N_v(K-1)}{N_v(K)}. \tag{1} \] Give each tuple a complete fresh ENS helper of accuracy at least \(t\), including all coefficient Booleanity equations. There are binary constant rows, zero-padded to those accuracies, and \(f\) of degree at most \(K\), such that \(W=w_0f\notin J_D\) and every weighted helper companion lies in \(J_D\). All helper coefficient-field images are zero. If an earlier fixed interpreted obligation satisfies \(w_0E\in J_D\), it remains true after multiplication by \(f\) whenever \(\deg(w_0E)+K\le D\).

Proof. Apply the single-branch case of surviving branch removal, with branch weight \(w_0\), identical actual and reference affine tuples, cutoff \(K\) and \(t\) rows. This is the Boolean-pseudoideal form of the recorded weighted approximation argument. Its rate and degree conditions are exactly (1). Its chosen row sums are constant binary combinations of the actual indexed inputs, hence legal coefficient assignments for the complete helpers. Zero-padding leaves the selected products unchanged; all fresh coefficient domains vanish. The new weight has degree at most \(b+K\) and survives by that theorem. The last assertion is bounded absorption in the old degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, reusing the earlier membership rather than multiplying an entire NS derivation. \(\square\)

The same application may normalize the original movable old-affine layer and all proposed helpers together, starting from weight one. It can instead extend a fixed surviving weight as stated. These are different timing choices for one coherent construction; no separately selected weights are glued. Boundary values already fixed by the compiler retain their existing certificates and are not freely reassigned. The corollary accounts only for the selected old-affine layer: later source inputs are actually specialized, and any final full proof replay must still fit the new weight degree plus its original replay ceiling.

On the fixed charged family with \(D=\lfloor\gamma v\rfloor\), choosing \(K=\lfloor\theta v\rfloor\) with fixed \(0<\theta<\min(1/2,\gamma)\) and a prescribed \(b\) leaving a positive linear reserve makes \(\eta\) bounded below. A polynomial helper inventory then needs \(t=O_A(\log v)\). Their original affine companion costs are \(2h+1\) for nonconstant positions and \(2h\) for constant positions in a mixed tuple, with \(h\ge t\); none is reset after padding. A polynomial collection of explicitly described affine bases also has polynomial tuple-description size. This is a finite chosen completion, not the entire closure under all subspaces or all word unions.

After simultaneous normalization, supplied prefixes and weighted companions give the pencil identity at its already proved buffer. Completion can make the containing image larger

Let \(r\ge0\), \(m\ge2\), and use Boolean coordinates \(z_1,\ldots,z_r,x_1,\ldots,x_m\). Set \[ P_H=\prod_{i=1}^r(1-z_i),\qquad P_a=P_H(1-x_1),\quad P_b=P_H(1-x_2),\quad P_{a+b}=P_H(1-x_1-x_2). \] These are exact zero-flat products for the common core and its three extensions. They have ordinary affine prefixes and complete Boolean companion identities. Fix the same weight \(w=P_H\), the scalar identity coordinate \(g=1\), prefix \(Q=1\), and multiplier domain \(U=V_K\) on these coordinates, with \(0\le K<m\). This is a dimension calibration; the scalar unit coordinate is trivially removable as an ENS input and is not a hard parent.

Proposition (pencil image enlargement). In the Boolean function algebra, the length-one feature-word image for the original feature list \(\{P_H\}\) has dimension \(N_m(K)\). After adjoining the three pencil extensions its dimension is \[ N_m(K)+\binom{m-1}{K}+\binom{m-2}{K}>N_m(K). \tag{2} \] Nevertheless the core feature is redundant in the enlarged image by the pencil identity. The same comparison can be embedded in any proper Boolean hard quotient with an unsatisfiable active family of maximum degree \(u\), provided \[ D\ge\max\{u,\,4(r+m+1)\}, \tag{3} \] using a chosen free-coordinate subspace for \(U\), not necessarily the whole old multiplier space.

Proof. Multiplication by \(P_H\) restricts to \(z=0\), and the original core word multiplies it by itself, so its image is exactly \(P_H V_K(x)\), of dimension \(N_m(K)\). The enlarged image is \[ P_H\operatorname{span}(V_K(x),x_1V_K(x),x_2V_K(x)). \] Besides degree-at-most-\(K\) monomials, it contains exactly the degree-\(K+1\) monomials using \(x_1\) or \(x_2\). Their number is \(\binom{m-1}{K}+\binom{m-2}{K}\), by choosing those using \(x_1\), then those using \(x_2\) but not \(x_1\). They are independent, and the first binomial is positive for \(K<m\). This proves (2), equivalently the earlier coordinate-word count. Meanwhile \(P_a+P_b+P_{a+b}=P_H\) literally over \(\mathbb F_2\), so deleting the core after completion changes no enlarged image.

For the hard-quotient version, apply weighted restriction cube at weight one. Under (3) it supplies at least \(r+m\) coordinates whose entire Boolean algebra injects linearly modulo \(J_D\). Choose those coordinates and use only their degree-at-most-\(K\) polynomials as \(U\). Boolean reduction and all displayed products fit (3); even the conservative pencil-image buffer \(4r+K+4\) is below it. Injectivity of that coordinate algebra preserves exactly the preceding dimensions and the nonzero weight. This is not a global satisfying assignment of the old unsatisfiable family, and no claim is made about replacing this chosen \(U\) by every old degree-\(K\) polynomial. \(\square\)

Result of the prescribed test
