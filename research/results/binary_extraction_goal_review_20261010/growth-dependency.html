A boundary inequality for quotient balls

The primary source is Diskin–Samotij, Isoperimetry in product graphs, Theorem 1 and Section 3.1, equation (3). Its stated inequality and entropy proof were read. For the Hamming graph on \(\mathbb F_q^r\), whose neighbors differ in exactly one coordinate, it gives \[ e(A,A^c)\ge(q-1)|A|\bigl(r-\log_q|A|\bigr) \] for every nonempty \(A\). We use this elementary weaker boundary bound, not an assertion identifying arbitrary quotient codes with Reed–Muller codes. No independent novelty is claimed for the entropy ingredient.

Let \(B\subseteq\mathbb F_q^m\) be linear, \(q\) a prime power, and \(r=m-\dim B\ge1\). Let \(A_j\subseteq\mathbb F_q^m/B\) consist of cosets with a representative of Hamming support at most \(j\); write \(H_B(j)=|A_j|\).

Corollary (code ball growth). Let \(R,K,s\) be nonnegative integers with \(K+s\le R\), and let \(0\le\eta<1\). If \(H_B(R)\le q^{\eta r}\), then \[ \frac{H_B(K+s)}{H_B(K)}\ge(2-\eta)^s. \] It suffices to verify the stronger numerical hypothesis \(\sum_{j\le\min(R,m)}(q-1)^j\binom mj\le q^{\eta r}\).

Proof. Images of the original coordinate vectors span the quotient. Choose \(r\) of them forming a basis and identify the quotient with \(\mathbb F_q^r\) using that basis. Adding a nonzero multiple of any chosen basis vector increases the original Hamming representative support by at most one. Therefore every Hamming-graph neighbor of \(A_j\) belongs to \(A_{j+1}\). Each vertex outside \(A_j\) is incident to at most \((q-1)r\) boundary edges. The imported boundary bound consequently gives \[ |A_{j+1}|-|A_j| \ge |A_j|\left(1-\frac{\log_q|A_j|}{r}\right) \ge(1-\eta)|A_j| \] for \(j<R\). Iterate from \(K\) to \(K+s\). The sufficient numerical hypothesis follows by counting all Hamming-ball representatives before quotienting. \(\square\)

This is a statement about finite coset sets. It uses no multiplication or evaluation model for the charged pseudoideal. Choosing a quotient basis merely supplies a subgraph of allowed one-coordinate moves; dependent original generators can only add further neighbors.

A concrete ternary range

For \(q=3\), suppose \(r\ge m/3\) and \(R\le m/16\). Then the numerical hypothesis holds with \(\eta=15/16\). Here is a bound with no floating-point constants. Put \(H_3(x)=(-x\ln x-(1-x)\ln(1-x)+x\ln2)/\ln3\). For \(0<\rho\le2/3\), the generating polynomial \((1+2z)^m\), evaluated at \(z=\rho/(2(1-\rho))\le1\), bounds the cumulative ball through \(\rho m\) by \(3^{mH_3(\rho)}\). At \(\rho=1/16\), use \(-(1-\rho)\ln(1-\rho)\le\rho\) to obtain \[ \sum_{j\le R}2^j\binom mj \le(32e)^{m/16} <3^{5m/16}\le3^{(15/16)r}. \] Indeed \(e<3\) and \(32\cdot3=96<3^5\). The case \(R=0\) is immediate; nonintegral \(\rho m\) causes no issue since the sum uses integer \(j\le R\le\rho m\). Thus \[ H_B(K+s)/H_B(K)\ge(17/16)^s \quad(K+s\le R\le m/16,\ r\ge m/3). \]

The constants are deliberately coarse. Optimizing them is not needed to decide whether a degree growing with the instance can be paid by this certificate.

Thus \(b+L\le D\) also holds. Under these direct-shortening admissibility hypotheses, the sufficient estimate obtained by using only \(m_w(L)\le h(L)\) and \(h_w(K)\ge\alpha h(K)\) cannot certify the required strict inequality in this range. This does not lower-bound \(m_w(L)/h_w(K)\).

Proof. Apply the preceding code-ball bound with \(s=t\delta\). Since \[ (17/16)^{12}\ge1+\frac{12}{16}+\frac{\binom{12}{2}}{16^2} =\frac{257}{128}>2, \] the displayed factor exceeds one for every \(\delta\ge12\). This proves the numerical statement. The direct prefix estimate supplies the upper bound \(M2^{-t}m_w(L)/h_w(K)\), and \(m_w(L)\le h_w(L)\). Substituting the two coarse comparisons yields precisely \(\mathcal C\). Its failure says nothing about whether either comparison discarded the needed gain. All Frobenius, original companion, weight-selection, and full-cofactor margins remain separate required hypotheses. \(\square\)
