Gram correction transport

Setup. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(m>n\ge1\), \(A=(a_{i\ell})\) have \(m\times n\) independent entries, \(E=AA^{\mathsf T}\), \(s_i=\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}\), and \(b_{i\ell}=a_{i\ell}^2+a_{i\ell}\). \[L_i=1+s_i,\qquad E_{ij}\ (i\lt j),\qquad W=\prod_{i\lt j}(1+E_{ij}),\qquad R=W\prod_i s_i.\]

The unique-upper quadratic Gram tuple instead has diagonal inputs \(G_i=1+\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}^2\) and the same off-diagonal inputs. All tuples include every Boolean generator. Do not identify the quadratic tuple's unique-upper residual with \(R^2\); the square below comes from the original rank tuple with both off-diagonal orientations retained.

Full generator transport. Restrict an original rank certificate at shape \((m,n)\) by \(B=A^{\mathsf T}\), identify paired Boolean placeholders with \(Z_{i\ell}\), send both \(Y_{ij},Y_{ji}\) to the one Gram placeholder for \(E_{ij}\), and retain the diagonal placeholder for \(G_i\). These are old-variable and placeholder identifications. They preserve the full general-IPS identities and do not increase gate/wire size, depth or formal degree. The diagonal identity

\[G_i=L_i+\sum_\ell b_{i\ell}\]

then permits transport in either direction between the quadratic Gram and parity/orthogonality tuples: replace the diagonal placeholder by the other diagonal placeholder plus \(\sum_\ell Z_{i\ell}\). This retains all fields, costs \(O(mn+1)\) shared wires and one layer, and preserves general-IPS identities, placeholder linearity when supplied, and formal joint-degree ceilings. Matching contraction

Statement. For every row set \(S\), write \(T_S\) for the sum over all matchings on \(S\), multiplying \(E_{ab}\) for each matched pair and \(s_a\) for each unmatched vertex. Put \(T_\varnothing=1\). Then \(T_S^2=\det E_{S,S}\). Thus \(T_S=0\) formally if \(|S|>n\). Define

\[D_i=\prod_{a\lt i}s_a,\quad S_{ij}=\{i+1,\ldots,m\}\setminus\{j\},\quad C_{ij}=T_{S_{ij}},\quad W_{ij}=\prod_{a\lt b,\ (a,b)\ne(i,j)}(1+E_{ab}).\]

With the complete original bilinear witnesses \(K_{ij}\) above, the full Gram correction is

\[Q_R(A,Z)=\sum_{i\lt j}D_iW_{ij}C_{ij}K_{ij},\qquad Q_R(A,b)=R,\qquad Q_R(A,0)=0.\]

It is linear in all original Boolean placeholders. Terms with \(i\lt m-n-1\) vanish formally. Given joint remaining matching-register circuits of size \(H\), depth \(e\), the complete correction costs \(O(H+m^4+m^2n+1)\), depth at most \(e+8\). Its old coefficient degree is at most \(m^2-2\), and its specialized output degree at most \(m^2\). These are conditional circuit and formal output bounds; matching polynomials or Pfaffians are not primitive gates, and no bound on supplied circuits' internal degrees is implied.

Proof. In the determinant expansion of a symmetric matrix in characteristic two, pair every permutation containing a cycle of length at least three by reversing the cycle containing its smallest vertex that belongs to such a cycle. The chosen vertex and cycle remain the same under reversal; the involution has no fixed permutation, and symmetry gives equal monomials. Those terms cancel. Remaining permutations are involutions: fixed points contribute \(E_{aa}=s_a^2\), and transpositions contribute \(E_{ab}^2\). Their sum is exactly \(T_S^2\). Rank makes the determinant zero for \(|S|>n\); the original polynomial ring is a domain, so squaring is injective and \(T_S=0\). This is a proof about an explicitly defined matching polynomial, not a circuit square-root algorithm.

Group nonempty matchings by their first matched vertex \(i\) and its partner \(j>i\). Every earlier vertex is unmatched, and the remaining matching is arbitrary on \(S_{ij}\). The complete identity is

\[T_{[m]}=\prod_a s_a+\sum_{i\lt j}D_iE_{ij}C_{ij}=0.\]

Multiply by \(W\), use \(W=W_{ij}(1+E_{ij})\), and replace \(E_{ij}(1+E_{ij})\) by \(K_{ij}(A,b)\). This proves the full correction and its truncation. Store all bilinears and witnesses once, build each literal mask and prefix product, then graft the supplied registers and two final layers; the claimed conservative depth and wire bounds follow. Each \(T_S\) is homogeneous of old degree \(|S|\), since an edge has degree two and an unmatched row sum degree one. The coefficient bound adds \((i-1)+2(\binom m2-1)+(m-i-1)+2=m^2-2\); substitution adds at most two. No stronger internal-circuit or PC bound is inferred.

Register test. These principal matching registers still contain generic determinants. For order \(2k\), use an alternating dual basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_k,v_1,\ldots,v_k\) in \(\mathbb F_2^{2k+1}\), constructed explicitly in the lift below. Give the first \(k\) rows the constant values \(u_r\), and the next \(k\) rows \(\sum_r x_{rs}v_r\), where \(X\) is a generic \(k\times k\) matrix. Row sums and same-role inner products vanish; cross inner products are \(x_{rs}\). Only perfect bipartite matchings remain, so \(T_S=\operatorname{perm}X=\det X\) in characteristic two. This is a binary affine old-variable substitution with polynomial wires and one added layer. At \(m=2n\), choose \(k=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\), \(i=m-2k-1,j=i+1\), so the tail has \(2k\) rows and fits the original \(n\) columns. For unbounded \(n\), cor:binary-determinant-depth-gap excludes a polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth implementation of those individual registers. No corresponding restriction of the complete masked \(Q_R\) is proved.

Structural mask test. At a Boolean old assignment, \(G_{ij}=D_iW_{ij}C_{ij}\) can be nonzero only if every off-diagonal inner product except \(E_{ij}\) is zero. Under that condition \(C_{ij}=\prod_{a\in S_{ij}}s_a\), so the other \(m-2\) rows must have odd parity and be pairwise orthogonal. They are linearly independent: dotting a linear relation with each such row gives its coefficient zero. Thus all weights are Boolean-zero when \(m-2>n\). Conversely, when \(m-2\le n\), set the two marked rows to zero and the other rows to distinct standard basis vectors, giving \(G_{ij}=1\). A nonzero weight alone need not make \(Q_R(a,Z)\ne0\): the marked witness then vanishes. At \(m=n+1\), set the marked rows both to \(e_1\) and all others to \(e_2,\ldots,e_n\). Only that marked group's mask survives and \(Q_R(a,Z)=Z_{i1}+Z_{j1}\ne0\). Hence a one-surplus Gram family evades this formula's Boolean-zero coefficient obstruction. These values do not prove formal circuit hardness; the earlier cube-erasure normalization still applies to supplied full certificates.

\[u_r=e_{2r-1}+e_{2r},\qquad v_r=e_{2r}+e_{2n+1}+\sum_{a\lt r}u_a.\]

\[p_i=f_i+\sum_rA_{ir}u_r,\qquad t_i=f_i+\sum_rB_{ri}v_r.\]

\[p_i\cdot p_j=\delta_{ij},\qquad t_i\cdot t_j=\delta_{ij},\qquad p_i\cdot t_j=\delta_{ij}+(AB)_{ij}.\]
