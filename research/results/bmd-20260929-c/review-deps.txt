The normalized binary face has a monic relation
Obstruction. In the two-root degeneration let \(N=M+2\ge5\), \(r=\binom M2\), and roots be \((\varepsilon b_1,\ldots,\varepsilon b_M,s,t)\). Use the same internal-pair Vandermonde and cross-row confluence as that proof, now retaining columns through the original first post-boundary order. Over the localized coefficient field in the \(b_i\), the normalized limit of the pair matrix is \[ \begin{pmatrix}I_r&0\\0&H_M^+(s,t)\end{pmatrix}, \] where \(H_M^+\) has \(2M+1\) rows and \(2M+3\) columns indexed \(j=r,\ldots,r+2M+2\). Indeed the first \(r\) internal columns have an invertible normalized block, and an internal column \(j\ge r\) has expansion in column \(i<r\) with factor \(\varepsilon^{j-i}\), so vanishes in this limit. Eliminating its first \(r\) columns leaves the same cross-row leading coefficients as before.
After the nonzero rational column scalings and polynomial cross-row changes of the binary-face proof, the first \(2M\) rows are the sequences \(j^a s^j,j^a t^j\), \(0\le a<M\). Using local column index \(k=j-r\), their polynomial null vectors are coefficient vectors of \[ L_M(u)(A+Bu+Cu^2),\qquad L_M(u)=(u-s)^M(u-t)^M. \] The full moment polynomial is \(u^rL_M(u)(A+Bu+Cu^2)\). For proof, the moment equations are equivalent over the fraction field to vanishing of the first \(M\) derivatives at \(s,t\). Monic division extends individual divisibility to \(k[s,t][u]\). The two monic irreducibles \(u-s,u-t\) are distinct prime elements there, so their powers divide the polynomial jointly, without inverting \(s-t\). The converse follows by differentiation. Polynomial division by the monic \(L_M\) makes \(A,B,C\) polynomial.
Put \(\alpha=3/2,\ \beta=\alpha-M+1\), and \[ h_j(s,t)=\sum_{a=0}^j\binom{-3/2}{a}\binom{-3/2}{j-a}s^at^{j-a}, \qquad f_j=\frac{(-1)^jj!}{(\beta)_j}h_j. \] The last row applied to the three null vectors \(L_M,uL_M,u^2L_M\) is, by the proved all-\(r\) finite-difference identity, \[ (st)^M(d_rh_r,\ d_{r+1}h_{r+1},\ d_{r+2}h_{r+2}), \quad d_j=\frac{(1-\alpha)_M(\alpha)_M}{(-j-\alpha)_M(j+\alpha+1)_M} \frac{(-1)^jj!}{(\beta)_j}\in\mathbb Q^\times. \] This is the homogeneous version of the identity at \((s,t)=(1,x)\); both sides have degree \(j+2M\), so it holds polynomially everywhere. Logarithmically differentiating \((1+sT)^{-\alpha}(1+tT)^{-\alpha}\) gives \[ (r+2)h_{r+2}=-(r+\alpha+1)(s+t)h_{r+1}-(r+2\alpha)st\,h_r. \] Thus take \(A=(r+2\alpha)st/d_r\), \(B=(r+\alpha+1)(s+t)/d_{r+1}\), \(C=(r+2)/d_{r+2}\). Their last-row image is zero and \(C\) is a nonzero constant. Undoing the rational column scalings retains a constant top coefficient. Its lower coefficients have precisely the degree differences of their columns, so its excess is zero.
The actual first-step ideal is proper, with initial degree at least two. Therefore this normalized limit cannot itself supply a positive lower bound by vanishing of its polynomial kernel. The row normalizations used powers of \(\varepsilon\) and Vandermonde denominators in the \(b_i\); this argument never identifies specialization of the actual kernel with the larger kernel of the limit. It rejects that direct vanishing test, not every possible degree-costed lifting argument. No numerical experiment is used in this obstruction.
