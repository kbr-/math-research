The common setting and statement

Work in \(\mathbb F_2[X]\), with every old variable's Boolean axiom \(X_i^2+X_i\) in a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal \(J_D\). This means a linear subspace of degree-at-most-\(D\) polynomials, closed under multiplying a member of degree at most \(D-1\) by an old variable. Successive multiplication and linearity give bounded absorption: \(A\in J_D\) implies \(BA\in J_D\) when \(\deg A+\deg B\le D\). No ordinary untruncated ideal quotient is substituted. For every binary polynomial \(A\) of degree at most \(r\), \(A^2+A\) has a Boolean certificate through degree \(2r\): sum the monomial identities, and telescope each monomial's repeated factors using the variable fields. Constants give zero. These certificates can be multiplied whenever the displayed total ceiling fits.

Lemma (unit cone filtering). Let a finite directed graph have a distinguished root, with each vertex either a leaf or equipped with two labelled child edges. Every vertex is reachable from the root by child edges. Sharing, repeated children and cycles are allowed. At each vertex take old binary polynomials \(P_v,F_v\), with \(\deg P_v\le L\), \(\deg F_v\le\delta\), where \(L,\delta\ge0\). Set \(Q_v=F_vP_v\), \(R=\max(1,L+\delta)\), and require the literal root values \(P_o=F_o=1\). For every internal vertex with children \(a,b\), define \[E_v=P_v+P_aP_b,\qquad E'_v=Q_v+Q_aQ_b.\] For \(\deg w\le b\), \(K\ge0\), and \(b+K+3R\le D\), these lists induce multiplication maps on the same full domain \(V=[wV_K]\) modulo \(J_D\), and \[\ker M_{E'}\subseteq\ker M_E,\qquad \operatorname{rank}M_{E'}\ge\operatorname{rank}M_E.\tag{UC}\] The weight need not survive for this algebraic statement. No assumption that the filters coincide, or that the old comparison errors vanish, is made.

Proof with degree resets

Every error has degree at most \(2R\). If \(wf\) represents zero in \(V\), bounded absorption kills its product with every error through \(b+K+2R\le D\); hence the maps are representative-independent. Fix a class in \(\ker M_{E'}\), represented by \(t=wf\), and put \(s=b+K\). Thus \(tE'_v\in J_D\) at every internal vertex. The literal unit root gives \(t(1+Q_o)=0\).

Suppose \(t(1+Q_v)\in J_D\). Adding \(tE'_v\) gives \(t(1+Q_aQ_b)\in J_D\), with its actual polynomial degree at most \(s+2R\). The identity \[(1+Q_a)(1+Q_aQ_b)+Q_b(Q_a^2+Q_a)=1+Q_a\] then gives \(t(1+Q_a)\in J_D\) through \(s+3R\le D\). The first term uses bounded absorption by \(1+Q_a\); the second uses the Boolean certificate of \(Q_a\), multiplied by \(tQ_b\), within that same reserve. Interchanging the children gives \(t(1+Q_b)\in J_D\). The result has actual degree at most \(s+R\), independently of the degree of the derivation just used. This is the reset that permits the same reserve at the next vertex.

Induct on the length of a shortest root-to-vertex path. Each reachable vertex therefore satisfies \(t(1+Q_v)\in J_D\). Cycles do not interfere with shortest-path induction; acyclicity is an unused source convenience and has been dropped from the statement. Sharing needs no independent choices, and repeated child edges simply repeat a valid conclusion.

Now use \(Q_v=F_vP_v\) and the exact identity \[(1+P_v)(1+Q_v)+F_v(P_v^2+P_v)=1+P_v.\] After multiplication by \(t\), the first term lies in \(J_D\) through \(s+R+L\le s+2R\), and the second by the Boolean certificate of \(P_v\) through \(s+\delta+2L\le s+2R\). Hence \(t(1+P_v)\in J_D\) at every vertex. Finally \[E_v=(1+P_v)+(1+P_a)+P_a(1+P_b)\] gives \(tE_v\in J_D\) through \(s+2L\le D\). This proves the kernel inclusion. Finite-dimensional rank-nullity on the same \(V\) gives the rank inequality. No sum of degrees over the number of vertices or the graph depth appears. \(\square\)

Scope and controls

Degenerate cases. A single root leaf has empty error lists and equal full kernels. With \(w\in J_D\), the domain is zero and both ranks are zero. Constant values and zero filters are permitted: a zero \(Q_v\) reachable from a unit root forces every proposed kernel class to be zero, as the proof predicts. Repeated children are covered by the same two identities. An unreachable component is excluded by the reachability hypothesis; it is not silently pruned if its errors are still counted.

Nonvacuity. Take one old Boolean variable \(x\), its Boolean ideal truncated at \(D=4\), \(w=1\), \(K=1\), \(L=1\), \(\delta=0\), \(R=1\). Use a unit root with the same leaf twice, \(P_a=x\). If all filters are one, the maps coincide with error \(1+x^2\); on \(\operatorname{span}(1,x)\) its quotient rank is one and its kernel is \(\operatorname{span}(x)\). If the leaf filter is zero while the root filter stays one, the new error is one, its rank is two and its kernel is zero. Thus the inclusion can be strict. Adding any number of unused old Boolean variables gives unbounded variable counts in the abstract lemma, with the same paid reserve. This is a symbolic control in a satisfiable quotient, not a new numerical experiment or a hard family.

The boundary assumptions matter. If the root filter is allowed to be zero, take a unit root with both children the zero leaf and set every filter to zero. The old root error is one and every new error zero, reversing the rank inequality on any nonzero domain. If the root old value may be nonunit, set it to zero, both leaf values to one, root filter one and leaf filters zero: again the old root error is one and the new one zero. An isolated unit root together with an unreachable internal vertex of value one and zero children gives the same failure if that component's filters are zero. These literal controls identify why fixed unit and reachability are used; they are not compiler-source counterexamples.

The proof's structural reason is Boolean conjunction at a fixed unit boundary: a product acting as one forces both factors to act as one on the same kernel class; a filtered factor acting as one forces its old factor to act as one.
