Question. The missing implication for the goal is a construction, for each admissible pair \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\), of a matching with both exponential moments bounded. [T4] proves the special case of Shor's theorem by reducing the matching to a discrepancy bound through linear-programming duality (Proposition 4.3.2) and a regularity step (Proposition 17.2.4, proved in Section B.6, read in this cycle). The question is whether that reduction extends to exponential costs, and to which class of functions. The test ends with a proved reduction or with the step where it fails.
From the regularity step to a semigroup. Lemmas B.6.1 and B.6.2 of [T4] bound the variation of the inf-convolution \(h(k,\ell)=\inf\{u(r,s)+|k-r|:|\ell-s|\le1\}\) by \(\sum n(\tau)(u(\tau)-h(\tau))\), using that the horizontal cost is linear and the vertical cost is \(0\) within distance \(1\) and infinite beyond. For a convex cost \(\Psi\) the corresponding quantity is the conjugate: if \(u(x)=Mx\) on the line, then \(u(x)-\inf_r[u(r)+\Psi(|x-r|)]=\sup_d(Md-\Psi(|d|))=\Psi^*(M)\) for \(M\ge0\). The lemma below replaces the discrete regularity estimate by a Hopf–Lax interpolation, in which this conjugate appears as the penalty \(C^*(\nabla g)\).
General statement. lem:hopf-lax-matching-bound: for every separable convex finite cost vanishing at \(0\) and every two \(N\)-point configurations in the unit square whose cell counts on some square grid are all at least \(\lambda\) times the cell area, the minimal matching cost is at most the supremum over Lipschitz \(g\) of the cell-averaged discrepancy of \(g\) minus \((1-2h)\lambda\int C^*(\nabla g)\), plus a cell-size error \(2hN\sum_j\Psi_j(\delta/h)\).
Hopf–Lax matching bound
Setting. \(\Omega=[0,1]^2\). \(\Psi_1,\Psi_2:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) are convex, nondecreasing, finite, with \(\Psi_j(0)=0\). \(C(z)=\Psi_1(|z_1|)+\Psi_2(|z_2|)\) for \(z\in\mathbb R^2\); \(C\) is convex, finite, \(C\ge0\), \(C(0)=0\). Its conjugate \(C^*(\xi)=\sup_q(q\cdot\xi-C(q))=\Psi_1^*(|\xi_1|)+\Psi_2^*(|\xi_2|)\), with \(\Psi^*(x)=\sup_{t\ge0}(xt-\Psi(t))\), is convex with values in \([0,\infty]\). \(\mathcal Q\) is the partition of \(\Omega\) into \(k^2\) squares of side \(\delta=1/k\), each half-open \([\cdot,\cdot)\times[\cdot,\cdot)\) except that the squares of the last row and column contain their top or right edges. Points \(X_1,\dots,X_N\) and \(Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) lie in \(\Omega\); \(a(Q)\) and \(b(Q)\) count the \(X_i\) and the \(Y_j\) in \(Q\); \(\mu_X\) and \(\mu_Y\) are the measures with density \(a(Q)/|Q|\) and \(b(Q)/|Q|\) on each \(Q\). Assume \(a(Q),b(Q)\ge\lambda|Q|\) for every \(Q\), with \(\lambda>0\).
Statement. For every \(h\in(0,1/2)\), \[\min_\pi\sum_{i\le N}C(X_i-Y_{\pi(i)})\le\sup_g\Big[\int_\Omega g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-(1-2h)\lambda\int_\Omega C^*(\nabla g)\,dx\Big]+2hN\big(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h)\big),\] the supremum over Lipschitz functions \(g\) on \(\Omega\) (\(\nabla g\) exists almost everywhere; a term \(\int C^*(\nabla g)=\infty\) counts as \(-\infty\)).
Proof. Duality. By Proposition 4.3.2 of [T4] with \(c_{ij}=C(X_i-Y_j)\), the minimum equals \(\sup\sum_i(w_i+w'_i)\) over families with \(w_i+w'_j\le C(X_i-Y_j)\). Fix such a family and put \(f(y)=\max_i[w_i-C(X_i-y)]\) for \(y\in\Omega\); \(f\) is Lipschitz on \(\Omega\), \(C\) being convex and finite, hence Lipschitz on the compact \([-1,1]^2\). Then \(w'_j\le\min_i[C(X_i-Y_j)-w_i]=-f(Y_j)\).
Hopf–Lax functions. For \(t>0\) and \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) set \(Q_t(x)=\min_{y\in\Omega}[f(y)+tC((x-y)/t)]\), a minimum of a continuous function on a compact set. (i) \(Q_1(X_i)\ge w_i\), since \(f(y)\ge w_i-C(X_i-y)\). (ii) For \(t,s>0\), \(x,q\in\mathbb R^2\): \(Q_{t+s}(x+sq)\le Q_t(x)+sC(q)\). Indeed, if \(y\) attains \(Q_t(x)\), then \(\frac{x+sq-y}{t+s}=\frac t{t+s}\cdot\frac{x-y}t+\frac s{t+s}\,q\), and convexity gives \((t+s)C(\frac{x+sq-y}{t+s})\le tC(\frac{x-y}t)+sC(q)\). (iii) For \(y'\in\Omega\), \(Q_s(y')\le f(Y_j)+sC((y'-Y_j)/s)\), taking \(y=Y_j\). (iv) On \(U\times[h,1-h]\), with \(U=(-1,2)^2\supset\Omega\), \((x,t)\mapsto Q_t(x)\) is Lipschitz. In \(x\): for \(x\in U\), \(y\in\Omega\), \(t\ge h\), the point \((x-y)/t\) lies in \([-2/h,2/h]^2\), so each \(x\mapsto tC((x-y)/t)\) has the Lipschitz constant \(\Lambda\) of \(C\) on that square, and so has their minimum \(Q_t\). In \(t\): (ii) with \(q=0\) gives \(Q_{t+s}\le Q_t\). Conversely, if \(y\) attains \(Q_{t+s}(x)\) and \(z=x-y\), put \(x'=y+\frac t{t+s}z\), a convex combination of \(x\in U\) and \(y\in\Omega\subset U\), so \(x'\in U\); then \(Q_{t+s}(x)=f(y)+tC(\frac z{t+s})+sC(\frac z{t+s})\ge Q_t(x')\ge Q_t(x)-\Lambda|x-x'|\ge Q_t(x)-3\Lambda s/h\), using \(C\ge0\), \((x'-y)/t=z/(t+s)\) and \(|x-x'|=\frac s{t+s}|z|\le3s/h\).
Hamilton–Jacobi inequality. By Rademacher's theorem, \(Q\) is differentiable at almost every \((x,t)\in\operatorname{int}\Omega\times(h,1-h)\). At such a point, (ii) with \(s\downarrow0\) gives \(\partial_tQ+q\cdot\nabla_xQ\le C(q)\) for every \(q\), so \(C^*(\nabla_xQ)\le-\partial_tQ\); in particular \(C^*(\nabla_xQ)\) is finite there.
Interpolation. Let \(\rho_t=\frac{(1-h-t)\mu_Y+(t-h)\mu_X}{1-2h}\) for \(t\in[h,1-h]\), with density at least \(\lambda\) on \(\Omega\), and \(F(t)=\int_\Omega Q_t\,d\rho_t\). \(F\) is Lipschitz, and for almost every \(t\), by Fubini \(Q\) is differentiable at \((x,t)\) for almost every \(x\), so dominated convergence (difference quotients in \(t\) bounded by \(3\Lambda/h\)) gives \(F'(t)=\int\partial_tQ_t\,d\rho_t+\frac1{1-2h}\int Q_t\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\le-\lambda\int_\Omega C^*(\nabla Q_t)\,dx+\frac1{1-2h}\int Q_t\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\), using \(C^*\ge0\). Integrating over \([h,1-h]\), an interval of length \(1-2h\), \(F(1-h)-F(h)\le\sup_g[\int g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-(1-2h)\lambda\int C^*(\nabla g)]\), each \(Q_t\) being Lipschitz on \(\Omega\).
Smearing. By (i) and (ii) with \(t=1-h\), \(s=h\), \(q=(X_i-x)/h\): \(w_i\le Q_1(X_i)\le Q_{1-h}(x)+hC((X_i-x)/h)\) for every \(x\); averaging over \(x\) in the cell of \(X_i\), whose points differ from \(X_i\) by at most \(\delta\) in each coordinate, and summing, \(\sum_iw_i\le\int Q_{1-h}\,d\mu_X+hN(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h))\). By (iii) with \(s=h\), \(-f(Y_j)\le-Q_h(y')+hC((y'-Y_j)/h)\); averaging over \(y'\) in the cell of \(Y_j\), \(\sum_jw'_j\le-\int Q_h\,d\mu_Y+hN(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h))\). The sum of the two main terms is \(F(1-h)-F(h)\). Taking the supremum over dual families gives the statement. \(\square\)
Scope. The lemma is deterministic and uses the points only through the cell counts. Superlinearity of the cost is not needed: with \(C(z)=|z_1|+|z_2|\) it gives a smeared Kantorovich–Rubinstein bound over functions with \(|\partial_jg|\le1\). Only this direction is proved: a matching of small cost need not give a small value of the right-hand side. The continuous form, for densities bounded below by \(\lambda\), appears to be, by the reviewer's unverified recollection, the dual of the flux bound for the linear interpolation of the two measures (Benamou–Brenier), with the semigroup proof of Bobkov, Gentil and Ledoux; neither source was read in this cycle. What is written here is that bound for point configurations, through cell smearing and the finite duality of [T4]; no novelty is claimed.
Exponential matching from discrepancy
Setting. For \(\alpha\in[1,\infty)\) put \(\psi_\alpha(t)=e^{t^\alpha}-1\), and for \(N\ge2\) put \(\psi_\infty(t)=N(t-1)_+\); each is convex, nondecreasing, finite and \(0\) at \(0\). Their conjugates \(\psi_\alpha^*(x)=\sup_{t\ge0}(xt-\psi_\alpha(t))\) include \(\psi_\infty^*(x)=x\) for \(0\le x\le N\) and \(+\infty\) for \(x>N\). For \(L>0\) put \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\), \(k=\lceil32/s\rceil\), \(\delta=1/k\), and use the grid \(\mathcal Q\) of side \(\delta\). For points \(X,Y\) let \(\mu_X,\mu_Y\) be as above and define \[D_N=\sup\Big\{\int_\Omega G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y):G\text{ Lipschitz},\ \int_\Omega\big(\psi^*_{\alpha_1}(|\partial_1G|)+\psi^*_{\alpha_2}(|\partial_2G|)\big)dx\le2\Big\}.\]
Statement. Let \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[1,\infty]\), \(L\ge132\), \(X\) i.i.d. uniform on \(\Omega\) and \(Y\) evenly spread, and suppose \(P(D_N\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N})\to1\). Then with probability tending to one there is a matching \(\pi\) that, for \(j=1,2\), satisfies \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp[(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|)^{\alpha_j}]\le2N\) when \(\alpha_j<\infty\) and \(\max_i|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\le2L\sqrt{\log N/N}\) when \(\alpha_j=\infty\). By part (1) of lem:two-sample-matching-transfer, applied in each coordinate to the same composed matching and with the union bound, the same follows for two i.i.d. samples with each coordinate's constant doubled.
Proof. Apply the bound with \(\Psi_j(t)=\psi_{\alpha_j}(t/s)\) and \(h=1/4\). Then \(C^*(\xi)=\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(s|\xi_j|)\), since \(\Psi_j^*(x)=\psi_{\alpha_j}^*(sx)\).
Cell counts. \(\delta\le s/32\) and, for large \(N\), \(\delta\ge s/33\), so \(N\delta^2\ge L^2\log N/1089\ge16\log N\). Each \(a(Q)\) is binomial with mean \(N\delta^2\), so by the multiplicative Chernoff bound \(P(a(Q)<N\delta^2/2)\le e^{-N\delta^2/8}\le N^{-2}\); there are \(k^2\le N\) cells for large \(N\), so all \(a(Q)\ge N\delta^2/2\) with probability at least \(1-1/N\). For evenly spread \(Y\), let \(Q'\) be \(Q\) shrunk by \(21/\sqrt N\) on each side; the rectangles of the definition that meet \(Q'\) have diameter at most \(20/\sqrt N\), so they lie in the interior of \(Q\) with their points \(Y_j\), and they cover \(Q'\). Hence \(b(Q)\ge N(\delta-42/\sqrt N)^2\ge N\delta^2/2\) for large \(N\), since \(\delta\sqrt N\to\infty\). So \(\lambda=N/2\) and \((1-2h)\lambda=N/4\).
Error. \(\delta/h=4\delta\le s/8\), so \(\Psi_j(4\delta)=\psi_{\alpha_j}(4\delta/s)\le\psi_{\alpha_j}(1/8)\), which is \(0\) for \(\alpha_j=\infty\) and at most \(e^{1/8}-1<0.14\) otherwise; the error is at most \(2\cdot\frac14\cdot N\cdot0.28=0.14N\).
Main term. For Lipschitz \(g\) put \(G=sg\), so \(\int C^*(\nabla g)=\int\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(|\partial_jG|)=:I(G)\) and \(\int g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)=\frac1s\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\). On the event \(D_N\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}=\frac{sN}2\), every \(G\) with \(I(G)\le2\) has \(\frac1s\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\le N/2\). If \(2<I(G)<\infty\), then \(G'=(2/I(G))G\) has \(I(G')\le\frac2{I(G)}I(G)=2\) by convexity of \(\psi^*\) with \(\psi^*(0)=0\), so \(\frac1s\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\le\frac N4I(G)\). In all cases \(\frac1s\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-\frac N4I(G)\le N/2\).
Conclusion. With probability tending to one, the minimal cost is at most \(N/2+0.14N<N\), so some \(\pi\) has \(\sum_i\sum_j\psi_{\alpha_j}(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|/s)<N\). For finite \(\alpha_j\), \(\sum_i\exp[(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|/s)^{\alpha_j}]=N+\sum_i\psi_{\alpha_j}(\cdot)<2N\), and \(|\cdot|/s=\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|\cdot|\). For \(\alpha_j=\infty\), each term satisfies \(N(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|/s-1)_+<N\), so \(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|<2s\). \(\square\)
The reduced statement. conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy: there is \(L\ge132\) such that for every pair \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[2,\infty]\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), \(P(D_N\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N})\to1\), \(D_N\) being defined with that \(L\) (which fixes the grid). By the corollary it implies the ultimate matching conjecture at every admissible pair with one universal constant (\(L\) for finite exponents, \(2L\) for the maximum bound; doubled again for two samples). A version with \(L\) depending on the pair would give only the pair-dependent form. At the endpoint \((2,\infty)\) the class is \(\int\psi_2^*(|\partial_1G|)+|\partial_2G|\le2\) with \(|\partial_2G|\le N\), a continuous counterpart of the class of Research Problem 17.2.2 of [T4] (weights \(x(\log(3+x))^{1/2}\) and \(x\)). Here the step from this class to the matching is complete, whereas [T4] says a positive answer to its problem would still need "significant extra work". The two statements differ: continuous and cell-smeared here, grid-based there; a bound in probability here, an expectation there; and the weights agree only up to constants. It is stated as a conjecture to be tested, not believed.
Comparison with weights at least linear. Taking \(t=1\) in the conjugate gives \(\psi_\alpha^*(x)\ge x-(e-1)\) for every \(\alpha\in[1,\infty)\); for \(\alpha=\infty\), \(\psi^*_\infty(x)\ge x\). Put \(\theta_\alpha=\max(\psi_\alpha^*,x)\), convex and \(0\) at \(0\). Then \(\psi^*_\alpha\le\theta_\alpha\le\psi^*_\alpha+e-1\), so on the unit square \(\{\int\sum_j\theta_{\alpha_j}\le2\}\subset\{\int\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}\le2\}\subset\{\int\sum_j\theta_{\alpha_j}\le2e\}\), and by the homogeneity step the last class is contained in \(e\) times the first. So replacing \(\psi^*\) by the weight \(\theta\ge x\), as [T4]'s Research Problem 17.2.2 requires of its weights, changes \(D_N\) by at most the factor \(e\), uniformly in the pair. For large \(x\), \(\psi^*_\alpha(x)\ge x(\frac12\log x)^{1/\alpha}-\sqrt x\) (take \(t=(\frac12\log x)^{1/\alpha}\)) and \(\psi_\alpha^*(x)\le x\max(1,(\log(1+x))^{1/\alpha})\) (for \(t>T=\max(1,(\log(1+x))^{1/\alpha})\), \(\frac d{dt}(xt-\psi_\alpha(t))=x-\alpha t^{\alpha-1}e^{t^\alpha}<x-(1+x)<0\)); at \(\alpha=2\) this is the weight \(x(\log(3+x))^{1/2}\) of Research Problem 17.2.2 up to a factor between about \(2^{-1/2}\) and \(1\) for large \(x\).
Degenerate cases. \(X=Y\): the minimal cost is \(0\), and the right-hand side is at least \(0\) (take \(g=0\)), so the bound holds. A cell with no point forces \(\lambda=0\), which the hypothesis \(\lambda>0\) excludes; with \(\lambda=0\) the supremum may be infinite and the bound empty. \(h\to0\) removes the error but the Lipschitz constant \(\Lambda\) in (iv) blows up; the proof needs \(h>0\) only there. An infinite cost is excluded (\(C\) must be finite for the duality and for \(\Lambda\)); the maximum constraint enters the corollary only through the finite cost \(\psi_\infty\), whose slope \(N\) makes a violation of the bound \(2s\) cost more than the total. \(N=1\) is excluded by \(\log N>0\).
Nonvacuity. For the bound: \(N=n^2\) points \(X_i=Y_i\) at the centres of the \(n\times n\) subsquares, cells of side \(\delta=m/n\) for \(m\mid n\), so \(a(Q)=b(Q)=m^2=N|Q|\) and \(\lambda=N\); any \(\Psi_j=\psi_{\alpha_j}\). For the reduction: at every \(N\ge2\) and \(L\ge132\) the class defining \(D_N\) contains every \(G\) with \(|\partial_jG|\le1\) (from the upper bound above, \(\psi^*_\alpha(x)\le1\) for \(x\le1\)), and the cell-count event has probability at least \(1-1/N\) for large \(N\). No instance of its hypothesis on \(D_N\) is known: that hypothesis is the open conjecture, and the corollary is a conditional result.
New mathematics. The Hopf–Lax matching bound, a point-configuration and cell-smeared form of what the reviewer recalls as a standard dynamic duality bound, turns any separable convex finite matching cost into a supremum of discrepancies penalized by the conjugate cost; its proof uses the finite duality of [T4], a semigroup inequality proved here, Rademacher's theorem and Fubini's theorem. Its corollary reduces the ultimate matching conjecture, in the evenly-spread and the two-sample forms, at every admissible pair including the endpoints, with one universal constant, to a single discrepancy bound of Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády size for the class with gradient penalty \(\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}\); up to the factor \(e\), the penalty may be taken at least linear, as in [T4]'s Research Problem 17.2.2.
