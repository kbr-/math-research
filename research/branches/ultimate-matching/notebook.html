Ultimate Matching Conjecture

Prove or refute Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture (Conjecture 5.8 of The Status of my favorite problems): for \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\) there is a permutation \(\pi\) of \(\{1,\dots,N\}\) such that, for \(j=1,2\), \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\big(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\big)^{\alpha_j}\big]\le2N\), where \(X_1,\dots,X_N,Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) are independent and uniform on \([0,1]^2\), superscripts denote coordinates and \(L\) does not depend on \(N\).

Starting point

The source is Talagrand's survey The Status of my favorite problems (22 pages, PDF dated 1 October 2026; its hash is in research/references/redistribution.json), Section 5, pages 11–14; a personal copy and its text extraction are held in the ignored private folder. It defers all details and references to [T4]: Talagrand, Upper and lower bounds for stochastic processes: decomposition theorems, Ergeb. Math. Grenzgeb. (3) 60, Springer, 2021, not yet read here. Its introduction states that the conjecture has seen no progress since it was formulated. The thread starts from no earlier result of this repository.

Known results in the source. Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády (Theorem 5.1) controls \(\sum_i d(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\) by \(L\sqrt{N\log N}\); Leighton–Shor (Theorem 5.2) controls \(\max_i d(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\) by \(L(\log N)^{3/4}/\sqrt N\); both powers of \(\log N\) are stated optimal. Shor (Theorem 5.4) controls the first-coordinate sum by \(L\sqrt{N\log N}\) and the second-coordinate maximum by \(L\sqrt{\log N}/\sqrt N\) in one matching. Problem 5.3 (both bounds of 5.1 and 5.2 at once) has a cited solution [C5] (ResearchGate, doi:10.13140/RG.2.2.26527.93606/2), unread here, which the source says does not touch the real issues.

Best partial result toward the conjecture. For the special case Conjecture 5.5 (\(\alpha_1=2,\alpha_2=\infty\)) the source proves only the exponent \(\alpha<1/2\) in place of \(2\), with \(L\) depending on \(\alpha\); the proof is in [T4].

Open statements

  1. Ultimate Matching Conjecture — open, the full target. The statement of the goal above, for every pair \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\). Whether \(L\) may depend on \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) is not fixed by the source; see the ambiguities in the working context.
  2. Conjecture 5.5 — open, a special case. With high probability some matching has \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\big(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^1-Y^1_{\pi(i)}|\big)^2\big]\le2N\) and \(\max_i|X_i^2-Y^2_{\pi(i)}|\le L\sqrt{\log N}/\sqrt N\). The printed (5.1) places the square outside the exponential; the intended reading \(\exp(x^2)\) is the one consistent with Conjecture 5.8 and with the source's remark on the exponent \(\alpha<1/2\).
  3. Grid discrepancy and Gaussian size — open, sufficient for the special case. Conjectures 5.6 and 5.7 on the class \(\mathcal F\) defined in the working context. The source states, citing [T4], that an appropriate version of 5.6 suffices for 5.5, with the details not written out.

The remaining route

  1. Mechanism for the full conjecture. Find a discrepancy formulation of the anisotropic exponential-moment condition for general \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\), analogous to the one [T4] gives for Conjecture 5.5, and bound the size of the resulting function class; or find a construction showing the conjecture fails for some pair. The source gives no route for this beyond the special case.
  2. Close Conjecture 5.5. Raise the proved exponent from \(\alpha<1/2\) to \(2\), through Conjectures 5.6 and 5.7 or otherwise. The source calls the obstacle more technical than conceptual: the true size of \(\mathcal F\) in \(L^2(P)\) is not understood.
  3. Fix the exact statements. Read [T4]'s statements of the conjecture, of the reduction of 5.5 to 5.6, of the \(\alpha<1/2\) theorem and of the lower-bound constructions for Theorems 5.1, 5.2 and the \(p^{3/4}\) example, with their hypotheses and constants.

Working mathematical context

Setting

\(X_1,\dots,X_N\) and \(Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) are independent, uniform on \([0,1]^2\); \(X_i^j\) is the \(j\)-th coordinate. A matching is a permutation \(\pi\) of \(\{1,\dots,N\}\). \(L\) is a universal constant, independent of \(N\). "With high probability" means with probability tending to \(1\) as \(N\to\infty\). The source does not define \(d\); the Euclidean distance is the convention of [T4]. Natural scale: \(1/\sqrt N\), with irregularity factor \(\sqrt{\log N}\).

Elementary consequences (unreviewed)

Write \(x_i=\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\). Each summand is at least \(1\), so each is at most \(2N\): \(x_i\le(\log 2N)^{1/\alpha_j}\), hence \(\max_i|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\lesssim(\log N)^{1/2+1/\alpha_j}/\sqrt N\). For \(\alpha_j\ge1\), \(x\le1+x^{\alpha_j}\le e^{x^{\alpha_j}}\) gives \(\sum_ix_i\le2N\), an Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády bound in each coordinate; the constraint forces \(\alpha_j>2\) whenever both are finite. At \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=4\), with \(d\le|\Delta_1|+|\Delta_2|\), the conjecture implies Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 in one matching, hence Problem 5.3. At \(\alpha_1=2,\alpha_2=\infty\) (a limit, read as the maximum bound) it gives Conjecture 5.5, which implies Theorem 5.4. The product of the two coordinate maxima is \(\lesssim(\log N)^{3/2}/N\) for every admissible split; why the constraint is \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\) is not explained in the source.

Discrepancy class

Grid \(G=\{1,\dots,2^p\}^2\), points \(u=(k,\ell)\), uniform probability \(P\), \(\theta(x)=x\log(x+3)\). \(\mathcal F\) is the set of \(f\) on \(G\) with \(\sum_uf(u)=0\) and \(\sum\theta(|f(k+1,\ell)-f(k,\ell)|)+\sum|f(k,\ell+1)-f(k,\ell)|\le2^{2p}\), sums over \(1\le k\le2^p-1,1\le\ell\le2^p\) and \(1\le k\le2^p,1\le\ell\le2^p-1\). Conjecture 5.6: for \(N\ge2^{2p}\), with probability at least \(1-2^{-p}\), \(\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\sum_{i\le N}f(U_i)\le L\sqrt{Np}\,2^p\), \(U_i\) independent uniform on \(G\). Conjecture 5.7: \(\mathbb E\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}\sum_ug_uf(u)\le L\sqrt p\,2^{2p}\), \(g_u\) independent standard Gaussians. With \(\theta_1=\theta_2=x\) in both directions the best bound is \(Lp^{3/4}2^{2p}\), by the Leighton–Shor optimality construction. Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 both follow from discrepancy bounds and duality, with class size from the Ellipsoid theorem of [T4].
