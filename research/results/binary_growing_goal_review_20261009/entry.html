Analytic profile controls

Proposition (profile hypothesis controls). Two elementary graded-algebra controls show why field and generation hypotheses cannot be dropped.

First let \(A=\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(xy(x+y))\). Its degree-three component has dimension three: four cubic monomials have one nonzero cubic relation. Every nonzero binary linear form is one of \(x,y,x+y\), hence divides the defining cubic; consequently \(A/(\ell)\cong\mathbb F_2[t]\) and its degree-three dimension is one for every legal nonzero binary \(\ell\). Over an infinite characteristic-two extension, take \(\ell=x+\lambda y\), \(\lambda\notin\{0,1\}\). The cubic restricts to \(\lambda(\lambda+1)y^3\), so the quotient's degree-three component is zero. Green's numerical bound here is zero, since \(3=\binom33+\binom22+\binom11\) and lowering each upper index gives zero. This is a graded genericity control, not an assertion that this algebra is an actual Boolean source-error kernel.

Second, for any field and integer \(T\ge1\), the graded rings \(k[z]\) and \(k[z]/(z^{T+2})\) have identical Hilbert values one through degree \(T+1\), including maximal Macaulay growth from \(T\) to \(T+1\). At degree \(T+2\) their values differ. The new relation has degree \(T+2\), so the required generation-through-\(T\) hypothesis of persistence fails. This disproves inference of future persistence merely from the observed equalities, not Gotzmann's theorem. Both calculations are exact. \(\square\)
