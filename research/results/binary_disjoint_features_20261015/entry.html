Faithful features and finite statement

Keep the proper binary charged graph quotient \(J_D\), the original one-hot encoding, and its phase-adjusted cut-code dictionary. Fix a spanning tree and \(s\ge1\) disjoint nonempty edge sets \(S_j\) outside it. Let \(z_j\) be binary polynomials supported on \(S_j\), of ordinary degree at most \(1\le\delta\le h\), nonconstant as functions on the local three-state edge domain. Use multilinear local normal representatives; each is Boolean modulo the old Boolean equations. Define \(\psi(Z_j)=z_j\) from the formal binary feature cube.

Faithfulness. If \(F(Z)\) is a nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most \(k\), and \(\delta k\le D\), then \(\psi(F)\notin J_D\). For \(F=1\) this is properness. Otherwise choose an inclusion-maximal nonempty feature support \(T\) with nonzero coefficient in \(F\). Each nonconstant \(z_j\), \(j\in T\), has a nonzero nonconstant ternary Fourier character coefficient. Choose one such exponent \(\beta_j\). Its edge support has size at most \(\delta\), since the local indicator/character change preserves degree. Form the exponent equal to \(\beta_j\) on \(S_j\), \(j\in T\), and zero on the other feature blocks.

Only feature monomials containing \(T\) contribute to this exponent, since \(\beta_j\ne0\) on each of its disjoint blocks. Maximality leaves just \(Z_T\); its coefficient contributes the nonzero product of the chosen Fourier coefficients. Every exponent in \(\psi(F)\) is supported off the tree and has support at most \(\delta k\). Distinct such exponents cannot differ by a nonzero cut: a vertex potential constant along the spanning tree is constant everywhere. The charged dictionary therefore prevents cancellation of the selected coefficient. This proves faithfulness over \(\mathbb F_4\), and hence for binary \(F\), without a global charged evaluation algebra.

Every feature Boolean certificate through degree \(c\) pulls back to an old Boolean certificate through \(\delta c\). Indeed \(z_j^2+z_j\) has an old Boolean certificate through \(2\delta\), by square reduction of its binary polynomial; each pulled-back cofactor costs at most \(\delta(c-2)\). This certificate statement uses binary coefficients, not pointwise equality on charged solutions. No incoming arbitrary weight is assumed; the initial weight is one.

Theorem (disjoint feature transfer). Suppose a complete two-layer accuracy-\(h\) source has a coherent constant bottom interpretation, with all bottom companions Boolean-zero within their original ceilings, and every actual parent input is an affine binary combination of the specified \(z_j\)'s. There are at most \(M\ge1\) parents. No raw coefficient readers, parent dependencies or extra old-affine inputs outside that feature span are allowed. The source may realize a feature by a constant combination of several genuine bottom products; its original degrees remain unchanged. Put \(L=\log(4M)\), and choose \[ k=\begin{cases} \max(1,\lceil s/h\rceil),&s\le256hL,\\ \lceil2\sqrt{sL/h}\rceil,&s>256hL, \end{cases} \qquad T=\delta k. \] If \(B\) covers the original fresh generators and source proof, with \(B\ge2h^2+3h\) and \(T(B+1)\le D\), there is one surviving binary weight \(W\), \(\deg W\le T\), and one old coefficient substitution of maximum degree at most \(T\), killing every weighted fresh generator and every used old axiom through degree \(T(B+1)\). The complete source has no degree-\(B\) PC or ordinary NS refutation. Every old axiom used in that proof automatically has degree at most \(B\). No small circuit or expanded-size bound for the interpretation polynomials is claimed.

The cap \(\delta\le h\) ensures compatibility with the complete literal-bottom realization used here. Once a coherent bottom normalization is supplied, the abstract feature-cube and replay proof needs only its stated degree bound and certificates.

Common weight in the feature cube

The following is the full earlier packing/common-vanishing argument specialized to this cube. Zero input spans use zero rows; a span containing one uses a constant unit row. All other spans are proper affine spaces of input functions, with independent homogeneous parts after choosing a basis.

Small feature count. If \(s\le256hL\), every proper affine rank is at most \(s\le h(k+1)\). Partition its affine basis into \(h\) bins of at most \(k+1\) forms. The prefix telescoping identity gives coefficient degree at most \(k\); the whole product is the common-zero indicator of that basis, and every companion is Boolean-zero. Take feature weight \(F=1\).

Large feature count. Put \(r_0=h(k+1)\). Since \(s>256hL\) and \(L>1\), \[ k<s/(8h)+1<s/6,\qquad r_0<s/8+2h<s/4, \qquad k\le(s-r_0)/3. \] Call a proper parent high when its rank exceeds \(r_0\). Its affine zero flat has restriction-image dimension at most \[ \sum_{j\le k}\binom{s-r_0}{j} \le2e^{-r_0k/s}\binom sk. \] The flat count follows by affine coordinates, degree-preserving Boolean reduction, and the consecutive binomial comparison when \(k\le(s-r_0)/3\). The total restriction rank of all high flats is less than \(\binom sk\), since \[ 2M e^{-r_0k/s}\le2M e^{-hk^2/s}\le2M e^{-4L}<1. \] Hence some nonzero homogeneous squarefree degree-\(k\) feature polynomial \(F\) vanishes on every high flat. All low proper parents are packed as in the small case.

For a high parent, affine Gaussian elimination followed by Boolean division gives \(F-H_a\) a Boolean certificate through \(k\), where \(H_a=\sum_i A_{ai}g_{ai}\) and \(\deg A_{ai}\le k-1\). To see the degree, choose affine coordinates for its independent basis, expand \(F\) in those coordinates, reduce multilinearly, and subtract its identically zero restriction to the zero flat. Every remaining monomial contains a constrained coordinate, giving the stated cofactors. Affine substitution and square reduction preserve the ceiling. Set the first coefficient row to \(A_{ai}\), the rest to zero. Then \[ Fg_{ai}(1-H_a)=g_{ai}\bigl(F(1-F)+F(F-H_a)\bigr) \] has a Boolean certificate through \(2k+1\). Low-parent weighted certificates cost at most \(h(k+1)+1+k\le k(2h+1)+k\). The high bound also fits this ceiling, and weighted coefficient fields cost at most \(3k\). These are one simultaneous substitution; parent costs are not multiplied or summed. This proves the feature-cube construction, including the possibility that no high parent is present.

Original source replay

Pull back \(F\) and all parent coefficient polynomials using \(\psi\). Faithfulness gives \(W=\psi(F)\notin J_D\), of degree at most \(T\). Every parent coefficient image has degree at most \(T\). Pulled-back weighted parent certificates cost at most \(T(2h+1)+T\), and weighted fields cost at most \(3T\), both covered by their original weighted substitution ledger \(Te+T\). The original parent ceiling is \(e=2h^2+3h\); it is not replaced by a specialized feature degree. By hypothesis every bottom companion already has a Boolean certificate through its original ceiling; multiplying by \(W\) fits the same conservative ledger, and its constant fields vanish. Old axioms are multiplied by \(W\).

Map each original proof line \(p\) to \(W\Phi(p)\). The old variables stay fixed; bottom coefficients are constant and parent coefficients have degree at most \(T\). Multiplying an original predecessor of degree at most \(B-1\) by one substituted variable stays through \(TB+T\). Linear steps preserve membership. Every source axiom has the paid weighted membership just proved, so a final line one would put \(W\) in \(J_D\), a contradiction. Ordinary NS has its same-degree PC replay. This proves the theorem with all original companions, coefficient fields, shared bottoms and whole-proof costs retained.

Majority mixtures at the original encoding
