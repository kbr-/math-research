Row-union weight aggregation

Lemma. Let \(J\subset\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a Boolean degree-\(D\) PC pseudoideal. Fix binary old inputs \(g_i\) of degree at most \(\delta\ge0\), accuracy \(h\ge1\), coefficient bound \(T\ge1\), and a common binary old-polynomial coefficient space of degree at most \(T\). Its basis and all input slots identify a row with a binary scalar vector. Put \(s=h(\delta+1)\), \(B\ge\max(2,s+\delta)\), and \(b+TB\le D\). Suppose finitely many maps \(\Phi_j\), each using \(h\) rows, satisfy \[W_jg_iP_j\in J\quad\text{for every }i,j,\qquad \deg W_j\le b.\] Let \(R_j\) be their coefficient row spaces and \(k=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\sum_jR_j\). If \(k\le h\), choose any basis of the union and pad it by zero rows to the original accuracy. The resulting common map is successful at every \(W_j\), hence at every binary linear combination of those weights. No survival of a chosen sum is guaranteed.

Proof. Fix \(j\). By paid binary row invariance, replace its rows by a basis of \(R_j\) and zeros, retaining weighted companion memberships at the original ceiling. Write \(r_j=\dim R_j\). Extend that basis to one of the union space using \(k-r_j\) further rows. This multiplies the old product by the corresponding complementary factors. Each row value has degree at most \(T+\delta\), so every resulting weighted companion has actual degree at most \[b+\delta+k(T+\delta)\le b+T(s+\delta)\le D.\] Multiplication in \(J\) is therefore legal. All union bases are related by invertible binary row operations, so another application of the paid gauge lemma gives the selected common basis, with the original \(h\) and \(B\) still charged. This works for each \(j\) separately. Linearity of \(J\) then gives success at any linear combination of the weights. The argument's degree does not grow with the number of witnesses. The binary coefficient-field images also have paid Boolean certificates through \(b+2T\le D\). \(\square\)

Scope. The inputs are fixed throughout. This is not an unproved rebuilding rule for maps at different weights in an arbitrary acyclic source. Nor does it bound the union dimension, find a surviving linear combination in intersections for different blocks, or prove that a uniform map is necessary. The original product weight is not reset to \(k(\delta+1)\) for replay.

Nonvacuity. For inputs \((x,y)\), accuracy two and constant coefficient rows, one map uses only \((1,0)\) and succeeds at weight \(x\); another uses only \((0,1)\) and succeeds at weight \(y\). The two-dimensional union fits the accuracy. Its common map has product \((1+x)(1+y)\) and succeeds at both weights and their sum. With \(T=1,b=1,\delta=1,B=5,D=6\), all stated costs fit in the proper Boolean-only quotient. Unused old variables give unbounded ambient size.

Degenerate cases. A zero-dimensional union yields product one; success then means the weighted inputs were already zero. Zero weights or a zero sum give no lower-bound witness. A single witness recovers its original row space. A union larger than \(h\) is outside the sufficient construction; the next theorem shows that this condition is not necessary for adaptive feasibility of each weight in a space. It does not characterize necessity for a single common map.

Adaptive feasible weights

Theorem. For every \(h,T\ge1\), put \(r=h(T+1)+1\). Take the independent Boolean inputs \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\), one complete accuracy-\(h\) block, and permit every binary coefficient map of degree at most \(T\) in these variables and any number of additional old Boolean variables. Let \(J_D\) be the degree-at-most-\(D\) part of the Boolean vanishing ideal, where \[D\ge1+T(2h+1).\] Call a degree-at-most-one weight feasible when some permitted map kills every weighted companion modulo \(J_D\). Then:

  • Every homogeneous linear weight \(\sum_i a_ix_i\) is feasible, but no single permitted map is successful at all those weights.
  • Both \(x_1\) and \(1+x_1\) are feasible, while their sum one is infeasible. In particular the set of feasible weights is not a linear space, even with all degree-at-most-\(T\) maps available.

All nonzero displayed weights survive in \(J_D\). Complete fresh coefficient fields have the required weighted certificates in the positive constructions.

The unit-weight obstruction. Any mapped product has ordinary degree at most \(h(T+1)=r-1\) and value one when all input variables are zero. If its companions vanish on the full Boolean cube at weight one, the product is zero whenever any input is nonzero. Restrict every additional old variable to zero. Its unique multilinear representative must then be \(\prod_{i=1}^r(1+x_i)\), of degree \(r\). Boolean reduction and restriction never increase degree, a contradiction. This is the historical packed zero-test degree barrier, also used by the packed-chart control; no new degree-barrier principle is claimed.

Adaptive maps on a whole linear space. For \(W_a=\sum_i a_ix_i\), use one constant row with coefficients \(a_i\) and pad all other rows by zero. Its product is \(1+W_a\), and \[W_ax_i(1+W_a)=x_i(W_a+W_a^2)\] is an old Boolean consequence through degree three. This fits the stated \(D\). If one common map succeeded for every such weight, it would succeed for every \(x_j\). At any nonzero Boolean input, choose \(j\) with \(x_j=1\); success at that weight forces the product to vanish there. At the zero input the product is one. This contradicts the preceding degree argument. Thus adaptive choices can serve the whole homogeneous linear space even though no common map serves it. The elementary witness rows for its basis span dimension \(r>h\); their large union is not an obstruction to adaptive feasibility.

A pair whose sum fails. The weight \(x_1\) is already covered. Partition \(x_2,\ldots,x_r\) into \(h\) ordered groups of size \(T+1\). In a row assigned to a group \((y_1,\ldots,y_{T+1})\), give input \(y_j\) coefficient \(\prod_{t<j}(1+y_t)\), and give all other inputs zero coefficients. Every coefficient has degree at most \(T\). The row factor telescopes literally to \(\prod_j(1+y_j)\); hence the product is \(Q=\prod_{i=2}^r(1+x_i)\). For \(W=1+x_1\), every \(Wx_iQ\) is Boolean-zero: use \(x_1(1+x_1)\) for the first input and \(x_i(1+x_i)\) for the others. Their degrees are at most \[r+1=h(T+1)+2\le1+T(2h+1),\] the difference being \((h+1)(T-1)\). Thus degree-nonincreasing Boolean division gives the required memberships in \(J_D\). Weighted fresh fields cost at most \(2T+1\le D\). Both weights are nonzero functions, but their sum one was proved infeasible. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. The smallest permitted parameters \(h=T=1\) give three input bits and \(D=4\). The two weights are \(x_1\) and \(1+x_1\); the latter uses the single-row coefficients \((0,1,1+x_2)\). The proof covers every larger \(h,T\), arbitrary additional old coordinates and any larger \(D\). It therefore includes growing accuracies and map degrees, not a constant-only coefficient test.

Degenerate cases. The zero weight is feasible but does not survive; it is not used to prove nonlinearity. The conditions \(h,T\ge1\) are explicit: no assertion about a zero-row or negative-degree source is needed. Additional old variables cannot repair the unit-weight case, since restricting them to zero preserves the map degree. The quotient here is a consistent Boolean-only one, not the goal's charged hard quotient. Extra old relations can change feasibility, so this is not a hard-family lower-bound obstruction.
