A surviving feature pattern contains a cube

Corollary. Partition \(v=mr\) Boolean variables into \(m\ge1\) blocks of size \(r\ge1\), and let \(z_j\) be their full products. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), take any affine feature tuple \(g=Az+b\), any \(h\)-row binary matrix \(C\), and \[P_C=\prod_{u=1}^h(1+(Cg)_u),\qquad E_i=P_Cg_i,\qquad k=\operatorname{rank}(CA),\qquad s=(m-\min(m,k+1))(r-1).\] Work first in the full Boolean function algebra. Let \(c(j)\) be the rank of \(f\mapsto(E_if)_i\) on multilinear polynomials of degree at most \(j\), and put \(c(-1)=0\). Either every error is zero, or, for every \(0\le j\le v\), \[c(j)-c(j-1)\ge\binom{s}{j}.\tag{1}\] The binomial coefficient is zero when \(j>s\). With no constant offset, \(b=0\), the zero-error alternative occurs exactly when \(\operatorname{rank}(CA)=\operatorname{rank}A\). For a finite Boolean truncation it suffices to pay \(K+(h+1)r\le D\) for all asserted cutoffs \(j\le K\); original source replay has its separate carried ceiling.

Proof. If some error is nonzero, there is a feature vector \(y\in\mathbb F_2^m\) such that \(C(Ay+b)=0\) and \((Ay+b)_i=1\) for some \(i\). Every feature vector is realized by the disjoint old-variable blocks. This is a consistent affine linear system of rank at most \(k+1\). Choose a basis of its coefficient columns and solve using only those columns, setting all other unknowns to zero. The resulting solution \(y_*\) has at most \(\min(m,k+1)\) nonzero coordinates. This is elementary column elimination; no distribution of solutions is assumed.

For each block with \((y_*)_j=1\), pin all its variables to one. For each remaining block, pin one variable to zero and leave the others free. This defines a Boolean subcube \(F\) of dimension \[s_*=(m-|\operatorname{supp}y_*|)(r-1)\ge s.\] On it the whole feature vector is \(y_*\), so \(P_C=1\) and \(E_i=1\) for the selected coordinate. The joint error kernel is therefore contained in the ideal of functions vanishing on \(F\).

Let \(I=\{f:E_if=0\text{ for all }i\}\). Restriction gives a well-defined surjection from the filtered quotient of the Boolean algebra by \(I\) onto functions on \(F\). It is strict for the degree filtrations: restriction does not increase degree, and every degree-at-most-\(j\) polynomial on the free coordinates lifts using those same coordinates. Thus the induced map on every associated graded level is onto. The target has degree-\(j\) dimension \(\binom{s_*}{j}\), proving (1). This uses one strict quotient map, not addition of component level bounds. If \(b=0\), the error support is \(\ker(CA)\setminus\ker A\). Its emptiness is exactly equality of these two kernels, equivalently equality of the ranks, because \(\ker A\subseteq\ker(CA)\). \(\square\)

Compare the floor with the exact shadow requirement

Suppose \(1\le\delta\le K\le s\), and attempt to majorize the actual degree levels throughout \(K-\delta<j\le K\) by \(\binom Xj\), with \(X\ge K\). The top level alone forces \(X\ge s\). The corrected harmonic condition requires \[X\le 2K-2\delta+1+(K-\delta+1)/\delta\le3K-1.\] Consequently \(s>3K-1\) rules out this particular sufficient certificate for every shift \(1\le\delta\le K\). It does not rule out all polynomial-in-\(\delta\) rates or every actual rank contraction.

Take \(v=mr\to\infty\), \(r=\Theta(\log v)\), and \(K/v\to\kappa\in(0,1/3)\). If \(hr=o(v)\), then \(k\le h=o(m)\) and \(s/v\to1\). Thus every nonzero error tuple from those rows fails the displayed cap for all large parameters. This is a statement about the actual kernel, including any additional relations it has; it is stronger than merely observing that the supplied nonlinear row generators have large degrees.

At the same time, set \(A=I_m,b=0\). The existing code theorem gives, for every fixed polynomial inventory \(M\le v^a\), a check matrix with \(hr=o(v)\) and \(c(K)/N_v(K)\le1/(2M)\). Its proof gives \(h=o(m)\), so the error tuple is nonzero for all large parameters and (1) applies. Hence polynomially small cumulative error rank and failure of the harmonic top-level cap coexist. The old coding theorem, not (1), supplies that positive rank bound. Neither inference differentiates a sum of cumulative estimates.

A hard quotient realization with a restricted domain

Let a proper binary Boolean \(J_D\) contain an unsatisfiable old family \(\Gamma\), let \(w\notin J_D\) have degree at most \(b_w\), and assume \(D-b_w\ge\max(2,\max\deg\Gamma)\). The weighted restriction cube supplies independent weighted images of all functions on \(D-b_w-1\) chosen old coordinates. Choose \(v=mr\) of them. Use only polynomials in these \(v\) coordinates as the multiplier domain, and retain \(b_w+K+(h+1)r\le D\). The maps \(f\mapsto([wE_if])_i\) have exactly the Boolean kernels just used: Boolean-zero products have paid certificates, and any nonzero reduced product in these coordinates has nonzero weighted class by injectivity. Their complete filtered ranks therefore satisfy (1).

This is not a statement about degree levels when multipliers from all old coordinates are allowed. Enlarging a filtered domain can introduce lower-degree representatives of an image, so a cumulative rank lower bound does not automatically transfer level increments. Nor is the chosen free cube independent of the incoming weight. The statement provides a hard-quotient control for the specified restricted domain; it does not identify the error kernel of every compiled proof.

For a literal two-level realization, exact bottom products \(z_j\) arise from \(r\) rows selecting the inputs \(1-x\) in their blocks. Bottom original product and companion ceilings are \(2r\) and \(2r+1\). A parent reading their affine mixtures with \(h\ge1\) rows retains original ceiling \[B=\max(2r+1,\,h(2r+1)+2r).\] Constant row substitution kills coefficient fields and costs no variable-degree increase. Require \(b_w+K+B\le D\), or a larger original ceiling supplied by an actual proof. The asymptotic \(hr=o(v)\) leaves such a window when \(v\) is a fixed fraction of \(D-b_w\); it does not remove the original companion costs.

There is a cheap positive weight control. For \(A=I_m\), \(m\ge2\), choose a row selecting \(z_1\) and pad the others by zero. The errors are nonzero, and may have the large degree levels above, but the weight \(wz_1\) survives in the free cube and kills every companion because \(z_1(1-z_1)=0\). Its original replay reserve is \(b_w+r+B\le D\). This prevents treating failed thinness at a fixed weight as failure of optimized source removal.

Degenerate cases. Empty or identically zero error tuples take the zero-error alternative. With \(r=1\), the floor has \(s=0\) and only asserts \(c(0)\ge1\) for a nonzero tuple. Zero check rows and \(h=0\) are allowed in the algebraic statement; its literal source realization uses \(h\ge1\). Overlapping blocks are outside the disjoint-feature hypothesis. None of these cases forces a nonzero error when a legal row kills the tuple.

Count surviving columns rather than degree levels

This is an elementary variant of the full fiber-diversity theorem, prompted by the review. It changes the objects whose possible errors are counted: degree-bounded basis vectors rather than complete assignment fibers. It is not the output-contraction code, whose row support has a different meaning.

Theorem. Let \(J_D\) be a proper binary Boolean pseudoideal containing the old base, and let \(w\notin J_D\), \(\deg w\le b_w\). Fix a subspace \(U\subseteq V_K\), with \[H=\dim[wU]>0,\] and choose binary lifts \(f_1,\ldots,f_H\in U\) of a basis of \([wU]\). All these choices precede the random source rows. Take a complete acyclic binary ENS source whose inputs use old variables and earlier whole products, not raw earlier coefficients or their own coefficients. Retain its original ceiling \(B\), covering the supplied generators and the hypothetical refutation, and require \(b_w+K+B\le D\). In block \(a\), use \(1\le t_a\le h_a\) independent uniform binary rows and pad the others by zero, sharing each choice wherever its coefficient occurs.

For every basis index \(\ell\) and every assignment of earlier rows, suppose the number of attainable nonzero input vectors of block \(a\), on the full Boolean support of \(wf_\ell\), is at most \(R_{a\ell}\). These are uniform bounds over the preceding choices, not values measured after a favorable specialization. Put \[p_\ell=\min\left(1,\sum_a R_{a\ell}2^{-t_a}\right).\] The expected rank of the stacked actual weighted companion map on \([wU]\) is at most \(\sum_\ell p_\ell\). In particular, if this sum is less than \(H\), some coherent constant source map and \(f\in U\) satisfy \(wf\notin J_D\) while every mapped fresh generator is annihilated by \(wf\) within the original reserve. The complete source then has no degree-\(B\) PC refutation over the old base.

Proof. Conditional on earlier rows, a fixed nonzero input vector is missed by all \(t_a\) rows with probability \(2^{-t_a}\). A union bound over its at most \(R_{a\ell}\) values shows that the \(\ell\)-th weighted column has some nonzero Boolean companion value in block \(a\) with probability at most \(R_{a\ell}2^{-t_a}\). Future rows do not change that block. Average over the past and union over blocks to obtain the bound \(p_\ell\) for any error in that column. If the column is Boolean-zero, each of its entries has a Boolean-axiom certificate through its unreduced degree, at most \(b_w+K+B\), hence is zero in \(J_D\). The rank of a matrix is at most its number of nonzero columns. Taking expectations proves the rank bound.

The map on \([wU]\) is well-defined: if \([wf]=0\), multiplying this membership by any specialized companion fits \(b_w+K+B\). Thus a specialization of rank less than \(H\) has a nonzero kernel vector in that very quotient. Its binary linear combination of the chosen lifts gives \(f\) of degree at most \(K\) with \(wf\ne0\) in the old quotient. Fresh coefficient fields specialize to zero. All old generators and the mapped fresh generators have their required weighted memberships. Replaying each original PC line after the constant substitution and multiplication by \(wf\) stays through \(b_w+K+B\), including reuse after actual-degree cancellation. The final unit would kill \(wf\), a contradiction. This is the earlier weighted replay, with no new inference rule. \(\square\)

The proof bounds field rank by the count of columns proved zero; it does not equate a real pointwise error density with quotient rank. No low-degree point-indicator basis is assumed. Conversely, an arbitrary monomial basis may see exponentially many input values on each support, making the bound useless. Choosing a basis after the random rows would require a different argument.

Degenerate cases. The theorem excludes \(H=0\). An empty source or zero input tuple contributes no nonzero attainable values. A zero support has \(R_{a\ell}=0\), though a basis vector with surviving weighted class cannot be Boolean-zero in the paid window. An identity error column need not be removed: the union bound may equal one. Raw-coefficient readers are outside the stated source grammar.
