Pair merging gap

Statement. Work in \(\mathbb F_2[z,a,b,c]\), with independent indeterminates, and put \[B=z(z+1),\quad s=a+b+c,\quad P_0=z(z+a)(z+b)(z+c),\quad P_1=(z+1)(z+a+1)(z+b+1)(z+c+1),\quad P_2=(a+1)(b+1)(c+1).\] For every \(M=2^t\), \(t\ge0\), the three supplied affine products \(Q_i=P_i^M\) are pairwise coprime, no pair sum \(Q_i+Q_j\) is a product of binary affine polynomials, and \[Q_0+Q_1+Q_2=B^M s^M.\] Both root orders are exactly \(M\); the formal quotient by \(B^M\) is the cheap polynomial \(s^M\). Every original repetition is counted. The factor lists have size \(O(M+1)\). A procedure requiring an affine-split residual pair before reducing to the pair-division theorem cannot start on this representation.

Identity. Write \(e_2=ab+ac+bc\), \(e_3=abc\). Then \[P_0=z^4+s z^3+e_2 z^2+e_3 z.\] Translation by one sends \(P_0\) to \(P_1\). In characteristic two, \[P_0(z)+P_0(z+1)=s(z^2+z)+(1+s+e_2+e_3)=sB+P_2.\] Adding \(P_2\) proves the base identity. Frobenius gives the displayed identity for every \(M\). The old polynomial \(s\) is nonzero and independent of \(z\), so it contributes neither root factor. This proves the exact two root orders and the explicit quotient.

Coprimality. The affine-factor lists are respectively \(z,z+a,z+b,z+c\); \(z+1,z+a+1,z+b+1,z+c+1\); and \(a+1,b+1,c+1\). These are distinct nonassociate irreducible polynomials over \(\mathbb F_2\). The lists are disjoint across pairs, so all pair gcds are one, also after taking powers.

No split pair sum. First consider \(P_0+P_1=sB+P_2\). Its degree in \(z\) is two and its leading coefficient is the affine prime \(s\). In any binary-affine product representation, every factor involving \(z\) has leading coefficient one. There would therefore be exactly two such factors, and the product of all pivot-free factors would be \(s\). In particular its constant term would be divisible by \(s\). Its actual constant term is \(P_2\), whose three affine primes are none associate to \(s\). This is impossible.

Next consider \(P_0+P_2\), whose degree in \(z\) is four and leading coefficient is one. A split representation could contain no nonconstant pivot-free factor and would have exactly four factors \(z+u_j(a,b,c)\). The nonzero constant term forces every \(u_j\ne0\), and \[\prod_{j=1}^4 u_j=(a+1)(b+1)(c+1).\] Unique factorization forces three \(u_j\)'s to be \(a+1,b+1,c+1\), in some order, and the fourth to be the scalar one. Hence that split polynomial would be exactly \(P_1\). But \(P_0+P_2+P_1=Bs\ne0\), a contradiction. Translation \(z\mapsto z+1\) swaps \(P_0,P_1\), fixes \(P_2\), and preserves affine splitting; it therefore proves that \(P_1+P_2\) is not split either.

Finally \(Q_i+Q_j=(P_i+P_j)^M\). In the unique factorization domain, a nonzero polynomial that is not an affine product has at least one irreducible factor of degree greater than one. Taking a positive power retains that factor. Thus none of the powered pair sums splits. This is not a statement about choosing a different representation of the entire sum.

Nonvacuity. More strongly, let any unit-generating binary input tuple \(F\) contain the Boolean generator \(B\) and have four distinct old variables \(z,a,b,c\). No small certificate is assumed. Write \(L_\ne=\sum_{i\ne B}F_iZ_i\), and supply the actual probe circuit \[H=(sL_\ne)^M+Z_B^M(P_0^M+P_1^M+P_2^M).\] The proved identity and Frobenius show \(H=s^M(F\cdot Z)^M\). Hence its common formal value on every coefficient certificate is \(s^M\). Under the reciprocal substitution, the first branch is zero and the remaining three branches have denominator weight \(M\) and numerators exactly \(P_i^M\). Thus the recorded numerator construction itself produces this supplied triple after the zero branch is removed. It is not merely an unrelated presentation added afterward.

For inputs of total circuit size \(u\), depth \(\delta_F\), and \(r\) generator labels, the probe has size \(O(u+r+M+1)\) and constant added depth: compute \(L_\ne\) by one product and one sum layer beyond the given inputs, the powers by ordinary repeated wires, and the final products/sum. The quotient \(s^M\) has size \(O(M+1)\), depth at most two. Any growing Boolean-unsatisfiable CNF family with its complete Boolean generators has a nonempty full polynomial certificate class by the recorded finite-grid argument, even when its truncated PC quotient is proper. In particular the goal's charged input families can supply these four formal old variables. Taking \(M\) polynomial in the original encoding \(N\) gives a polynomial-size fixed-depth probe and dividend description while keeping that input family fixed independently of the proposed proof-size exponent. This proves source admissibility at unbounded variable counts, not a small refutation or a hard common value. An easy independent control consists of contradictory units on \(z\), units on the other old variables so they occur in the CNF, and all Boolean generators.

Degenerate cases. \(M=1\) already prevents a pair merger; larger powers transport the same obstruction rather than supplying more finite cases. Specializing the independent old variables can change splitting or make \(s\) zero, so the assertion is about the stated formal polynomial ring, not every point or quotient. Empty and unit terms are absent from this family. No odd-characteristic or nonlinear-factor extension is claimed.

Structural reason. Divisibility of the complete sum imposes cancellations involving all three terms. It does not require a split sum of any two. The leading and constant coefficients of those pair sums are incompatible with affine splitting, while translation of the quartic exposes the split complete sum. This defeats any candidate whose mandatory first move is the stated affine pair merger. It leaves complete-sum regrouping, primitive deflation and direct quotient operations available.

Alternative representation and remaining step

The whole dividend already has the supplied factorization \(B^M s^M\); removing its root factors gives the quotient at constant depth and size \(O(M+1)\). Another costed route recognizes the common Frobenius power \(M\), deflates to the base triple, applies the recorded fixed-root divided differences once at each root, and restores the paid power. Thus the failure of the pair-first procedure is not an optimized-division obstruction. Conversely the previous primitive-triple control has common power one and unbounded root order, yet admits the pair merger. Neither control answers a procedure that combines the two moves.
