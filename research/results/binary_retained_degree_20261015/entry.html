A top monomial survives the charged quotient

Normalize \(w\) in the local three-state algebra. Its normal polynomial \(\bar w\) is nonzero and has actual degree \(b_0\le b\). Choose a nonzero labeled monomial \(m_{B_0}\) of degree \(b_0\). At most \(b_0\) edges of \(\mathcal M\) meet its edge support, so (1) supplies an \(r\)-set \(S\subseteq\mathcal M\setminus B_0\). In the locally reduced product \(\bar wZ_S\), the monomial \[ m_{B_0}\prod_{e\in S}x_e \] has degree \(b_0+r\) and nonzero coefficient. To reach this degree, a term must choose every \(x_e\) from \(Z_S\) and a degree-\(b_0\) term of \(\bar w\) disjoint from \(S\). A different such term gives a different labeled monomial. Terms with overlap or smaller degree cannot contribute. On the other hand local normalization of \(\bar wR_S\) has degree at most \(b_0+r-1\). Hence their difference is a nonzero local function. This is the older maximum-monomial argument, with the selected group chosen from one fixed matching.

Every Fourier frequency of \(\bar wZ_S\) is supported inside a weight monomial plus a subset of the matching \(S\), with incidence at most \(a+1\). Every frequency of \(\bar wR_S\) has incidence at most \(a+r-1\), since a degree-\((r-1)\) normal monomial uses at most \(r-1\) edges. Since \(r\ge2\), both bounds are at most \(a+r-1\). For two distinct frequencies, their difference is supported on a graph of maximum degree at most \(2(a+r-1)<\varepsilon\). Such a graph contains no nonzero ternary cut: the near-half level-set inequality, in the notation of incidence cleanup, gives \[ |H\cap C|\le\frac{\max\deg H}{\varepsilon}|C|<|C| \] for every nonzero cut support \(C\). The frequencies therefore have distinct charged coordinates. All have degree at most \(b+r\le D\), so the exact cut-code dictionary preserves the nonzero local difference in \(J_D\). Local normalization and multiplication cost at most \(b+r\). This contradicts (2) after (3), proving the proposition.
