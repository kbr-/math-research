First proposal: normalized reverse paths

Family 112, OpenAI, Beyond the Square-Root Exponent for Depth-Three Boolean Circuits (23 September 2026), contains the Restriction bound, the named Restriction bound. For a width-at-most-\(k\) CNF, independently retain variables with probability \(p\), assign the rest uniformly, and take \(b\ge1\). If \[ \theta=\sum_{j=b+1}^{k}\binom kj\left(\frac{2p}{1-p}\right)^j<1, \] the paper constructs a signed combination of width-at-most-\(b\) CNFs whose coefficient mass \(W\) has \(\mathbb EW\le(1-\theta)^{-1}\). Consequently every real test function's correlation with the restricted CNF is at most \(W\) times its largest absolute correlation with a width-\(b\) CNF. The choice of expansion does not depend on the test function. The bound has no clause-count factor.

Proof inspected in full. The finite expansion follows compatible sequences of violated clauses. Each step fixes more than \(b\) new live coordinates. A path receives reciprocal counts of already determined violated clauses. A marker-permutation experiment represents the remaining reciprocal functions as convex mixtures of narrow CNFs. Counting paths backward cancels the number of eligible clauses against the reciprocal count and gives a geometric series in \(\theta\). Duplicate clauses are explicitly allowed and counted in both numerator and denominator. This is a concrete way to avoid paying the entire inventory at every local correction.

Proposed import. The main session should test normalized reverse-path counting against the actual retained-error inventory, rather than importing the headline circuit theorem. The first missing bridge is a degree-compatible finite-field certificate or a rank inequality on one surviving quotient. The source uses positive real probabilities and divides by integer violation counts; those denominators need not be invertible modulo the proof characteristic. A signed representation with small real coefficient mass need not have few terms, low algebraic degree, or a nonzero surviving weight. Uniform Boolean restrictions must also preserve the selected hard family. The near-half session may inspect this as a restriction mechanism, but it presently supplies no lower bound on PC degree and its main theorem has only depth three and no MOD gates.

Second proposal: branch-centered matrix smoothing

Family 126, OpenAI, Exponential PSD rank of positively shifted matching matrices (5 October 2026), has a separate Fourier smoothing of matrix squares theorem, the named Fourier smoothing theorem. It assumes product channels from finite \(X_i\) to \(Y_i=(\mathbb F_2)^a\), uniform averaged marginals, and a scalar variance estimate with constant \(\theta\) for every real test function. Put \(L=2^a\), \(C_L=2^{L-1}\). For a real PSD matrix function \(F\) of size \(r\), with \(\operatorname{tr}F\le B\), and \(\lambda>1\) satisfying \(L\lambda^2C_L\theta\le1\), it constructs an exact square decomposition of the product average with a separate Fourier center \(q\) for each branch. Some channel choice satisfies \[ \sum_{q,p}\lambda^{\operatorname{dist}(q,p)}\|\widehat H_{q,p}\|_F^2 \le L^t\sqrt r\,B\,2^{t/2}. \] This includes exponential costs in the number \(t\) of blocks; it is not dimension-free in every parameter.

Proof inspected in full with its splitting lemma. Orthogonal row tags make product averages exact. Spectral splitting assigns frequency Gram matrices to branches, controlling misplaced compressions by pairwise overlaps. A fourth-order Gram potential grows by at most two per block. The final rank bound uses the number of columns, not the growing row dimension. Multiplication by a branch's real sign character recenters its Fourier support while preserving its square. Future channel parameters stay independent of choices already made; no unjustified conditioning independence is assumed.

Proposed import. This is a concrete comparison object for the main notebook's graded/matrix polymer item: try separate centers for surviving error pieces, retaining a joint overlap potential, rather than bounding each piece around zero. Its direct use fails at the current interface: PSD order, real Fourier characters and Frobenius norms are not finite-field rank; over \(\mathbb F_2\), the signs \(+1\) and \(-1\) coincide. A suitable replacement must pay translation degrees and all branch costs, and establish its channel variance hypothesis on the actual quotient. The reread truncated-kernel dependence example rules out inferring independence merely from disjoint supports. The legal-row counterexample also prevents an automatic relative-rank improvement. The source's shifted matching PSD lower bound is not a near-half PC theorem.

Third proposal: a finite backward scale induction

Family 274, OpenAI, Product-projection localization and the QAC0 parity lower bound (24 September 2026), states localization, the named localization theorem. On \((\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes N}\), let \(M,D\) count mismatches against product vectors and let \(U\) have fixed depth of disjoint product-reflection layers and local unitaries. For every fixed \(0\le s<t\), uniformly in these choices for sufficiently large \(N\), \[ \|[M\ge N^t]U[D=0]U^\dagger[M=0]\|\le e^{-N^s}. \] The allowed gates and complex operator norm are essential parts of the statement.

Mechanism inspected. The complete backward-step proof replaces only one projection after squaring a transition norm. The remaining tail is conjugated into the inverse circuit with the two mismatch counts exchanged. Assuming localization at the preceding depth for every fixed exponent pair, the same-depth implication runs from \((t,b)\) to \((s,t)\) for \(t<b<2t-s\). A finite chain reaches a mismatch exponent above one, where the tail vanishes, then returns backward. The polynomial-approximation and preceding-depth lemmas were identified but not fully audited here.

Proposed import. Test whether the complete retained-source error map admits a dual formulation closing the high-degree tail at a larger cutoff. The first task is an exact interface, not another cutoff optimization: give an adjoint or reversible map on the same filtered quotient, a finite terminal regime, and the full degree ledger. PC inference and the present compiler are not unitary or automatically inverse-closed. Rank has no replacement supplied here for the source's norm-squaring estimate. A quantum circuit parity lower bound does not imply an \(\mathrm{AC}^0[2]\)-Frege lower bound; parity gates are allowed in the latter. This remains a method proposal, not a reduction.

Lex-plus-powers and the actual Ext target

A bound for the canonical image, not just its ambient representation

Family 210, OpenAI, Quadratic stabilization of the canonical Foulkes–Howe map (25 September 2026), states for every finite-dimensional complex vector space \(V\), \(a\ge2\), and \(b\ge a(a-1)\) that the specified map \[ \mu_{a,b,V}:\operatorname{Sym}^b(\operatorname{Sym}^aV) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^a(\operatorname{Sym}^bV) \] is surjective. See the source, main theorem. The convention matters: it is multiplication in the invariant tensor algebra, identified by averaging. If \(A\) is the subalgebra generated in degree one and \(R\) its stated normalization, the conclusion is \(A_b=R_b\) in this range, uniformly in \(\dim V\). It is stronger than existence of some equivariant embedding in the opposite direction, and does not claim the optimal threshold.

Proof read through completion. The proof converts the canonical image to a diagonal-vanishing criterion for symmetric multilinear forms. A directional derivative moving degree from an independent variable block \(U\) to another block \(Y\) is injective when \(\deg_U>\deg_Y\), by the positive Fischer inner product. Two sequences of those operators first free one slot and then remove a shared power. The degree accumulated in the shared block produces the quadratic bound. Polarization, factorial divisions and positivity are characteristic-zero inputs.

Proposed BMD use. Test whether a characteristic-zero coefficient-image or polynomial-descent obstruction is an explicit quotient or compatible subdiagram of this canonical map. This targets the difference between an actual polynomial image and its normalized ambient module, which the recorded image obstruction makes essential. It does not license replacing BMD's image by its normalization: the six-root witness and its formal extensions must remain falsifying controls. Nor does ambient surjectivity imply surjectivity on the marked constrained subspace. The first test is to exhibit the exact map and parameter correspondence on the known six-root control, including its grading; without that diagram there is no BMD application. The source proof supplies no characteristic-three analogue.

Theta degeneration and a two-coordinate interpolation criterion

Family 039, OpenAI, Nagata's Conjecture for Plane Curves (23 September 2026), states the strict Nagata inequality for arbitrary multiplicities at \(r\ge10\) very general complex plane points. Complete rank–trace argument

General statement. lem:hermitian-rank-trace is the all-dimensional algebraic ingredient, reconstructing source Lemma 3.2. For Hermitian matrices \(P\succeq0\) and \(Q\), with \(r=\operatorname{rank}P\) and \(n_+(Q)\le b\), \[r\ge2\operatorname{tr}P+4\operatorname{tr}Q-4b-\|P+Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.\] This uses ordered real eigenvalues and complex/real positivity, not finite-field rank.

Proof. Write \(Q=Q_+-H\), where both spectral parts are positive semidefinite and \(Q_+H=0\). Diagonalize \(P\) with nonzero eigenvalues \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\); let \(h_i\ge0\) be the diagonal entries of \(H\) in this basis. Dropping off-diagonal squares and then the remaining diagonal squares gives \[\|P-H\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge\sum_{i=1}^r(p_i-h_i)^2\ge2\operatorname{tr}P-r-2\sum_{i=1}^rh_i\ge2\operatorname{tr}P-r-4\operatorname{tr}H.\] The middle inequality is \((x-1)^2\ge0\); the last uses \(\sum_{i\le r}h_i\le\operatorname{tr}H\) and \(\operatorname{tr}H\ge0\). There are at most \(b\) strictly positive eigenvalues \(q_j\) of \(Q_+\), and \((q_j-2)^2\ge0\) implies \(\|Q_+\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge4\operatorname{tr}Q_+-4b\). Expanding, using \(\operatorname{tr}(PQ_+)\ge0\), yields \[\|P+Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\|P-H\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|Q_+\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+2\operatorname{tr}(PQ_+)\ge2\operatorname{tr}P-r+4\operatorname{tr}Q-4b.\] Rearrange. This includes zero-rank and degenerate matrices. Equality is attained by \(P\) an orthogonal projection and \(Q\) twice a projection on an orthogonal subspace, so the inequality itself admits no universal improvement with these quantities.

Zero-side construction and analytic gate

Use \(z_\rho=(\rho-1/2)/i=\gamma-i(\beta-1/2)\), not just the real ordinate. Functional-equation partners at the same positive height are \(\rho\) and \(1-\bar\rho\), hence their \(z\)'s are conjugate. With \(L=\log(T/2\pi)\), let \(\psi\) be either the indicator on \([-1/2,1/2]\) or \(\cos(\sqrt2u)\) there, and smooth its square root at both endpoints: \[\phi(u)=\chi(L/2+u)\chi(L/2-u)\sqrt{\psi(u/L)},\quad a=L^{-1}\|\phi\|_2^2.\] Here \(\chi\) is smooth, zero on \(( -\infty,0]\) and one on \([1,\infty)\). Set \(\alpha_k=T+2\pi k/L\), \(d=\lfloor LT/(2\pi)\rfloor\), \(v_\rho=(\widehat\phi(z_\rho-\alpha_k))_{0\le k<d}\) and \(I'=[T-\sqrt T,2T+\sqrt T)\). Fourier normalization is \(\widehat f(z)=\int f(u)e^{-iuz}\,du\). Define \[G={1\over aL^2}\sum_{\gamma\in I'}m_\rho v_\rho v_\rho^{\mathsf T}.\] The transpose is deliberate. Off-line conjugate partners make the total real symmetric. On-line atoms are positive. An off-line pair with \(v=x+iy\) contributes \(2m(xx^{\mathsf T}-yy^{\mathsf T})/(aL^2)\), a pullback with positive index at most one; degeneracy can reduce that index.

Poisson summation at lattice step \(2\pi/L\), with support of the convolution in \([-L,L]\) and vanishing endpoint modes, gives \[\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\widehat\phi(z-\alpha_k)\widehat\phi(z'-\alpha_k)=L\widehat{\phi^2}(z-z').\] At equal real arguments this is \(aL^2\), so a truncated simple on-line atom has trace at most one. Let \(P_1\) sum those simple atoms and put \(Q'=G-P_1\). If \(s_1,s_2,p\) count simple on-line points, multiple on-line points and off-line pairs in \(I'\), respectively, then \[\operatorname{rank}P_1\le s_1,\quad\operatorname{tr}P_1\le s_1,\quad n_+(Q')\le s_2+p,\quad s_1+2s_2+2p\le N(I').\] The last inequality retains every multiplicity; summing pullbacks bounds positive index without any positivity of the off-line zero kernel.

Analytic premises. The source's Propositions 4.2/4.3 and Theorem 5.7 assert \[N(I')=N(T,2T)+o(N),\quad\operatorname{tr}G=N+o(N),\quad \|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=(R(\psi)+o(1))N,\] where \[R(\psi)={\int\psi^2+\iint|u-v|\psi(u)\psi(v)\,du\,dv\over(\int\psi)^2}.\] Endpoint discrepancies are \(O(\sqrt T\log T)=o(N)\). These are the outstanding independent analytic gate; the following conclusion is proved from them, not from an assumed RH.

Deduction. Apply the matrix inequality to \(P_1,Q'\), using \(\operatorname{tr}P_1+2n_+(Q')\le N(I')\): \[s_1\ge4\operatorname{tr}G-2N(I')-\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=(2-R(\psi)-o(1))N.\] The same inequality, with \(\operatorname{tr}P_1\le s_1\), gives \(3s_1+4(s_2+p)\ge(4-R(\psi)-o(1))N\). Subtract \(s_1+2(s_2+p)\le N(I')=N+o(N)\) to obtain \(2(s_1+s_2+p)\ge(3-R(\psi)-o(1))N\). Since \(D(I')=s_1+s_2+2p\), deleting endpoint points proves the distinct-zero bound. For cumulative counts, fix an error tolerance, sum over dyadic blocks whose lower endpoints exceed its fixed threshold, and discard the bounded initial block; Riemann–von Mangoldt makes its relative contribution vanish.

Window evaluation. For the flat window, \(\int\psi^2=\int\psi=1\) and \(\iint|u-v|=1/3\), hence \(R=4/3\). For the cosine window, \(H(u)=\psi(u)+\int|u-v|\psi(v)\,dv\) satisfies \(H''=\psi''+2\psi=0\) in the interval and is even, hence constant. Direct integration gives \(H(0)=\cos(1/\sqrt2)+(1/\sqrt2)\sin(1/\sqrt2)\), while \(\int\psi=\sqrt2\sin(1/\sqrt2)\). Integrating \(H\psi\) gives \(R=H(0)/\int\psi=R_*\), proving the exact constants from the analytic premises.
