\section{The required chessboard homology}\label{sec:topology}

We include the homological input used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:moments}. All homology in this appendix has coefficients
in \(\F\). A finite simplicial complex is called \(q\)-acyclic if its
reduced homology vanishes in every degree from \(-1\) through \(q\).
Thus \((-1)\)-acyclic means nonempty. Below degree \(-1\) the condition
is vacuous. Nonempty here means having at least one vertex.

\begin{lemma}[Homological cover lemma]\label{lem:nerve}
Let a finite simplicial complex \(K\) be covered by subcomplexes
\(L_1,\ldots,L_t\), and let \(\mathcal N\) be the nerve of this cover.
Fix \(q\ge0\).
If every nonempty intersection of \(s\) members is
\((q-s+1)\)-acyclic and \(\mathcal N\) is \(q\)-acyclic, then \(K\)
is \(q\)-acyclic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here is the chain-complex comparison behind the assertion.
Form the finite double complex
\[
 D_{a,b}=
  \bigoplus_{\substack{J\subseteq[t]\\|J|=a+1,\ L_J\ne\varnothing}}
       C_b(L_J;\F),
 \qquad L_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}L_j,
 \qquad a,b\ge0.
\]
The vertical differential is the simplicial boundary and the horizontal
differential omits one cover index and includes the chain in the larger
intersection. In characteristic two their sum is a differential.
Augment the horizontal rows to \(C_b(K;\F)\).
For each individual simplex of \(K\), the set of cover members containing
it is a nonempty simplex of indices. Its augmented chain complex is
exact. Taking the direct sum over simplices proves that every augmented
horizontal row is exact. Thus computing the total complex first in the
horizontal direction gives the homology of \(K\).

Compute instead first vertically.
The first page consists of
\(\bigoplus_{|J|=a+1}H_b(L_J;\F)\).
For \(b>0\) and \(a+b\le q\), these groups vanish by the assumed
\((q-a)\)-acyclicity.
For \(b=0\) and \(a\le q\), each nonempty \(L_J\) is connected,
so the horizontal chain groups are precisely those of the nerve.
In degree \(a=q+1\), the augmentation
\(H_0(L_J)\longrightarrow\F\) is still onto for every nonempty
intersection. Hence the incoming boundaries contain all the nerve
boundaries needed in degree \(q\).
It follows that the second page has zero reduced groups in total
degrees at most \(q\), since the nerve is \(q\)-acyclic.
No later page can create a group where the preceding one is zero.
The double complex is finite, so its filtration converges and the
same vanishing holds for \(K\). Augmenting degree zero throughout
gives the assertion for reduced homology as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Homological chessboard bound {\cite[Theorem 1.1]{BLVZ94}}]
\label{thm:chessboard}
For \(a,b\ge1\), let
\[
 \nu(a,b)=\min\left\{a,b,\left\lfloor\frac{a+b+1}{3}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Then the chessboard complex \(\Delta_{a,b}\) is
\((\nu(a,b)-2)\)-acyclic.
In particular,
\[
 b\ge2a-1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \widetilde H_{a-2}(\Delta_{a,b};\F)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The stated homological bound follows from the stronger connectivity
theorem in \cite{BLVZ94}. For completeness, the standard star-cover
argument gives its homological form directly as follows.
Transpose the board so that \(a\le b\), and induct on \(a\).
If \(\nu(a,b)=1\), nonemptiness suffices; this includes \(a=1\).
Otherwise put \(q=\nu(a,b)-2\ge0\), so \(a\ge2\).

For each column \(j\), let \(L_j\) be the closed star of the vertex
\((1,j)\). It consists of matchings containing that cell or extendable
by it, and is a cone. These stars cover the complex: a matching
avoiding row one occupies at most \(a-1<b\) columns and can therefore
be extended in that row.
An intersection of \(s\ge2\) stars excludes row one and their \(s\)
columns, so it is \(\Delta_{a-1,b-s}\). Every proper subfamily of the
\(b\) stars has nonempty intersection, while the intersection of all
stars has no vertex. The nerve is therefore the boundary of a
\((b-1)\)-simplex, a sphere of dimension \(b-2\).

Write \(\nu=\nu(a,b)\). For \(a\ge2\) and \(a\le b\), one has
\(\nu\le b-1\); hence the nerve is \((\nu-2)\)-acyclic.
For a nonempty \(s\)-fold intersection with \(s\ge2\), put
\(\mu=\nu(a-1,b-s)\). Each entry of its defining minimum is at least
\(\nu-s+1\):
\[
 a-1\ge\nu-s+1,\qquad b-s\ge\nu-s+1,
\]
and
\[
 a+b-s\ge3\nu-s-1\ge3(\nu-s+1).
\]
The last inequality uses \(s\ge2\).
Thus \(\mu\ge\nu-s+1\), and induction shows that the intersection is
\((\nu-s-1)=(q-s+1)\)-acyclic. If necessary transpose this smaller
board before applying induction; its smaller side is at most \(a-1\).
The cover lemma proves the claim.
Finally \(b\ge2a-1\) makes \(\nu(a,b)=a\), giving the required special
case.
\end{proof}
