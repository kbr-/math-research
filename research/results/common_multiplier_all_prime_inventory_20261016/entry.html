All-prime small-inventory exclusion

Setting. As in the main notebook's thm:all-prime-affine-family-exclusion (its proof): a prime \(p\), \(n=2^\ell\), \(\ell\ge2\), \(m=n+1\) pigeon rows of \(\ell\) bits, \(v=(n+1)\ell\), the compact base \(\mathcal Q_{n,p}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) (collision equations and Boolean bit equations, the encoding of lem:all-prime-compact-bit-decoder), accuracy \(h=3(p-1)\ell\), and complete one-level ENS blocks with inputs affine in the old bits and coefficient domains \(r^p-r\). A proper block of rank \(r\) is high when \((p-1)r>h(k+1)\), that is \(r>3\ell(k+1)\). A coordinate block has input span spanned by literals \(x_b-c_b\), \(c_b\in\mathbb F_p\); if it is proper they involve distinct bits, since two constants on one bit give a unit span, and its zero flat in \(\mathbb F_p^v\) is \(\{x_b=c_b,\ b\in P\}\), \(|P|=r\).

Parameter ledger. The budget the statement checks: kernel degree \(k\ge1\); replay ceiling \(B=k(D+1)\); separation conditions \(1\le k\le B\), \(4(k-1)<n\), \(n\ge2B-1\), of which only the last is a hypothesis; high threshold \(r>3\ell(k+1)\); inventory \(M\le k\), or the greedy inequality below.

Statement. Let \(k\ge1\), and suppose the proper high blocks are \(M\) coordinate blocks. If \(D\ge1\), \(2k(D+1)-1\le n\), and either \(M\le k\), or for some integer \(0\le t\le k-1\), with \(r=3\ell(k+1)+1\) and \(V=pv\) (\(V=v\) when every bit pinned by several of these blocks has one constant), \(M\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(1-(r-\ell i)/V)\le k-t\), then the augmented system has no ordinary-PC refutation through degree \(D\). In particular \(M\ge1\) such blocks are excluded through \(D_M=\lfloor(n+1)/(2M)\rfloor-1\) whenever \(D_M\ge1\), that is \(M\le(n+1)/4\).

Proof. The kernel under \(M\le k\). Each high pinned set has more than \(3\ell(k+1)\ge\ell(k-1)\) bits, at most \(\ell\) per row, so it meets at least \(k\) rows; choose for each block in turn a pinned bit \(b_a\) in a row not used before, and put \(f=\prod_a(x_{b_a}-c_{b_a})\), or \(f=1\) if \(M=0\).

The kernel under the product inequality. Call a pair \((b,c)\), \(c\in\mathbb F_p\), a literal, and let \(\Lambda_a\) be the literals block \(a\) pins: \(|\Lambda_a|\ge r\), at most \(\ell\) in each row. Build a set \(X\) of literals in distinct rows. With \(R_0\) all high blocks, suppose \(i<t\) literals are chosen in \(i\) rows and \(R_i\) are the blocks not yet met. Each block of \(R_i\) pins at least \(r-\ell i>0\) literals in unused rows, and at most \(V\) literals in unused rows are pinned by any block (one per bit with consistent pins, at most \(p\) per bit otherwise); by double counting some such literal lies in at least \(|R_i|(r-\ell i)/V\) of the sets \(\Lambda_a\), \(a\in R_i\). Add it; \(|R_{i+1}|\le|R_i|(1-(r-\ell i)/V)\), so the inequality gives \(|R_t|\le k-t\). For each remaining block add one of its literals in a row not yet used; one exists, since at most \(k-1\) rows are used and \(r-\ell(k-1)>0\). Then \(|X|\le k\); put \(f=\prod_{(b,c)\in X}(x_b-c)\). This is the proof of lem:greedy-transversal-certificate, written over \(\mathbb F_p\)-literals.

In both cases \(f\) is a product of at most \(k\) affine factors in distinct rows, so it expands into monomials with at most one variable per row: \(f\in\mathcal L_k\), \(f\ne0\), and substituting a block's pins makes one factor zero, so the ordinary restriction of \(f\) to every high zero flat is the zero polynomial. The rest is the all-prime proof with its counting step replaced; it uses its count only to produce this kernel. Affine coordinates give literal expressions \(f=\sum_ia_ig_i\), \(\deg a_i\le k-1\), for each high tuple; the \(p-1\) coefficient rows \((\alpha a_i)_i\), \(\alpha\ne0\), turn the high product into \(1-f^{p-1}\); weighting by \(f\), a high companion is \(g_i(f-f^p)\), certified through \(pk+1\); low blocks, \((p-1)r\le h(k+1)\), use factor packing with companions through \((p-1)r+1\); zero and unit spans are removed by scalar cleanup. These ceilings fit \(k(e+1)\) for every axiom of original degree \(e\) at every \(k\ge1\), as that proof checks; a refutation through \(D\) uses only axioms with \(e\le D\), so it replays to an old derivation of \(f\) through \(B=k(D+1)\), whatever \(D\ge1\). The signed row separation (lem:signed-cube-row-linear-separation, Section 3 of that entry) excludes it when \(1\le k\le B\), \(4(k-1)<n\) and \(n\ge2B-1\): the first holds for \(D\ge0\), the second follows from the third as \(4(k-1)<2k(D+1)-1\) for \(D\ge1\), and the third is the hypothesis. The last sentence is the case \(k=M\).

Relation to the \(\mathbb F_2\) theorem and the count. At \(p=2\), \(h=3\ell\) and \(V=2v\), this is thm:small-inventory-coordinate-exclusion on Braun's base with \(m=n+1\), there with the hypothesis \(D\ge2h+1\), which Braun's removal lemma states and the present statement does not need at \(m=n+1\); that theorem also allows other \(m\), so neither statement contains the other. In the case \(M\le k\) the range \(D_M\) is the same at every \(p\); only the greedy refinement changes, through \(V=pv\), which lowers each greedy fraction by the factor \(2/p\) against \(p=2\); since the bearing here is none, it was not tabulated at odd \(p\). Against the counting kernel, \(k\approx\sqrt{(n+1)\log4M}\) gives about \(\sqrt{n/\log M}/2\), so the transversal gives the higher degree for \(M\) up to about \(\sqrt{n\log M}\), the crossover of the previous entry, which is field-independent.

Nonvacuity. In the parameter ledger at \(p=3\), \(\ell=16\), \(n=65536\), \(h=96\): one coordinate block pinning \(97\) bits to constants in \(\mathbb F_3\), with \(97>3\ell(1+1)=96\), is high and satisfies the statement with \(M=k=1\), \(D_1=32767\), \(2(D_1+1)-1\le n\).

Degenerate cases. No proper high block: \(f=1\), and the exclusion holds at every \(k\) with the degree conditions. \(M>k\) without the product inequality: nothing. \(D<2h+1\): the companions cannot occur, and the exclusion holds by the same proof, or by old stability with \(f=1\). \(M>(n+1)/4\): \(D_M\) is zero and the last sentence says nothing. A high block that is not a coordinate block is outside the hypothesis.
