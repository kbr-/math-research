The line's general claim. The thread tests Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture (conj:ultimate-matching) on its curve; its route is the Hopf–Lax reduction to conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy and the flow form of lem:flow-matching-bound. Since the second review the route has been sharpened from both sides: the class form's refutation side, its order, and a sufficient condition for its converse.

What the goal needs. One matching at every pair of the curve with one universal \(L\), or a refutation at one pair; in the route's terms, a bound on the class (or a flow of bounded cost) at Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády size with a constant.

The six entries

The aligned superposition bound and the band overlap penalty decided the second review's test in the class form, for vertically scaled, positively oriented members: superposing Leighton–Shor test functions at several aspects gains at most \(1+o(1)\) over the best member. The Lipschitz baseline forced the class bound's order \(\sqrt{N\log N}\) at every pair, endpoints included. The threshold flow was dropped: a layered capacity needs a cut bound the record lacks. The tube density converse and the segment flow cost showed that a good matching of bounded tube density gives the class bound, and a flow of bounded cost through its segment flux. Every entry left the goal unchanged.

Falsification attempt. A refutation through the class form now needs a single test function beyond \(\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}\) that is not a large-gradient superposition gaining on its members, and it refutes only the sufficient statement, the converse being proved only under the density condition; a refutation of the conjecture itself still needs a mixed level outside the low-level mixed counts or the coupling of levels in one matching. No refutation was found; the record holds no candidate single function of the needed value.

Assessment of this line. The line has made its route precise: the class bound is pinned in order, its refutation side reduced to single functions, and its relation to the goal reduced to one question (do good matchings have bounded tube density, or give bounded-cost flows). It has not produced a mechanism for any upper bound above exponent \(2\), and the open-item count has stayed at four since the first review, twelve research entries back: the line is not converging on its open statements, which is why this review is goal-level.

Obstacle

The line is stuck on producing, at one pair of the curve, a single object that controls all levels at once: a matching good at every level, or equivalently (up to the density condition) a flow whose \(\psi_4\)-Orlicz cost is bounded. Every construction on record is either linear in the empirical measure (Gaussian per-point values, which fail above exponent \(2\)) or serves one level; no nonlinear construction with a provable bound exists, and the converse that would let a good matching certify the class needs a density control nobody has.

Outside leads

  • Finite convex duality for Beckmann's problem on the grid (Fenchel–Rockafellar; the version needed is proved here). For a finite convex cost \(F\) on grid edge flows and the grid divergence \(A\), \(\inf\{F(\Phi):A\Phi=f\}=\sup_g\{\langle g,f\rangle-F^*(A^{\mathsf T}g)\}\) whenever \(f\) has total mass zero. It targets conjugate-class-discrepancy through the missing implication C ⇒ F. Answers. With F ⇒ C (Fenchel–Young) it would make the class bound and the flow form equivalent up to discretization, so the converse question would be the single step from good matchings to flows. Test. Passed. Proof of the duality used: the value function \(v(f')=\inf\{F(\Phi):A\Phi=f'\}\) is convex and finite on the subspace of mass-zero \(f'\) (the grid is connected, \(F\) finite), hence continuous there and subdifferentiable at \(f\); a subgradient \(g\) gives \(F(\Phi)\ge v(f)+\langle g,A\Phi-f\rangle\) for all \(\Phi\), that is \(\langle g,f\rangle-F^*(A^{\mathsf T}g)\ge v(f)\), and weak duality gives the reverse. Break point. The grid problem is not the continuous one: a grid function \(g\) extended piecewise linearly on the triangles of the grid has, on each triangle with axis edges, gradient components equal to grid differences over \(\delta\), so its continuous penalty equals the grid penalty; but its pairing with the cell-smeared measures is a fixed average of vertex values (weights \(2,1,2,1\) over \(6\) on a split square), not \(\langle g,f\rangle\), and converting a grid flow on edges back into a bounded continuous flow for lem:flow-matching-bound was not checked.
  • Cyclical monotonicity of optimal matchings (the swap inequality; Rockafellar–Rüschendorf, recalled, and checked here in the form used). An optimal matching for a cost \(c\) cannot gain by exchanging two partners; for the quadratic cost this gives \(\langle a,a+b\rangle\ge0\) and hence separation of the moving points, \(|a+tb|\ge(1-t)|a|\), checked in the segment flow entry. It targets the converse's density condition. Answers. If optimal matchings for the exponential cost have separated moving points, their tube density is bounded by the cell density and the converse holds, so the route asks exactly the goal. Test. Passed for the quadratic cost. Break point. For a separable cost \(\sum_j\psi_j(|z_j|/s)\) the swap inequality gives separation only within the ratio of \(\psi''\) over the displacements involved; for \(\psi_4(u)=e^{u^4}-1\), \(\psi''(u)=(12u^2+16u^6)e^{u^4}\), which tends to \(0\) as \(u\to0\) and grows like \(e^{u^4}\): the ratio is unbounded between short and long displacements, so the quadratic argument does not transfer as it stands.
  • Ambrosio, Stra and Trevisan, "A PDE approach to a 2-dimensional matching problem" (arXiv:1611.04960; its main theorem read in this cycle): for the quadratic cost, \(\frac n{\log n}\mathbb EW_2^2(\mu^n,\nu^n)\to\frac1{2\pi}\), proved through the Poisson equation for the heat-regularized empirical difference and a Dacorogna–Moser coupling. It targets conjugate-class-discrepancy through a flow: \(\nabla f^{n,t}\). Answers. A flow defined by an elliptic equation is one object at all scales, which the obstacle asks for. Test. Not run: the flow \(\nabla f^{n,t}\) is linear in the empirical measure, the class of flows for which the dyadic flow obstruction proves Gaussian per-point lower tails only for the dyadic kernel; its per-point tails were not computed for the heat-regularized Poisson kernel. Retrieval. tools/arxiv_source.py fetched arXiv:1611.04960 and the main theorem and the strategy section were read; the per-point estimates of its later sections were not.

Absurd bridges

  • Congested traffic and Wardrop equilibria (transportation engineering; Brasco, Carlier and Santambrogio, "Congested traffic dynamics, weak flows and very degenerate elliptic equations", J. Math. Pures Appl. 2010, read in this cycle: Theorems 2.1 and 3.2). Theorem 3.2: for densities in \(L^p\), the minimal-flow problem \(\min\int H(\sigma)\), \(\operatorname{div}\sigma=\mu_0-\mu_1\), has the same value as the path problem over probability measures \(Q\) on curves whose traffic intensity \(i_Q\) is penalized by \(H\), the optimal \(Q\) being built from the optimal \(\sigma\) along \(\mu_t=(1-t)\mu_0+t\mu_1\) by the superposition principle; Theorem 2.1 gives \(\sigma=\nabla H^*(\nabla u)\) with \(u\) solving a very degenerate elliptic equation. Translation: our flow form is Beckmann's congested problem with \(H(\sigma)=\lambda'C(\sigma/\lambda')\), and traffic intensity is our tube density. Answers. It turns the converse's density condition into congestion: a flow of bounded cost is a traffic pattern whose intensity is the flow's magnitude, so the question "do good matchings give bounded-cost flows" becomes "is the matching's traffic congested", a quantity this theory measures. Test. Passed. Break point. Their analysis is for isotropic \(H(\sigma)=\frac1p|\sigma|^p+\lambda|\sigma|\), \(p\in(1,2]\), and densities in \(L^p\); ours is anisotropic and exponential, and the follow-up of Brasco and Carlier on anisotropic costs was not read. Theorem 3.2's construction couples smeared measures, not points, so it gives traffic patterns, not matchings.
  • Moment characterization of exponential Orlicz norms and \(p\)-Laplacian (nonlinear electrical) networks. A function \(f\) has \(\int e^{|f|^\alpha}\le2\) up to constants exactly when \(\|f\|_p\lesssim p^{1/\alpha}\) for all \(p\ge1\) (checked here in one direction: \(|f|^p\le(p/(e\alpha))^{p/\alpha}e^{|f|^\alpha}\), so \(\|f\|_p\le2^{1/p}(p/(e\alpha))^{1/\alpha}\); the other direction by expanding \(e^{|f|^\alpha}\) in moments). Translation: a flow of bounded \(\psi_4\)-cost is one flow whose \(L^p\) norms, in the reduction's units, grow at most like \(p^{1/4}\) for all \(p\le\log N\); for each fixed \(p\) the optimal flow is \(p'\)-harmonic, \(\Phi=|\nabla u|^{p'-2}\nabla u\) with \(\operatorname{div}\Phi=f\) (the case \(H=\frac1p|\sigma|^p\) of Theorem 2.1 above). Answers. It splits the obstacle into a family of classical nonlinear potential problems, one per \(p\), and isolates the coupling as the demand that one flow be nearly optimal for all \(p\) at once. Test. Passed. Break point. The per-\(p\) statements (\(\min\|\Phi\|_p\lesssim p^{1/4}\) for each \(p\)) are not on record, and they are only necessary: the obstacle reappears as simultaneity across \(p\), the same coupling of levels in another form.

Architecture review

Necessary target. For every pair \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[2,\infty]\) on the curve and one universal \(L\), with \(X\) i.i.d. uniform and \(Y\) evenly spread, with probability tending to one a matching good in both coordinates; its negation, for every \(L\) some pair with \(\liminf\) of that probability below one. Downstream. Through lem:two-sample-matching-transfer it gives the two-sample goal with constant \(2L\): the goal. Through cor:exponential-matching-discrepancy-reduction, conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy gives it: the goal. Through lem:flow-matching-bound, a flow of bounded cost gives it: the goal. Conversely, through lem:tube-density-converse and lem:segment-flow-cost, the goal plus a good matching of bounded tube density gives the class bound and a bounded-cost flow up to constants and grid: weaker than an equivalence.

Extra requirements. The route asks for a bound uniform over the conjugate class (or a flow of bounded cost); the goal asks for one matching. By the two lemmas above, the extra demand is at most the density condition on one good matching. Whether that demand is real is a question about optimal matchings: if a good matching exists, so does an optimal one for the cost \(C(z)=\sum_j\psi_{\alpha_j}(|z_j|/s)\), and it is good; if optimal matchings have bounded tube density, the route asks exactly the goal up to constants.

Independent alternative. Before seeing the route, the medium reviewer received the goal, the survey's Section 5, the registered statements and the open conjectures with their evidence, and the earlier stage-one proposals so as not to repeat them (stage-one record). Its premise: flows linear in the sample have Gaussian marginals, so a proof must route nonlinearly using the sample. Its proposals, ranked: (1) a coarea splitting of a test function by gradient level, the level \(2^k\) treated as a boundary at scales coarser than \(2^{-k}\) (Leighton–Shor chaining over only those \(k\) scales, about \(\sqrt N\,k^{3/4}\) per unit length) and as a Lipschitz function at finer scales (Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády, about \(\sqrt{N\log N}\)), against a cost per unit length times \(k^{1/4}\) at \((4,4)\), which balances on the curve; (2) the variable-capacity max-flow min-cut form, which is the threshold flow dropped this period for want of a layered cut bound; (3) an entropy-number calibration of the class as a logarithmic Orlicz–Sobolev ball, heuristically showing Dudley's integral reaches \(\sqrt p\) exactly when \(\alpha\le2\). Comparison: (2) is on record and was dropped; (3) is a calibration of method limits, consistent with the dyadic flow obstruction; (1) is new: a dual-side mechanism for an upper bound, aimed at the obstacle, and where the route's own dual form had no concrete mechanism.

Decisive test. Trivial cases. The quantity is \(B_w=\sup_f\int G_f^{(w)}\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\), the supremum over graphs \(f\) in the Leighton–Shor class \(\mathcal C\) of (4.117) of [T4] of the discrepancy of the unit-amplitude band function \(G_f^{(w)}=\min\{1,\max\{0,(f+w-y)/w\}\}\) of width \(w=2^{-k}\), equal to the average over \(t\in[0,w]\) of the set discrepancies of \(S(f+t)\). At \(w\) of the grid side it is the Leighton–Shor set discrepancy, of order \(\sqrt N(\log N)^{3/4}\) (Theorem 4.8.1 of [T4] from below, Leighton–Shor from above); at \(w\) of order one it is the discrepancy of a Lipschitz function of bounded gradient, of order \(\sqrt{N\log N}\) by (4.38) and lem:lipschitz-class-lower-bound. Shifting \(f\) or reflecting the square maps the class to itself; no trivial instance gives either outcome at intermediate \(w\). Power. The record bounds \(B_w\) above only by the set discrepancy, \(\sqrt N(\log N)^{3/4}\) for every \(w\) (averaging set discrepancies cannot exceed their supremum), and below by \(\sqrt{N\log N}\) (Lipschitz baseline) and, through the Leighton–Shor sets scaled at width \(w\), by about \(\sqrt N\min(k,\log N)^{3/4}\) (the construction of lem:leighton-shor-class-lower-bound with its finest scale cut at \(w\), not checked). So the predicted \(\sqrt N(k^{3/4}+\sqrt{\log N})\) sits between bounds that differ by \((\log N)^{3/4}/k^{3/4}\) at small \(k\), and the test is decided by an upper bound. No computation: the difference is a power of \(\log N\). Attempt. Tried from the cited statements: the two endpoints follow from the set bound and from (4.38); for intermediate \(w\), averaging \(S(f+t)\) over \(t\in[0,w]\) smooths the boundary at scale \(w\), so the chaining of the Leighton–Shor upper bound should run only over the dyadic scales coarser than \(w\), with the finer scales contributing an Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády term; it stopped at that restriction, which needs the proof of the Leighton–Shor upper bound (Section 4.7 of [T4], Theorem 4.7.1 and its chaining), not read in this cycle. Prove or refute the truncated Leighton–Shor bound: with probability tending to one, for every \(k\) with \(2^{-k}\) at least the grid side, \(B_{2^{-k}}\le K\sqrt N(k^{3/4}+\sqrt{\log N})\), by reading and restricting the chaining of Theorem 4.7.1 of [T4] to scales coarser than \(2^{-k}\); then its anisotropic form with weights \(k^{1/\alpha_j}\) on the normal components. If it holds, the coarea assembly of the independent alternative gives the class bound at \((4,4)\) once the layers are made uniform over gradient levels, which becomes the next obligation; if \(B_w\) stays of order \(\sqrt N(\log N)^{3/4}\) for \(w\) well above the grid side (the band averaging does not kill fine-scale Leighton–Shor oscillation), the coarea mechanism fails at \((4,4)\) and the line learns that the Leighton–Shor excess lives at all scales at once, which is evidence for a refutation through single test functions.
