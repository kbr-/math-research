Facts the candidates rely on (checked by the reviewer):
1. On the curve both exponents are at least 2; the curve with an infinite exponent consists of
   (2,infinity) and (infinity,2), so the hard endpoint is (2,infinity), Conjecture 17.1.4.
2. The two joint constraints reduce to one cost at a constant loss: with c = exp(term 1) +
   exp(term 2), an optimal permutation for this single linear cost with total at most C N has each
   coordinate sum at most C N; enlarging L by t^(1/alpha), t >= log2(C), brings each below 2N
   (power mean). So the problem is one optimal-transport bound, with Kantorovich duality available.
3. Layer-cake form: with s = sqrt(log N/N), the condition is, up to constants, that for each
   k = 1..log N at most N e^(-k) pairs have |d_j| > s k^(1/alpha_j) for some j, and none at
   k = log N. The level-k box has area s^2 k^(1/alpha_1+1/alpha_2) = s^2 sqrt k for every pair on
   the curve; only its aspect ratio kappa_k = k^(1/alpha_1-1/alpha_2) changes, between k^(-1/2) and
   k^(1/2). An area-preserving stretch makes each level isotropic on a torus of aspect at most
   (log N)^(1/2).

Candidates, ranked:
A. Prove each level separately, then glue. (i) Fact 3. (ii) For each k a Hall deficiency bound:
   for every union of cells A, #X(A) - #Y(A expanded by the level-k box) <= C N e^(-k).
   (iii) A deterministic gluing lemma for nested graphs G_k (edges inside the level-k box): if each
   G_k has a matching missing at most m_k vertices, one perfect matching M has |M \ G_k| <= C m_k
   for all k at once. (ii) at fixed k is the Leighton-Shor upper bound on elongated tori, but the
   other levels need its large-deviation version (no source recalled). (iii) has no known theorem
   (bipartite matching with laminar edge-capacity constraints is not totally unimodular). Breaks
   at (iii): two-edge examples show a factor-2 loss, G_1 = {au}, G_2 = G_1 + {av, bu}; chains might
   lose a factor growing with the number of levels; the weights e^k may absorb it. Smallest test:
   random small nested bipartite graphs, compare the optimal sum_k e^k |M \ G_k| with
   sum_k e^k m_k, and search adversarially for a growing ratio.
B. Shor's coordinate-separated scheme for (2,infinity): horizontal strips of height L s with
   vertical balancing, then 1D matching within strips. Breaks (checked arithmetic): a strip holds
   about L sqrt(N log N) points, so independent within-strip matching has moves of order
   (N log N)^(-1/4), larger than s by N^(1/4)(log N)^(-3/4); strips must exchange mass at every
   horizontal scale. Endpoint only.
C. Randomized transport plans (Markov kernels) with a martingale bound per level: martingales give
   sub-Gaussian, not bounded, tails, so alpha_2 = infinity forces deterministic vertical moves, and
   different levels want different aspect ratios. May coincide with the current route.
D. Refutation by subgraphs whose aspect ratio varies with scale: the below-curve exclusion uses one
   aspect ratio; a curve pair would be refuted if letting kappa vary across the log N tent scales
   gained an unbounded factor. Guess: no gain, by self-similarity and convexity. Smallest test:
   optimize the excess-versus-penalty functional over kappa(.) numerically at r = 10..30.

Not found: a link supplying the cross-level gluing of A(iii) other than the original problem; fact 2
makes the gluing equivalent to one optimal-transport bound with cost sum_k e^k 1[e not in G_k], so
the reduction alone is circular. A, B and C each move the difficulty into making one matching serve
every level, each with its own aspect ratio.

Numerical control (weak): the smallest L meeting 2N at (2,infinity), (4,4), (3,6) for the optimal
