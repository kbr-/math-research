General statement. lem:marginal-refinement-gain: in Pang's setting, refining one family's approximator by his one-step choice, made on the joint kernel \(W_\nu\) of the other families, enlarges the joint kernel of all families by at least \((1-1/p)\) times the family's marginal rank on \(W_\nu\) one degree window down, and by at most its marginal rank at the window; joint Hilbert monotonicity holds; and the marginals can all vanish while the joint rank is full, so no refinement of one family contracts the joint rank at every state; one simultaneous round of all families can gain nothing either.

Marginal refinement gain

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_p\), let \(\mathcal C_D\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal in \(n\) variables containing the Boolean axioms, \(V_k\) all polynomials of degree at most \(k\) (as in Pang; \(N_n(k)\) counts multilinear monomials, and for \(k\ge n\) both \(\varphi(k)\) and \(N_n(k)\) are constant, so no \(K\le n\) is needed), families \((g_{\mu,i})_i\), \(\mu\in[S]\), of degree at most \(d\), approximators \(P_\mu\) of degree at most \(a\), \(Z_\mu(k)=\{f\in V_k:P_\mu g_{\mu,i}f\in\mathcal C_D\ \forall i\}\), \(Z(k)=\bigcap_\mu Z_\mu(k)\), \(\varphi(k)=\dim V_k-\dim Z(k)\), and for a family \(\nu\), \(W_\nu(k)=\bigcap_{\mu\ne\nu}Z_\mu(k)\) and \(m_\nu(k)=\dim W_\nu(k)-\dim Z(k)\). Let \(K\ge d\) and \(a+K+pd\le D\). (a) Joint monotonicity: \(\varphi(k)/N_n(k)\) is nonincreasing for \(0\le k\le K\). (b) There is \(q\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_p}\{g_{\nu,i}\}\) such that, with \(P_\nu\) replaced by \(P_\nu'=P_\nu(1-q^{p-1})\) and the other approximators kept, the new joint kernel \(Z'(K)\) satisfies \(\left(1-\tfrac1p\right)m_\nu(K-d)\le\dim Z'(K)-\dim Z(K)\le m_\nu(K)\); the upper bound holds for every refinement of family \(\nu\) alone. (c) There are states with \(\varphi(K)>0\) and \(m_\nu(K)=0\) for every \(\nu\): over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathcal C_3\) the degree-\(3\) vanishing ideal of \(\{0,1\}^2\), \(K=d=1\), \(P_\mu=1\), and the families \(\{1+y\}\), \(\{x+y\}\), \(\{y\}\), with \(\varphi(1)=3\). (d) A round refining every family at once, each with its own \(q_\nu\), need not gain either, even when \(\varphi(K-d)>0\): over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(n=4\), \(\mathcal C_4\) the degree-\(4\) vanishing ideal of \(\{0,1\}^4\), \(K=d=1\), all \(P_\mu=1\), and the six families \(\{x_i,x_j\}\), \(i<j\), with \(\varphi(1)=5\) and \(\varphi(0)=1\): for every choice of \(q_{ij}\in\operatorname{span}\{x_i,x_j\}\), the refined joint kernel in \(V_1\) is still \(0\). So no bound of the form gain \(\ge c\,\varphi(K-d)\) holds for one round with any \(c>0\), even one depending on \(S\), \(n\) or \(K\); dependence on \(S\) could enter only through an additive loss term.

Proof. (a) Pang's proof of his Lemma hilbert uses only that \(x^\alpha Z(k)\subseteq Z(k+|\alpha|)\) for \(k+|\alpha|\le K\), that the Boolean axioms lie in \(Z(k)\) for \(k\ge2\), and that \(Z(k)=Z(K)\cap V_k\); each holds for every \(Z_\mu\), by absorption of \(\mathcal C_D\) within degree \(a+d+K\le D\), and so for their intersection. (b) Restrict the maps \(M_i:f\mapsto([P_\nu g_{\nu,\alpha}g_{\nu,i}f])_\alpha\) of Pang's Lemma onestep to the \(\mathbb F_p\)-subspace \(W_\nu(K-d)\). Corollary descent's condition \(\ker_EM(\lambda)\subseteq\ker_EM(\lambda^p)\) on \(V_{K-d}\otimes E\) restricts to \(W_\nu(K-d)\otimes E\) by intersecting both sides, so Lemma generic-descent applies there and gives \(q=\sum_ic_ig_{\nu,i}\) with \(\operatorname{rank}\bigl(f\mapsto([P_\nu g_{\nu,\alpha}qf])_\alpha\bigr)\ge(1-\tfrac1p)\operatorname{rank}\overline M\) on \(W_\nu(K-d)\); as in Pang's proof, \(\ker\overline M=Z_\nu(K-d)\cap W_\nu(K-d)=Z(K-d)\), so \(\operatorname{rank}\overline M=m_\nu(K-d)\). The left side is \(\dim\bigl(qW_\nu(K-d)+Z_\nu(K)\bigr)/Z_\nu(K)\), which is at most \(\dim\bigl(qW_\nu(K-d)+Z(K)\bigr)/Z(K)\) since \(Z(K)\subseteq Z_\nu(K)\). Now \(qW_\nu(K-d)\subseteq Z'(K)\): for \(f\in W_\nu(K-d)\) and \(\mu\ne\nu\), \(P_\mu g_{\mu,i}qf\in\mathcal C_D\) by absorption (degree at most \(a+d+K\)), and \(P'_\nu g_{\nu,i}qf=P_\nu g_{\nu,i}f(q-q^p)\in\mathcal C_D\) as in Pang's proof (degree at most \(a+K+pd\)); and \(Z(K)\subseteq Z'(K)\) since \(Z_\nu(K)\subseteq Z'_\nu(K)\), again as there. This gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, \(Z'(K)=Z'_\nu(K)\cap W_\nu(K)\subseteq W_\nu(K)\), whatever \(P'_\nu\) is. (c) Membership in \(\mathcal C_3\) is vanishing on the square, and \(V_1\), of dimension \(3\), restricts bijectively onto the functions on any three of its four points. The families' supports are \(\{y=0\}\), \(\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\) and \(\{y=1\}\); \(Z_\mu(1)\) is the set of \(f\in V_1\) vanishing on the support, \(Z(1)=0\), so \(\varphi(1)=3\); each union of two supports has at least three points, so \(W_\nu(1)=0\) and \(m_\nu(1)=0\) for every \(\nu\). The hypotheses: \(a=0\), \(K=d=1\), \(a+K+pd=3\le D\). This \(\mathcal C_3\) is Pang's own degree-\(3\) space for the Boolean axioms alone, so (c) is a state of his construction at its start. (d) Membership in \(\mathcal C_4\) is vanishing on the cube, and every product involved has degree at most \(1+1+2=4\). Initially \(f\in V_1\) kills family \(\{i,j\}\) exactly when it vanishes where \(x_i=1\) or \(x_j=1\), so the joint kernel is the set of functions in \(V_1\) vanishing at every nonzero point, which is \(0\); \(\varphi(1)=\dim V_1/\mathcal C_{\le1}=5\), and \(\varphi(0)=1\). After the round, \(P'_{ij}=1+q_{ij}\), and \(f\) kills the family exactly when it vanishes where \(q_{ij}=0\) and \((x_i,x_j)\ne(0,0)\). Each of \(0,x_i,x_j,x_i+x_j\) vanishes at one of the nonzero patterns \((0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\) of \((x_i,x_j)\), so \(f\) must vanish at every point whose \((i,j)\)-pattern is that forbidden one. The set \(A\) of points where \(f\) may be nonzero therefore avoids one nonzero pattern on every pair of coordinates, so, read as a family of subsets of \([4]\), it shatters no pair, and by the Sauer–Shelah lemma \(|A|\le\binom40+\binom41=5\). A nonzero function of degree at most \(1\) on \(\mathbb F_2^4\) is nonzero on at least \(8\) points, so \(f=0\).

Hypotheses, each with its step. \(K\ge d\) places \(W_\nu(K-d)\) in degree at least \(0\); \(a+K+pd\le D\) is used for \(P'_\nu g q f\) and Corollary descent, \(a+K+d\le D\) for absorption by the other families; the Boolean axioms give Pang's \(g^p\equiv g\) and his Lemma hilbert's multilinearity; the prime field is Corollary descent's.

Degenerate cases. \(S=1\): \(W_\nu=V\), \(m_\nu=c_{P_\nu}\), and (b) is Pang's Lemma onestep with the upper bound added. A family whose errors already lie in \(\mathcal C_D\): \(Z_\nu=V\), \(m_\nu=0\), and refining it changes nothing. \(W_\nu(K)=Z(K)\): (b) says family \(\nu\)'s refinement cannot help, as in (c). \(1\in\mathcal C_D\): every kernel is everything, and \(\varphi=0\).

Nonvacuity. The example of (c) satisfies every hypothesis: \(p=2\), a degree-\(3\) pseudoideal containing the Boolean axioms on two variables, three families of degree \(1\), \(P_\mu=1\) of degree \(a=0\), \(K=d=1\), \(a+K+pd=3=D\); the state has \(\varphi(1)=3\), individual ranks \(2,2,2\), and all marginals \(0\). Refining one family there leaves the joint rank \(3\), by the upper bound in (b). A second step can gain, by direct computation outside (b)'s budget (after the first refinement \(a=1\) and \(a+K+pd=4>D\)): with \(q_1=1+y\), \(P'_1g_1=y(1+y)\) vanishes on the square, so \(Z'_1(1)=V_1\); refining family \(2\) with \(q_2=x+y\) then gives \(Z'_2(1)=V_1\), and the joint kernel becomes \(Z_3(1)\), a gain of \(1\), where (b)'s formula would give \(0\), since \(m_2(0)=0\), though (b) does not apply there. The example of (d) satisfies the same hypotheses with \(n=4\), \(D=4\), \(a=0\), \(a+K+pd=3\le4\), and \(S=6\). No parameter ledger applies: the witness verifies no budget of the goal.

Relations. (b) with \(S=1\) is Pang's Lemma onestep, and its lower bound is the same argument on a subspace domain; (a) is his Lemma hilbert for an intersection of kernels; (c) is a lattice phenomenon, the marginals of an intersection of kernels can all vanish, here because the three kernels are independent lines, spanned by \(y\), \(1+x+y\) and \(1+y\), so every pairwise intersection is \(0\); compare the non-distributivity recorded in prop:unit-flag-kernel-gap. (d) is consistent with Pang's condition, which fails there at \(t=1\) (\(S=6\), \(\eta=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac15\), \(S(1-\eta)>1\)); it shows only that no multiplicative round bound holds.

New mathematics. lem:marginal-refinement-gain: a refinement of one of Pang's families gains at least \((1-1/p)\) times its marginal rank one window down and at most its marginal rank at the window; joint normalized ranks are monotone; and all marginals can vanish while the joint rank is full; and one simultaneous round of six pair families on \(\{0,1\}^4\) gains nothing although the joint rank one window down is positive.
