What the primary theorem transfers

Bostan–Lecerf–Schost, Section 3, Proposition 2, works over a commutative ring and reverses a specified linear register program with unchanged register and instruction counts. Nonlinear precomputations remain outside that reversal. Its complete instruction definition and proof, plus the multiplication interface at the start of Section 4, were read. This is an algorithmic transposition statement, not a bound on the map's rank or on degrees hidden in the chosen coefficient ring.

First representation: scalar coordinates of the actual quotient

Fix the original degree window, coherent earlier values and one legal coefficient choice \(C\). The actual map is \[ T_C:[wU]\longrightarrow\bigoplus_a Q_{d_*}^{r_a},\qquad [wf]\longmapsto([wP_aR_{a,C}g_{a,i}f])_{a,i}, \] with the existing safe ceiling \(d_*=b+p_0+K+(h+1)\delta\) and a separate original replay charge. In chosen finite-field bases, a linear program for this map can be transposed by the primary theorem. Its matrix is precisely the canonical transpose already written in the later actual-batch entry.

The registers in this representation store scalar coordinates. The number of source formula objects does not count the dimensions of \([wU]\) and the quotient outputs. Even the identity map on an \(H\)-dimensional input can use its input registers as output registers with no arithmetic, or with one harmless multiplication by one, while its rank is \(H\). Thus short operation count alone cannot imply the strict deficit \(\operatorname{rank}T_C<H\). This is elementary linear algebra, not a circuit lower bound or a rejection of fast transposed computations.

The same distinction occurs in an actual complete block: on the unit input, choosing all rows zero gives the identity error multiplier, while selecting that input makes the error zero. This is the already recorded single-input choice control. Transposing the first map preserves its full rank; transposing the second preserves zero rank. The theorem does not choose between them. Treating coefficients and domain coordinates together as linear inputs is invalid in general: even a one-row prefix can create coefficient–domain products, and larger batches generally add higher coefficient degrees.

Second representation: polynomial or Boolean-ring registers

The primary theorem genuinely permits a commutative coefficient ring, so a register may hold an entire polynomial. That observation does not rescue a finite-field rank bound. Over a polynomial ring, multiplication by a fixed polynomial is a scalar operation; over the underlying coefficient field it acts on many polynomial coefficients. The two operation counts measure different objects.

Moreover, the truncated proper quotient is not supplied as a full module over the entire old Boolean algebra. Its multiplication maps move from \(Q_k\) to \(Q_{k+\delta}\) inside a paid cutoff. Passing first through the full polynomial ring and then to this quotient requires the corresponding filtered maps and their duals; those are exactly what the existing transport theorem retains. Promoting them to uncharged endomorphisms would add the full-module assumption rejected by the near-half import. That warning is about a stronger construction, not a claim that every filtered representation fails.

Canonical duals also avoid choosing polynomial densities. If densities are imposed instead, the adjoint-degree control remains: a degree-preserving rank-one evaluation map can have a prescribed adjoint density of full degree, even with nonsingular pairings and exact rank repair. The full proof was reread. It does not rule out a degree-affordable adjoint for a particular source, but the transposition theorem alone supplies none.

Third representation: coefficient sequences and middle products

The paper's univariate multiplication transpose is a middle-product computation in coefficient coordinates. To use such a representation for the relevant multivariate quotient, one must supply a degree-respecting encoding of its input and output spaces. A linear injection of an \(H\)-dimensional space into univariate polynomials of degree at most \(L\) requires \(L+1\ge H\). Treating that univariate length as the number of old variables, or as the source formula count, would discard the coordinate dimension.

Kronecker-style exponent encodings likewise do not identify univariate exponent with Boolean total degree. Factored representations can still be computationally useful, but their efficient arithmetic is not an error-rank contraction. No needed large numerical matrix-vector job is currently waiting for such an implementation, so no unrelated benchmark or helper is added.

The later actual-source test exhausts this interface

The later test already performed more than formal transposition: it used the actual input-generated spaces \[ U_a^+=\operatorname{span}\{g_{a,i_1}\cdots g_{a,i_j}f:0\le j\le h,\ f\in U\} \] and proved the sufficient bound \(2^{-h}\sum_a d_a<\dim[wU]\), with both the Frobenius buffer and the original full-row replay charge. Its missing estimate is on those generated images, not on how to reverse a given program. The newer full-rank-row factor and local/support-density constructions retain their own hypotheses; none makes that missing arbitrary-source estimate automatic.

This is therefore not a closure based on a single bad example or an unread source match. The primary proof and the actual source proof were read, and scalar-coordinate, ring-register and coefficient-sequence implementations were compared. All recover the same mathematical interface or an additional representation obligation, without a new quantitative source mechanism. Re-proving them under a different name would duplicate completed work.
