Lemma (rectangular minor content). Let \(R\) be any commutative ring with identity, \(M\in R^{m\times H}\), \(m,H\ge1\), and let \(U\) and \(V\) be generic matrices of sizes \(H\times m\) and \(H\times H\), with independent commuting indeterminates. Write \[ \det(xI_H-UMV)=x^H+\sum_{k=1}^H g_k(U,V)x^{H-k}. \] The ideal generated in \(R\) by the coefficients of \(g_k\) is exactly the ideal \(I_k(M)\) generated by all \(k\)-by-\(k\) minors of \(M\), with \(I_k(M)=0\) when \(k>m\). Every nonzero \(g_k\) has total parameter degree \(2k\). Over a discretely valued coefficient ring, its coefficient valuation is therefore the minimum valuation of those minors.

In particular let \(R=\mathbb F_q\) and \(r=\operatorname{rank}M\). Over any finite extension of size greater than \(H(H+2)\), there are specializations \(U_0,V_0\), with \(V_0\) invertible, such that \(B=U_0MV_0\) has rank \(r\), its characteristic coefficients \(g_k(U_0,V_0)\) are nonzero for every \(1\le k\le r\), and all later ones vanish. Its nonzero eigenvalues, counted algebraically over a splitting field, consequently number exactly \(r\). Moreover \[ \ker B=V_0^{-1}\ker M. \tag{1} \] The left compression loses no error constraint on the original domain after extending scalars.

Proof of contents. Expand a characteristic coefficient as the signed sum of principal minors, and apply Cauchy–Binet twice: \[ g_k=(-1)^k\sum_{\substack{S,J\subseteq[H],\ I\subseteq[m]\\|S|=|I|=|J|=k}} \det U_{S,I}\det M_{I,J}\det V_{J,S}. \tag{2} \] Thus every coefficient lies in \(I_k(M)\). Conversely fix increasing index sets \(I=(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\) and \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\). The monomial \[ u_{1,i_1}\cdots u_{k,i_k}v_{j_1,1}\cdots v_{j_k,k} \] occurs only with \(S=[k]\), those particular \(I,J\), and the identity permutation in both displayed generic determinants. Its coefficient is \((-1)^k\det M_{I,J}\). This proves reverse ideal containment over an arbitrary ring, including rings with nilpotents. Every term has degree \(2k\), giving the degree assertion. The valuation statement follows by taking the valuation of a finitely generated ideal. \(\square\)

Proof of specialization. Over the field, \(g_k\ne0\) precisely for \(k\le r\), by the ideal assertion and the rank/minor criterion. The polynomial \[ \det(V)\prod_{k=1}^r g_k(U,V) \] is nonzero and has total degree \(H+r(r+1)\le H(H+2)\); for \(r=0\), use only \(\det V\). A nonzero polynomial of degree smaller than the field size cannot vanish on the entire grid: induct on its variables using the univariate root bound. Hence a nonzero evaluation exists over the stated extension. For \(r>0\), its \(g_r\ne0\) forces \(\operatorname{rank}B\ge r\), while matrix multiplication gives the reverse bound. For \(r=0\), both maps are zero. Since \(V_0\) is invertible, \(U_0\) is injective on \(\operatorname{im}M\), proving (1). All characteristic coefficients after \(r\) vanish by rank. The polynomial has the form \(x^{H-r}(x^r+g_1x^{r-1}+\cdots+g_r)\), with nonzero constant term in its second factor, proving the eigenvalue count. No diagonalizability at nonzero eigenvalues is asserted. \(\square\)
