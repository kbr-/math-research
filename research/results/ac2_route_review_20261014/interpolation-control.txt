Use the actual graph and clause encoding
For \(r\ge2\), let the ladder have left vertices \(L_1,\ldots,L_r\), right vertices \(R_1,\ldots,R_r\), rail edges \(a_i:L_i\to L_{i+1}\) and \(b_i:R_i\to R_{i+1}\) for \(i<r\), and rungs \(x_i:L_i\to R_i\). Edge labels are in \(\mathbb F_3\). Charge every vertex zero except \(R_r\), whose charge is one. With absent exterior rails denoted by the constants \(a_0=a_r=b_0=b_r=0\), its divergence equations are \[ a_i-a_{i-1}+x_i=0,\qquad b_i-b_{i-1}-x_i=\mathbf1_{i=r}. \tag{1} \] The total charge is nonzero, so the full system is inconsistent.
Use the usual three one-hot Boolean indicators for each edge: one positive three-literal clause and its three pair exclusions. At a vertex forbid every incident-label tuple violating (1), using the corresponding negative indicator clause. Every vertex has degree at most three, so the CNF has \(O(r)\) constant-width clauses and variables. Let \(A_r\) contain the left vertex constraints and their private/shared domains, and \(B_r\) the right ones and domains. Rung indicators are shared; the two rail indicator families are private. Duplicate shared domain clauses may be retained or merged. Both induced sides are connected paths, and their common boundary has \(r\) edges.
Proposition (ladder interpolation gap). This CNF has resolution-with-weakening refutations of \(O(r)\) proof-lines and width at most fifteen, hence polynomial-size Frege refutations at some absolute constant formula depth in the recorded unbounded-fan-in Boolean presentation. For every fixed circuit depth, every Boolean separator of its displayed side projections requires superpolynomial size in \(r\) when its gates are AND, OR, NOT and MOD2. Therefore a polynomial-size depth-preserving interpolation theorem cannot cover all these genuine Tseitin instances. The statement does not concern the constant-expansion family used for the main goal.
Small refutations, proved through local clauses
Proof of the refutation claim. For \(i<r\), write \(C_i\) for the six binary clauses forbidding label pairs with \(a_i+b_i\ne0\). The first two vertex equations imply \(C_1\). At every internal column, the previous relation \(a_{i-1}+b_{i-1}=0\) and the two vertex equations imply \[ a_i+b_i=a_{i-1}+b_{i-1}=0. \] The final two vertex equations imply \(-(a_{r-1}+b_{r-1})=1\), contradicting \(C_{r-1}\).
Here semantic implication has a uniform bounded-resolution implementation. A local internal step uses at most five edge labels, hence fifteen Boolean indicators; endpoints use at most nine. If a CNF on at most \(k\) variables implies a clause \(C\), fix the variables of \(C\) to falsify it. For every assignment of the remaining variables select an input clause falsified by that full assignment, and weaken it to that assignment's full blocking clause, which contains \(C\). Resolve pairs differing in one remaining variable, then continue along the assignment tree, ending with \(C\). This uses width at most \(k\) and at most a constant multiple of \(2^k\) proof-lines. The empty-clause case is the same construction. No new hypotheses are added.
Apply that fixed construction to each of the six clauses in \(C_i\), and at the final contradiction. There are \(O(r)\) local steps and \(k\le15\), proving the bounds. Resolution and weakening on these constant-width clauses have fixed finite Frege simulations, so the formula depth remains bounded independently of \(r\). If one insists on a tautology proof rather than a refutation from CNF clauses, discharge the full CNF antecedent consistently, represented with unbounded-fan-in AND/OR (or its constant-depth De Morgan form); repeating its \(O(r)\) symbols yields at most a polynomial size increase and only constant additional depth. Ordinary variable-index bit encoding adds a logarithmic factor, not a superpolynomial change. \(\square\)
The exact shared-boundary promise
On valid shared rung labels, eliminating a connected rail gives \[ \exists a\ A_r(a,x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_i x_i=0, \qquad \exists b\ B_r(b,x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_i x_i=2. \tag{2} \] Indeed the left recurrence uniquely sets \(a_i=-\sum_{j\le i}x_j\), and the last left equation asks for total zero. On the right \(b_i=\sum_{j\le i}x_j\) for \(i<r\); its last charged equation asks for total minus one, namely two. These are two disjoint residue classes, not complementary subsets of the whole ternary cube.
Proof of the separator claim. Fix the separator convention \(I_r=1\) on the first projection in (2), and \(I_r=0\) on the second. Its behavior on residue one and on invalid one-hot strings is unconstrained. For Boolean inputs \(u_1,\ldots,u_{r-1}\), encode each as a ternary rung label zero or one, using indicators \((1-u_i,u_i,0)\). Form two copies of \(I_r\), with the last rung fixed respectively to labels zero and two. Then \[ J(u)=I_r(u,0)\wedge\neg I_r(u,2) \tag{3} \] is exactly the indicator of \(\sum u_i=0\pmod3\). For residue zero the two outputs are forced to one and zero. For residue one the second is forced to one; for residue two the first is forced to zero. Thus the unconstrained residue never creates an error.
Composition (3) increases size by at most a constant factor plus \(O(r)\), and depth by an absolute constant. Smolensky, Theorem 2, forbids polynomial-size fixed-depth AND/OR/NOT/MOD2 circuits for MOD3. The source uses the complementary convention for divisibility, which one NOT changes. Therefore a polynomial-size fixed-depth separator family would contradict that theorem. This is an imported circuit lower bound, not an independent proof of it or a new Frege lower bound. \(\square\)

The natural boundary annotations are the three prefix-charge bits \(p_i(s)\), with \(p_0(s)=\mathbf1_{s=0}\), and \[ p_i(s)=\bigvee_{a\in\mathbb F_3}\bigl(p_{i-1}(s-a)\wedge[x_i=a]\bigr). \tag{4} \] This explicitly composes the local vertex/counter step with the shared rung. Each update is constant size and adds a constant number of Boolean layers. The resulting circuit DAG has linear size but depth \(O(r)\), even though every resolution line and its Frege simulation has constant formula depth. The ladder has vanishing expansion. Taking the first \(t=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\) columns gives \(2t\) vertices and just two outgoing rail edges, so its edge expansion is at most \(1/t\).
