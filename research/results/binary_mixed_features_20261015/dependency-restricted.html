Restricted block mixture exclusion

Fix constants \[0<\beta<\alpha<1,\qquad 0\le\gamma<\beta,\qquad \kappa\ge0, \qquad \alpha+\beta+2\kappa<1.\tag{1}\] Use the recorded growing graph family with \(\Delta=\lceil n^\alpha\rceil\), maximum degree at most \(2\Delta\), edge expansion at least \(\Delta/4\), \(n\Delta/8\le m\le n\Delta\), nonzero total ternary charge and canonical CNF length \(N\) satisfying \(\log N=\Theta(n^\alpha)\). The graph is fixed independently of the proposed proof-size exponent. Put \[h=\lceil n^{\alpha+\kappa}\log n\rceil, \qquad B=\lceil h^2\log n\rceil.\tag{2}\] For fixed \(A>0\), consider at most \(M\le N^A\) complete old-affine bottom blocks and at most \(M\) complete parents, all of original accuracy \(h\). Every proper bottom span has rank at most \(h\); zero and unit spans are allowed. Parents are old-affine plus constant binary mixtures of genuine shared bottom products, with no raw bottom-coefficient readers or parent dependencies. All companions and coefficient fields are retained.

Assign every bottom its full affine basis in constant rows, with the usual zero/unit cases. Its product is now its exact Boolean zero-flat indicator, independent of the chosen basis. For each parent \(a\), require that the resulting inputs have local three-state function values \[g_{a,v}=\ell_{a,v}+\sum_j F_{a,v,j}(x_{S_{a,j}},y_{S_{a,j}}),\tag{3}\] where \(\ell_{a,v}\) is old-affine, the nonempty edge sets \(S_{a,j}\) are disjoint within that parent, and \[|S_{a,j}|\le\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{span}_v\{F_{a,v,j}\}\le\lfloor n^\kappa\rfloor.\tag{4}\] The functions are otherwise arbitrary. Their dimension is in the full local function algebra on that block, not in the inconsistent charged quotient. There is no disjointness requirement between different parents. The original actual specialized input polynomials still have degree at most \(h\); semantic decompositions do not replace original source degrees.

Here \(M\ge1\) is an integer upper bound; empty inventories use \(M=1\).

Corollary. For all sufficiently large \(n\), depending on the fixed constants and \(A\), these complete sources have no ordinary degree-\(B\) PC or NS refutation over \(\mathbb F_2\). One restriction controls every parent

Retain each edge independently with probability \(p=n^{-\beta}\). The hard-restriction lemma applies with \(\tau=p\Delta\), eventually \(\tau\ge128\log(2n)\). With probability \(1-o(1)\), the live graph \(H\) has maximum degree at most \(4\tau\), expansion at least \(\tau/8\), and a proper binary charged space through \[D_H=\lfloor \tau n/128\rfloor.\tag{5}\] This event works for every assignment of the fixed labels, because their contributions to total charge cancel.

Fix an integer \(L\ge1\) such that \((\beta-\gamma)(L+1)>3\). A block is bad if more than \(L\) of its edges remain live. Its bad-event probability is at most \[\binom{|S|}{L+1}p^{L+1}\le n^{-(\beta-\gamma)(L+1)},\] with probability zero when \(|S|\le L\). There are at most \(m\le2n^{1+\alpha}\) nonempty blocks in one parent. Their bad events are independent, since their edge sets are disjoint. Consequently their count \(X_a\) has mean \(\mu_a\le n^{\alpha-1}\) eventually.

Choose a constant \(C_M\) with \(\log M\le C_M n^\alpha\), and set \[r=\left\lceil\frac{4(C_M+1)n^\alpha}{\log n}\right\rceil.\tag{6}\] The factorial-moment bound for a sum of independent Bernoulli variables gives \[\Pr(X_a\ge r)\le\frac{\mu_a^r}{r!}\le(e\mu_a/r)^r \le n^{-r/2}\le e^{-2(C_M+1)n^\alpha}\tag{7}\] for large \(n\); if \(r\) exceeds the block count the probability is zero. Union-bound over all parents to get failure at most \(e^{-(C_M+2)n^\alpha}\). No independence between parents, or between their event and the hard graph event, is assumed. Their two exceptional probabilities sum to less than one, so choose one live set satisfying both. Any fixed-label assignment now suffices for the following degree argument.

Restriction of a local function to at most \(L\) live edges has a local normal representative of degree at most \(L\). On each bad block, specialization cannot increase the dimension in (4). Thus, for each parent, the image of its actual input span modulo old degree-at-most-\(L\) local polynomials has dimension at most \[R_*=r\lfloor n^\kappa\rfloor=O_A(n^{\alpha+\kappa}/\log n).\tag{8}\] This uses linearity of (3); it would not follow for arbitrary products between different block functions. All small blocks contribute only to the low-degree part.

Legal rows, one denominator and the original replay

Put \(t=\lceil\eta_0^{-1}\log(2\max(1,M))\rceil=O_A(n^\alpha)\).

Normalize each actual parent input by the local Boolean and pair-exclusion reductions, obtaining polynomials \(\bar g_{a,v}\) of degree at most \(h\). Their local functions and constant input-span relations are unchanged; each difference from the actual polynomial has a local certificate through degree at most \(h\). These are identities between the unique local normal polynomials, not an uncharged reduction modulo the full old ideal.

Use the \(q_{a,j}\)'s as a deterministic prefix. Their product \(Q_a=\prod_j(1+q_{a,j})\) kills the high directions by Booleanity through \((R_*+1)h\). The needed reserve is \[K+(R_*+1)h+tL\le D_H.\tag{10}\] Indeed \((R_*+1)h=O_A(n^{2\alpha+2\kappa})+O(h)\), while \(tL=O_A(n^\alpha)\), and (1) gives \(2\alpha+2\kappa<1+\alpha-\beta\). Overlapping block mixtures

The same source conclusion extends to overlapping blocks with the following explicit change of parameters. Fix \(\nu\ge0\), require each old edge to belong to at most \(k=\lfloor n^\nu\rfloor\) blocks of any one parent, keep \(\gamma<\beta<\alpha\) and the local dimension bound, and replace (1)–(2) by \[\alpha+\beta+2\kappa+2\nu<1,\qquad h'=\lceil n^{\alpha+\kappa+\nu}\log n\rceil, \qquad B'=\lceil(h')^2\log n\rceil.\tag{11}\] All source blocks must have this original accuracy \(h'\), and all proper bottom ranks are at most \(h'\). This is a different declared source budget, not extra rows added to a source of accuracy \(h\).

Product-measure tail. For independent retention bits with product law \(\mu\), let \(Y_j\) indicate the bad event for block \(S_j\), \(X=\sum_jY_j\), and \(\bar\mu=\mathbb E_\mu X\). If each bit is read by at most \(k\) indicators, then for \(r\ge\bar\mu>0\), \[\Pr(X\ge r)\le(e\bar\mu/r)^{r/k}.\tag{12}\] Here is a complete entropy proof of the needed generalized Hölder estimate. Let \(Z\) denote the full retention vector. For any law \(\lambda\) on its product space, append singleton sets until every coordinate occurs exactly \(k\) times. The entropy chain rule and the fact that conditioning reduces entropy give \(\sum_j H_\lambda(Z_{S_j})\ge kH_\lambda(Z)\) for this enlarged cover. Since \(\mu\) is a product, its cross-entropy terms sum exactly \(k\) times; hence \(\sum_j D(\lambda_{S_j}\Vert\mu_{S_j})\le kD(\lambda\Vert\mu)\). Dropping the appended nonnegative divergences preserves this inequality for the original sets.

For positive functions \(f_j\) on \(S_j\), apply nonnegativity of relative entropy against the tilted marginal \(f_j^k\mu_{S_j}/\mathbb E_\mu f_j^k\) to obtain \[k\mathbb E_\lambda\log f_j \le\log\mathbb E_\mu f_j^k+D(\lambda_{S_j}\Vert\mu_{S_j}).\] Now take \(\lambda\) proportional to \(\mu\prod_jf_j\), whose normalizer is \(Z\). Its defining identity gives \(\log Z=\sum_j\mathbb E_\lambda\log f_j-D(\lambda\Vert\mu)\). Summing the preceding inequalities proves \(Z\le\prod_j(\mathbb E_\mu f_j^k)^{1/k}\). Set \(f_j=e^{sY_j}\) to get \[\mathbb E e^{sX}\le\exp\bigl(\bar\mu(e^{ks}-1)/k\bigr).\] Exponential Markov with \(e^{ks}=r/\bar\mu\) gives (12). If \(\bar\mu=0\), then \(X=0\) almost surely; the tail is zero for \(r>0\), and is one at \(r=0\). This is an ordinary probability argument about retention bits, not a finite-field rank inequality.

Simultaneous application. Nonempty blocks now number at most \(km\). Choose fixed \(L\) with \((\beta-\gamma)(L+1)>3+\nu\); the same one-block estimate gives \(\bar\mu\le n^{\alpha-1}\) eventually. Replace (6) by \[r=\left\lceil\frac{4k(C_M+1)n^\alpha}{\log n}\right\rceil. \] Then \(e\bar\mu/r\le n^{-1/2}\), so (12) gives the same \(e^{-2(C_M+1)n^\alpha}\) bound and simultaneous hard event. The high-degree quotient dimension is at most \(R_*=r\lfloor n^\kappa\rfloor=O_A(n^{\alpha+\kappa+\nu}/\log n)\). Its prefix and the \(t=O_A(n^\alpha)\) low-degree rows fit \(h'\). Their reserve is \[K+(R_*+1)h'+tL=K+O_A(n^{2\alpha+2\kappa+2\nu})+O(h')+O_A(n^\alpha)<D_H, \] by (11), and \(K+B'<D_H\). Normalize and compare actual inputs exactly as above, now through \(K+(h'+1)h'\le K+B'\); original companions retain \(2(h')^2+3h'\). Thus the same one global constant map, surviving weight and PC/NS replay prove the bounded-overlap extension in full.

A positive-overlap witness uses a cyclic ordering of the old edges and all intervals of length exactly \(\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor\): each edge belongs to exactly that many intervals. Take \(\nu=\gamma=1/32,\kappa=1/32,\alpha=1/4,\beta=1/8\), and arbitrary local function spaces of dimension at most \(n^{1/32}\). These are genuinely overlapping blocks with the strict budget in (11). This concerns their placement on an already expanding old graph, not a cyclic old constraint graph. Repeating one retention event \(k\) times gives \(X=kY\), showing why an independence bound with exponent \(r\) cannot replace the paid \(r/k\) in (12).

Their table descriptions cost \(\exp(O(n^{1/16}))=\exp(o(n^{1/4}))\), so an unbounded such source fits the original encoding budget. Different parents may use different partitions and functions.
