Product tails retain full relative rank

Proposition (product tail rank). For every \(1\le m\le N\), there are self-adjoint product projections \(P,Q\) on \((\mathbb F_{16}^2)^{\otimes N}\), with the standard Hermitian form for \(\bar z=z^4\), such that the external Hamming-tail projection \(H_{\ge r}\) on the first \(m\) coordinates satisfies \[ \operatorname{rank}(H_{\ge r}QP)=\operatorname{rank}P=2^{N-m} \quad(1\le r\le m). \] At \(r=m\), writing \(A=H_{\ge m}QP\), one even has \(AA^\dagger=H_{\ge m}\), hence no rank defect.

Proof. Choose \(\zeta\in\mathbb F_{16}\) of order fifteen and put \(v=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}\), \(w=(\zeta,\zeta^2)^{\mathsf T}\). With \(\omega=\zeta^5\), \[ w^\dagger w=\zeta^5+\zeta^{10}=\omega+\omega^2=1. \] Thus \(P_0=vv^\dagger\) and \(Q_0=ww^\dagger\) are rank-one self-adjoint idempotents. Set \(P=P_0^{\otimes m}\otimes I\) and \(Q=Q_0^{\otimes m}\otimes I\), with the identity on the remaining \(N-m\) factors. On the counted factors, \(QP\) kills every basis vector except \(0^m\), and its image on that vector has a nonzero coefficient at every Boolean string. The \(1^m\) coefficient is \(\zeta^{6m}\), since each local coefficient is \(\zeta^2\bar\zeta\). Every indicated tail retains this nonzero vector, proving its rank is one on the counted factors and \(2^{N-m}\) after the identity tensor factor. At the extreme tail, \[ A=\zeta^{6m}|1^m\rangle\langle0^m|\otimes I, \qquad \zeta^{6m}\overline{\zeta^{6m}}=\zeta^{30m}=1, \] which proves the final identity. \(\square\)

The count labels here are integers external to the field; no reduction of Hamming weight modulo two is used. This tests the finite-field product-projection analogue of family 274 already with \(U=I\). For fixed \(0<s<t<1\), choose \(m=\lceil N^t\rceil\): the relative tail rank is one, rather than tending to zero like \(\exp(-N^s)\). It is not a binary compiler realization, a counterexample to the original complex norm theorem, or an obstruction to all degree-restricted source classes. Normalizing by the ambient \(2^N\) instead would conceal the failure by changing the surviving-domain denominator.

The reverse kernel need not be PC-closed

Proposition (adjoint kernel gap). In the one-bit Boolean algebra over a finite characteristic-two extension \(\mathbb L\), let \(\mu(1)=1\) and require the moment pairing \(\mu(fg)\) to be nonsingular. For the inclusion \(T:\mathbb L1\to\mathbb L[x]/(x^2-x)\), its moment adjoint \(T^\dagger f=\mu(f)1\) satisfies \(T^\dagger T=I\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(TT^\dagger)=\operatorname{rank}T=1\), but \(\ker T^\dagger\) fails bounded multiplication closure through degree two. This holds for every such normalized nonsingular moment.

Proof. Write \(a=\mu(x)\). In the basis \(1,x\), the Gram matrix and determinant are \[ G=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det G=a+a^2. \] Thus nonsingularity is exactly \(a\notin\{0,1\}\). The adjoint formula follows by pairing against constants. Normalization gives \(T^\dagger T=I\), and \(TT^\dagger\) is a nonzero projection onto constants. But \(q=x+a\) satisfies \[ T^\dagger q=0,\qquad T^\dagger(xq)=(a+a^2)1\ne0. \] The first polynomial has degree one and its multiplied representative has degree two, so this is a legal bounded multiplication failure, not a high-degree extrapolation. \(\square\)

This fits the common-adjoint lemma with the proper Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, \(D\ge4\), \(S=Q_0\), actual multiplier \(E=1\), and the loose degree ceiling \(e=2\), hence \(L=2\). All its hypotheses hold. Finiteness is retained only to match that lemma's analysis fields; the displayed proof works over any characteristic-two field admitting such a moment. The control is satisfiable and uses an analysis-field kernel; \(q\) may have nonbinary coefficients. No claim is made that its intersection with binary polynomials has the same closure failure. The result prevents treating an arbitrary such adjoint as another multiplication-error map on its whole output domain. It does not require every useful analysis map to have an ideal kernel. A different reverse class with a separately proved inequality remains possible.
