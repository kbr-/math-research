A local filter and its simultaneous requirement

Write one comparison as \(R=C-AB\), where \(A,B,C\) are the two subgroup and union falsity products. If a legal new row \(q\) is available in both the first subgroup and union input spans, and both have an unused row slot, replacing \(A,C\) by \(A(1-q),C(1-q)\) and leaving \(B\) unchanged gives exactly \(R'=(1-q)R\). This is a literal identity over any field. It needs no pairwise exclusivity, but does not yet define an update to all other uses of these groups.

For simultaneous binary use, take a finite group registry and declared comparisons \(U=I\cup J\). Choose masks \(\epsilon_G\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\epsilon_U=\max(\epsilon_I,\epsilon_J)\) on every declared comparison. Let a single binary polynomial \(q\), degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), be a legal row in every active group's input span, with an unused slot there. Set \(P'_G=P_G(1-q)^{\epsilon_G}\). With all current value degrees at most \(L\), every comparison satisfies \[R'_{I,J}\equiv (1-q)^{\epsilon_U}R_{I,J}\pmod{J_D},\] through actual degree \(2L+2\delta\), provided \(q^2-q\in J_D\) with the required bounded multiplications. Indeed the only unequal exponents occur when both children are active: \((1-q)^2-(1-q)=q^2-q\). After weight degree \(b\) and multiplier degree \(K\), reserve \(b+K+2L+2\delta\le D\). Binary old polynomials have the needed Boolean certificate through \(2\delta\). Original source row costs remain separate and cannot be reset to these actual degrees.

This is a restricted mechanism. The same polynomial must be legal in every active input span, not just in the union; row slots and all fixed boundary choices must also be compatible. A mask does not prove those hypotheses. The identity is valid for overlaps because it uses the group products directly, not the incorrect additive union rule.

The boundary charge preserves the joint rank

Corollary (boundary filter rank). Over any field, let \(J\subseteq V_D\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal, let \(\deg w\le b\), \(\deg q\le\delta\), and let errors \(E_1,\ldots,E_s\) have degree at most \(e\). Let \(U\subseteq V_K\) be linear, choose arbitrary masks \(\eta_i\in\{0,1\}\), and put \(E'_i=(1-q)^{\eta_i}E_i\). If \(b+K+e+\delta\le D\), the maps \[f\longmapsto([wqf],([wE'_if])_i),\qquad f\longmapsto([wqf],([wE_if])_i)\] have exactly the same kernel on \(U\), and hence equal ranks. In particular that rank is at least the rank of \(f\mapsto([wE_if])_i\). Neither Booleanity, survival of \(w\), nor normalization is required.

Proof. Once \(wqf\in J\), bounded absorption gives \(wqE_i f\in J\) through the displayed ceiling. Thus \([wE'_if]=[wE_if]\) for every active mask, and inactive components are identical literally. The two kernel conditions are equivalent. Rank-nullity on the same domain proves the assertion. The same argument proves that if \(wq\in J\), the filtered and original error maps themselves are identical on \(U\). \(\square\)

If a fixed unit boundary \(P_T=1\) is active, its updated value is \(1-q\), so its discrepancy from one is \(-q\). Retaining that discrepancy as a component invokes the corollary. Demanding instead that it vanish under the common weight, \(wq\in J\), makes the proposed filter neutral on all weighted errors. The comparison congruences above fit the larger \(b+K+2L+2\delta\) reserve; any prior error components can be retained in both maps without affecting the kernel argument. This uses the same quotient and multiplier domain throughout.

Alternatives and limits

Keep the unit mask zero. On a registry generated by declared binary unions from its leaves to that unit node, \(\epsilon_T=0\) forces both child masks zero at every such comparison, recursively through that ancestry. This rejects a nontrivial common-factor mask on that connected union cone. It is not a claim that every proof registry has this form or that every useful mask must touch its unit node. Disconnected groups can still change.

Override the unit value. Holding a parent fixed while filtering its children introduces a residual. For a Boolean factor \(F=1-q\), if at least one child is active and the parent is not, then \(R'=C-FAB=FR+qC\) modulo the same Boolean certificate. When \(C=1\), the residual is exactly \(q\). At other cuts the residual is \(qC\), whose rank need not equal that of \(q\). We have no bound on the joint collection of these cut residuals. They cannot be silently dropped, but the corollary does not rule out a gain after retaining them.

Use different filters or change the weight. Different group filters avoid the common-mask premise but create additional mismatch terms. They are outside the proved rank identity unless their own discrepancies are included. A new weight can also help, as the earlier literal-prefix repair shows; the corollary only compares maps at one fixed weight. It therefore supplies no impossibility of source normalization. The previously proved rank-retaining selection theorem remains available once an affordable error estimate is supplied.

Control. In the Boolean quotient on one variable, take \(w=1,q=x,E=x,U=V_0\). The filtered error \((1-x)x\) is zero, but the added boundary error \(x\) has rank one, exactly the original error rank. Taking \(w=1-x\) makes the boundary vanish and the original weighted error was already zero. These are exact algebraic controls of the boundary accounting, not hard instances or numerical evidence.
