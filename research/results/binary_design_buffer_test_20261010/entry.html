The degree-two system

Fix \(u\ge2\). Use five variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_5\) and, for each \(s\in[ u ]\), ten variables \(y^{(s)}_{ab}\), \(1\le a<b\le5\). There are \(v=5+10u\) old variables. Let \(X_s\) be the vector space spanned by the fifty cross monomials \(x_i y^{(s)}_{ab}\). Define a surjective linear map \[ \phi_s:X_s\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^5,\qquad \phi_s(x_i y^{(s)}_{ab})= \begin{cases}e_b&i=a,\\e_a&i=b,\\0&i\notin\{a,b\}.\end{cases} \tag{1} \] Let \(\Gamma_u\) consist of all Boolean equations and a basis of each \(\ker\phi_s\), interpreted as homogeneous quadratic polynomials. It has the all-zero solution. Put \(J_2=C_2(\Gamma_u)\) and \(Q_2=V_2/J_2\).

The degree-two leading parts of the Boolean equations use square monomials, while the additional equations use only the separate cross spaces. Thus their linear span has no nonzero polynomial of degree at most one. No nonzero variable-multiplication step is available inside degree two, so \(J_2\) is exactly that span. In particular it is a genuine PC-closed proper space, not a static span being assumed closed. It has no linear relations. Modulo it, cross block \(s\) is a five-dimensional space with basis classes \(t^{(s)}_b\) corresponding to \(e_b\).

Take \(A=1\), inputs \(g_i=x_i\), and the full affine domain \(U=V_1\). Let \[ T:V_1\to Q_2^5,\quad f\mapsto([x_i f])_i, \qquad W=T(V_1),\quad c=\dim W. \] For \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_2^5\), let \(R_\alpha:W\to Q_2\) sum coordinates with coefficients \(\alpha\). These are literal legal input rows. All required input equations \(x_i^2+x_i\) are present. The available window is \(a=0,L=1,\delta=1,D=2\), one degree short of Frobenius shortening.

Every nonzero row has the same insufficient loss

Proposition (shortening buffer gap). For this family, \[ c=10u+6,\qquad \operatorname{rank}R_\alpha=4u+5\quad(\alpha\ne0), \qquad \dim\ker R_\alpha=6u+1\quad(\alpha\ne0). \tag{2} \] The zero row has kernel dimension \(c\). Consequently \[ \mathbb E_{\alpha\in\mathbb F_2^5}\dim\ker R_\alpha =\frac{196u+37}{32} >\frac c2, \qquad \mathbb E_{\alpha\ne0}\dim\ker R_\alpha =6u+1>\frac{15}{31}c. \tag{3} \] At the degree-three closure \(J_3=C_3(\Gamma_u)\), the same tuple on \(V_1\) instead has image dimension six, every nonzero row has rank five and kernel dimension one, and the uniform mean kernel dimension is \(37/32\). Thus the valid larger-window theorem is not contradicted.

Proof at degree two. Split \(V_1=\operatorname{span}(1,x_1,\ldots,x_5)\oplus\bigoplus_s\operatorname{span}(y^{(s)}_{ab})\). Images of these summands have disjoint quotient supports: the first lies in the pure \(x\) Boolean polynomials, and the others in the independent cross spaces from (1). On one \(y\)-summand, \(T\) identifies its ten coefficients with a symmetric zero-diagonal five-by-five matrix, with row \(i\) giving the coefficients of \(t^{(s)}_b\). This is injective. On the first six-dimensional summand, \(T\) is also injective: if \(x_i(c_0+\sum_j c_jx_j)=0\) for every \(i\), the pure squarefree \(x_i x_j\) coefficients force every \(c_j=0\), then the linear coefficients force \(c_0=0\). This proves \(c=10u+6\).

For nonzero \(\alpha\), contraction of the alternating matrix summand maps \(S\) to \(S\alpha\). Its image is exactly \(\alpha^\perp\), of dimension four. Containment follows from \(\alpha^{\mathsf T}S\alpha=0\). For the reverse containment choose \(j\) with \(\alpha_j=1\); for \(z\in\alpha^\perp\), the alternating matrix \(ze_j^{\mathsf T}+e_jz^{\mathsf T}\) sends \(\alpha\) to \(z\). On the first summand, contraction is multiplication by the nonzero linear form \(q_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_i x_i\) in the Boolean algebra. Its affine kernel is exactly \(\langle1+q_\alpha\rangle\): an invertible binary affine change making \(q_\alpha\) one coordinate proves this by restriction to its one-fibre. Thus its rank is five. The independent supports give rank \(4u+5\), proving (2).

Averaging the zero row and the 31 nonzero rows gives (3). The two strict differences are \((36u-59)/32\) and \((36u-59)/31\), respectively, positive for every \(u\ge2\). The independent-row factor \(15/31\) is exactly \(\beta_2(5,1)\).

Proof of the degree-three comparison. Represent \(t^{(s)}_b\) by any \(x_a y^{(s)}_{ab}\) with \(a\ne b\); all such representatives agree through degree two by (1), with unordered subscripts understood. Choose distinct \(a,c\ne b\). The relations \[ x_a y^{(s)}_{ab}=t^{(s)}_b, \quad x_c y^{(s)}_{cb}=t^{(s)}_b, \quad x_a y^{(s)}_{cb}=0 \] are degree-two consequences. Multiplying the first by \(x_a\) and reducing its square gives \(x_a t^{(s)}_b=t^{(s)}_b\) through degree three. Multiplying the other two by \(x_a\) and \(x_c\) gives \(x_a t^{(s)}_b=0\) through the same degree. Hence every \(t^{(s)}_b\), and then every mixed \(x_i y^{(s)}_{ab}\), lies in \(J_3\).

All \(y\)-summands therefore disappear from the tuple image. No extra relation among pure \(x\) Boolean functions can appear: setting every \(y=0\) leaves arbitrary Boolean \(x\) as a solution of \(\Gamma_u\). The remaining six-dimensional affine calculation is unchanged, giving image dimension six, nonzero-row rank five and the claimed mean. \(\square\)

What this decides for the source route

The counterexample uses the full affine domain, so the lost hypothesis cannot be blamed only on an artificially selected multiplier subspace. It also contains every old Boolean equation and is an actual multiplication image. The failure is at the exact point where multiplying an already derived relation by another input is unavailable in the old cutoff. At degree three those extra products derive the relations exhibited above. Extending the cutoff while pretending that its lower-degree quotient stays unchanged would miss that fall.

It does not prove that the design method gains a useful source estimate at the smaller window. The direct prefix theorem still requires its actual flag profile. In this control a one-row source with linear inputs has original companion degree three, so a degree-two old window cannot pay its full replay. More generally, for a fresh block with input degree at most \(\delta\), accuracy \(h\), and original companion ceiling \(B\ge h(\delta+1)+\delta\), the replay reserve \(b+K+B\le D\) already covers the shortening reserve \(b+K+(h+2)\delta\le D\) whenever \(h\ge\delta\). Their difference is \(h-\delta\). This is a comparison of these stated ceilings, not an assertion about arbitrary earlier polynomial substitutions or smaller individual companion degrees.

Thus the smaller-buffer opportunity relevant to a complete source must survive the original ledger, for example outside that \(h\ge\delta\) regime. This family does not supply it. For the exact input-word enlargement from \(U_0=V_0\) and these five inputs, the generated domain is \(\operatorname{span}(1,x_1,\ldots,x_5)\), not all of \(V_1\); its tuple dimension is only six. Our counterexample concerns the full affine enlargement and the general arbitrary-domain shortening assertion, not a failed generated-word theorem in that special source.
