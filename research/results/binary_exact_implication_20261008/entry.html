The actual input list

The source convention flattens an OR cluster into maximal non-OR children. A union and its proper OR subgroups may be at the same ENS level. In particular, for an implication whose conclusion is an OR group, the conclusion product is generally not one of the union's input coordinates. The implication reads the conclusion's flattened children instead. Selecting a row equal to the complement of that whole conclusion product is therefore not a legal constant coefficient choice merely because it would implement the desired two-input Boolean operation.

The existing MP module supplies the relation \(c-(1-a)b\) by a costed OR comparison; it does not assert that its two subgroup values are raw inputs. The unbalanced compiler reuses the resulting bounded PC proof, not an exact polynomial identity. Changing to a fresh nested OR block changes the input list, sharing and source level and needs a separate compiler theorem.

A local reserve can repair one interface. If the parent input list contains a coordinate equal to \(a\) and the conclusion's actual inputs \(g_i\), and the conclusion product already uses at most \(h-1\) active rows, selecting \(a\) in an additional row and copying those active rows gives \((1-a)b\). All unused rows are zero. The construction is legal on this specified list, but a global supply of such reserves is not provided by fixed structural depth. The following test develops that issue beyond a single missing input slot.

A complete prefix test

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\) be independent old Boolean variables, \(m\ge2\). Use a fixed order of non-OR children with truth values \(x_j\), and let \(B_i\) be the OR of the first \(i\) children. Write \(P_1=1-x_1\); for \(i\ge2\), its genuine accuracy-\(h\) product is \[ P_i=\prod_{r=1}^h\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^i c_{i,r,j}x_j\right),\qquad c_{i,r,j}\in\mathbb F_2. \] All these groups have the same ENS level. They are keyed once each, and \(B_{i-1}\) is the proper subgroup used in the comparison at \(B_i\). The comparisons in question are \[ P_i=(1-x_i)P_{i-1}\qquad(2\le i\le m). \] They are exactly the OR-interface form of an MP semantic comparison with antecedent \(\neg x_i\), conclusion \(B_{i-1}\), and implication obtained by adding its negated antecedent to that subgroup. Literal double negations, if retained as syntax, have truth value \(x_i\). This tests legal flattened lists and coherent subgroup reuse; it does not assert a complete proof from a particular hard CNF. In particular no wide initial clause is being assigned at an accuracy below its required width, and these interior groups are not a final-goal block forced to one.

Corollary (literal prefix capacity). For every \(m\ge2,h\ge1\): (i) one simultaneous constant coefficient assignment realizes all displayed comparisons, even just as Boolean functions, if and only if \(m\le h\); (ii) replacing these values by exact Boolean-only costed profiles within their original genuine product weights \(2h\) is possible if and only if \(m\le2h\); (iii) for every \(m,h\), the legal common choice \(P_i=1-x_1\) has all these comparisons and all its companions annihilated by the surviving weight \(W=x_1\). On degree-at-most-\(K\) multipliers in the Boolean function algebra, \[ h_W(K)=N_{m-1}(K)\ge\tfrac12N_m(K),\qquad N_s(K)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(s,K)}\binom{s}{j}. \] The third assertion is a Boolean calibration, not the full old inconsistent system.

Proof of (i). Induction on the prescribed comparisons forces \(P_i=\prod_{j\le i}(1-x_j)\) as a Boolean function. Its unique multilinear representative has degree \(i\). After constant coefficient substitution, an accuracy-\(h\) product of affine forms has degree at most \(h\), and Boolean reduction never raises degree. Hence \(m\le h\) is necessary. When it holds, assign one coordinate row to each \(x_j\) in every group, with all remaining rows zero. The resulting products are exactly the required ones, simultaneously on the coherent prefix registry. \(\square\)

Proof of (ii). The same terminal value has degree \(m\), so every profile has cost at least \(m\). Conversely \(v_i=\prod_{j\le i}(1-x_j)\) has prefixes \(U_j=\prod_{k<j}(1-x_k)\) and literal identity \(1-v_i=\sum_{j\le i}U_jx_j\). Its companions have the Boolean certificates \[ x_jv_i=-(x_j^2-x_j)\prod_{k\le i,\ k\ne j}(1-x_k) \] through degree \(i+1\); Booleanity has the product-profile certificate through \(2i\). Thus its exact profile cost is \(i\), agreeing with the previously proved free-parameter bound in the empty-base case. The original affine-input genuine block has weight \(h(1+1)=2h\). Therefore all profiles fit exactly when \(m\le2h\). These are the Boolean profiles required by the existing product-closure construction. No transfer of a whole fixed-boundary source is inferred here; that would additionally require the virtual-fold theorem's registry and carried-witness hypotheses. Profile reconstruction is not a constant map on the original coefficient variables when \(h<m\le2h\). \(\square\)

Proof of (iii). Every prefix contains the first coordinate. Select it in the first row of every genuine block and zero every other row. Then \(P_i=1-x_1\), consistently with the literal value \(P_1\), and the discrepancy at the \(i\)-th comparison is \(x_i(1-x_1)\). Multiplication by \(W\) gives \[ W\bigl(P_i-(1-x_i)P_{i-1}\bigr) =-x_i(x_1^2-x_1),\qquad W x_jP_i=-x_j(x_1^2-x_1). \] These certificates have degree at most three, or \(K+3\) after a degree-\(K\) multiplier. The weight survives because it is nonzero at points with \(x_1=1\). Multiplication by \(x_1\) identifies its degree-\(K\) image with degree-at-most-\(K\) functions in the other \(m-1\) variables, proving the dimension formula. The inequality follows from \(N_m(K)=N_{m-1}(K)+N_{m-1}(K-1)\), with \(N_{m-1}(-1)=0\). \(\square\)

Zero padding retains the original product cost \(2h\) and companion cost \(2h+1\). The small specialized certificates do not reset them: the displayed weighted comparison and companion errors can retain the sufficient original-charge ceiling \(K+2h+2\). Every other source axiom and the actual proof replay keep their separate original charges. If an old pseudoideal already contains \(x_1\), this chosen weight does not survive. Nor does the construction authorize changing a shared final-goal value fixed to one. Those qualifications are precisely why this positive control does not solve source coverage.
