The supported gluing control

Proposition (acyclic support gluing). Over any field, normalized tables on two overlapping binary-variable bags can have matching separator marginals but no joint extension whose support lies in both prescribed local supports. This does not rule out signed extensions allowed to cancel on forbidden local entries.

Proof. Use bags \((x,y)\) and \((y,z)\), an acyclic two-bag system. In row order \(x=0,1\) and column order \(y=0,1\), let \[ p=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}. \] For \(q\), use row order \(y=0,1\) and column order \(z=0,1\): \[ q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \] Both total sums are one, and both separator marginals are \((0,1)\). Any joint table supported within \(q\)'s nonzero entries has no point with \(y=0\). Its \((x,y)\) marginal is therefore zero there, contradicting \(p(0,0)=1\). The argument holds in characteristic two too, where \(-1=1\). In real nonnegative probabilities this cancellation could not occur. The support requirement is the hypothesis that represents local legal choices, rather than merely equal signed marginal sums. \(\square\)
