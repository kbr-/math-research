
###pair-space-setting
1. The pair space and the restricted slack tree
Readers are role-separated as in the multi-row setting: at most \(M\le n^c\) terms \([i\to x]\wedge\bigwedge_{j\in J}[j\in p_j]\) with \(i\in A\), \(J\subseteq B\), \(A\cap B=\varnothing\), \(|B|\le(n-2N-2)/2\), at most \(r\) rows per term, at most \(w\) literals per tail row with \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\), pin-free terms allowed; \(A\) is taken to be every row outside \(B\), so \(|A|\ge n/2+N+2\), and \(X\) is the set of labels pinned by some term. The reader is sparsely pinned if \(|X\cap C(p)|\le|C(p)|/4\) for every pattern \(p\) of a tail row; a reader with \(|X|\le n2^{-w-2}\) is sparsely pinned whatever its pins.
The pair space. Fix an \(e\)-set \(S\subseteq A\) of rows outside every tail. Let \(\Phi_e(S)=\{\rho\in\Phi_e:\ S\subseteq R(\rho)\}\) and let a pair be \((\rho,\rho'')\) with \(\rho\in\Phi_e(S)\) and \(\rho''\) a bijection of \(S\) onto \(E(\rho)\); \(\rho'=\rho\cup\rho''\) then lies in \(\Phi_0^S=\{\rho'\in\Phi_0:\ S\cap R'=\varnothing\}\), the compact family with \(S\) matched, and the map is a bijection from pairs onto \(\Phi_0^S\), since \(\rho'\) determines \(\rho''=\rho'|_S\) and \(\rho\). So the uniform pair gives the uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0^S\), and given \(S\) the slack restriction has \(R(\rho)=R'\sqcup S\) with \(R'\) a uniform \((N+1)\)-subset of the rows outside \(S\), \(E(\rho)\) a uniform \(e\)-subset of \(O\), and \(\mu\) a uniform bijection of the other rows onto \(O\setminus E(\rho)\). Write \(b=|A|\).
Lemma A0 (uniform \(\rho'\)). If \(e(N+1)\le n/8\), then for every event \(\mathcal E\) on \(\Phi_0\), \(\Pr_{\rho'\in\Phi_0}[\mathcal E]\le\tfrac75\,\mathbb E_S\Pr_{\rho'\in\Phi_0^S}[\mathcal E]\), the expectation over a uniform \(e\)-subset \(S\) of \(A\). Proof. \(|\Phi_0^S|=q|\Phi_0|\) with \(q=\binom{n+1-e}{N+1}/\binom{n+1}{N+1}\le1\) for every \(S\), and a given \(\rho'\) lies in \(\Phi_0^S\) for \(\binom{b-|A\cap R'|}{e}\ge\binom{b-N-1}{e}\) sets \(S\). Hence \(\mathbb E_S\Pr_{\Phi_0^S}[\mathcal E]=\sum_{\rho'\in\mathcal E}\binom{b-|A\cap R'|}{e}/(\binom be\,q|\Phi_0|)\ge\Pr_{\Phi_0}[\mathcal E]\binom{b-N-1}{e}/\binom be\), and \(\binom{b-N-1}e/\binom be\ge(1-(N+1)/(b-e))^e\ge1-e(N+1)/(b-e)\ge5/7\), as \(b-e\ge7n/16\). \(\square\)
The slack tree. \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) is the complete-term tree on the residual instance of \(\rho\), pigeons \(R\) and holes \(Q\cup E(\rho)\), with three changes. The current term is chosen by the matched-first preference among the terms not falsified by \(\rho\cup\pi\) and not killed. If its pin \((i,x)\) is alive (\(i\) unassigned, \(x\) unused and not emptied) and \(x\in Q\), the round queries the pigeon \(i\) over the unused holes of \(Q\cup E(\rho)\) and, on every answer other than \(x\), the hole \(x\) over the unassigned rows of \(R\) and the empty branch (the hole after every answer, not only after answers in \(Q\)). If its pin is alive and \(x\in E(\rho)\), a pin test follows: two branches, "\(i\mapsto x\)", which assigns \(i\) to \(x\), and "not", which kills the term; a killed term is skipped by the preference from then on and its node is left at once, without tail queries. Otherwise every uncovered tail row is then queried in order. As on the complete-term tree, a term whose pin's hole is used or emptied while its row is unassigned counts as falsified, and a 1-leaf is a node where some term is satisfied by \(\rho\cup\pi\). Lemma 1 of the complete-term rule holds verbatim for the rounds: the trigger pairs of a path are pairwise disjoint pairs \((i,x)\) with \(i\in R\), \(x\in Q\), whose host is not falsified by \(\rho\) and has no \(\mu\)-matched pair, so the rounds number at most \(N\); a pin test triggers no round.
Lemma A (restriction by filling). Restrict \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) by \(\rho''\): at a pigeon query of a row of \(S\) follow the branch of its filling label; at a pigeon query of another row keep the branches into \(Q\); at a hole query keep the branches of the rows of \(R'\) and the empty branch; at a pin test \((i,x)\) follow "\(i\mapsto x\)" if \(i\in S\) and \(\rho''(i)=x\), and "not" otherwise. The result \(\mathcal T'\) is a matching decision tree for \(\rho'\) over \(Q\), with pigeon and hole queries at rows of \(R'\) and holes of \(Q\), whose 1-leaves contain a term satisfied by \(\rho'\) and the leaf and whose 0-leaves falsify every term; its height is the largest number of real queries, the queries of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) at rows of \(R'\) and at holes, along a path. Proof. Along a surviving path the labels of \(E(\rho)\) are used only by rows of \(S\), each at its filling label, which is unused when the row is queried, so the followed branches exist; a row of \(R'\) receives a hole of \(Q\); a hole query keeps only rows residual in \(\rho'\). A surviving path together with \(\rho''\) is a path of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) on which every killed term is falsified by \(\rho'\), since its pinned row is either in \(R'\), and then cannot reach the filled label \(x\), or in \(S\) with \(\rho''(i)\ne x\); so a 0-leaf falsifies every term under \(\rho'\cup\pi_{\rm real}\) and a 1-leaf contains a satisfied term. A pigeon query of a row of \(R'\) at which every hole of \(Q\) is used or emptied keeps no branch: a dead leaf, whose leaf term vanishes modulo the row axiom and the column exclusions, as in the light lemma's Section 1. Hole-query nodes are admissible. \(\square\)
Two remarks. A pin test costs nothing in \(\mathcal T'\), and rows of \(S\) are queried only as pigeons: they lie in no tail. This is what the choice \(S\subseteq A\) buys; with a filling on arbitrary residual rows the slack tree passes through nodes whose only queries are at filled tail rows, of which a path may have of order \(e\), and the encoding below has to pay for them (Section 5 records the failure).


###pair-space-encoding
2. The encoding and its decoder
Call \(\rho\) \(s\)-rich' if \(|E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X|\ge s\) for every pattern \(p\) of a tail row, the richness' of the two-role decoder with the global pinned set \(X\) in place of the row's own; for a sparsely pinned reader, \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\) and \(e\ge2^{w+3}s\), \(\Pr[\rho\text{ not }s\text{-rich'}]\le Mr\,e^{-e2^{-w-5}}\) by the same proof, since \(E(\rho)\) is a uniform \(e\)-subset of \(O\) on \(\Phi_e(S)\) and \(|C(p)\cap O\setminus X|\ge n2^{-w-2}\). A move is a real query of a tail row; a real light query moves the row to a hole of \(Q\).
Fix a pair with \(\rho\) rich' whose restricted tree has a path with at least \(s\) moves and fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move, and take the lexicographically first such path, cut after its \(s\)-th move. Its code is \((\rho^\ast,\sigma,\beta,\delta)\): \(\rho^\ast\in\Phi_{e-s}(S)\) is \(\rho\) with each moved row taken out of \(R\) and matched to the smallest unpicked label of \(E(\rho)\cap C(p)\setminus X\), \(p\) its pattern in the node's term, the code labels; \(\sigma\) is the filling written as a bijection from \(S\) onto \(E(\rho^\ast)\sqcup\{1,\dots,s\}\), the index \(k\) standing for the code label of the \(k\)-th moved row; \(\beta\) lists, in path order, one bit per round, whether the node moves a row, and per moved row its position among the at most \(r\) tail rows of its term and one bit marking the last move of its node; and \(\delta\) lists, in path order, the moves' holes and the rounds' answers, a hole of \(Q\) for the pigeon when the pinned row lies in \(R'\), and a row of \(R'\) or the empty branch for the hole. Rows of \(S\) are recorded by nothing: a pin test is decided by the decoder, and a pigeon query of a row of \(S\) at a round has answer \(\rho''(i)\), which the decoder reads off \(\sigma\) when it lies in \(E(\rho^\ast)\) and replaces by a mark "outside \(Q\), at no pinned label" when it is a code label, which is at no pinned label.
Lemma B (decoder). The code determines the pair. Proof. The decoder starts from \(\alpha=\rho^\ast\) with no term killed, knows \(S\), \(E(\rho^\ast)\) and \(\sigma\), and processes nodes until it has found \(s\) moved rows. At a node it chooses the current term by the preference with falsification tested on \(\alpha\), killed terms skipped, and a pin counted as matched when \(\rho^\ast\) matches its row to its label. Claims: (i) the matched pins of \(\rho^\ast\) are those of \(\mu\), since moved rows lie in \(B\) and pinned rows are never moved; (ii) every term preceding the real current term in the preference is killed in both, or falsified by \(\rho\cup\pi_{<m}\) through a row assigned before \(m\), on which \(\alpha\) agrees (matched rows; rows moved earlier, reassigned to their holes; rows of \(S\) assigned at earlier rounds or pin tests, whose labels the decoder knows or has marked as outside \(Q\) at no pinned label, which decides every pin at them; rows assigned by hole answers), or through a killed pin, whose hole is used or emptied in \(\alpha\) as in reality: holes of \(Q\) are used only by recorded assignments, and a pin at a code label does not exist; (iii) the current term is not falsified by \(\alpha\): its rows assigned before \(m\) agree, its tail rows moved at \(m\) carry consistent code labels, its other tail rows are unassigned in both (a row of \(B\) moved later is unassigned in reality and, were it in this term's tail, would be queried now), and its pin is alive in \(\alpha\) exactly when alive in reality, the pinned row's status agreeing as in (ii) and its hole being a hole of \(Q\), used or emptied in \(\alpha\) as in reality, or a label of \(E(\rho^\ast)\), used or free in both since rows of \(S\) assigned before \(m\) are in \(\alpha\), or a label of \(O\setminus E(\rho)\), used in both, since a pinned label is never a code label. So the decoder finds the current term. If its pin is alive at \(x\in E(\rho^\ast)\), the decoder decides the pin test: "\(i\mapsto x\)" exactly when \(i\in S\) and \(\sigma(i)=x\), which agrees with reality because a row of \(R'\) never reaches \(x\) and a slot value of \(\sigma(i)\) is a code label, not \(x\). If its pin is alive at \(x\in Q\), it resolves the round: the pigeon answer from \(\delta\) when \(i\in R'\), from \(\sigma\) or the mark when \(i\in S\); the hole answer from \(\delta\) whenever the pigeon answer differs from \(x\); then it reads the round's bit of \(\beta\). If the node has moves, by the bit or because the node has no round and the path continues, it reads the positions from \(\beta\) up to the last-of-node bit, and for each the hole from \(\delta\); a node with neither a round nor a move does not occur before the cut, since a current term with an unassigned tail row makes a move, a killed term is left at once, and a pin test "\(i\mapsto x\)" whose tail is covered is a 1-leaf. After the \(s\)-th move it returns the moved rows to the residual set, which gives \(\rho\), and reads \(\rho''\) off \(\sigma\), the code labels now being known. \(\square\)
Lemma C (count). Let \(\theta_A=(N+1)e/(n-N-e+1)\) and \(A''=2rN\theta_A\). Among the pairs of \(\Phi_e(S)\) with \(\rho\) rich', the fraction whose restricted tree has a path with at least \(s\) moves and fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move is at most \(A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\). Proof. The codes number at most \(|\Phi_{e-s}(S)|\) times \(e!\) for \(\sigma\), times \((2r)^s2^{\nu}\) for \(\beta\), \(N^s\) for the moves' holes and \((N(N+2))^{\nu}\) for the rounds' answers, summed over \(\nu<\nu_0\); the pairs number \(|\Phi_e(S)|\,e!\); Lemma B makes the code injective. Counting \(\Phi_k(S)\) as the choice of the \(N+1+k-e\) residual rows outside \(S\), of \(E\), and of the matching, \(|\Phi_k(S)|=\binom{n+1-e}{N+1+k-e}\binom{n-N}{k}(n-N-k)!\), so
\[\frac{|\Phi_{e-s}(S)|}{|\Phi_e(S)|}=\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\frac{(N+1-i)(e-i)}{n-N-e+1+i}\le\theta_A^{\,s},\]
and \(\sum_{\nu<\nu_0}(2N(N+2))^\nu\le(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\). \(\square\)
The formula for \(|\Phi_k(S)|\) and the ratio were checked by enumeration on boards with \(n\le9\), \(N\le3\), \(e\le3\) (research/results/pair_space_20260918_207/count_check.txt).
The ratio is the light lemma's \(|\Phi_{e-s}|/|\Phi_e|\) with the residual-row factor \(N+1+e-i\) replaced by \(N+1-i\): a moved row is one of the \(N+1\) rows of \(R'\), not one of the \(N+1+e\) residual rows of the slack restriction, because the rows of \(S\) are never moved; and a move's answer is one of \(N\) holes of \(Q\), not of \(N+e\) holes. Both savings come from encoding the restricted tree rather than the slack tree. This narrows the scope of the recorded observation that the \((1/3)\log_2n\) limit is intrinsic to extension encodings in flat families: its counting keeps the residual factor \(N+e+1-i\) because its encodings move any residual row of the slack tree in \(\Phi_e\), and for those the limit stands, as does its point that a fixed free pool cannot help; the pair-space code moves rows of \(R'\) only, keeps the free-set entropy \(e/n\) per label, and pays \(2rN(N+1)\) per move instead of \(2r(N+e)^2\), so \(e\) may grow to \(n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\) before \(A''\) reaches \(1/(4\sqrt N)\). The observation's claim-index row records the narrowing.


###pair-space-round-bound
3. The round bound on the slack tree and the composition
Lemma E (steps (H1) to (H3) on \(\Phi_e(S)\)). Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), \(d_\varepsilon=\lceil(c+1)/\varepsilon\rceil+1\), \(N\ge2^{d_\varepsilon+6}(c+1)\log_2n\) with \((Nn^{-\varepsilon})^{d_\varepsilon+1}\le n^{-c-1}\), and \(e\le n/16\). Call a host heavy if \(m_h\ge2(N+2e)(n+1)\), light otherwise, let \(U^{\rm L}_x\) be the union of the \(I_{h,x}\) over light hosts, \(D_2^{(e)}=n/(1600\,\mathrm e\,(N+1+e))\) with \(\mathrm e\) Euler's number, and \(X^{(e)}_{\rm L}=\{x:\ |U^{\rm L}_x|\ge D_2^{(e)}\}\), all fixed by the reader. If \(|X^{(e)}_{\rm L}|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\), then, for a uniform \(e\)-subset \(S\) of \(A\) and a uniform \(\rho\in\Phi_e(S)\), every path of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) before a 1-leaf has at most \(N/48-1\) rounds except with probability \(3n^{-c-1}\). In particular this holds for a reader with at most \(n^c\) terms when \(1600\,\mathrm e\,(N+1+e)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), since \(|X^{(e)}_{\rm L}|\le\sum_x|U^{\rm L}_x|/D_2^{(e)}\le n^c/D_2^{(e)}\).
Proof. Follow the multi-row theorem, part (i). (H1) Given \(Q\), \(S\) and \(E(\rho)\), the occupants of the labels of \(O\setminus E(\rho)\) form a uniform injection of a uniform subset of the rows outside \(S\), so the occupant of a label is uniform over the \(n+1-e\) rows outside \(S\) and the events "the occupant of \(x\) lies outside \(I_{h,x}\)" are decreasing on distinct columns, each of probability at most \(1-|I_{h,x}\setminus S|/(n+1)\). The labels of \(Q\cup E(\rho)\) carry mass at most \((N+e)(n+1)\) and the rows of \(S\) at most \(e(n+1)\), so for a heavy host \(\sum_{x\notin Q\cup E}|I_{h,x}\setminus S|\ge m_h/2\), and the host has no \(\mu\)-matched pair with probability at most \(e^{-m_h/(2(n+1))}\le e^{-N}\le n^{-2c-3}\); over at most \(n^c\) hosts this costs \(n^{-c-3}\). Outside this event, by Lemma 1 the trigger pairs of a bad path come from light hosts at holes of \(Q\), so they form a matching in \(\{(i,x):\ x\in Q,\ i\in R\cap U^{\rm L}_x\}\) with \(R=R'\sqcup S\). (H2) The sets \(U^{\rm L}_x\) are fixed by the reader. Given \(Q\), \(S\) is a uniform \(e\)-subset of \(A\) and, given also \(S\), \(R'\) is a uniform \((N+1)\)-subset of the rows outside \(S\). For \(k\) given rows of \(A\), the probability that all lie in \(R=R'\sqcup S\) is at most \(\sum_{T_1\sqcup T_2}(e/(b-k))^{|T_1|}((N+1)/(n+1-e-k))^{|T_2|}\le(4(N+1+e)/n)^k\), as \(b\ge n/2+N+2\), \(e\le n/16\) and \(k\le N/192\). So the expected number of \(k\)-matchings at labels outside \(X^{(e)}_{\rm L}\), \(k=N/192\), is at most \(\binom Nk(D_2^{(e)})^k(4(N+1+e)/n)^k\le(4\mathrm eN(N+1+e)D_2^{(e)}/(kn))^k=0.48^k\), as in the activated union lemma, and Markov's inequality gives fewer than \(N/192\) such rounds outside probability \(2^{-N/192}\le n^{-c-1}\). (H3) \(X^{(e)}_{\rm L}\) is fixed by the reader, so by the rank lemma in its independent-points form, \(\Pr[|X^{(e)}_{\rm L}\cap Q|\ge2^{d_\varepsilon}]\le(Nn^{-\varepsilon})^{d_\varepsilon+1}\le n^{-c-1}\); outside this event a path before a 1-leaf, whose rounds have distinct holes, has fewer than \(N/192+2^{d_\varepsilon}\le N/192+N/128\le N/48-1\) rounds, as \(N\ge2^{d_\varepsilon+7}\ge256\). \(\square\)
Theorem D (composition). Let \(F\) be sparsely pinned, \(s=\lfloor N/12\rfloor-2r\ge1\), \(\nu_0=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\), \(2^{w+3}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\), and suppose that, for a uniform \(e\)-subset \(S\) of \(A\) and a uniform \(\rho\in\Phi_e(S)\), every path of \(\mathcal T(F,\rho)\) before a 1-leaf has fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds except with probability \(p_R\). Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) admits a matching decision tree over \(Q\), with pigeon and hole queries, of height below \(N/8-2r\), whose 1-leaves contain a term satisfied by \(\rho'\) and the leaf and whose 0-leaves falsify every term, except with probability at most
\[\tfrac75\Bigl(Mr\,e^{-e2^{-w-5}}+p_R+2^{-N/8+(2r+1)(2+\frac12\log_2N)}\Bigr).\]
Proof. By Lemma A0 it suffices to bound the failure for a fixed \(S\) and average. Take the pair uniform and \(\mathcal T'\) of Lemma A; it is the required tree unless some path has at least \(s+2\nu_0\) real queries. A round contributes at most two real queries and a pin test none, so such a path has at least \(\nu_0\) rounds or at least \(s+2\) moves with fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move; the first is the event of probability \(p_R\) (at most \(N/48-1\) rounds means fewer than \(\nu_0\)), and the second, with \(\rho\) rich', has probability at most \(A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\) by Lemma C, the richness' failure costing the first term. The height bound is \(s+2\nu_0\le N/12-2r+N/24=N/8-2r\). For the numeric bound, \(N+e\le n/2\) gives \(\theta_A\le2(N+1)e/n\), so \(A''\le4rN(N+1)e/n\le1/(4\sqrt N)=2^{-2-\frac12\log_2N}\), and with \(s\ge N/12-2r-1\) and \(2N(N+2)\le4N^2\),
\[A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\le2^{-(N/12-2r-1)(2+\frac12\log_2N)+(N/48)(2+2\log_2N)}=2^{-N/8+(2r+1)(2+\frac12\log_2N)}.\ \square\]
Theorem F (switching for sparsely pinned role-separated readers). Let \(F\) be a sparsely pinned role-separated reader as in Section 1 with at most \(n^c\) terms and \(|X^{(e)}_{\rm L}|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\), in particular any such reader when \(1600\,\mathrm e\,(N+1+e)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\); let \(N\) be as in Lemma E with \(N\ge8(c+1)\log_2n+(2r+1)(16+4\log_2N)\) and \(s\ge4((2c+1)\ln n+\ln r)\), and let the tails satisfy \(2^{w+3}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\). Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) admits a matching decision tree over \(Q\) of height below \(N/8-2r\), with the leaf properties of Theorem D, except with probability at most \(7n^{-c-1}\). Proof. Theorem D with Lemma E's \(p_R=3n^{-c-1}\); the richness' term is at most \(n^{-c-1}\) because \(e2^{-w-5}\ge s/4\ge(2c+1)\ln n+\ln r\), and the count term is at most \(n^{-c-1}\) by the bound on \(N\). \(\square\)
Scope. The per-reader failure \(7n^{-c-1}\) is what a union over \(n^c\) readers of a source needs, so this is the composition that the correction found missing, for sparsely pinned role-separated readers. What is new per reader is the tails' width beside a pin of width \(\ell\): with \(|X^{(e)}_{\rm L}|\le n^{1-\varepsilon}\) given directly, \(e\) may be as large as \(n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\), so tails of width up to \(\log_2n-\log_2(2^8rN^{5/2}s)\) are admitted; through the size route, \(e\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}/4400-N-1\) and the width is up to \((2-\varepsilon-c)\log_2n-\log_2(2^{16}s)\), at \(c=1\) up to \((1-\varepsilon)\log_2n-O(\log N)\). This is a per-reader statement and makes no claim on the window of the window structure, whose upper end \((c-1)\log_2n\) is a total-size statement of the dichotomy; per reader, the dichotomy covers rows of width at least \((2c-1)\log_2n+O(\log N)\), and for a reader of size \(n^c\) with failure \(n^{-c-1}\) nothing, so rows of width between \((2-\varepsilon-c)\log_2n\) and \(\ell\) remain open per reader. The recorded light lemma's range \(2^w\lesssim n^{1/3}/(rNs)\) is the case of arbitrary pins; sparse pinning is the price of the pair space, and every column class at one label, and every reader with at most \(n2^{-w-2}\) pinned labels, pays it.


###pair-space-obstruction
4. What remains: dense pinning, two-role rows, the satisfied-term steps
Dense pinning. If some tail pattern cube \(C(p)\) is pinned on more than a quarter of its labels, the code labels for \(p\) may have to be pinned labels, and then the decoder cannot tell a pin at a code label, alive in reality because the label is free, from a pin at an occupied label, dead: the code shows every code label as occupied. This is the failure of the tool's first design (Section 5, batch 3 off \(G\)). Naming the code labels costs \(n/s\) per move against a gain of \(\theta_A\approx(N+1)e/n\), and recording, for each pin test at a code label, which slot the pinned row's filling occupies costs \(s\) per such test with no bound on their number. Readers whose pins cover a pattern cube, such as a class pinning one row to every label, remain without a composition; the dichotomy switches them below total size \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\).
Two-role rows. The filling avoids every tail row, so a row pinned in one term and light in another lies outside \(S\) and is moved like any light row; the two-role decoder's device (code labels outside the moved row's own pinned labels) is then needed for it, and richness' with both exclusions holds under the two conditions together. The transfer is not written here.
The satisfied-term steps (H4) of the multi-row theorem, the few-light-rows and combined theorems' exposure steps, and the two-role theorems are not transferred to \(\Phi_e(S)\); the previous entry's remark that exposure transfers for \(\varepsilon>\theta\) stands, and the pair space's uniformity given \(S\) is what those proofs would use.


###compact-composition-code
2. The code and its decoder
Code labels. For a pattern \(p\) of a tail row call a label \(y\in E(\rho)\cap C(p)\) valid if the row filling it is not pinned to it, \(\rho''^{-1}(y)\notin A_y\), and call the pair \(s\)-rich if every tail pattern has at least \(s\) valid labels. Fix a rich pair whose compact tree has a path with at least \(s\) moves and fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move, and take the lexicographically first such path, cut after its \(s\)-th move. Its code is \((\rho^\ast,\sigma,\beta,\delta)\): \(\rho^\ast\in\Phi_{e-s}(S)\) is \(\rho\) with each moved row taken out of \(R\) and matched to the smallest unpicked valid label of its pattern in the node's term, its code label; \(\sigma\) is \(\rho''\) written as a bijection of \(S\) onto \(E(\rho^\ast)\sqcup\{1,\dots,s\}\), the index \(k\) standing for the \(k\)-th code label; \(\beta\) lists, in path order, one bit per round, whether the node moves a row, and per moved row its position among the term's tail rows and a last-of-node bit; \(\delta\) lists, in path order, the moves' holes and the rounds' answers, a hole of \(Q\) for the pigeon and a row of \(R'\) or the empty branch for the hole.
Lemma B (decoder). The code determines the pair. Proof. The decoder simulates \(\mathcal T_c(F,\rho')\) node by node from the assignment \(\alpha\) that extends \(\rho^\ast\) by \(i\mapsto\sigma(i)\) for the rows of \(S\) whose value lies in \(E(\rho^\ast)\), and by a mark "at the \(k\)-th code label" for the others; it evaluates a term as follows: a tail row is consistent or not by its label in \(\alpha\), or uncovered if unassigned; a pin \((i,x)\) with \(x\in Q\) is satisfied, dead or alive by \(\alpha\) and the emptied holes as in the tree; a pin \((i,x)\) with \(x\notin Q\) is satisfied if \(\alpha(i)=x\) and dead otherwise, a marked row satisfying no pin, since by validity the row at the \(k\)-th code label is not pinned to it. The preference counts a pin as matched when \(\rho^\ast\) or \(\sigma\) matches its row to its label. Claims, at a node \(m\) with real current term \(T\): (i) the terms with a matched pin are the same in \(\alpha\) and in \(\rho'\), by validity; (ii) every term before \(T\) in the preference is falsified by \(\rho'\cup\pi_{<m}\) through a row assigned there, on which \(\alpha\) agrees (matched rows; rows of \(S\), whose pins the decoder decides exactly; rows moved earlier, reassigned to their holes; rows assigned by pigeon answers or hole answers, recorded in \(\delta\)), or through a pin at a hole of \(Q\) used or emptied, which \(\alpha\) shows, or through a pin at an outside label to which its row is not matched, which the decoder's rule shows; (iii) \(T\) is not falsified in \(\alpha\): its rows assigned before \(m\) agree, its tail rows moved at \(m\) carry consistent code labels, its other tail rows are unassigned in both, since a row of \(B\) moved later is unassigned in reality and, were it in \(T\)'s tail, would be queried at \(m\) by the complete-term rule, and its pin's status agrees as in (ii). So the decoder finds \(T\); if its pin is alive it reads the pigeon answer and, when that differs from the pin's hole, the hole answer from \(\delta\), then the round's bit of \(\beta\); if the node has moves, by the bit or because the node has no round and the path continues, it reads the positions from \(\beta\) up to the last-of-node bit and their holes from \(\delta\). After the \(s\)-th move it returns the moved rows to the residual set, which gives \(\rho\), and reads \(\rho''\) off \(\sigma\), the code labels now being known. \(\square\)
Lemma C (count). With \(\theta_A=(N+1)e/(n-N-e+1)\) and \(A''=2rN\theta_A\), among the rich pairs of \(\Phi_e(S)\) the fraction whose compact tree has a path with at least \(s\) moves and fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move is at most \(A''^{\,s}(2N(N+2))^{\nu_0}\). Proof. As in Lemma C of the previous entry: \(e!\) values of \(\sigma\), \((2r)^s2^\nu\) of \(\beta\), \(N^s(N(N+2))^\nu\) of \(\delta\), the ratio \(|\Phi_{e-s}(S)|/|\Phi_e(S)|\le\theta_A^{\,s}\) checked by enumeration there, and Lemma B. \(\square\)


###compact-composition-richness
3. Richness with the own-row rule
Call a label \(y\) heavy if \(|A_y|>b/64\), and the reader column-sparse if, for every flat \(Q\) and every tail pattern \(p\), at most a quarter of the labels of \(C(p)\cap O\) are heavy. A reader with at most \(n^c\) terms has fewer than \(n^c/(b/64)\le128n^{c-1}\) heavy labels, and \(|C(p)\cap O|\ge n2^{-w-1}\) by the richness lemma's count, so it is column-sparse, for every flat, whenever \(2^w\le n^{2-c}/1024\).
Lemma R. If the reader is column-sparse, \(w\le\ell-\ell_2-1\) and \(2^{w+4}s\le e\), then, for a uniform \(e\)-subset \(S\) of \(A\) and a uniform pair over \(\Phi_e(S)\), the pair fails to be \(s\)-rich with probability at most \(n2^{-e/29}+2Mr\,e^{-s/3}\). Proof. For a label \(y\) that is not heavy, \(|A_y\cap S|\) is hypergeometric with mean at most \(e/64\), so \(\Pr[|A_y\cap S|>e/16]\le(\mathrm e/4)^{e/16}\le2^{-e/29}\); over at most \(n\) labels this costs \(n2^{-e/29}\), and outside it every non-heavy label has \(|A_y\cap S|\le e/16\). Fix a tail pattern \(p\) and let \(L\) be the non-heavy labels of \(C(p)\cap O\), \(|L|\ge\tfrac34|C(p)\cap O|\ge\tfrac38n2^{-w}\). Given \(S\), the set \(E(\rho)\) is a uniform \(e\)-subset of \(O\), so \(|E(\rho)\cap L|\) is hypergeometric with mean at least \(\tfrac38e2^{-w}\ge6s\), and \(|E(\rho)\cap L|\ge4s\) outside probability \(e^{-e2^{-w}/48}\le e^{-s/3}\). Given \(S\) and \(E(\rho)\), list the first \(m=\min(|E(\rho)\cap L|,\lceil e/2\rceil)\ge4s\) labels of \(E(\rho)\cap L\) and reveal their filling rows one at a time; the \(t\)-th is uniform over the \(e-t+1\ge e/2\) rows of \(S\) not yet revealed, so it lies in \(A_{y_t}\) with conditional probability at most \((e/16)/(e/2)=1/8\), and the number \(Z\) of the \(m\) labels whose filling row is pinned to them is dominated by a binomial with parameters \(m\) and \(1/8\): \(\Pr[Z\ge m/2]\le2^m8^{-m/2}=2^{-m/2}\le2^{-2s}\). Outside these events the pattern has at least \(m/2\ge2s\) valid labels, of which at most \(s-1\) are picked. Over \(Mr\) patterns the two terms cost at most \(Mr(e^{-s/3}+2^{-2s})\le2Mr\,e^{-s/3}\). \(\square\)


###compact-composition-theorem
4. The composition and the restored switching clauses
Theorem G (composition on \(\Phi_0\)). Let \(F\) be a column-sparse role-separated reader, \(s=\lfloor N/12\rfloor-2r\ge1\), \(\nu_0=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\), and let the tails satisfy \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\). If every path of \(\mathcal T_c(F,\rho')\) before a 1-leaf has at most \(\nu_0-1\) rounds except with probability \(p_R\) over \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\), then \(\mathcal T_c(F,\rho')\) has height below \(N/8-2r\), and \(F\) switches within the height budget, except with probability at most
\[p_R+\tfrac75\Bigl(n2^{-e/29}+2Mr\,e^{-s/3}+2^{-N/8+(2r+1)(2+\frac12\log_2N)}\Bigr).\]
Proof. A path of height at least \(s+2\nu_0\) has at least \(\nu_0\) rounds or at least \(s+2\) moves with fewer than \(\nu_0\) rounds before its \(s\)-th move. The second is an event on \(\Phi_0\); by Lemma A0 its probability is at most \(\tfrac75\) times its average over \(S\) of its probability on \(\Phi_0^S\), which is at most the richness failure of Lemma R plus the count of Lemma C, and \(e(N+1)\le n/8\) holds. The bound \(A''\le1/(4\sqrt N)\) and the exponent are as in Theorem D of the previous entry. \(\mathcal T_c(F,\rho')\) is a matching decision tree for \(\rho'\) with the leaf properties of the switching lemmas, as recorded, a pigeon query at which every hole of \(Q\) is used or emptied being a dead leaf whose term vanishes modulo the axioms as in the light lemma's Section 1, and \(s+2\nu_0\le N/8-2r\). \(\square\)
Corollary (switching clauses restored). Let \(N\) and \(J_0\) satisfy the hypotheses of the cited round bound, \(N\ge8(c+1)\log_2n+(2r+1)(16+4\log_2N)\), \(s\ge3((2c+1)\ln n+\ln(2r))\) and \(e\ge29(c+2)\log_2n\), so that the three terms in the bracket are each at most \(n^{-c-1}\); and let the tails satisfy \(2^{w+4}s\le e\le n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\). Then a uniform \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) switches, within the height budget, except with probability at most \(p_R+5n^{-c-1}\): (a) every role-separated reader with at most \(n^c\) terms, \(1\le c<2-\varepsilon\), \(800\,\mathrm e\,(N+1)\le n^{2-\varepsilon-c}\), and \(2^w\le n^{2-c}/1024\), with \(p_R=3n^{-c-1}\) (the multi-row size corollary, part (i) of the multi-row theorem transferred to the complete-term tree by Lemma 2 as stated); (b) every column-sparse role-separated reader with tails on at most \(t\) rows, total width \(W\), \(J_0\le n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) and \(1600\,\mathrm e\,t(N+1)N^4J_0n^c\le n^{3-2\varepsilon}\), with \(p_R=6n^{-c-2}\) (the crowded size corollary); (c) every column-sparse role-separated reader with at most \(n^{1-\varepsilon}/N^4\) light rows and at most \(n^c\) terms for any \(c>0\), with \(p_R=4n^{-c-2}\) (the few-light-rows theorem), and every column-sparse role-separated reader of the combined theorem with its \(p_R\); in (b) and (c) the fewer than \(r_0\) rounds that Lemma 2 adds are absorbed by the thresholds, as recorded. Proof. Theorem G with the recorded round bound, which lives on \(\Phi_0\) and on this tree; the recorded proofs bound the rounds of a good path by \(N/192+2^{d}+r_0\le5N/384+r_0\le\lfloor N/48\rfloor-1\) once \(N\ge128(r_0+2)\), which their bounds on \(N\) give, so their events are Theorem G's hypothesis with \(\nu_0=\lfloor N/48\rfloor\); in (a) the reader is column-sparse by the count of Section 3. \(\square\)
Scope. These are per-reader statements, failure \(p_R+5n^{-c-1}\), the convention of the round bounds, so a union over the \(n^c\) readers of a source costs \(n^c\) times that. The light range is \(2^w\le e/(16s)\) with \(e\) up to \(n/(16rN^{3/2}(N+1))\), so width up to \(\log_2n-\log_2(2^9rN^{5/2}s)\), for every size; the light lemma's \(n^{1/3}/(rNs)\) is exceeded at every \(c\). The one hypothesis beyond the round bound's is column-sparseness, automatic below width \((2-c)\log_2n-10\) for readers of size \(n^c\); it covers the light lemma's whole range for \(c\le5/3-o(1)\), and above that a reader pinning every row to every label of one pattern cube of width \(\tfrac13\log_2n\) (about \(n^{5/3}\) terms) is not column-sparse and is not covered, though its tree is short for other reasons (a matched tail row satisfies a term). The previous entry's slack tree, pin test and transferred round bound are superseded: they were the detour that hid the compact tree.


###compact-composition-remaining
5. What remains
Two-role readers: the filling avoids every tail row, so a row pinned in one term and light in another is moved like a light row, and in \(\alpha\) it sits at a code label while in reality it is unassigned, which changes the status of its own pins; the recorded two-role decoder handles this on the slack tree by the complete-term rule and code labels outside the row's pinned labels, and the combination with the own-row rule is the next step. Dense pattern cubes: readers above size \(n^{5/3}\) whose pins fill a tail pattern cube of width in the light range are not column-sparse; for them the count of Lemma R has no valid labels, and a different argument, for instance one that treats such a cube's terms as heavy terms of the dichotomy, is needed. Per reader, tails of width between \((2-c)\log_2n\) and \(\ell\) beside a pin remain open without column-sparseness.
