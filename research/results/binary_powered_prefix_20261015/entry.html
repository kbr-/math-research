Zero context bridge

Lemma. In the recorded binary sequent presentation, suppose positive roots \(\to T\) and \(\to\zeta\) have been proved, with falsity translation \(t(\zeta)=0\) literally after the fixed arithmetic simplification. Both roots can be made ancestors of a proof of \(\to\sigma\), \(\sigma=\bigvee(\zeta,\neg\zeta)\), by a constant number of new inference nodes and symbol overhead \(O(|T|+|\zeta|+1)\). Every new line has literal translation zero. In the fixed zero-respecting prefix compiler every new contribution is zero, and its prefix factors are one. This asserts neither minimal proof size nor distinctness for arbitrary supplied roots; the application chooses fresh syntax and checks distinctness separately.

Proof. Right-weaken the first root to \(\to T,\zeta,\neg\zeta\). Right-weaken the second, adding \(\neg T\) first and then \(\neg\zeta\), to \(\to\neg T,\zeta,\neg\zeta\). Their common context is \(\zeta,\neg\zeta\). The one-sided cut on \(T\) gives \(\to\zeta,\neg\zeta\). Add the empty disjunction and use the two tail OR-right steps to group it as \(\to\sigma\). All lines after either first weakening contain a factor \(t(\zeta)=0\), and negation gives \(t(\neg\zeta)=1\). Thus their translations are zero.

The first use of the \(T\)-root multiplies it by the literal zero context; in the exact premise absorption identity \[-U(L_j^2-L_j)Vab+U(1-L_j)[Va,L_j]b,\] the added-factor multiplier is zero, so the Booleanity product and commutator certificate are zero under the prescribed zero simplification. References to a zero-translated line use its zero Booleanity and commutator certificates. The cut and OR grouping have zero local corrections: every reordering involving the context has a zero or unit scalar factor. Hence each new prefix contribution vanishes and each new factor \(1-L_i\) is one. Both input roots lead through the displayed cut to the new root. \(\square\)

Seed proofs and a usable zero tag

Fix an original variable \(x_0\). For each \(j=1,\ldots,r\), choose \(M_j=S+q+r+j\) and let \(D_j=\operatorname{XOR}_{2M_j}(x_0,\ldots,x_0)\). The corrected parity convention gives \(t(D_j)=1\). The inflated \(U_j\) is larger than every old proof formula, so its primitive logical axiom is fresh rather than a reused old axiom. This preserves the original variable set.

Prove \(\theta_j\) by the five-node excluded-middle construction from \(U_j\to U_j\). Its five translations are all \(B_j\). Add \(e-1\) right weakenings to get \(e\) copies of \(\theta_j\), with translations \(B_j^2,\ldots,B_j^e\). Add the empty disjunction and perform the \(e\) tail OR-right steps to group the copies into \(\tau_j\); these \(e+1\) translations are \(B_j^e\). This is a chain with \(2e+5\) nodes, each used by its root; ordinary repeated formula occurrences are paid. Its ordered prefix is precisely (2), with \(b=B_j\).

Apply the zero-context bridge to the powered root and the root \(\zeta_j\), obtaining \(\sigma_j=\bigvee(\zeta_j,\neg\zeta_j)\). The bridge uses both roots and all its new lines are literal zero. Let \(\eta_j=\neg\sigma_j\), so \(t(\sigma_j)=0\), \(t(\eta_j)=1\). This resolves the earlier discharge problem: the nonzero powered seed is consumed in a zero context before the old proof is carried, rather than using \(\neg\tau_j\) as that context. The deepest bridge formula is \(\neg\tau_j\), of depth nine.

Why the scalar values are cheap

At \(x\in\mathbb F_q\), \(B_j(x)=0\) exactly for binary coordinates. If \(B_j(x)\ne0\), its \(e\)-th power is one and the seed prefix (2) is zero. If all coordinates are binary, every used clause polynomial is binary and at least one is one, since those inputs support a refutation; thus \(P(x)=0\). This proves pointwise equality with \(A\) in (1) for arbitrary scalar placeholders.

More is needed than that equality: generic evaluation over \(\mathbb F_q\) is not being granted. In a seed block, after the fixed simplifications every nonzero line and local multiplier uses just \(x_j\). The canonical commutator recursion has no distinct-variable terminal, and its identical-variable terminals are zero. Its only possible nonzero original placeholder is the Boolean field \(Z_j\), appearing once per term. After scalar commutative evaluation the block contribution is \(v_e(B_j)Z_j\), where uniqueness after specialization gives \[ bv_e(b)=1-R_e(b). \] There are five exponent-one factors in (2) and \(e\ge3\), so \(v_e(0)=1\). At a nonzero \(B_j(x)\) in \(\mathbb F_q\), \(R_e=0\) and \(v_e=B_j(x)^{-1}\). This does not assert that \(Z_j\) commutes formally with old variables: it describes scalar evaluation of the homogeneous one-placeholder expression.

Each completed seed block's scalar prefix is therefore a binary flag. If the first nonbinary coordinate is \(j\), all later blocks and all input-round terms disappear. The scalar value is exactly \[ \sum_{i<j} Z_i+B_j(x)^{-1}Z_j.\tag{3} \] The zero-context bridge contributes zero. All input-ladder terms have the whole seed prefix on the left of their certificates, including premise absorptions, since their referenced original input nodes occur after the seeds; they vanish on this branch. A two-input table on \((x_j,Z_j)\) computes the inverse-weighted term, with \(O(q^2a^2)\) gates/wires and constant depth. Prefix flags and field addition are Boolean tests/XORs. No product of an unbounded number of general field values is evaluated.

On the all-binary old branch, use the known formula \(A\) rather than a guessed simple coefficient vector. For each of its \(v\) original placeholders evaluate \(A(x,e_k)\) on the binary old bits. These are binary arithmetic formulas of size \(O(\mathcal R^{48})\), depth at most nineteen; AND/XOR evaluates them with the same asymptotic cost. Placeholder homogeneity makes the scalar output \(\sum_k A(x,e_k)Y_k\), implemented coordinatewise by bit AND and XOR. All commutator labels remain present; coefficients may be nonzero even though their generators vanish at scalar points. Values computed on nonbinary first-coordinate bits are ignored by the branch flag. Together with (3), the lookup, first-nonbinary selection and output gates fit the displayed size and conservative depth twenty-eight. Boolean query/probe composition adds the supplied entry and outer depths and their full sizes.

At polynomial \(S,N,r,q,v\), fixed proof depth and polynomial entry/probe parameters, the value bound is polynomial in the original encoding and fixed-depth. Polynomiality in a smaller Boolean input length requires those quantities to be polynomial in that length too. No arbitrary arithmetic outer probe, formal coefficient extractor, large-extension-free field descent, arbitrary finite-algebra evaluator, minimum-size proof preservation or exact resource-cutoff closure follows. Both original and transformed images must lie in any restricted class for which the probe asserts invariance.
