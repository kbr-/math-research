Whole correction gap

Statement. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with only constants zero and one in arithmetic circuits. Let \(M=2r\), \(r\ge1\), and \(r\le N<2r\). Retain every original field and placeholder of lem:symmetric-matching-correction, and its complete polynomial \(Q_{\rm match}(A,Z)\). There is a constant/variable input projection to \(\det_r X\), where \(X\) is an independent \(r\times r\) matrix. The same is true of the selected full certificate \(C_{\rm match}=1+\prod_i(1+Y_i)\prod_{i<j}(1+Y_{ij})+Q_{\rm match}\). Hence neither of these chosen polynomial families has polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth binary arithmetic circuits as \(r\to\infty\). This conclusion includes \(M=2N\), \(N=M-1\), and their dyadic row subfamilies. It is not a lower bound for every certificate.

Projection and proof. Set every old entry of \(A\) to zero. Then all \(s_i\) vanish, all literal masks \(1+E_{ij}\) equal one, and \(K_{ij}=(ZZ^{\mathsf T})_{ij}\). Every matching with an unmatched vertex contributes zero, so the complete correction is precisely the perfect-matching polynomial of that off-diagonal matrix. In the first \(r\) columns set

\[Z=\begin{pmatrix}I_r\\X\end{pmatrix},\]

and set any remaining columns to zero. Distinct rows in the first block have inner product zero, while the cross entry between row \(i\) and row \(r+j\) is \(x_{ji}\). Every first-block vertex must therefore be matched to the second block. There are equally many vertices in the blocks, so all matching edges are cross edges; none of the possibly nonzero second-block inner products can be used. The resulting complete polynomial is \(\operatorname{perm}(X)=\det(X)\) in characteristic two. This evaluates every term and every mask of \(Q_{\rm match}\); no coefficient, derivative or growing homogeneous component is extracted. Setting the parity and off-diagonal placeholders \(Y\) to zero makes \(1+\prod(1+Y)=0\), proving the full-certificate assertion.

Every substitution is a constant or a single independent variable, so gate/wire size and depth do not increase. Circuit existence at polynomial size and fixed depth for these chosen polynomials would imply the same for binary determinants, contrary to cor:binary-determinant-depth-gap. Its complete explicit reduction was reread: MOD3 on \(n\) bits is an affine substitution into two determinants of order \(2n+3\), followed by adding one. It uses only binary constants; arithmetic sums/products become parity/AND gates on Boolean inputs. This is an application of the registered Smolensky consequence, not a new audit of its primary source. If only \(r=2^a\) is supplied, pad any determinant of order \(k\) by an identity block to the next power of two, of order less than \(2k\). Thus the sparse row subfamily does not evade that contradiction.

Exact scope. The free-placeholder projection is not the substitution \(Z=b(A)\). At \(A=0\), that actual Boolean tuple is zero, whereas the projection retains an identity block and free \(X\). It is a valid restriction of a formal certificate polynomial, and a hard Boolean function on those free placeholder inputs. No circuit complexity of the original source's values follows. The older register embedding was killed on the Boolean cube by its literal masks; here those masks are identically one because they depend on old \(A\), not on free \(Z\). The two calculations are different. The polynomial-size growing-depth circuit remains valid; its growing depth is consistent with this fixed-depth obstruction.

Gram row cut

Statement. For any \(M>N\ge1\), put \(s_i=\sum_\ell a_{i\ell}\), \(W=\prod_{i<j}(1+(AA^{\mathsf T})_{ij})\), \(R=W\prod_i s_i\), and \(R_i=W\prod_{j\ne i}s_j\). Suppose a supplied correction satisfies \(Q(A,0)=0,Q(A,b)=R\). Define

\[Q_i(A,Z)=s_iQ(A,Z)+R_i(A)\sum_\ell z_{i\ell}.\]

Then \(Q_i(A,0)=0,Q_i(A,b)=R\), and \(Q_i(0,Z)=0\) for every free \(Z\). From a size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) unbounded-fanin binary circuit for \(Q\), this has size \(O(S+M^2N)\), depth at most \(\max(d+2,6)\), joint degree at most \(D'=\max(\deg Q+1,M^2)\), and placeholder degree at most \(\max(\deg_ZQ,1)\). All original Boolean placeholders remain available. Placeholder linearity is preserved if supplied; row invariance and exact simulation provenance are not asserted for this residual transformation.

Proof. The sum of the original Boolean fields in row \(i\) is \(\sum_\ell b_{i\ell}=s_i^2+s_i\). Consequently

\[Q_i(A,b)=s_i(s_iR_i)+R_i(s_i^2+s_i)=s_iR_i=R.\]

The zero-placeholder identity is immediate from the two summands. Since \(M\ge2\), at \(A=0\) both \(s_i\) and \(R_i\) are zero; the latter still contains the other rows' sum factors. This proves whole-slice erasure independently of the free placeholders. Share \(Q\), compute the row sums and Gram masks with \(O(M^2N)\) wires, and form the displayed two products and sum. The Gram inner products have depth two, masks depth three, \(R_i\) depth four, its placeholder product depth five, and the final sum depth six. This proves the circuit bounds. The degree of \(R_i\) is at most \(M^2-1\); multiplying it by a linear placeholder sum and multiplying \(Q\) by \(s_i\) gives the stated joint and placeholder degrees. No PC-degree transfer is inferred.

Cyclic repair. The erased slice can remain cheap even when cyclic invariance is imposed. Apply lem:residual-orbit-norm to \(Q_i\), the invariant target \(R\), and the full row cycle \(G=C_M\). With \(H_M(R)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-2}R^j\), the resulting correction is

\[Q_{\rm cyc}=\prod_{g\in C_M}gQ_i+\bigl(R(A+Z)+R(A)\bigr)H_M(R(A)).\]

It is cyclically invariant and correct by that full recorded proof. At \(A=0\), every transformed \(Q_i\) is zero, \(R(0)=0\), and \(H_M(0)=1\), so

\[Q_{\rm cyc}(0,Z)=R(Z).\]

This is a polynomial-size constant-depth formal polynomial: use exactly the product-of-row-sums and Gram masks defining \(R\). In particular it is cheap under the determinant projection, even formally, and its Boolean values vanish because \(M>N\). The norm costs size \(O(MS+M^3N+M^2)\) and depth at most \(\max(d+4,9)\), using the preceding row-cut bounds and the target's depth four. Its joint degree is at most \(\max(MD',(M-1)M^2)\), and its placeholder degree at most \(\max(M\max(\deg_ZQ,1),M^2)\), exactly the recorded norm ledger. It need not remain placeholder-linear or in a simulation image. This does not contradict the sharp cyclic placeholder-degree bound: neither the new correction nor its slice is claimed to have low placeholder degree.

Prior mechanism and limit. This is an explicit residual-target use of the axiom-cut principle, not a new normalization paradigm. For the full parity input \(F_i=1+s_i\), lem:axiom-cut-normalization already gives \(C'=Y_i+s_iC\); after setting \(A=0,Y=0\), it is zero. The full cube-erasure theorem is an older broader point-value control. The residual formula above additionally keeps the specific target \(R\) and permits the paid cyclic composition. Every construction starts with a supplied correction or certificate. Applying it to a hard representative does not make the complete transformed polynomial cheap, and applying it hypothetically to a small one does not construct that missing small one. Thus the determinant restriction is not forced in either the general residual class or its cyclic subclass.
