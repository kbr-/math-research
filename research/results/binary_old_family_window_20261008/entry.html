8 October 2026 — Two expansion certificates cannot supply the moving midpoint window

Working bounds on published certificate parameters; no upper bound on actual PC hardness.

Question. On general-step and the binary lower-bound statement, can the strongest applicable clustered-expansion or immunity theorem furnish the new moving cutoff? The target approaches half the actual Boolean variable count. A theorem saying only that degree is linear does not establish this coefficient.

New mathematics. The respectful-expansion certificate is at most \(v/3\) in its nontrivial range, with an explicit singleton-range alternative. The recorded immunity certificate is at most \(v/4\) on an unsatisfiable system. These are general parameter ceilings, not another list of small hard families.

Goal progress. Unchanged. The results exclude two direct sources of the sufficient old-degree window, including respectful regroupings, but do not establish the lower bound or exclude sharper theorems for the same formulas.

General statement. prop:respectful-cutoff-ceiling and prop:immunity-cutoff-ceiling hold for every finite system in their stated regimes, over any field. They bound the numerical guarantees of the specified criteria, not refutation degree from above.

Respectful expansion has a certificate ceiling

Here is a self-contained formulation of the quantities used. Let \(\mathcal U\) be a finite family of polynomial clusters, \(Q\) a background system, and \(\mathcal V\) a finite family of variable sets covering their \(v\ge1\) Boolean variables. All polynomials are multilinear over a field. Adjacency means sharing a variable. A pair \((P,V)\) is respectful if a Boolean assignment to all variables in \(V\) satisfies every polynomial in \(P\), for every completion, and every background polynomial it touches. The respectful boundary of \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\) consists of its unique neighbouring variable sets whose unique edge is respectful. Put \(L=\max_x|\{V:x\in V\}|\ge1\).

Proposition (respectful cutoff ceiling). Suppose the full system \(Q\cup\bigcup\mathcal U\) is Boolean-unsatisfiable, while \(Q\cup\bigcup S\) is satisfiable for every \(|S|\le s\), where \(s\ge1\) is integral. Suppose \(|\partial_Q S|\ge\delta|S|-\xi\) in that range, with \(\delta>0\), \(\xi\ge0\). Let \(w\) bound the number of essential variables in each nonzero input polynomial. Then the certificate number \[C=\frac{\delta s-2\xi}{2L}\] satisfies \[ C\le \begin{cases} w/2-\xi/(2L),&s=1,\\ v/3-\xi/(2L),&s\ge2. \end{cases} \] Consequently the journal polynomial theorem, when its additional support hypothesis \(w\le C\) holds and \(C>0\), can certify at most \(C\le v/3\). For CNFs of width \(w=o(v)\), its threshold also stays at most \(v/3\) for sufficiently large instances. The zero-slack corollary is covered via its small-subfamily satisfiability conclusion.

Minimal-core reduction. Choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily \(\mathcal U_0\) such that \(Q\cup\bigcup\mathcal U_0\) is unsatisfiable. Write \(m=|\mathcal U_0|\); the local-satisfiability assumption gives \(m>s\), and includes satisfiability of \(Q\). A variable set having exactly one neighbour \(P\) in \(\mathcal U_0\) cannot have a respectful edge to \(P\). Otherwise combine a satisfying assignment of \(Q\cup\bigcup(\mathcal U_0\setminus\{P\})\) with that respectful assignment on \(V\). Other clusters in the core do not touch \(V\); touched background polynomials and \(P\) are satisfied by the respectful assignment; untouched ones retain their old values. The combined assignment satisfies the core, a contradiction. Respectfulness depends on \(P,V,Q\), not on which other clusters are currently selected.

Singleton-intersection bound. If a uniformly random \(s\)-subset of an \(m\)-set meets a fixed \(t\)-subset, with \(s,t\ge2\), in a random number \(X\) of elements, then \(\Pr(X=1)\le2/3\). If \(p_1=\Pr(X=1)=0\), this is immediate. Otherwise put \(z=m-s-t+2\ge1\). Hypergeometric counting gives \[ \frac{p_0}{p_1}=\frac{z-1}{st},\qquad \frac{p_2}{p_1}=\frac{(s-1)(t-1)}{2z}. \] For \(s=t=2\), their sum minus \(1/2\) is \((z-1)(z-2)/(4z)\ge0\), since \(z\) is a positive integer. Otherwise \(st\ge6\) and \((s-1)(t-1)/(st)\ge1/3\); the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives a sum at least \(\sqrt{2/3}-1/6>1/2\). Thus \(1\ge p_0+p_1+p_2\ge3p_1/2\). Equality in the bound occurs, for example, at \(m=3,s=t=2\). The restriction \(t\ge2\) is essential; that is why the minimal-core step precedes averaging.

Finish when \(s\ge2\). Choose a uniform \(s\)-subset \(S\) of the core. A variable cluster with zero core neighbours never contributes. One with exactly one core neighbour cannot contribute respectfully by the preceding argument. Every other cluster contributes with probability at most \(2/3\). Empty variable sets contribute nothing. The number of nonempty variable clusters is at most \(\sum_V|V|\le Lv\). Hence \[ \delta s-\xi\le\mathbb E|\partial_Q S|\le\frac23 Lv. \] Subtract another \(\xi\) and divide by \(2L\) to obtain the claim. This argument allows arbitrary overlapping clusters, non-respectful edges and additive slack; it does not use the input-width bound or multilinearity. Those are needed for the singleton branch. Full variable coverage is retained for the published source setting, although containment in the counted variable universe suffices for this bound.

Finish when \(s=1\). Some cluster contains a nonzero polynomial \(f\), since the background is satisfiable and the full system is not. Every respectful neighbour of that cluster must meet \(\operatorname{Vars}(f)\): otherwise assigning its variables would leave the nonzero multilinear function \(f\) unchanged. There are at most \(Lw\) such neighbours. Singleton expansion gives \(\delta-\xi\le Lw\), and therefore \(C\le w/2-\xi/(2L)\). If the polynomial theorem also requires \(w\le C\), a positive \(C\) in this case is impossible. \(\square\)

The proof needs no PC inference or degree-lower-bound theorem. It bounds the parameters that the published theorem can receive. Its strict degree conclusion may add the usual integer rounding of at most one; this does not approach the moving half-variable target. In particular, this is not the statement that these formulas have PC refutations of degree at most \(v/3\).

The immunity certificate has a separate ceiling

Proposition (immunity cutoff ceiling). Let an unsatisfiable Boolean system \(f_1=\cdots=f_m=0\) of multilinear polynomials over a field have an incidence matrix with \(v\) columns, row supports \(J_i\) of size at most \(w\), and \(\operatorname{Vars}(f_i)\subseteq J_i\). Suppose every row subset of size at most the positive integer \(R\) has boundary at least \(c\) times its size, and all \(f_i\) have common semantic immunity \(\nu\): every nonzero Boolean polynomial, represented multilinearly, vanishing on all Boolean zeros of \(f_i\) has degree at least \(\nu\). Then any positive certificate \[ B=R\bigl(\nu/4-(w-c)\bigr) \] satisfies \(B\le v/4\). In particular the additional applicability requirement \(w\le B\) in the recorded theorem cannot enlarge this threshold.

Proof. Some \(f_i\) is nonzero, and it semantically implies itself; hence \(\nu\le\deg f_i\le w\). Singleton expansion gives \(c\le w\). Positivity implies \(\nu>0\) and \(w-c<\nu/4\).

We first justify \(R<m\), rather than assume it. If \(R\ge m\), every nonempty row subfamily has a row with at most \(w-c<\nu\) variables shared with the other rows: its total private-variable count is the boundary, at least \(c\) times the row count, while total incidences are at most \(w\) times that count. Solve the remaining rows inductively. Every assignment to the removed row's shared variables extends to a zero of its polynomial using its private variables. Otherwise the nonzero indicator of that shared assignment would be a semantic consequence of \(f_i\), of degree below \(\nu\), contradicting immunity. A zero polynomial is automatic. This constructs a solution of the full system, a contradiction.

We can now take a row subset of size exactly \(R\), so \(cR\le v\). Put \(b=w-\nu/4\ge0\). Since \(c>b\) and \(c\le w\), \[ B=R(c-b)\le v\left(1-\frac bc\right) \le v\left(1-\frac bw\right) =\frac{v\nu}{4w}\le\frac v4. \] This proves the parameter ceiling. It allows incidence padding; the added columns still count among the \(v\) variables. \(\square\)

The stronger assignment-indicator criterion is not omitted. Its equations are forbidden Boolean assignments, hence clause falsity polynomials. With singleton variable clusters and empty background, all incidence edges are respectful, and zero-slack boundary expansion gives the required local satisfiability by the usual private-variable peeling. The respectful proposition therefore bounds its \(Rc/2\) certificate by \(v/3\) when \(R\ge2\), and by \(w/2\) when \(R=1\). This again concerns the stated certificate, not actual degree.

Consequences for the family search

The strongest checked respectful criterion cannot supply the moving midpoint window merely by choosing a better expander or regrouping the same bounded-width formula. The general immunity threshold and its assignment-indicator variant also do not supply it. A different degree theorem or a sharper proof of the old system's hardness is needed. The earlier private-coefficient audit had ruled out one particular ENS clustering; the present result is a different obstruction on the achievable certificate coefficient, even when respectful hypotheses do hold.

The preliminary web search found no applicable near-half-variable old lower bound. An apparent half-variable random-CNF search snippet came from a badly extracted lecture slide, not a usable theorem; the primary formulas and field restrictions were checked before relying on it. This is a bounded search result, not a claim that all literature has been exhausted.
