Arithmetic nuclear rank

Lemma. Let \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z\), reducing to \(\mathbb F_2\), or \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\zeta]\), \(\zeta^2+\zeta+1=0\), embedded in \(\mathbb C\) and reducing modulo two to \(\mathbb F_4\). For every rectangular matrix \(A\) over \(\mathcal O\), \[\operatorname{rank}(\bar A)\le\|A\|_*,\qquad \|A\|_*\ge\sqrt{\#\{(i,j):\bar A_{ij}\ne0\}},\] where \(\|A\|_*\) is the sum of its real or complex singular values. If \(A\) instead has a positive odd common integer denominator \(q\), so \(qA\) is integral, the valid rank bound is \(\operatorname{rank}(\bar A)\le q\|A\|_*\). More generally fix a matrix \(R\) over \(\mathcal O/2^{v+1}\) divisible by \(2^v\), in the specified coordinate basis. An explicitly chosen integral representative \(\widetilde R\in2^v\mathcal O^{u\times H}\) of \(R\) satisfies \[\operatorname{rank}(\operatorname{def}_v R)\le2^{-v}\|\widetilde R\|_* .\] This last norm belongs to the chosen representative, not intrinsically to a residue class.

Proof. If the residue rank is \(r>0\), some \(r\)-square minor has determinant nonzero modulo two. Its determinant is a nonzero integer, or a nonzero Eisenstein integer. In the latter case \(|a+b\zeta|^2=a^2-ab+b^2\) is a positive integer, so in both cases its absolute value is at least one. If \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r\) are the singular values of this square minor, their product is at least one. Arithmetic-geometric mean gives \(\sum_i\sigma_i\ge r\). Row and column coordinate compression cannot increase nuclear norm: compress the singular-value rank-one decomposition of the full matrix, and use the triangle inequality and the fact that projected unit vectors have norm at most one. Thus \(\|A\|_*\ge r\); rank zero is immediate. Each entry with nonzero residue is a nonzero lattice element of modulus at least one, so \(\|A\|_*\ge\|A\|_F\ge\sqrt{s}\), proving the support bound. Multiplication by odd \(q\) preserves residue rank and scales nuclear norm by \(q\). Finally \(2^{-v}\widetilde R\) is an actual integral lift of the deflated residue matrix; apply the first statement to it. \(\square\)

For an actual binary error map extended to \(\mathbb F_4\), binary descent preserves its rank and a nonzero common kernel. Thus an explicit lift with norm below \(H\) is sufficient at the existing degree window. This does not follow merely from real signed mass or a low-degree description. Identity and zero matrices give the exact full-rank and zero controls. If an explicit factorization \(A=UV\) is available over the real or complex numbers, \(\|A\|_*\le\|U\|_F\|V\|_F\): expand the product by matching columns/rows, use rank-one nuclear norms and Cauchy–Schwarz. The equality must be for the chosen integral lift, not for an unrelated real approximation.

Selector lift norm floor

Corollary. In the character basis of the full \(r\)-coordinate ternary label algebra over \(\mathbb F_4\), \(r\ge1\), multiplication by the indicator that all labels are zero has rank one, but every Eisenstein-integer lift of its matrix has nuclear norm at least \(H=3^r\). The bound is attained. This is a basis-specific limitation, not a statement that no useful lift exists after a justified basis change.

Proof. In characteristic two the indicator is \(E=\prod_{i=1}^r(1+U_i+U_i^2)\) with \(U_i^3=1\): the usual factor \(3^{-r}\) equals one in the residue field. Multiplication by any character fixes \(E\), so every column in the character basis consists of ones. The matrix is \(J_H\), of rank one. Its \(H^2\) nonzero residues force norm at least \(H\) by the lemma. The integer all-one matrix itself has a single singular value \(H\), attaining the bound. In the point-indicator basis the same operation is a diagonal rank-one projector and has a lift of norm one. Changing bases is therefore mathematically consequential for this estimate. This full label-algebra control does not identify a global satisfying-point algebra for the inconsistent charged base. \(\square\)

Two-local splitting gap

Corollary. For each \(r\ge1\), there are two integral real PSD matrices with zero pairwise overlap for which the zero-loss orthogonal splitting has unavoidable denominators divisible by two. Consequently the real orthogonal-splitting theorem cannot in general be performed by projections over \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), even on integral inputs.

Proof. Put \(U=\binom{I_r}{I_r}\), \(V=\binom{I_r}{-I_r}\), \(A=UU^{\mathsf T}\), \(B=VV^{\mathsf T}\). Their images are orthogonal over the reals, and \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)=0\). Any complementary orthogonal projections \(P_1,P_2\) satisfying the real splitting estimate must have \(P_1BP_1=P_2AP_2=0\), because the sum of their squared Frobenius norms is bounded by zero. Positivity gives \(P_1V=0\) and \(P_2U=0\), hence \(P_1U=U\). These two images span the full space, so \[P_1=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}I_r&I_r\\I_r&I_r\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_2=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}I_r&-I_r\\-I_r&I_r\end{pmatrix}.\] Their entries are not in \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\). Modulo two, \(U=V\); the existing radical-rank split correctly pays their isotropic image cost instead of predicting a zero-loss split. This is the same characteristic-two collapse made explicit at the arithmetic projection level, not a refutation of real smoothing or of the field-native rank theorem. \(\square\)
