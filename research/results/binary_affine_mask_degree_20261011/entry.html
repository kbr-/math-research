Affine flat vanishing degree

Theorem. Let \(\mathbb F_q\) be a finite field, \(K\) a field containing it, and \(v\ge1\), \(0\le k

Lower bound. Choose an affine \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace \(W\) of smallest dimension \(r\) on which \(f\) restricts to a nonzero polynomial \(g\). Such a space exists, since the whole ambient space is allowed. A flat of dimension at most \(k\) extends to one of dimension \(k\), so the hypothesis implies \(r\ge k+1\). Parameterize \(W\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) by \(z\in K^r\). By minimality, \(g\) vanishes formally on every rational affine hyperplane of \(K^r\).

For each one-dimensional normal direction, choose a nonzero vector \(a\in\mathbb F_q^r\) whose first nonzero coordinate is one. Its \(q\) hyperplanes have equations \(a\cdot z-c=0\), \(c\in\mathbb F_q\). A linear coordinate change over \(\mathbb F_q\) makes \(a\cdot z\) a coordinate, so zero formal restriction implies \(a\cdot z-c\mid g\). These normalized linear polynomials are pairwise nonassociate irreducibles even over \(K\): equality up to a scalar would, from the normalized coordinate, force that scalar to be one and both \(a,c\) to agree. Unique factorization therefore makes their product divide \(g\). There are \((q^r-1)/(q-1)\) normal directions and \(q\) translates each. Consequently \[ \deg f\ge\deg g\ge q\frac{q^r-1}{q-1}\ge\mu_q(k). \]

Upper bound. On the first \(k+1\) variables put \[ H(x)=\prod_{[a]\in\mathbb P^k(\mathbb F_q)}\ \prod_{c\in\mathbb F_q} \bigl(a\cdot(x_1,\ldots,x_{k+1})-c\bigr), \] using the same normalized representatives. It is a product of \(\mu_q(k)\) nonzero linear polynomials, hence is nonzero of exactly that degree. For any \(\psi(t)=Ut+c_0\) with \(k\) parameters, its first \(k+1\) coordinates have linear rank at most \(k\). A nonzero rational row vector \(a\) annihilates that projected linear part; normalize it. Then \(a\cdot\psi_{[k+1]}(t)\) is the constant \(a\cdot(c_0)_{[k+1]}\in\mathbb F_q\), and the corresponding factor of \(H\) is identically zero. This proves the required vanishing for every affine flat, completing equality. \(\square\)

Detection interpretation. The joint restriction map to all these flats is injective on polynomials of total degree less than \(\mu_q(k)\): the difference of two polynomials with identical restrictions would violate the lower bound. No bound on the number of flats needed, efficient reconstruction, or circuit complexity of the joint data is implied.

Nonvacuity. The explicit \(H\) works for every \(v\ge k+1\) and every finite field; its construction supplies unbounded variable counts. At \(k=0\), \(H=x_1^q-x_1\) and the threshold is \(q\). At \(k=v-1\), it is the product of all rational affine hyperplanes. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) at \(k=1\), the two-variable product is \(x_1(x_1+1)x_2(x_2+1)(x_1+x_2)(x_1+x_2+1)\), of degree six. These examples instantiate one all-parameter proof, not an enumerated-case inference.

Degenerate cases. At \(k=v\), the identity restriction forces \(f=0\), so the nonzero minimum does not exist; that case is excluded. The zero polynomial is excluded throughout. Merely vanishing at rational points would have minimum \(q\) regardless of \(k\), witnessed by \(x_1^q-x_1\); for \(k\ge1\), its restriction to a line varying \(x_1\) is formally nonzero. Thus the stronger restriction hypothesis is essential. Nothing about Hasse multiplicity is assumed.

Proof. The previous necessary-condition argument applies to each flat: \(\rho(M)\rho(C)=0\), while \(\rho(C)\) specializes at \(\rho(F)\) to one and is nonzero. The polynomial ring in parameters and placeholders is a domain, so \(\rho(M)=0\). Apply the theorem with \(q=2\). For the circuit bound, every gate has fan-in at most \(S\); by induction, formal degree at a depth-\(j\) gate is at most \(S^j\), since sums take maxima and products sum the degrees of their inputs, counting repeated wires. Actual degree is no greater. The asserted inequality and asymptotic consequence follow. \(\square\)

Cost control. The explicit hyperplane product has size \(O((k+1)\mu_q(k))\) and a constant number of arithmetic layers when written as linear factors followed by their product. Its exponential size is paid. Repeated squaring can create large degree at growing depth, so the corollary is not a size lower bound for unrestricted-depth circuits. For \(k=O(\log N)\), the degree bound alone yields at most a polynomial size lower bound; it does not settle even the fixed-depth superpolynomial goal.

The zero-mask alternative. If the patch is defined by \(M=1-A(x,F)\), then \(M=0\) says precisely that \(A\) is already a complete formal certificate (provided it vanishes at zero placeholders). The theorem does not exclude that possibility. More generally it bounds this scalar patch form only: neither cheap individual slice data nor expensive common masking proves that an arbitrary full certificate is large.

Parameter application. In the rank family \(m=2n\), there are \(v=4n^2\) variables and input length \(N\) polynomial in \(n\). Taking \(k=n\) gives the displayed exponential mask degree and the size lower bound \((2^{n+2}-2)^{1/d}\) for fixed \(d\). No claim is made that every refutation supplies such a nonzero mask. For polynomial-degree certificates, logarithmic-dimensional affine restrictions collectively detect polynomial differences, but no efficient global hard invariant or reconstruction is furnished. That collective compatibility task is still an open sufficient approach, not a necessary form of every proof of the Frege goal.
