Four fibre ideals in one graded algebra

Let \(F\) be any field. Let \(A,B,C\) be disjoint finite sets of Boolean coordinates, where \(C\) consists of free spectators, and let \(z\) be another coordinate. Put \[R=F[A,B,C]/(x^2-x),\qquad \mathcal B=R[z]/(z^2-z),\] with their ordinary multilinear degree filtrations. Take an ideal on \(\{z\}\cup A\) and one on \(\{z\}\cup B\), and extend them to ideals \(I,J\subseteq\mathcal B\). Their fibres at \(z=\epsilon\) extend to ideals \(U_\epsilon,V_\epsilon\subseteq R\), respectively. In particular \(U_\epsilon\) is generated on \(A\), and \(V_\epsilon\) on \(B\). Empty or full allowed fibres are permitted.

Let \(T=\operatorname{gr}R\cong F[t_x:x\in A\cup B\cup C]/(t_x^2)\). Write \(\mathfrak a_\epsilon=\operatorname{gr}U_\epsilon\), \(\mathfrak b_\epsilon=\operatorname{gr}V_\epsilon\), as homogeneous ideals in this same algebra. Define \[\mathcal M= \frac{(\mathfrak a_0+\mathfrak b_0)\cap(\mathfrak a_1+\mathfrak b_1)} {(\mathfrak a_0\cap\mathfrak a_1)+(\mathfrak b_0\cap\mathfrak b_1)}.\tag{1}\] The denominator is contained in the numerator and both are ideals, so this is a graded \(T\)-module. Also put \[L=I+J,\qquad K_d=(I\cap\mathcal B_{\le d})+(J\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}),\qquad H_{d+1}=\frac{K_{d+1}\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}}{K_d}.\tag{2}\] Closure at cutoff \(D\) means \(x(K_D\cap\mathcal B_{\le D-1})\subseteq K_D\) for every variable. All intersections and sums in (1) are taken inside \(T\); no ranks from different quotients are subtracted.

Theorem (separator defect module). For every \(d\ge0\), taking the degree-\(d\) leading part of the fibre at \(z=0\) induces canonical isomorphisms \[\frac{L\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}}{K_d}\ \cong\ H_{d+1}\ \cong\ \mathcal M_d.\tag{3}\] At cutoff \(d+1\), the fixed-cutoff defect map for a private or spectator coordinate \(x\) corresponds to \[t_x:\mathcal M_d\longrightarrow\mathcal M_{d+1}.\tag{4}\] Its target is the subspace \((L\cap\mathcal B_{\le d+1})/K_{d+1}\) of the full quotient \(\mathcal B_{\le d+1}/K_{d+1}\), not that entire quotient. The defect map for \(z\) is zero. Consequently \(K_{d+1}\) is closed under variable multiplication of predecessors of degree at most \(d\), at cutoff \(d+1\) exactly when every map (4) vanishes. Unit survival must still be checked separately.

Strict splitting within a fixed fibre. Because \(U_\epsilon\) and \(V_\epsilon\) are generated on disjoint private sets, every element of \((U_\epsilon+V_\epsilon)\cap R_{\le e}\) splits into pieces of degree at most \(e\) in those ideals. One direct proof chooses filtered bases of the two private Boolean algebras adapted to their respective ideals, completing the ideal basis at each filtration level. Tensor them, with the spectator monomial basis. The resulting tensor basis is adapted to total degree, and the sum ideal is spanned by the basis tensors having at least one ideal factor. Separating those tensors proves the assertion, including zero or unit ideals. In particular \[\operatorname{gr}(U_\epsilon+V_\epsilon)=\mathfrak a_\epsilon+\mathfrak b_\epsilon.\tag{5}\] This is the disjoint-fibre step of the existing decomposition proof.

The obstruction to spending no separator degree. Write \(f\in\mathcal B\) uniquely as \(f_0+z(f_1-f_0)\). It has degree at most \(d\) exactly when both fibres have degree at most \(d\) and their degree-\(d\) leading parts agree. An element of \(L\) has \(f_\epsilon\in U_\epsilon+V_\epsilon\). By (5), its common leading part belongs to the numerator of (1). Every element of that numerator occurs: choose two fibre polynomials with that common leading part and interpolate them with \(1-z,z\).

The leading part of the zero fibre of an element of \(I\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}\) lies in \((\mathfrak a_0)_d\cap(\mathfrak a_1)_d\), and every such element is obtained by choosing its two lifts separately and interpolating; their equal leading parts keep the degree at \(d\). The same holds for \(J\). Hence \(K_d\) maps onto the denominator of (1).

It remains to check the kernel, not just count dimensions. Suppose the common leading part of \(f\) is \(a+b\), with \(a\) in the first intersection of the denominator and \(b\) in the second. Choose \(g_\epsilon\in U_\epsilon\), \(h_\epsilon\in V_\epsilon\) of degree at most \(d\) lifting \(a,b\). Then \(f_\epsilon-g_\epsilon-h_\epsilon\) has degree at most \(d-1\) and belongs to \(U_\epsilon+V_\epsilon\). Split it through that degree using the preceding paragraph. Adding those lower-degree pieces to \(g_\epsilon,h_\epsilon\) gives separate fibre pairs with common leading parts \(a,b\). Their interpolants belong to \(I\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}\) and \(J\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}\) and sum to \(f\). For \(d=0\), the lower-degree remainders are zero. This proves the first quotient identification.

The one-step fall and its action. Independently split each fibre of an element of \(L\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}\) through degree \(d\). Multiplying its zero-fibre pieces by \(1-z\) and its one-fibre pieces by \(z\) puts it in \(K_{d+1}\). Thus \(K_{d+1}\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}=L\cap\mathcal B_{\le d}\), proving (3). For \(x\ne z\), the top private part of \((xf)|_{z=0}\) in degree \(d+1\) is exactly \(t_x\) times the degree-\(d\) top of \(f_0\); repeated variables vanish at the associated-graded level. This yields (4) in the target subspace specified above. For \(zf\), the zero fibre is zero, so its class in that target vanishes. Finally, \(xK_d\subseteq K_{d+1}\) for every variable. Therefore only the classes in \(H_{d+1}\) can obstruct multiplication at the cutoff, as in prop:fixed-cutoff-cover-transport, giving the closure criterion. \(\square\)

The complete majority module

Corollary (majority fall module). Consider the opposite-majority pair from the earlier obstruction, with \(|A|=|B|=2k\), \(k\ge1\), shared bit \(z\), and \(c\ge0\) spectators. Over every field, its module (1) has one basis vector for every pair of \(k\)-subsets of \(A,B\), tensored with the square-zero spectator algebra, shifted to degree \(2k\). The \(A,B\) coordinates annihilate this module. In particular \[\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal M;t)=\binom{2k}{k}^{\!2}t^{2k}(1+t)^c.\tag{6}\] At cutoff \(D\ge2k+1\), put \(j=D-1-2k\). Then \[\dim H_D=\binom{2k}{k}^{\!2}\binom cj.\tag{7}\] For \(c\ge1\), each spectator defect map has rank \(\binom{2k}{k}^{2}\binom{c-1}{j}\), taking binomial coefficients outside their usual range as zero. All private and separator defect maps are zero. The complete separate local sum is multiplication-closed at such a cutoff exactly when \[D\ge2k+c+1.\tag{8}\] It preserves one at every cutoff because the pair is satisfiable.

Proof. Complement the \(A\) variables and leave \(z,B,C\) unchanged. The first allowed fibre is the Hamming ball of radius \(k-1\) at \(z=0\), and radius \(k\) at \(z=1\). Its leading ideals are generated by the \(A\)-monomials of degrees \(k\) and \(k+1\), respectively. For the second member, the \(B\)-fibre ideals have thresholds \(k+1\) and \(k\). The Hamming-ball monomial description follows from the inclusion-evaluation basis, as proved in the earlier majority record; no numerical interpolation hypothesis is added.

All four leading ideals are monomial ideals. In the numerator of (1), a monomial must either have at least \(k+1\) variables in either private block, or have at least \(k\) variables from each private block. The denominator removes exactly the monomials with more than \(k\) variables in either block. The remaining basis monomials use exactly \(k\) coordinates from each block, and any spectator subset. This proves the module description and (6)–(7). A private multiplication either repeats a variable or raises its block count to \(k+1\), so it acts as zero. A spectator multiplies precisely the subsets not already containing it, giving the rank formula. For \(0\le j<c\) some such action is nonzero; for \(j\ge c\) all actions vanish. This proves (8), including \(c=0\). The original pair has the joint model \(z=0,A\equiv1,B\equiv0\), with arbitrary spectators, proving unit survival. \(\square\)

The ordinary input-width cost is unchanged: local monotone consequences follow by summing original forbidden indicators through \(2k+1\), and final-line multiplication derives every local ideal element through the maximum of that width and its own degree. Thus the module refines prop:majority-local-span-gap rather than treating Boolean reduction as free PC. With no spectators, the fall in degree \(2k\) is nonzero but all its next actions vanish; with spectators, every nonzero fall below the top spectator layer can be moved outside the local sum by some spectator. This proves the full pattern, not only the earlier single witness.

This is a local, satisfiable calibration. At fixed width and increasing free-variable count, (8) is far above the desired near-half cutoff. Taking \(k\) proportional to the variable count would instead make the canonical truth-table width and encoding grow beyond the fixed-width source setting. Neither choice is an unsatisfiable-family non-vacuity argument.

A compatibility control from the source

Control (Hardy selector failure). On contexts \((a,b),(a,b'),(a',b),(a',b')\), take the source's coefficient vectors, in tuple order \(00,01,10,11\), \[r_1=(1,0,0,0),\quad r_2=(0,1,1,-1),\quad r_3=r_4=(0,0,1,0).\tag{9}\] They have matching overlap marginals over every field. Apply the selector \(1-a\) to the components that contain \(a\), leaving the other two components unchanged. Then \(r_2\) becomes \((0,1,0,0)\): its \(b'\)-marginal is \((0,1)\), whereas that of \(r_4\) is \((1,0)\). Hence this specified selector does not preserve compatibility, even though all component masses remain one. This does not exclude operators that update other components, or a different filtered construction. The source's compatible family is not itself a PC invariant.

Checks and remaining global obligation

The Hardy marginal and selector identities pass over \(\mathbb Q,\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3\); their displayed algebraic argument covers every field. The initial census already passed, but lacked the opposite-majority and full spectator-module checks, so those were added in a second bounded run. Both full outputs are retained. No larger computation is needed to prove the all-parameter monomial description.

Decision. Use the module action, not the fall-space dimension alone, when testing this local construction. The one-bit fibre cost disappears at degree \(d\) exactly when \(\mathcal M_d=0\), whereas fixed-cutoff multiplication only asks that the next actions vanish. These are different requirements. For a full unsatisfiable family, additional members may absorb the pair's images, while genuinely multi-member relations may create new ones. The two-member formula proves neither global generation nor preservation of one. The old degreewise-gluing counterexamples already forbid assuming pairwise checks settle the whole cover.

Follow-up. Continuing the local-section lead. The one-bit separator calculation and majority test are complete. A larger local matrix or another additive separator bound would leave the necessary implication untouched.
