
###term-value-clamping
3. What a generic source needs instead: a joint choice of term values
The weight is one way to make a design give every high product the value zero. Nothing forces a design to be of weight form. Let \(\mathcal F\) be a base containing the Boolean equations, let \(T\) be a set of first-level blocks with exact flat indicators \(Z_c=\prod_i(1+L_{c,i})\) of degree at most \(w\), and let \(e\in\mathbb F_2^T\).
Reduction. Call a source pure over \(T\) if it consists of the blocks of \(T\) and of higher blocks each of whose inputs is an \(\mathbb F_2\)-affine combination of earlier products, with no old variables, no raw coefficients and no products of products. If \(\mathcal F\cup\{Z_c+e_c:c\in T\}\) has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree \(B\), then no source pure over \(T\) has a refutation of degree \(B/\max(1,w-1)\). Any accuracy is allowed. The statement uses of the base only its Boolean equations and of \(T\) only the exactness of the flat indicators; it extends the recorded constant-value profiles of eligible zero clamps (zero-value profile, shared core) from zero clamps supplied by the weight to arbitrary constants under a hardness hypothesis. Proof: the telescoping substitution gives each block of \(T\) the product image \(Z_c\) with coefficient images of degree at most \(w-1\). A reader of products of \(T\) has inputs congruent to constants modulo the added axioms. If some input \(g\) is congruent to 1, the first coefficient row that selects this input alone gives the product image \(1+g\), a constant combination of the added axioms, hence congruent to 0, and every companion image is a multiple of it. If all inputs are congruent to 0, the zero coefficient rows give the product image 1, and each companion image is an input, again a combination of the added axioms. Either way the reader's product is congruent to a constant, and the same argument applies one level up. The images of the axioms of the source have certificates within \(\max(1,w-1)\) times their original degrees (companions of \(T\): \(w+1\) against \(3(w-1)\) for \(w\ge2\); field equations: \(2(w-1)\); reader companions: at most \(2w\) against \(5(w-1)\)). Every line of a refutation of the source maps to a consequence of the enlarged base of degree at most \(\max(1,w-1)\) times its own. \(\square\) (A reader that also has a nonconstant affine part becomes a first-level block of the quotient and joins \(T\) with its own value; this case is not needed below.)
So for a generic pure source the general step would follow, with the factor \(w-1\), from a statement in the old variables about the base with the first-level terms of the source adjoined: some assignment of values to the terms keeps the PHP hard. For every reader both outcomes are acceptable, since a reader with one true term is 0 and a reader with no true term is 1; the signs \(e_c\) are ours to choose.
Lemma S (the recorded case split, in polynomial-calculus form). The Nullstellensatz form over every \(\mathbb F_p\), with loss \((p-1)s\) for a query of degree \(s\), its contrapositive and its iteration along a query tree are recorded in the case-split budget; the polynomial-calculus proof and the phrasing for term values are the only additions. Let \(Z\) be an old polynomial of degree \(w\le B\) with \(Z^2-Z\) in the Boolean ideal through \(2w\). If \(\mathcal F\cup\{Z\}\) and \(\mathcal F\cup\{1+Z\}\) both have refutations of degree \(B\), then \(\mathcal F\) has one of degree \(B+w\). Proof: multiply every line of the first refutation by \(1+Z\) and of the second by \(Z\). The axiom \(Z\) becomes \(Z(1+Z)\), a Boolean consequence through \(2w\le B+w\); the old axioms and the rules stay within \(B+w\). The two derivations end at \(1+Z\) and \(Z\), whose sum is 1. \(\square\)
Hence for any \(a\) flats some sign vector keeps the base hard through \(D_0-1-aw\), where \(D_0\) is its refutation degree. This loss is additive in the number of terms. The same conclusion is recorded: committing the values of whole input spaces by case splits costs up to \((p-1)rs\) and reproduces a rank-control obligation. A loss linear in the number of terms up to a logarithmic factor does occur for adversarial terms: the \(r\log_2N\) parity constraints that fix the labels of \(r\) rows lower the refutation degree of the PHP by about \(r/2\). The question left by the generic model is whether independent generic terms lose almost nothing:
Question H. The new content is whether generic terms lose less than the case-split ceiling. Let \(Z_1,\dots,Z_a\) be independent uniform flats of codimension \(w\) in the residual bit space, \(a\le n^c\), below the covering threshold with a margin: \(a\ln(1/(1-2^{-w}))\le(1-\delta)v\ln2\), and for \(w=1\) also \(v-a\ge CN\) (for \(w=1\) the constraints leave an affine subspace of dimension \(v-a\), and a search over its free bits refutes within about \(v-a\); for \(a>v\) the linear system is inconsistent and there is a refutation of degree one). Is \(\mathrm{PHP}_N\cup\{Z_c\}\) without refutations of degree \(N/8\), with high probability? And near and above the covering threshold, is there always either a covering certificate of affordable degree or a hard sign vector?
The recorded mechanisms do not answer it. The counting argument for a weight fails as above. A restriction fails by Lemma I: a generic form stays uniform under every restriction, and a sub-board on which \(a\) chosen dense forms are constant exists only for \(a\) up to about \(\log n/\log\log n\) (a row is usable with probability \(N'^{-a}\) per direction space). Lemma S loses \(w\) per term. The rank-cutoff failure does not apply, because values are imposed as axioms and hardness is asked of the whole system, so all relations among the terms are respected; but it shows that the values cannot be chosen block by block without the joint hardness statement. Question H is also not a statement about actual sources: for the terms "row \(i\) has label \(z\)" every sign vector contradicts the row axiom or a hole axiom at once, and such terms belong to the switching side. A hybrid (values for generic-looking terms, switching for literal terms) is not formulated here.

###dense-review-claim
2. The general claim of the line, stated once
Claim G (image or value). Let a source compiled from a proof of polynomial size over the compact bit base have \(n^c\) first-level terms, flats of codimension at most \(r\) in the bit space, and readers over them through a fixed number of levels. Then there are value axioms \(E\), each a linear equation in the bits or an equation \(Z_c=e_c\) for a term of the source, chosen with knowledge of the source, such that (i) the base with \(E\) adjoined has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree \(B\), and (ii) modulo \(E\) every block of the source has an old image of degree \(s\), certified by consequences of the base and \(E\), with \(s\) and the accumulated substitution factors within \(B\) over the needed proof degree. The design for the source is then a design of the enlarged base composed with the images, so everything the source can derive is derived from the enlarged base. The claim is stated for compiled sources only, which is all the goal needs. For arbitrary sources of the same shape it has an untested candidate counterexample, found by the reviewer: the disjoint union of a wide literal reader, which needs a restriction to a residual board of polylogarithmic dimension, and the row-mixing twisted source of Section 4, which needs about \(0.7v\) generic linear equations; the two systems of equations are inconsistent with each other, and under the compact restriction alone the twisted part becomes about \(v^{3.75}\) generic flats of codimension \(3\log_2v\) on polylogarithmically many bits, far above the covering threshold, whose readers have no low-degree images. No property of compiled sources is known that excludes such a union, so this is the first falsification target for the claim, and it arises without any mixed term.
The two lines of the last weeks are its extreme cases. For literal terms \(E\) is a matching restriction of the compact family, (i) is the preservation of the compact base, and (ii) is switching (Conjecture L). For generic dense terms in a pure source \(E\) consists of term values, (ii) is trivial by the reduction of the last entry, and (i) is Question H. The recorded structure of the base fits the same language: the compact bit base is itself a system of \(\binom{n+1}2\) avoidance axioms for flats of codimension \(\ell\), the collision flats \(\{\mathrm{label}_i=\mathrm{label}_{i'}\}\), of total density about \(n/2\), which is below the generic covering threshold \(v\ln2\) of the last entry although the structured flats do cover the space. Part (i) asks that further avoidance axioms or linear equations, generic or chosen from a proof, leave this system hard.


###dense-review-need
3. What the main goal needs from it
The goal needs Claim G for every source compiled from a small proof, with \(B\) and \(s\) polylogarithmic and the factors multiplied along a fixed number of levels. The line has not touched three parts of this. First, (i) has a recorded proof only for structured \(E\): axioms that confine every label to one flat, or that fix rows by a matching, leave a compact base with fewer holes, which is hard by the recorded decoder and bridge (Section 4). For generic \(E\) the notebook has no technique: the weight gives hardness only for avoiding flats above the high threshold, restrictions do not act on dense forms, the case split loses total rank, and a complete affine block never imposes a value. Second, different parts of one source may need incompatible axioms, as in the union above, and mixed terms, with a literal part and a dense part, need both kinds at once; no statement for either exists. Third, the choice of \(E\) must work for all readers of a source simultaneously; for generic terms this is automatic, for structured terms it is a combinatorial problem that has not been posed. What the line does supply is a formulation in which the universal obstruction does not apply, since images are certified relative to the enlarged base and not on the Boolean cube; Section 4 checks this on the obstruction's own source.


###dense-review-falsification
4. Falsification attempt: the approximate-majority source in dense coordinates
The recorded obstruction is a three-level evaluation source for an approximate majority \(C\) of the \(v=(n+1)\ell\) old bits: \(v\) sampled DNFs with \(r=\lceil4v(3/2)^t\rceil\) terms of \(t=\lceil3\log_2v\rceil\) positive literals, and their conjunction. Any old-only map certified on the Boolean cube needs \(\deg W+\deg\sigma(Z)\ge\lfloor v/3\rfloor+1\). For \(p=2\), an invertible affine map \(u=Ay+c\) of the bit space is a bijection of the cube that preserves the degree of multilinear representatives and nonzero Boolean remainders, and a map \(\sigma\) for the twisted source \(C(u)\) transports to one for \(C\) with the same degrees; so the same barrier holds for the twisted source with \(A\) a generic invertible matrix. (For odd \(p\) a parity of bits is not affine and the twist leaves the grammar.) Its bottom blocks have the inputs \(1-u_i\), dense affine forms, and its terms are flats of codimension about \(t\). The twisted source is the natural hard instance for Claim G on the dense side: no restriction simplifies it, since its forms are generic, and no Boolean-certified image exists.
The original coordinates, and twists row by row. Here Claim G holds in full with recorded hardness (the reviewer's observation, checked against the records). Let \(J\) consist of the same \(\ell'=\lfloor0.3\ell\rfloor\) bit positions of every label, fixed before the circuit is sampled, and let \(E\) set all other bit positions of every label to zero. By the count below, which applies to any fixed set of at most \(0.3v\) coordinates, every term of a suitable circuit contains a zeroed bit, so (ii) holds with constant images. The fixed positions contribute the factor 1 to every collision polynomial, so the base with \(E\) adjoined is the compact bit base of \(n+1\) rows and \(2^{\ell'}\) holes. It has no refutation through \(2^{\ell'}/2\), about \(n^{0.3}/2\): the decoder transfer holds for every number of rows and every ceiling when \(\ell'\ge2\), and the imported bound for the functional PHP holds whenever there are more rows than holes. The same is true for a twist that applies one affine bijection of \(\mathbb F_2^\ell\) to every label, since the base is invariant under relabelling the holes. So the recorded obstruction source does not resist value axioms; this is the escape that the obstruction entry itself names, a justified restriction that preserves a sufficient old barrier, with the indicator of the subspace, a weight of degree about \(0.7v\), replaced by adjoined axioms and a hardness statement. What is open is exactly a twist that mixes rows.
Values for the row-mixing twist. Fix a set \(J\) of at most \(0.3v\) coordinates and let \(I\) be its complement. A term of the recorded construction is a sample of \(t\) independent uniform coordinates, so it lies inside \(J\) with probability exactly \((|J|/v)^t\le0.3^t\), and the expected number of the \(vr\) terms inside \(J\) is at most \(v(4v(3/2)^t+1)\,0.3^t\le4v^2(0.45)^t+v\,0.3^t\le4v^{-1.45}+v^{-4}\), since \(t\ge3\log_2v\). Adding this event to the recorded existence argument keeps the total failure probability below one for \(v\ge8\), so there is an approximate-majority circuit of the recorded form every term of which contains a coordinate of \(I\). Take \(E=\{u_i=0:i\in I\}\). Modulo \(E\) every bottom product has the telescoping image \(\prod u_i\equiv0\), every middle block has all inputs congruent to 0 and the image 1, and the top block has an input congruent to 1 and an image congruent to 0, by the two cases of the recorded reduction; the substitution factor is \(t-1\) once. Part (ii) of Claim G holds for the twisted obstruction source, and the barrier does not apply because the image of the top block is certified modulo \(E\), not on the cube.
What this leaves. Part (i) for this \(E\): the compact base restricted to the generic affine subspace \(\{u_i=0,i\in I\}\) of codimension about \(0.7v\) must have no refutation of degree \(B\). This is Question H for parity constraints at linear codimension, before any restriction to a small board. It cannot be moved to the residual board of the compact family: there \(0.7v\) generic equations meet a space of polylogarithmic dimension and are inconsistent. So the attempt does not falsify Claim G, and for row-mixing twists it does not support it either: it shows that, for \(p=2\), the claim escapes the twisted obstruction exactly if the PHP stays hard on a generic affine subspace of linear codimension. The finite counts of the last entry do not bear on it: on eight holes, in the unary encoding, random parity constraints on the label bits were tolerated up to 20 of \(v=27\) at degree two and up to 14 at degree three, so codimension \(0.7v=19\) was tolerated at degree two only, and the free dimension \(v-a^*\) needed to stay unrefuted grew with the degree, from 6 to 12. That agrees with refutation by search over the free bits and says nothing about \(0.3v\) free dimensions at polylogarithmic degree.


###safe-systems-lemma
2. Safe systems keep the base hard: a corollary of the recorded detector
Let \(n=2^\ell\), \(\ell\ge2\), \(m=n+1\), let \(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun}\) be the functional unary base and \(\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q_{n,2}\) the compact base, with the affine decoder \(\tau\). A linear system \(\Phi\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the label bits is safe of rank \(a\) (definition of Efremenko, Garlík and Itsykson, as quoted above) if it is equivalent to equations \(L_i=0\), \(L_i=b_{i,j_i}+w_i+c_i\), \(i\in I\), with \(|I|=a\) distinct rows, one pivot position \(j_i\) in each, constants \(c_i\), and linear forms \(w_i\) in the non-pivot variables.
Corollary SL. Let \(\Phi\) be safe of rank \(a\) with \(4(a-1)<n\). Then every polynomial-calculus refutation of \(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun}\cup\tau\Phi\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), and hence every refutation of \(\mathcal Q\cup\Phi\), has degree at least \(n/2-a+1\). The right-hand sides \(c_i\) are arbitrary.
Proof (the reviewer's derivation, checked against the records). Let \(R=\prod_{i\in I}(1+L_i)\), the indicator of the solution space, of degree \(a\). Its monomial \(\prod_ib_{i,j_i}\) has coefficient one, because no \(w_{i'}\) contains a pivot, and it uses one bit in each of \(a\) distinct rows. Suppose \(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun}\cup\tau\Phi\) has a refutation of degree \(B\); a refutation of \(\mathcal Q\cup\Phi\) gives one by the decoder transfer, since the images \(\tau L_i\) are axioms of the target. Multiply every line by the weight \(\tau R\), using the recorded weighted replay. An old axiom \(g\) costs \(\deg g+a\). For the axiom \(\lambda=\tau L_i\) the weighted line is \(\tau\bigl(\prod_{i'\ne i}(1+L_{i'})\bigr)(\lambda^2+\lambda)\), and in characteristic two \(\lambda^2+\lambda=\sum_e(x_e^2-x_e)\) over the cells of \(\lambda\), a Boolean consequence of degree two; the cost is \(a+1\le B+a\). So \(\tau R\in\mathcal C_{B+a}(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun})\). By the recorded top-monomial detector, with \(u=a\) and ceiling \(B+a\), this is impossible when \(4(a-1)<n\) and \(n\ge2(B+a)-1\). Hence \(2(B+a)-1>n\), that is \(B\ge n/2-a+1\). \(\square\)
This is the weight step of the one-affine-level theorem with the weight equal to the indicator of the subspace, which is affordable exactly because the rank is small. In the language of the literature: \(\Phi\) is safe if and only if the top-degree part of that indicator contains a monomial with one bit in each of \(a\) rows (the coefficient of a squarefree monomial of degree \(a\) is the corresponding minor, which is the criterion of their Theorem 3.1), and the detector's disjoint coordinate edges are the pairs of holes of Efremenko, Garlík and Itsykson.
A direct restriction shows what is left of the PHP. Choose labels \(h_i\), \(i\in I\), one after another so that the \(2a\) labels \(h_i^0,h_i^1\) (the two ends of the edge through \(h_i\) in direction \(j_i\), with pivot bit 0 and 1) are distinct, which is possible because before the \(i\)-th choice at most \(2(i-1)<n/2\) of the \(n/2\) edges of direction \(j_i\) are blocked. In unary variables send \(x_{i,h_i^1}\mapsto u_i\), \(x_{i,h_i^0}\mapsto1+u_i\) and the other cells of row \(i\) to zero, where \(u_i\) is \(\tau(w_i+c_i)\) with \(\tau(b_{i',t})\) replaced by the constant \((h_{i'})_t\) for every \(i'\in I\), the row \(i\) itself included, at \(t\ne j_{i'}\) (both ends of an edge agree there, so this is what the map gives), and with the reserved cells of the other rows set to zero; and send \(x_{k,h}\mapsto0\) for every row \(k\notin I\) and every reserved label \(h\). The row axioms of \(I\) go to zero, Booleanity and same-row products of \(I\) to \(u_i^2+u_i\) of degree two, the column exclusions that involve a row of \(I\) to zero by disjointness of the pairs, and \(\tau L_i\) to zero; what remains is exactly the functional PHP with \(n+1-a\) rows and \(n-2a\) holes, at ceiling \(\max(B,2)\). The imported bound for more rows than holes gives the same \(n/2-a+1\). (The reviewer checked every image of this map; the first draft carried out the same restriction in the bit variables, which needed the label-decoder certificate and an extra hypothesis \(n-2a>2\ell\) that the unary form does not need.)
Several pivots in one row. If the reduced system has pivot sets \(T_i\) in \(u\) rows, \(a=\sum_i|T_i|\), the indicator has the squarefree top monomial \(\prod_i\prod_{t\in T_i}b_{it}\), and the recorded multi-axis cube detector applies with ceiling \(B+a\) whenever pairwise disjoint coordinate cubes with free axes \(T_i\) exist (packing): with \(F=\sum_i2^{|T_i|}\) reserved labels, every refutation has degree at least \((n-F)/2-(a-u)+1\) (the lemma's other hypotheses, \(m\ge B+a\) and a residual label, are harmless: if they fail, \(B\) already exceeds the stated bound or the bound is at most one). Since \(2^p\ge2p\), one has \(F\ge2a\), so \(F\le n/2\) gives \(a\le n/4\) exactly, and a nonvoid bound needs \(a<n/2\) in any case: several pivots in a row never buy more rank than single pivots.
Generic systems. A uniformly random system of \(a\le n/4\) equations, with any right-hand sides, is safe except with probability at most \(a\,2^{-(m-a+1)\ell}\): reduce the equations one at a time; the reduced \(r\)-th equation is uniform on the non-pivot coordinates, so it has a nonzero coefficient in a block not used before except with probability \(2^{-(m-r+1)\ell}\); back-substitution then removes the pivots from the forms \(w_i\) and keeps the pivots. So Question H for parity constraints has the answer yes for up to \(n/4\) generic equations, with degree \(n/2-a+1\).
What the corollary is worth. Its loss, one degree per equation, equals that of the recorded case split; the gain over the case split is that it holds for every right-hand side and every safe system, not for some sign vector. It does not reach what the value method needs: the row-mixing twist of the review needs rank about \(0.7v\), far above the number of blocks, where no system is safe, the cubes cannot be packed, and any bound of the form \(n/2-a\) is void.

Conjecture H1. For a uniformly random affine subspace \(W\) of the bit space, the polynomial-calculus refutation degree of \(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun}\) with the decoded equations of \(W\) adjoined is at least \(c\min(n,\dim W/\ell)\) for a constant \(c>0\), with high probability. It is stated on the unary board, where the experiment lives; the compact form follows by the decoder. An upper bound of this order is elementary: for a fixed set of \(r\) rows with \(r\ell\ge\dim W+t\) the projection of \(W\) to the labels of those rows is injective except with probability \(2^{-t}\); then the sum of the label indicators \(X_y\) of these rows is one at degree \(r\), an indicator outside the image is refuted at degree \(r+1\), and one inside the image determines all labels and meets a collision at degree \(r+2\). So the refutation degree is at most \(\lceil\dim W/\ell\rceil+O(1)\) in unary degree, and at most \(n/2+1\) in any case, so that \(c\le1/2\). For the row-mixing twist of the review, \(\dim W=0.3v\) and the conjecture gives degree linear in \(n\).


###parity-subspace-literature
1. What is published
Locators and hashes are in the source audit; the statements are imported as context, and only the definition of a safe system is used below, with its attribution.
Mikša and Nordström, A generalized method for proving polynomial calculus degree lower bounds (CCC 2015; arXiv 1505.01358v1 read). Theorem 4.9: if a bipartite graph \(G\) is an \((s,\delta)\)-boundary expander with left degree at most \(d\), then refuting the graph functional PHP over \(G\) (pigeon, hole and functionality axioms on the variables \(x_{u,v}\), \((u,v)\in E\)) in polynomial calculus requires degree greater than \(\delta s/(2d)\). Theorem 4.5 gives \(\delta s/2-\xi\) for the graph PHP with onto axioms under a matching condition, after Alekhnovich and Razborov. For the middle level of the review, value axioms that confine each row to its own flat, this would give at most \(n/(2d)\), since \(\delta s\le n\): at most \(n^{0.7}/2\) for flats of \(n^{0.3}\) labels, and only if the graph of flat neighbourhoods is a boundary expander, which has not been checked. The transfer from the bit encoding to the graph formula (restrictions \(x_{i,z}=0\) are derivable in degree two from a bit equation and the functional axioms) has not been written out either.
Byramji and Impagliazzo, Lower bounds for bit pigeonhole principles in bounded-depth resolution over parities (arXiv 2511.20023v1 read: abstract and proof overview). For the bit PHP with \(n+1\) pigeons, every Res(\(\oplus\)) refutation of depth \(N^{2-\varepsilon}\) has size \(\exp(\tilde\Omega(N^{\varepsilon}))\), \(N\) the number of variables; general Res(\(\oplus\)) is described as open beyond such depth. The overview and Section 3.1 recall from Efremenko, Garlík and Itsykson (not retrieved) the notions used to handle linear systems over the label bits: a system of rank \(r\) is safe if it can be rewritten as fixing \(r\) variables from distinct blocks as affine functions of the other variables, equivalently (their Theorem 3.1) if its coefficient matrix has \(r\) independent columns no two of which belong to the same block; then, as long as \(r\) is not too large, the \(r\) pigeons can be sent to \(2r\) holes without collisions, each to a pair of holes differing in one bit. Every system \(\Phi\) has a closure \(\mathrm{Cl}(\Phi)\), a set of blocks, with \(\Phi\) equivalent to a part on the closure, of rank at least \(|\mathrm{Cl}(\Phi)|\), and a part that is safe once the closure is fixed. Their probability estimates for safe systems are stated for rank \(r\le cn\) with a small constant \(c\). So the technique, as far as this one overview shows it, handles linear systems of rank of order \(n\), the number of holes, and Question H at linear codimension asks for rank of order \(v=(n+1)\ell\); a system of rank above the number of blocks cannot be safe.


###subspace-residual-lemma
1. The linear equations are eliminated exactly
Setup: \(n=2^\ell\), \(\ell\ge2\), rows \(i\in[n+1]\), label bits \(b_{i,t}\), \(v=(n+1)\ell\), the compact base \(\mathcal Q_{n,2}\) (Booleanity and the collision polynomials \(E_{ii'}=\prod_t(1+b_{i,t}+b_{i',t})\)), polynomial calculus over \(\mathbb F_2\) with the recorded degree convention (a multiplication is charged the degree of its predecessor plus one). Let \(\Lambda=\{\Lambda_1,\dots,\Lambda_a\}\) be affine forms in the bits, linearly independent with the constant one outside their span, \(W\) their common zero flat, \(k=v-a\), and \(P:\mathbb F_2^k\to\mathbb F_2^v\) an injective affine map with image \(W\). Write \(\mathcal S_P=\{E_{ii'}\circ P:i<i'\}\cup{\rm BOOL}_y\), a system in the variables \(y_1,\dots,y_k\); its axiom \(E_{ii'}\circ P\) is the product of the \(\ell\) affine forms \(1+(P_{i,t}+P_{i',t})(y)\), the indicator of the collision flat \(\{y:P_i(y)=P_{i'}(y)\}\), of codimension \(e_{ii'}\le\ell\) in \(\mathbb F_2^k\) when it is not empty.
Lemma R (residual coordinates). Write \(D_b\) for the refutation degree of \(\mathcal Q_{n,2}\cup\Lambda\) and \(D_y\) for that of \(\mathcal S_P\). Then \(D_y\le D_b\le\max(D_y,\ell)\), and \(D_b\ge\ell\); hence \(D_b=\max(D_y,\ell)\). The same holds with the multilinear quotient ring \(\mathbb F_2[y]/(y_j^2+y_j)\) in place of ordinary polynomial calculus with \({\rm BOOL}_y\).
Proof. The coordinate-completion lemma gives an affine equivalence \(E\) of \(\mathbb F_2^v\) with \((E(b))_{e(c)}=\Lambda_c(b)\) and a parametrization \(P_0=E^{-1}\circ z\) of \(W\) by the free coordinates; any other injective affine \(P\) with image \(W\) is \(P_0\circ A\) for an affine bijection \(A\) of \(\mathbb F_2^k\), so \(P\) has an affine left inverse \(\lambda:\mathbb F_2^v\to\mathbb F_2^k\) (take \(A^{-1}\) of the free coordinates of \(E\)), and \(P(\lambda(b))=b+\sum_c\Lambda_c(b)\,h_c\) for fixed vectors \(h_c\in\mathbb F_2^v\): in the coordinates of \(E\) the map \(P\circ\lambda\) sets the designated coordinates to zero and keeps the others.
First inequality: apply the substitution replay with \(T=1\), \(w=1\) to \(b\mapsto P(y)\), at the ceiling \(D_b\). A Boolean axiom \(b^2+b\) goes to \(g^2+g\) for an affine form \(g=\sum_jg_jy_j+g_0\), which over \(\mathbb F_2\) equals \(\sum_jg_j(y_j^2+y_j)\), a combination of Boolean axioms of degree two (the cross terms cancel in characteristic two; this is the certificate used in the decoder). Each \(\Lambda_c\circ P\) is an affine polynomial that vanishes on all of \(\mathbb F_2^k\), hence the zero polynomial. Each \(E_{ii'}\) goes to an axiom of \(\mathcal S_P\). So every axiom image is derived within the ceiling and \(\mathcal S_P\) is refuted within \(D_b\).
Second inequality: apply the replay to \(y\mapsto\lambda(b)\) at the ceiling \(\max(D_y,\ell)\). Boolean axioms go to combinations of Boolean axioms as before. The axiom \(E_{ii'}\circ P\) goes to \(E_{ii'}(b+\sum_c\Lambda_ch_c)=\prod_t\bigl(1+b_{i,t}+b_{i',t}+\sum_c\eta_{t,c}\Lambda_c\bigr)\) with \(\eta_{t,c}=(h_c)_{i,t}+(h_c)_{i',t}\). Expanding the product, every term other than \(E_{ii'}(b)\) contains a factor \(\sum_c\eta_{t,c}\Lambda_c\), so the image is \(E_{ii'}+\sum_c\Lambda_cq_c\) with \(\deg q_c\le\ell-1\), derived from the axioms \(E_{ii'}\) and \(\Lambda_c\) within degree \(\ell\). This costs \(\ell\) even when the collision flat has smaller codimension, which is why the ceiling is \(\max(D_y,\ell)\).
Lower bound \(\ell\): \(\Lambda\cup{\rm BOOL}_b\) has the common zeros \(W\ne\varnothing\), so a refutation uses a collision axiom, a line of degree \(\ell\). Quotient ring: reduce an ordinary proof line by line as in the functional bridge (an ordinary multiplication with nonzero predecessor has \(\deg P+1\le D\), so the quotient inference has the same charge); conversely a quotient inference \(P\mapsto\overline{y_jP}\) is the ordinary product \(y_jP\) of degree \(\deg P+1\) minus multiples \(m'(y_j^2+y_j)\) of the same degree. The quotient-ring axiom is the multilinear reduction of \(E_{ii'}\circ P\), of degree \(e_{ii'}\), while the ordinary product can have degree up to \(\ell\) when its forms are dependent; passing between the two costs at most \(\ell\), inside the ceiling, so \(D_y^{\rm quot}\le D_y^{\rm ord}\le D_b\le\max(D_y^{\rm quot},\ell)\). \(\square\)
Hypotheses. The proof uses \(\mathbb F_2\) (affine forms are Boolean, cross terms cancel) and \(W\ne\varnothing\); \(\ell\ge2\) only to keep the Boolean images within the ceiling. It does not use \(m=n+1\), \(n=2^\ell\), genericity of \(W\), or the product form of the collision polynomials: the same argument gives \(D_y\le D_b\le\max(D_y,\ell)\) for any system over \(\mathbb F_2\) with Boolean axioms whose other axioms \(f\) have degree at most \(\ell\), since \(f(b+\sum_c\Lambda_ch_c)-f(b)\in\sum_c\Lambda_c\mathcal P_{\le\deg f-1}\). It is stated for the compact base because that is where it is used.
So the PHP on \(W\) is, exactly and in bit degree, the statement that the \(\binom{n+1}2\) collision flats of the \(n+1\) affine maps \(P_i:\mathbb F_2^k\to\mathbb F_2^\ell\) cover \(\mathbb F_2^k\). For a uniform \(W\) the maps are generic, the flats have codimension \(\ell\) once \(k\ge\ell\) (up to rank defects of probability about \(2^{\ell-k}\) each), and their total density \((n+1)/2\) is far below the covering threshold \(k\ln2\) of independent flats as soon as \(k\) exceeds a constant times \(n\): the covering is a pigeonhole fact, not a density fact. Through the decoder the bit degree is at least the unary degree, so Conjecture H1 implies \(D_b\ge c\min(n,k/\ell)\); this bit form is what the value method of Claim G uses. No upper relation between the two degrees is recorded, so the bit-degree counts below are not comparable with the unary tipping counts of the two previous entries.
Remark (dual picture, Nullstellensatz only, not used below). A functional on polynomials of degree at most \(d\) in \(y\) is a sum of evaluations over a set \(S\subseteq\mathbb F_2^k\). It is normalized exactly if \(|S|\) is odd, and it annihilates the multiples of the indicator of a collision flat \(A\) of codimension \(e_A\) through degree \(d\) exactly if every subflat of \(A\) of codimension at most \(d-e_A\) in \(A\) contains an even number of points of \(S\) (equivalently the restriction of the indicator of \(S\) to \(A\) has degree at most \(k-d-1\)). So a Nullstellensatz design of degree \(d\) for the PHP on \(W\) is an odd set that meets all these subflats evenly. No equality of polynomial-calculus and Nullstellensatz closures is recorded for this system (the recorded \(\mathcal C_B=\mathcal I_B\) concerns the unrestricted functional base), so such a set would not by itself exclude a polynomial-calculus refutation.


###subspace-residual-attempt
2. The proof attempt: why the recorded constructions do not extend
The plan was a design of the compact base that annihilates the multiples of the equations of \(W\), built from the recorded signed constructions and the local freedom of a generic subspace. It did not produce a design. What stops it, stated as far as it is proved:
(a) Weights supported on \(W\). Corollary SL uses the functional \(\mu(\tau R\,\cdot)\) with \(R\) the indicator of \(W\). A nonzero function on \(\mathbb F_2^v\) that vanishes outside \(W\) has multilinear degree at least \(a\): in the coordinates of \(E\) it is \(\prod_c(1+u_c)\,g(y)\) with \(g\ne0\), whose multilinear form contains \(u_1\cdots u_a\) times the top monomial of \(g\), and an invertible affine map over \(\mathbb F_2\) preserves multilinear degree (recorded remark). A polynomial of unary degree \(r\) is, on the labelings, a sum of functions of at most \(r\) labels each, of bit degree at most \(r\ell\). So a weight that vanishes outside \(W\) as a function on the labelings has unary degree at least \(a/\ell\), which for \(a=0.7v\) is \(0.7(n+1)\), above the barrier \(n/2+1\) of the base. This excludes pointwise weights at linear codimension. It does not exclude a weight \(f\) with \(f\Lambda_c\) in the old ideal only through the working degree; no such weight was found. For a single partial-matching monomial \(f=X_T\) with \(|T|<n/2\), \(B\ge|T|+1\) and \(n-|T|\ge2B-1\) the condition holds for all \(c\) exactly if every \(\Lambda_c\) has all its bits in the rows of \(T\) and vanishes at the labels of \(T\) (the restriction that sets \(T\) maps \(X_T\tau\Lambda_c\) to a polynomial of degree at most one on the residual board, the recorded \(\mathcal I_B\cap\mathcal P_{\le1}=\mathcal I_1\) makes it a combination of row axioms, and a nonzero linear functional of the label is not constant on the more than \(n/2\) free holes); this forces \(a\le|T|\ell\) and fails for a generic \(W\).
(b) Product sets. For a uniform \(W\) of codimension \(a\) with direction space \(W_0\), a fixed nonzero vector lies in \(W_0\) with probability \((2^k-1)/(2^v-1)<2^{-a}\). Hence, except with probability \(\binom{n+1}r2^{r\ell-a}\), any two points of \(W\) differ in more than \(r\) rows; for \(a=0.7v\) this holds with \(r=\lfloor0.7(n+1)\rfloor-\lceil(n+1+t)/\ell\rceil\) except with probability \(2^{-t}\). In particular \(W\) contains no product of label sets with more than one point once \(a\ge\ell+\log_2(n+1)+t\). The recorded designs (signed two-point, cube, row trades \(\bigotimes_i(\mathbf e_{a_i}-\mathbf e_{b_i})\)) are tensor products over rows: their marginal identities cancel row by row inside a product of label sets. The one recorded device that is not of product form, the affine restriction behind Corollary SL, reserves a pair of holes for each equation and so stops at rank of order \(n\). The local freedom of \(W\) is of another kind: the projection of \(W\) to fewer than about \(k/\ell\) rows is onto, but the \(2^r\) labelings in the support of a trade on \(r\) rows have different completions in \(W\), so the cancellation of one row's two labels changes all other rows. No replacement for the row-by-row cancellation was found.
(c) Locality. The published expander method read in the previous entry needs axioms whose variables lie in few groups. A generic equation of \(W\) has variables in every row, and in residual coordinates every collision axiom involves \(\ell\) forms that are generic in all \(k\) coordinates; no choice of coordinates was found that makes the system local, and nothing is proved about this.
So the obstruction is the type of the problem: a degree bound for a dense system of flat-avoidance axioms without product structure and without locality, for which neither the recorded detectors nor the methods read so far give a handle. This is the same wall as the generic-flats form of Question H.


###wall-review-wall
5. One wall, and its weakest form
Image methods stop at the universal obstruction; the value method needs the PHP to stay hard on a subspace cut out by linear constraints of rank of order \(v\); the union source and the unary parities lead to the same requirement. Every technique on record or read stops at rank of order \(n\): the recorded detectors at \(n/4\), where they reserve holes (Corollary SL), the case split at about \(n/2\), where its additive loss meets the barrier, the closure technique for Res(\(\oplus\)) at \(cn\) (literature), the expander method at locality. Rank \(n\) is where a system can still be solved for one bit in each of distinct rows; above it every row is touched and all but a \(2^{-t}\) fraction of the linear forms on \(W\) have no representation by bits of \(r\) rows or fewer when \(r\ell+n+1+t\le\dim W\) (there are at most \(\binom{n+1}r2^{r\ell}\) such representations), so an accounting that charges a bounded number of rows to a query stops.
Lemma T (parity decision trees give polynomial-calculus degree). Let \(W\), \(P\) and \(\mathcal S_P\) be as in Lemma R. Suppose a parity decision tree of depth \(d\ge\ell\) solves the collision search on \(W\): every query is a linear form that is not constant on the current subspace, and every leaf, an affine subspace \(U=\{q_1=a_1,\dots,q_s=a_s\}\) of \(W\) with \(s\le d\), lies inside the collision flat of some pair \(p(U)\). Then \(\mathcal S_P\) has a refutation of degree at most \(d\) in the multilinear quotient ring, in fact the Nullstellensatz certificate \(1=\sum_UE_{p(U)}\prod_{j\in J_U}(1+q_j+a_j)\) with \(|J_U|=\operatorname{codim}U-e_{p(U)}\). Hence \(D_b(W)\) is at most the larger of \(\ell\) and the parity-decision-tree depth of the collision search on \(W\), and at most \(\max(\ell,\dim W)\), by querying all coordinates (a point lies in a collision flat because there are more rows than holes).
Proof. Fix a leaf \(U\) with pair \(p\), let \(R_p\) be the span of the forms of the collision flat of \(p\) (dimension \(e_p\le\ell\)) and \(Q_j\) the span of the first \(j\) queries on the path. Since \(U\) is nonempty and lies in the flat, \(R_p\subseteq Q_s\), so \(\dim(R_p+Q_j)\le s\le d\) for every \(j\). The reduced line \(E_p\prod_{i\le j}(1+q_i+a_i)\) is the indicator of the flat cut out by \(R_p+Q_j\) with the given values, of degree \(\dim(R_p+Q_j)\), or zero. If \(q_{j+1}\) is constant on that flat, the next line is the same line or zero and no inference is made; otherwise the dimension is below \(d\) before the multiplication, the predecessor has degree at most \(d-1\), and multiplying by the affine form \(1+q_{j+1}+a_{j+1}\) (a sum of variable multiplications) stays within \(d\). The last line is the indicator of \(U\), which is obtained from \(E_p\) by the factors \(j\in J_U\) that raised the dimension. The leaves partition \(W\), so their indicators sum to the constant 1 as multilinear polynomials. \(\square\)
Position in the record. The recorded case-split lemma gives \(\ell+d\) by induction on the tree (each parity query costs one degree); the bound \(\max(d,\ell)\) without the additive \(\ell\) and the general fact behind it are new. Recorded analogues, none of which implies it: the hole-query trees on the unary board, the branch closure for products of at most three affine forms, and the affine-clause transfers of the publication branch, which go through extension variables. The containment \(R_p\subseteq Q_s\) is the argument of the recorded paragraph on parity decision trees at the end of the unary-query section (a leaf inside a flat of codimension \(r\) has queried rank at least \(r\)). The lemma uses \(\mathbb F_2\) (a linear query is a polynomial of degree one) and nothing about the PHP beyond the axioms being flat indicators; genericity of \(W\) and the non-constancy of the queries (constant queries can be skipped) are not used, and \(d\ge\ell\) only keeps the axiom within the ceiling and gives the passage to \(D_b\) through Lemma R.
Corollary (the compact base has bit degree of order \(n\)). \(n/2+1\le D_b(\mathcal Q_{n,2})\le2n-2+\ell\), and \(D_b(W)\le\min(\max(\ell,\dim W),\,2n-2+\ell)\) for every affine subspace \(W\). The lower bound is the recorded one. For the upper bound use the halving tree: query the first bit of all \(n+1\) rows and keep the \(n/2+1\) rows of the majority side, which share \(n/2\) holes; query the second bit of those rows, and so on; level \(j\) makes \(n/2^{j-1}+1\) queries, \(2n-2+\ell\) in all, and after level \(\ell\) two rows agree in every bit, so the leaf lies in their collision flat. The tree restricted to \(W\) solves the search on \(W\). This settles the question left open in the previous entry (order \(n\), not order \(n\ell\)); the exact values three and five on four and eight holes equal the lower bound. For the bit form of Conjecture H1 it means that at \(\dim W=0.3v\) the degree lies between \(c\,n\), if the conjecture holds, and \(2n-2+\ell\).
A depth lower bound of order \(n\) for parity decision trees on a generic subspace of linear codimension is therefore a necessary condition for the bit form of the conjecture, and it is the simplest question on which all the techniques above fail for the same reason. For the full space such a bound is published (the abstract of Byramji and Impagliazzo, read in the previous literature entry, states depth \(\Omega(n)\); imported context, not used).


###wall-review-falsification
3. Falsification attempt on the union source
The recorded candidate counterexample to Claim G for arbitrary sources is the union of a wide literal reader and the row-mixing twisted approximate-majority source. The statement of the claim already notes that the two systems of equations are inconsistent with each other; the corollary below is the uniform, quantitative form of that sentence. The two recorded choices of value axioms are a restriction \(\rho'\) of the compact family \(\Phi_0\) for the reader (family: a flat \(Q\) of dimension \(\ell_2\), \(N=2^{\ell_2}\), \(N+1\) live rows, the other rows matched bijectively to the labels outside \(Q\)) and \(a=0.7v\) generic linear equations for the twisted source.
Corollary U (incompatibility). Let \(W\) be a uniform affine subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^v\) of codimension \(a\ge(N+1)\ell+\log_2\binom{n+1}{N+1}+t\). Except with probability \(2^{-t}\), every restriction \(\rho'\in\Phi_0\) leaves at most one point of \(W\).
Proof. By the recorded observation, except with probability \(\binom{n+1}{r}2^{r\ell-a}\) no two points of \(W\) differ in at most \(r\) rows. A restriction of the compact family fixes every row outside its \(N+1\) live rows to one label, so two assignments that it leaves differ in at most \(N+1\) rows. Take \(r=N+1\); the event does not depend on \(\rho'\), so no union over the family is needed. \(\square\)
For \(a=0.7v\) and polylogarithmic \(N\) the hypothesis holds with room to spare. So on the union source the two recorded mechanisms cannot be combined by intersecting their axioms: whatever restriction serves the reader leaves at most one point of the subspace that serves the twisted source, and on a point the base is refuted at degree at most \(\ell\) (Lemma T below; on an empty intersection at degree one), so hardness is lost outright. The claim itself, which quantifies over all \(E\), is not refuted. With the equations of the twisted source alone, the general form of Lemma R (its Hypotheses paragraph: the substitution acts on the old variables only, with \(\ell\) replaced by the largest axiom degree) puts the whole system into the residual coordinates of \(W\), where every pin \([a\to y]\) of the reader is a flat indicator of codimension at most \(\ell\) in forms that are generic for a generic twist; the reader is then a reader of dense-form terms and falls under the term-value form of Question H. With a restriction alone, the twisted source becomes a reader of generic flats on the polylogarithmic residual space, as in the recorded invariance (which is stated for uniform forms; the rows of a uniform invertible matrix are only approximately uniform). Either way the union source leads back to the same hardness question, on one of the two spaces. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the distinction between literal and dense terms depends on the coordinates; the hardness question does not.
