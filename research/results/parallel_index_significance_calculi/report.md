## Binary predecessor: the same publication result

The original working corollary states that for every fixed K, sufficiently large
n=2^ell has no n^K-node unrestricted DAG-like Res(oplus) refutation of bit PHP
with n+1 pigeons, including the usual CNF, under both audited conventions. The
publication theorem states exactly that family and quantification (real K>0),
with arbitrary DAG reuse, complementary-parity resolution plus semantic weakening,
and the alternate sound-at-most-two-premises convention. Neither permits
extension definitions or imposes proof height/regularity. The publication entry
explicitly says it consolidates the earlier corollary and its second derivation.
Choosing the less sharp h=3ell instead of the original h=2ell changes the proof
ledger, not the asymptotic conclusion or the encoding.

Therefore this index item inherits the publication theorem's provisional novelty
and preprint status. It is not a second independent result to count alongside
that theorem. The exact future work is the publication's existing independent
expert review and exact-scope novelty review. No duplicate literature project is
needed for this predecessor. The stronger later exponential parameter theorem
is likewise not being credited to the earlier statement.

The existing September 14 audit distinguishes regular and depth-restricted
bit-PHP results from unrestricted superpolynomial size. A targeted current ECCC
lookup again located the February 2026 report's open-problem statement and its
matching binary rule convention; this is consistent with, not an exhaustive
replacement for, the saved audit. [Primary report](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/018/).

## Odd-prime result: distinct, explicitly defined scope

The notebook theorem concerns its own R_p^ne: disjunctions of affine
*disequalities*, full-field semantic weakening, p-way affine splitting, and
explicit Boolean-domain initial clauses. Its usual-CNF bit-PHP theorem is an
unrestricted-DAG working consequence at every fixed prime. The p=2 instance
recovers the binary result. The odd-prime extension is potentially independently
useful, but its title must name this calculus.

Part–Tzameret's Section 3 instead defines disjunctions of affine **equations**.
Its semantic variants use implication on Boolean assignments; the paper states
this convention explicitly. The PHP lower bound located there is tree-like,
and its PHP definition is unary. Thus neither that result nor those semantic
rules identify the notebook's odd-prime system or supply its exact claimed
unrestricted bit-PHP bound. [Revision 2, Sections 2.3.1 and 3, Theorem 37](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2018/117/revision/2/download/).

The notebook itself already explains the conversion issue: at odd primes an
equation becomes a conjunction of p-1 disequalities, and a linewise full-field
conversion has an exponential example. That does not prove separation from
every Boolean-domain-aware proof simulation. It also cannot justify calling
this a lower bound for ordinary equation-clause Res(lin_Fp), semantic Boolean
Res(lin_Fp), unary PHP, or AC0[p]-Frege.

The bounded searches recorded in `sources.json` found no exact named predecessor
for this defined odd-prime calculus. They are too narrow to certify novelty
across alternative system names or unpublished work. Recommended metadata:
`independent_result`, novelty `unknown`, publication `candidate`, with a reviewed
scope disposition. This closes the previous *unperformed comparison* task: the
