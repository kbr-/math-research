Value diversity on an independent fiber

Corollary. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), split Boolean variables as \((y,z)\), with \(y\in\mathbb F_2^r\). Suppose a fixed binary linear projection \(T\) of the input tuple satisfies \[Tg(y,z)=y+F(z),\] where \(F\) is any binary polynomial tuple in the other variables. If \(p\) is a nonzero Boolean polynomial of multilinear degree at most \(d\), the number of distinct nonzero values of \(g\) on \(\operatorname{supp}p\) is at least \[2^{\max(0,r-d)}-1.\tag{1}\] The floor is sharp as a function of \(r,d\). The same assertion holds on a product of three-state edge domains when each \(y_i\) is a chosen label indicator on a distinct outside edge and \(z\) consists only of the remaining edges, provided \(p\) is nonzero on that local domain and has an ordinary polynomial representative of degree at most \(d\).

Proof on a Boolean cube. Fix \(z=z_*\) so that \(p(y,z_*)\) is nonzero. Its multilinear degree \(t\) is at most \(\min(d,r)\). Choose a maximum-degree monomial with nonzero coefficient, on a set of \(t\) variables. After any assignment to the other \(r-t\) variables, its coefficient remains nonzero, since a term containing that monomial and another variable would have larger degree. Each of these \(2^{r-t}\) restricted polynomials is therefore a nonzero Boolean function. There are at least \(2^{r-t}\ge2^{\max(0,r-d)}\) points in the support. Their projected values \(y+F(z_*)\) are distinct. At most one distinct full tuple value can be the zero vector; deleting it proves (1). This is the same elementary support mechanism as the earlier weighted offset argument, applied after fixing the offset variables.

For sharpness take \(g=y\) and \(p=\prod_{i=1}^{\min(d,r)}(1+y_i)\). Its support contains exactly \(2^{r-\min(d,r)}\) values, including zero. Constants and unused variables cause no exception. This is an algebraic control, not a hard source instance.

Proof on local edge domains. Choose a local assignment where \(p\ne0\), and fix all other edges there. On each outside edge choose a two-label slice containing that assignment's label and the distinguished label; if the assignment already has the distinguished label, choose any other label for the second one. The indicator \(y_i\) ranges over both bits. Every original label indicator restricts to zero, one, \(y_i\) or \(1-y_i\), so the polynomial degree does not increase. The restricted polynomial remains nonzero at the chosen assignment, and the offsets are now constants. Apply the Boolean argument. Only local labels are evaluated; none is asserted to satisfy all old charge equations. \(\square\)

There is a direct one-block repair in the present paid setting. Let the interpreted inputs have degree at most \(\delta\), be binary polynomials, and retain original block ceiling \(B\ge2\delta\). Assume \(b+\delta+B\le D\). If every \([wg_i]=0\), use zero rows and keep \(w\). Otherwise select an input \(g_j\) with \([wg_j]\ne0\), use it as the first row, zero-pad the others, and take \(W=wg_j\). The product is \(1-g_j\), so \[Wg_i(1-g_j)=wg_i(g_j-g_j^2)\in J_D\] through \(b+3\delta\le b+\delta+B\). Coefficient fields vanish, earlier weighted memberships survive the extra factor within the same original ceiling, and replay costs \(b+\delta+B\). This recovers the earlier one-block weighted repair. Applying it separately to many blocks pays their accumulated degrees; no inventory-independent simultaneous repair is inferred.
