Predictable recentering preserves the law

Proposition (legal row centering). First consider a finite adaptive sequence of uniformly sampled coefficient vectors, each drawn from a fixed finite vector space conditional on the preceding history. Replace the next vector \(c\) by \(T(H)c+a(H)\), where \(T(H)\) is invertible and \(a(H)\) lies in that coefficient space; both depend only on the preceding transformed history \(H\). The resulting joint law is the same as independent uniform sampling in those spaces. This remains true when the current input tuple is any fixed function of that history. Therefore every actual prefix/error matrix statistic, including ranks at every cutoff, has the same distribution under these predictable bijective row centers.

Proof. Conditional on each transformed history, the fresh original vector is uniform and independent of it. The indicated affine map is a bijection, so its image is uniform. Induction gives the same probability for every history as the original process. Any deterministic statistic of that history and its induced source has the same law. No field norm or matrix positivity enters. Old-assignment-dependent polynomial coefficient functions, singular row restrictions and choices depending nonbijectively on the current row are not covered by this assertion.

In particular a fixed change of basis or translation within a legal row span cannot improve expected error rank merely by calling one input a center. An invertible change also leaves the full input-word space unchanged. It may improve a crude written upper bound by displaying existing relations, but that is not a change in the underlying error law. This does not say that all nonuniform legal row choices have the same performance.

Pay for a deterministic high-degree prefix

The second part works over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(J_D\) be a Boolean degree-\(D\) pseudoideal in \(n\) old variables, \(\deg w\le b\), \(w\notin J_D\), and let \(M\ge1\) fixed finite tuples \(g_{a,i}\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\). Suppose their constant input spans have supplied decompositions \[ g_{a,i}=\ell_{a,i}+\sum_{j=1}^{r_a}\alpha_{a,i,j}q_{a,j},\qquad r_a\le r,\quad \deg\ell_{a,i}\le d,\quad 1\le d\le\delta, \tag{1} \] where \(r,r_a\) are nonnegative integers and every \(q_{a,j}\) lies in the original constant input span and has degree at most \(\delta\). Thus each \(\ell_{a,i}\) also lies in that same span. These are literal polynomial equalities, not free relations modulo an inconsistent ideal. For integers \(d\le K\le n\), \(t\ge1\), put \(N_n(k)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom nj\) and assume \[ b+K+(r+1)\delta+td\le D,\qquad \eta=\tfrac12\frac{N_n(K-d)}{N_n(K)},\qquad M(1-\eta)^t<1. \tag{2} \] Then there are \(r_a+t\) legal constant coefficient rows for each original tuple and a multilinear \(f\) of degree at most \(K\) such that \(wf\notin J_D\) and every actual weighted companion for those rows belongs to \(J_D\). Larger supplied accuracies may be zero-padded, retaining their original charges. No survival of the chosen prefix times \(w\) is required.

Proof of the weighted construction. Use the first \(r_a\) rows to realize \(q_{a,j}\), and set \[ Q_a=\prod_{j=1}^{r_a}(1-q_{a,j}). \] Modulo Booleanity, \(Q_aq_{a,j}=0\), with a certificate through \((r_a+1)\delta\): use \(q_{a,j}^2-q_{a,j}\) through \(2\delta\) and multiply by the other \(r_a-1\) factors. This is an empty requirement when \(r_a=0\). Consequently \(Q_a(g_{a,i}-\ell_{a,i})\) has the same certificate ceiling. Every subsequent row can be a binary linear combination of the \(\ell_{a,i}\)'s, hence is a legal constant combination of the original inputs and has actual degree at most \(d\). Let \(R_a\) denote the product of their \(t\) complementary factors.

Apply the recorded conditional contraction on full multiplier spaces, at weight \(wQ_a\), to the reference tuple \(\ell_{a,i}\). Its squarefree error-kernel comparison gives contraction factor \(1-\eta\) per chosen row. Before the final row its required ceiling is at most \[ b+r_a\delta+(t-1)d+K+2d \le b+K+(r+1)\delta+td, \] since \(d\le\delta\). Let \(H=\dim[wV_K]>0\). Every initial reference tuple map \(f\mapsto([wQ_a\ell_{a,i}f])_i\) factors through \([wV_K]\), by bounded absorption, so its rank is at most \(H\). The same remains true after the selected rows. The sum of final ranks is at most \(M(1-\eta)^tH<H\); their joint map on \([wV_K]\) therefore has a nonzero kernel. Choose a representative \(f\in V_K\), reducing it multilinearly by Booleanity. It has \(wf\notin J_D\) and kills every reference companion.

Finally multiply the Boolean certificate for \(Q_a(g_{a,i}-\ell_{a,i})\) by \(wfR_a\). Its ceiling is \(b+K+(r_a+1)\delta+td\le D\). Thus \[ wfQ_aR_ag_{a,i}=wfQ_aR_a\ell_{a,i}\pmod {J_D}, \] and the right side vanishes. The product \(Q_aR_a\) is precisely a legal \((r_a+t)\)-row product on the original tuple. If \(wQ_a\in J_D\), that block already contributes zero, which is allowed. The denominator is always the common \([wV_K]\), not a separately assumed large prefix image. This proves the second part. \(\square\)

Both the actual product and the reference approximation use the same last rows, whose polynomial values are literally low-degree combinations in the original span. Thus no high-degree telescoping replacement is paid once per last row. The bound is a constant-coefficient assignment statement. Replaying an original degree-\(B\) PC refutation still separately requires \(b+K+B\le D\) and the memberships of every earlier weighted generator through its original cost; it does not reset \(B\) after specialization or padding.
