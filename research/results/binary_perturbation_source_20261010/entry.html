The perturbation theorem being used

Crainic, Main Lemma 2.4 and Sections 3.2–3.4, transfers a homotopy equivalence when \(d+\delta\) is a differential and \(1-\delta h\) is invertible. The transferred differential includes \(p(1-\delta h)^{-1}\delta i\); it is not generally the original target differential. The linear algebra is division-free when the inverse is a finite geometric series. A graph complex with an actual finite contraction

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\), with \(A=\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_v]\), \(R=A[r_1,\ldots,r_s]\), and prescribed old polynomials \(\beta_j(x)\). Choose positive integers \(a_j\ge\max(1,\deg\beta_j)\). Give \(x_i\) weight one and both \(r_j\) and the exterior symbol \(e_j\) weight \(a_j\). Let \(C_k=R\otimes\Lambda^k(e_1,\ldots,e_s)\). Exterior squares are zero; in characteristic two the usual Koszul signs are all positive. Define \[ d_0(e_I)=\sum_{j\in I}r_j e_{I\setminus j},\qquad \delta(e_I)=\sum_{j\in I}\beta_j e_{I\setminus j}. \] Both maps are \(R\)-linear, and \(d=d_0+\delta\) is the Koszul differential for \(r_j-\beta_j\). Let \(i:A\to C_0\) be old inclusion and \(\Phi:C\to A\) be substitution \(r_j=\beta_j\) in degree zero and zero in positive homological degrees.

Lemma (graph contraction). There is an explicit \(A\)-linear homotopy \(H:C_k\to C_{k+1}\) with \[ dH+Hd=1-i\Phi,\qquad H^2=0,\qquad Hi=0,\qquad \Phi H=0. \tag{1} \] All maps preserve the weighted filtration. On filtration at most \(L\), the perturbation series has at most \(L+1\) potentially nonzero terms, independently of \(s\). In degree zero, (1) recovers the existing graph-elimination certificate with coefficient of \(r_j-\beta_j\) of weighted degree at most \(L-a_j\).

Proof: initial contraction. Use the monomial basis \(x^\alpha r^\nu e_I\). Let \(j\) be the least fresh index with \(\nu_j>0\) or \(j\in I\). If none exists, or if this least index belongs to \(I\), put \(h=0\). Otherwise set \[ h(x^\alpha r^\nu e_I)=x^\alpha r^{\nu-\mathbf1_j}e_{\{j\}\cup I}. \] Let \(p_0\) evaluate \(r=0\) in degree zero and kill positive exterior degrees. Then \(d_0h+hd_0=1-ip_0\): if the least index is exterior, its removal and subsequent insertion give the original monomial; if it is polynomial, insertion and removal do so. All other terms occur twice and cancel. The old-only monomial is exactly the projected exception. Also \(h^2=0\), \(hi=0\), \(p_0h=0\). Dividing a monomial by its present \(r_j\) is polynomial division by a monomial, not inversion of a parameter. Removing \(r_j\) and adding \(e_j\) preserve the assigned weight.

Proof: perturb and identify the projection. On a monomial, fresh count \(|\nu|+|I|\) is preserved by \(h\), and lowered by one by \(\delta\), since each \(\beta_j\) is old-only. Hence \(\delta h\) is locally nilpotent. Count is at most weighted degree, so \((\delta h)^{L+1}=0\) on filtration at most \(L\). Both \(h\) and \(\delta\) preserve that filtration. The special-retract form of the perturbation lemma gives \[ H=h\sum_{k\ge0}(\delta h)^k,\qquad p'=p_0\sum_{k\ge0}(\delta h)^k . \] The target is \(A\) concentrated in homological degree zero, so its differential stays zero; inclusion stays \(i\). In degree zero the homotopy formula implies \(f-i p'f\) lies in the graph ideal \((r_j-\beta_j)\). Evaluation at \(r=\beta\) is the identity on \(A\) and kills this ideal, hence \(p'f=\Phi(f)\). Both projections kill positive homological degrees. The special-retract identities give (1), including \(H^2=0\). Writing \(Hf=\sum_j A_j e_j\) yields \(\deg_{\rm wt}A_j\le L-a_j\) and the stated certificate. \(\square\)

This construction uses the ordinary old polynomial ring. It does not promote the truncated quotient \(V_D/J_D\) to a ring or a full Boolean module. Its degree-zero content is the already recorded weighted feature retraction; the additional calculation exhibits the chain contraction and why its inverse terminates. With \(a_j\) chosen from actual coefficient-map degrees, source replay still requires proved weighted bounds for the original lines and axiom images. Bounding all \(a_j\) by \(T\) recovers the old \(TB\) worst-case cost, not a new discount.

The direct companion contraction behaves differently

Let \(B\) be any commutative algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\), and \(u,v\in B\) idempotents. The two-term companion complex \(C(u)\) is \(B e\to B\), with differential multiplication by \(u\). It has a special retract onto \(L=(1-u)B\) in both homological degrees, with zero target differential: projection is multiplication by \(1-u\), inclusion is literal, and \(h:C_0\to C_1\) is multiplication by \(u\). Indeed \(dh+hd=u^2=u=1-ip\). Positive homology is retained, not silently discarded.

Proposition (idempotent perturbation). Change the differential to multiplication by \(v\), so \(\delta=v-u\). The perturbation is small for this contraction exactly when \(u(1-v)=0\). When it is small, the effective differential on \(L_1\to L_0\) is multiplication by \(v(1-u)\), which need not vanish.

Proof. On \(C_0\), \(\delta h\) is multiplication by \[ q=(v-u)u=u(1-v); \] on \(C_1\) it is zero. The element \(q\) is idempotent. If \(1-q\) were invertible, \((1-q)q=0\) would force \(q=0\); conversely \(q=0\) makes the inverse the identity. Under that condition, the transferred differential is \(p\delta i\), equal to \((1-u)(v-u)(1-u)=v(1-u)\). This is a calculation of the chosen contraction, not a claim that every homotopy model fails. \(\square\)

Take the actual Boolean algebra on old bits \(x,y\) and one fresh coefficient bit \(r\). Compare two separate complete accuracy-one one-block sources, first with input \(x\), then with input \(y\), identifying their coefficient coordinate only for the perturbation comparison. This is not a simultaneous source with an illegally shared private coefficient. Their companions have Boolean representatives \[ u=x(1-r),\qquad v=y(1-r). \] Their original polynomial companions have degree three; this comparison does not relabel them as degree two in a source proof. Here \(q=x(1-y)(1-r)\ne0\), so \((\delta h)^k\) does not vanish on its supported component for any positive \(k\). Smallness fails at source depth one. Both sources nevertheless have the constant normalizer \(r=1\), so their graph contraction is immediate and preserves the old variables. The negative result concerns this direct companion perturbation, not source hardness.

For a positive smallness control replace the second input by \(x+y+xy\), the Boolean OR. Its original companion has degree five and represents \(v=(x+y+xy)(1-r)\); now \(u(1-v)=0\), but \(v(1-u)=y(1-x)(1-r)\ne0\). Thus the effective differential really changes. The unit still survives this satisfiable source. Preserving every old homology class, or requiring a zero effective differential, would be stronger than the lower-bound endpoint; the required marked surviving class must be checked instead.
