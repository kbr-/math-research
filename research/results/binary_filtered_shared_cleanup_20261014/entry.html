Degree filtered retractions

Statement. Over any field, let \(V\) be a finite literal polynomial span with its reduced-echelon basis in descending degree-compatible monomial columns. Use that ordered basis as coordinates. For each \(G_a\subseteq V\), take its local reduced-echelon basis in these coordinates, and let \(\pi_a\) read its pivot coordinates and expand them in that local basis. For any integer \(d\ge0\), let \(A_d\) span the ambient basis vectors of degree at most \(d\), and let \(B_d\) span the others. Then

\[V=A_d\oplus B_d,\quad A_d=V\cap V_d,\quad G_a=L_a\oplus Q_a,\quad L_a=G_a\cap V_d,\quad \pi_a(A_d)=L_a,\quad\pi_a(B_d)=Q_a.\]

Here \(Q_a\) is the span of the local basis vectors whose pivots are at ambient basis positions of degree greater than \(d\). In particular \(\dim Q_a=\dim G_a-\dim(G_a\cap V_d)\). All equalities are literal; no unpaid quotient relation changes the seed coordinates.

Proof. Each ambient basis vector has leading monomial at its pivot, zeros at all other ambient pivots, and only later monomials in its tail. Its degree is therefore its pivot's degree. A combination using a basis vector with highest nonzero pivot degree greater than \(d\) cannot cancel that pivot, so \(V\cap V_d\) is precisely the stated low-degree span. A local echelon vector also has zeros before its pivot, hence uses only ambient vectors whose degrees are no greater than its pivot degree. The old monomial at that ambient pivot has coefficient one and receives zero from every other ambient vector, so its degree equals that pivot degree. The same noncancellation proves that local low-pivot vectors span \(G_a\cap V_d\). Reading pivot coordinates of a vector in \(A_d\) gives only local low vectors; reading those of a vector in \(B_d\) gives only local high vectors. Every such local coefficient can be chosen freely by the corresponding ambient coordinate. This proves both surjections and the direct decompositions. The argument also proves \(\pi_a(V\cap V_d)\subseteq V_d\). It is the elementary filtration check for the existing canonical law, not a new source encoding.
