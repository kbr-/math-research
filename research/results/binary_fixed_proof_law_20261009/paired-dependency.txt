The published family and the degree baseline
All ordinary-PC, CNF-polynomial and retained-ENS assertions in this entry are over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Read Garlík–Gryaznov–Lu–Santhanam–Tzameret, Definition 24 and Theorem 25, including the selected proof. We use tensor order two only: binary matrices \(A\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times n}\), \(B\in\mathbb F_2^{n\times m}\), \(m>n\), with equations \[ E_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^n A_{ik}B_{kj}+\delta_{ij}=0. \tag{1} \] There are \(2mn\) Boolean variables. This is unsatisfiable by rank. The published PHP variable identification is the basis of our existing ordinary-PC transfer; the paper's full PCR-size result is not recertified here.
The same ordinary-degree transfer works for arbitrary \(m>n\), not just \(m=n+1\). Substitute \(A_{ik}=B_{ki}=X_{ik}\). Off-diagonal equations become sums of column collisions; diagonal equations become the unary row equation plus Boolean reductions, through degree two. Variable identification preserves every later inference. The matching-moment extension and stable PC filtration explicitly allow every number \(m\) of rows. Thus (1) has no ordinary degree-\(D\) PC refutation when \(D\ge2\) and \(n\ge2D-1\). This is the recorded/published transfer with its full row range, not a new hardness mechanism.
A width-three boundary without exponential parity expansion
Lemma (quadratic rank CNF). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), for \(m>n\ge2\), there is an unsatisfiable width-three CNF \(F_{m,n}\) with \[ v=2mn+m^2(2n-1),\qquad M=m^2(8n-3) \tag{2} \] variables and clauses, respectively. With binary variable names its encoding length is \(O(m^2n\log(mn))\). Every degree-\(D\) ordinary-PC refutation of this CNF, with Boolean axioms, yields a refutation of (1) through \(\max(2D,6)\). In particular no such refutation exists for \(D\ge3\), \(n\ge4D-1\).
Construction. For each ordered pair \(i,j\), use \(n\) binary AND gates for the products \(A_{ik}B_{kj}\), and \(n-1\) binary XOR gates forming a tree whose root is their sum. All gate outputs are fresh; input matrix entries are shared as indicated. Encode each gate by the four width-three clauses forbidding its four incorrect input/output triples. Add the unit clause forcing the root to \(\delta_{ij}\). This gives exactly (2). All triples have three distinct variable names, so these clauses are non-tautological. Every assignment satisfying the gate clauses gives its uniquely determined gate values, and the root units impose (1). The CNF is therefore unsatisfiable.
Polynomial substitution and degree. Map an AND output to its product \(A_{ik}B_{kj}\). Map an XOR output to the sum of the products below that node. Every image has old degree at most two and is Boolean on old Boolean assignments. Every gate-clause image vanishes on the full old Boolean cube; its original product of at most three literal factors has image degree at most six. Degree-nonincreasing Boolean division therefore gives its old-domain certificate through six. Each output-unit image is exactly \(E_{ij}\), and each auxiliary Boolean equation maps to a domain consequence through degree four. Old matrix domains are unchanged.
A degree-\(D\) proof line maps to degree at most \(2D\). For a nonzero variable-multiplication step, the predecessor has degree at most \(D-1\); multiplying its image by the substituted variable polynomial uses at most two more degrees. Insert the domain certificates just described and replay all linear combinations and multiplications, obtaining the asserted ceiling. Apply the old matrix barrier with cutoff \(2D\) when \(D\ge3\). All cancellations are handled in ordinary PC; no NS-flattening assumption is used. \(\square\)
Remaining-source consequence. Under the exact profile and presentation hypotheses of the binary CNF compiler, with \(h\ge3\), the CNF therefore yields a remaining ENS source over the original matrix variables at degree at most \(\max(2cL,6)\), where \(c=\max(M_{\rm fr},10)\). Apply the same degree-two substitution to every old CNF variable throughout that retained proof, including every later input; leave all fresh ENS coefficient variables unchanged. A remaining block stays complete with its substituted inputs and all original coefficient domains. Its original input, product and companion charges are bounded by twice their former values; no degree is reset after simplification. The substitution maps the old CNF axioms to the certificates above. This supplies a costed source interface, not a refutation of that source or its exclusion. For \(m=n+1\), the encoding is polynomial in \(n\), so polynomial proof-size exponents are still the relevant quantifier.
Paired rank-one summands preserve a residual contradiction
Lemma (paired column restriction). Let \(n\) be even and \(m>n\). Fix a pairing of the inner indices \([n]\). Choose any set of pairs to remain live. In every other pair \(\{k,\ell\}\), assign arbitrary vectors \(a,b\in\mathbb F_2^m\) by \[ A_{\cdot k}=A_{\cdot\ell}=a,\qquad B_{k\cdot}=B_{\ell\cdot}=b^{\mathsf T}. \tag{3} \] If \(n'\ge2\) inner indices remain, restricting (1) gives exactly the same rank system with shapes \(m\times n'\) and \(n'\times m\), after renaming. It has no degree-\(D\) PC refutation for \(D\ge2\), \(n'\ge2D-1\). The restriction of \(F_{m,n}\) on its original matrix variables has no degree-\(D\) PC refutation when \(D\ge3\), \(n'\ge4D-1\), with its gate variables still present.
Proof. A fixed pair contributes \(ab^{\mathsf T}+ab^{\mathsf T}=0\) to \(AB\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Summing over pairs leaves exactly the live terms and the unchanged identity matrix. Apply the arbitrary-row matrix degree bound above. For the restricted CNF, substitute its remaining gate variables by their restricted sum-of-products values. Constants from individual fixed summands can occur inside the tree, but cancel in its root in pairs; every internal image still has degree at most two. The preceding proof transfers a refutation to the smaller matrix system through \(\max(2D,6)\). The restricted CNF is not asserted to be literally the newly constructed smaller CNF. \(\square\)
One may now choose the live pairs independently with probability \(p\), and choose the vectors in (3) uniformly and independently for each fixed pair. Conditional on any outcome with sufficiently many live pairs, the displayed old barrier holds for every set of labels. This avoids the independent bad-vertex phenomenon of the earlier graph restriction. It does not prove that the remaining source has simplified. The published constant-label cancellation restriction is a related special choice of cancelling summands; this full-vector pairing is an elementary adaptation, with no novelty claim.
The variable-wise pruning estimate does not transfer
Proposition (paired column width gap). Under the preceding random live-pair law with \(p=1/10\), take any one inner index \(k\) and the single-clause CNF \[ C=A_{1k}\vee\cdots\vee A_{mk},\qquad m\ge3. \] Its restricted CNF has a minimal clause of width greater than \(b=m-1\) with probability exactly \(1/10\). Thus the independent-variable first-step bound cannot be reused for this law: its formal expression here would be \[ \sum_{\ell>b}\binom m\ell \left(\frac{2p}{1-p}\right)^\ell =(2/9)^m<1/10. \]
Proof. If the pair containing \(k\) remains live, all \(m\) variables of this single clause remain unassigned, so it is a minimal width-\(m\) clause. If the pair is fixed, every literal is assigned: the clause is either satisfied and removed, or becomes empty. Neither case has width greater than \(m-1\). The event is therefore exactly that the pair is live. The numerical comparison follows already from \((2/9)^2=4/81<1/10\), and decreases with \(m\). The earlier theorem assumes independent live bits; that hypothesis fails here. This refutes the transfer of its estimate, not the theorem, not every block restriction estimate, and not the existence of useful choices of source coefficients. \(\square\)
The obstruction is specific: an arbitrarily wide clause concentrated in one column is exposed by one live-pair coin. A block-based theorem must treat such functions separately instead of replacing their variable count by the number of independently sampled blocks. The new family has not yet supplied a common hardness-and-simplification theorem.
