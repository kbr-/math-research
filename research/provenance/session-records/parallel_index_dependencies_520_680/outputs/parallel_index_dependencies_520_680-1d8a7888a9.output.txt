  \]

  \]

   \[
    k\ge\max\left\{1,\,2t,\,
           \left\lceil\sqrt{m\log(4M)}\right\rceil\right\},

  \]

   \[
       4(k+t-1)&lt;n,\qquad n\ge2B-1.
       \tag{TARGET-range}
  \]

   \[
       \mathcal C_D(\Gamma)\cap\mathcal L_t=\{0\}.
       \tag{TARGET-separation}
  \] 
    Proof.  Suppose \(\Gamma\) proves a nonzero \(q\in\mathcal L_t\) through degree \(D\). Use the multiplier space \(\mathcal V_{q,k}\). It has dimension \(\sum_{j\le k}\binom{m-s}{j}\ell^j\), with \(s=\deg q\le t\).

    \frac{\binom{v-r_*+k}{k}}{\binom{m-t}{k}\ell^k}
      =\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}
          \left(1-
            \frac{r_*-1-\ell t-(\ell+1)j}

   For every fixed prime, polynomial \(M\), and polylogarithmic \(D,t\) with \(t\le D\), these conditions hold eventually with the displayed \(k\). Hence the qualitative consequence exclusion holds throughout that range, over the complete original affine family.

### 528 lem:multi-axis-cube-residual-detector
multi-axis-cube-residual-detector  1. A signed cube can detect several bits in one row 
   Work over \(\mathbb F_p\), with \(n=2^\ell\) labels and \(m=n+1\) pigeons. Suppose there are pairwise disjoint coordinate cubes \(C_i\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) with free axes \(T_i\). Put 
   \[
       F=\sum_{i\in S}2^{|T_i|}.
  \] 
   Assume \(m\ge B\) and \(n-F\ge2(B-u)-1\), with at least one residual label when a positive residual degree is needed. Since \(F\ge2u\), these bounds also imply \(n\ge2B-1\). Then 
   \[
       \tau R\notin\mathcal C_B(\mathcal F_n^{\rm fun}).
       \tag{HC-detector}
  \] 
    Proof.  Delete all \(F\) cube labels, and take a normalized residual matching design \(\lambda\) on the remaining rows through degree \(B-u\). Its existence follows from the  all-field matching-extension theorem ; at residual degree zero use the functional on constants. For a cube vertex \(z\), give row \(i\) weight 
   \[
       \varepsilon_i(z)=\prod_{a\in T_i}(2z_a-1)\in\{1,-1\}\subseteq\mathbb F_p.
  \] 
   This formula remains valid in characteristic two. Each cube's weights sum to zero. As in the  signed-edge construction , define a matching moment to be zero unless every selected row is present on its own cube and all other rows avoid every deleted label. In the remaining case put 
   \[
      \mu(X_A)=
          \left(\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i(A(i))\right)
          \lambda(X_{A|_{\overline S}}).
       \tag{HC-moments}
  \] 
   If a marginal is missing exactly one selected row, summing over that cube gives zero. If more selected rows are missing, every extension still has zero moment. An incorrect selected label or an outside row on a deleted label cannot become supported after adding one cell. Once all selected rows are present, each remaining marginal is a residual marginal multiplied by the fixed cube signs. Thus every old matching constraint holds. Boolean powers and row/column collisions give the full ordinary functional, annihilating \(\mathcal I_B\); old stability gives \(\mathcal C_B=\mathcal I_B\). 
   For any bit monomial, the decoded value factors into its signed finite differences in the selected rows and a residual moment. The residual factor is independent of the choices inside the cubes because  all  their labels were deleted. A missing selected bit makes one difference zero. A monomial containing all \(K\) selected bits has degree at least \(K\); since \(\deg R=K\), it must be exactly the chosen squarefree monomial, with no extra bits or powers.    This refines the earlier top-term detector and reuses the higher-dimensional differences from  old transported rigidity . The new point is the residual design with an explicit deleted-column cost, allowing the proof degree \(B\) to be much larger than the target degree.

  \]

  \]
