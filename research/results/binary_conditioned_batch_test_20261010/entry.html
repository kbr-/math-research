A conditional potential in one paid quotient

Work in a binary degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Let \(A\) be a fixed prefactor of safe degree at most \(a\), let \(U\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le K}\) be an allowed fixed binary linear space, and let \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), \(r\ge1\). Let \(h\ge1\) be the number of new rows. Assume \[ a+K+(h+2)\delta\le D. \tag{1} \] Let \(\mathcal B=\operatorname{span}(1,g_1,\ldots,g_r)\), with powers denoting spans of input words. For a prefix of \(j\) legal rows set \[ P_j=\prod_{u=1}^j(1-q_u),\qquad W_j=\{([AP_jg_i f])_i:f\in\mathcal B^{h-j}U\}, \quad d_j=\dim W_j, \qquad 0\le j\le h. \tag{2} \] The empty prefix is one. The terminal \(W_h\) is precisely the actual error image on \(U\). Every \(W_j\) has representatives through \(a+K+(h+1)\delta\), independent of \(j\). Earlier obligations are retained in the final domain \(U\); the larger word spaces are only used to bound errors.

Lemma (conditioned word bound). Conditional on any fixed first \(j<h\) rows, a fresh uniform binary coefficient row gives \[ \mathbb E(d_{j+1}\mid q_1,\ldots,q_j)\le\tfrac12d_j. \tag{3} \] If \(h\le r\) and the coefficient rows are sampled uniformly outside the span of their predecessors, then \[ \mathbb E(d_{j+1}\mid q_1,\ldots,q_j) \le\frac{2^{r-j-1}-1}{2^{r-j}-1}d_j. \tag{4} \] In particular \(2^{-(h-j)}d_j\) is a supermartingale for the first law, and iteration gives the already known \(2^{-h}d_0\) bound. The second law gives \((2^{r-h}-1)/(2^r-1)\) times \(d_0\). These bounds require no closure of \(U\) under input multiplication.

Proof. After fixing the history, \(W_j\) is an actual multiplication image with prefactor \(AP_j\) and fixed domain \(\mathcal B^{h-j}U\). Its degree parameters are \(a+j\delta\) and \(K+(h-j)\delta\). The one-row Frobenius reserve is their sum plus \(2\delta\), exactly (1). The existing arbitrary-domain shortening theorem therefore bounds its intersection with the fresh coefficient-row kernel by \(d_j/2\) in expectation.

For every realized row, \(W_{j+1}\) lies in that intersection. Indeed \((1-q_{j+1})\mathcal B^{h-j-1}U\subseteq\mathcal B^{h-j}U\), and applying the coefficient row to an output gives \(AP_jq_{j+1}(1-q_{j+1})f=0\) by Booleanity. This certificate fits (1). Hence (3).

For (4), all previous coefficient rows annihilate \(W_j\), since their factors occur in \(P_j\); multiplying their Boolean identities by the remaining factors and the generated domain fits the same window. Under unrestricted sampling, a new row is in their \(j\)-dimensional span with probability \(2^{j-r}\), and then its row-kernel intersection with \(W_j\) has dimension exactly \(d_j\). If \(E_j\) denotes expected intersection dimension outside that span, then \[ 2^{j-r}d_j+(1-2^{j-r})E_j\le d_j/2. \] Solving gives the displayed factor. Bound \(d_{j+1}\) by the corresponding intersection as before. The factors telescope, and (3)'s scaling proves the supermartingale statement. \(\square\)

The subtraction for rows in the old span is made for the intersection space, not for \(W_{j+1}\): shortening the word horizon can itself shrink the latter even on such a row. Keeping that distinction is necessary for the proof. If \(0\le\tau\le h\) is a stopping time for the revealed rows and the remaining rows continue to be fresh uniform rows, backward induction gives \(\mathbb E(d_h\mid\mathcal F_\tau)\le2^{-(h-\tau)}d_\tau\). This statement does not permit an arbitrary nonuniform continuation or promise a history with an improved source-specific value.

Why exposure alone supplies no new rate

The existing saturation control already has the full Boolean multiplier space and coordinate inputs. There \(W_j\) is the full vector-function space on the nonzero feature vectors in the coefficient kernel. Thus \[ d_j=2^{r-\operatorname{rank}C_j}-1. \tag{5} \] No adaptive or deterministic policy using at most \(h<r\) rows can make the rank of its final coefficient matrix exceed \(h\), so \(d_h\ge2^{r-h}-1\). Equality holds on every independent-row path. The factor in (4), and its product, are attained. A predictable bijective change of row coordinates also leaves the uniform law unchanged by legal row centering. This does not forbid deliberate nonuniform choices on other inputs.

For completeness, the same control is a genuine nonlinear two-layer source after a fixed legal bottom interpretation. Take \(r\) disjoint groups of \(d\ge2\) old Boolean variables \(x_{i,u}\), and \(z_i=\prod_{u=1}^d x_{i,u}\). In bottom block \(i\), use the literal inputs \(1-x_{i,u}\), accuracy \(d\), and select each input once. Its product becomes \(z_i\) and all bottom companions vanish by Booleanity. One parent reads these \(r\) whole bottom products, with accuracy \(h<r\). Every parent coefficient row is a legal combination of these actual input positions. Use the allowed multiplier space of all polynomials in \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\), with multilinear degree at most \(r\) in the features and old degree at most \(K=rd\).

The feature map onto \(\mathbb F_2^r\) is surjective, so distinct feature functions remain linearly independent in the full old Boolean quotient. This multiplier space is closed under feature multiplication, and words in (2) add no further functions. The error tuple \(P_{C_j}(z)z\) is supported on exactly \(\ker C_j\setminus\{0\}\); each such feature vector contributes one independent image direction. This proves (5) here too. It is the old saturation proof under an explicit feature substitution, not a new unavoidable hard family. Earlier bottom obligations vanish identically and are preserved for every allowed multiplier.

Keep original degrees: bottom products have carried degree \(2d\) and bottom companions \(2d+1\); parent products have carried degree \(h(2d+1)\) and companions \(h(2d+1)+2d\). Although the specialized parent inputs have degree \(d\), those original costs are not reset. One may choose the Boolean cutoff \(D\) to cover (1) and \(K\) plus all these original ceilings, so there is no unpaid transfer. This is a satisfiable control with a deliberately large allowed domain, not our hard charged quotient. It even has cheap weighted cleanup: choose \(W=z_1\) and a parent row selecting \(z_1\); then \(Wz_i(1-z_1)=0\) for every \(i\), with degree-at-most-\(3d\) Boolean certificates. Thus saturation is not hardness.

Compare the real opportunities with the old tools

Conditioning can make a particular \(d_j\) small, and (3) can certify such a chosen history. The existing input-word theorem already applies after that prefix. What is still absent is a theorem selecting histories with a source-dependent gain for arbitrary mixtures, beyond (3) or (4). Applying optional stopping or naming a conditional potential does not provide it.

The full chosen-prefix proof already shows that intersections can overestimate the same row's actual image, and factor normalization repairs a common-factor degree loss. The legal-centering proof already spends a deterministic prefix on a supplied high-degree complement before using low-degree rows. The input-filtration-return proof forbids dropping the ranks that return to a previously generated layer. These are concrete positive and negative comparators; none supplies the missing small complement or small return for arbitrary shared two-layer tuples.
