Tail cofactor correction

Statement. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(m>n\ge1\), with independent original entry variables \(A:m\times n\), \(B:n\times m\). Write \(E=AB\), \(b_x=x^2+x\), retain all \(2mn\) original Boolean placeholders \(Z_x\), and put

\[W=\prod_{a\ne b}(1+E_{ab}),\qquad P=W\prod_{a=1}^mE_{aa},\qquad D_i=\prod_{a\lt i}E_{aa}.\]

For \(1\le i\lt j\le m\), define ordered row and column sets and the literal omitted-factor product

\[R_i=\{i+1,\ldots,m\},\qquad S_{ij}=\{i,\ldots,m\}\setminus\{j\},\qquad C_{ij}=\det E_{R_i,S_{ij}},\] \[W_{ij}=\prod_{a\ne b,\ (a,b)\ne(i,j)}(1+E_{ab}),\qquad K_{ij}(X,Z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^n\bigl(B_{\ell j}Z_{A_{i\ell}}+A_{i\ell}^2Z_{B_{\ell j}}\bigr).\]

Then the full formal Boolean correction is

\[Q(X,Z)=\sum_{i\lt j}D_iW_{ij}C_{ij}K_{ij}(X,Z),\qquad Q(X,b)=P,\qquad Q(X,0)=0.\]

It is linear in the complete original placeholder tuple. Terms with \(i\lt m-n\) vanish formally and can be omitted: their minor order \(m-i\) exceeds \(n\). If the joint circuits for the remaining minors have size \(H\) and maximum depth \(e\), this full correction has gate/wire size \(O(H+m^4+m^2n+1)\) and depth at most \(e+8\), using division-free unbounded-fanin sums/products and binary constants. This ledger is conditional on actual minor circuits; determinant symbols are not extra primitive gates. No polynomial-size fixed-depth minor implementation or full correction implementation is supplied.

Proof of the whole contraction. The product Frobenius witness gives \(K_{ij}(X,b)=E_{ij}^2+E_{ij}\) formally, by summing \((A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j})^2+A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j}\). For any commutative characteristic-two square matrix, partition its nonidentity permutations by the first moved row \(i\) and \(j=\pi(i)\). Every earlier row is fixed, so all earlier columns are used and \(j>i\). The remaining bijection is exactly from \(R_i\) to \(S_{ij}\), without further restrictions. All signs are one, hence the formal identity

\[\det E+\prod_aE_{aa}=\sum_{i\lt j}D_iE_{ij}C_{ij}.\]

Multiply by \(W\). In each summand \(W=W_{ij}(1+E_{ij})\), so the right side is exactly \(Q(X,b)\). For \(E=AB\) and \(m>n\), \(\det E=0\) formally. To verify that without a Boolean argument, expand each row's inner-index choice in the determinant. Its remaining permutation sum is the determinant of the selected rows of \(B\), multiplied by the selected \(A\) entries. At least two row choices agree; swapping their determinant positions pairs equal terms and gives zero in characteristic two. Thus \(Q(X,b)=P\), not just agreement on cube points. The identical argument shows every square minor of \(AB\) of order greater than \(n\) is zero. This proves the stated truncation. Linearity follows because all multipliers are placeholder-free and each \(K\) is linear.

Actual cost. Compute all \(E\) entries once with \(O(m^2n)\) wires and two layers. All \(D_i\) are explicit prefix products; each \(W_{ij}\) is an ordinary product of at most \(m(m-1)-1\) complements. Building these products separately costs \(O(m^4)\) wires at depth at most four. The \(K\) circuits have total size \(O(m^2n)\) and depth at most three, including explicit squaring with two input wires. Graft the supplied shared minor circuits, then add one product and one outer sum layer. Depth \(\max(e+2,6)\le e+8\) is conservative. No succinct powers, field enlargement, cofactor extraction or original-variable replacement is used. This is an IPS circuit ledger, not a PC-degree reduction.

Original degree. Each original \(E\) has degree at most two, \(D_i\) degree at most \(2(i-1)\), \(W_{ij}\) degree at most \(2(m(m-1)-1)\), and \(C_{ij}\) degree at most \(2(m-i)\). Every coefficient in \(K_{ij}\) has old degree at most two. Adding these displayed costs gives old coefficient degree at most \(2m^2-2\) for \(Q\), and degree at most \(2m^2\) after \(Z_x=b_x\). This is a formal output ceiling; it does not reset the original product costs or bound internal degrees of supplied minor circuits, and supplies no PC refutation-degree transfer.

What the tail registers and masks do

Unmasked control. At \(m=2n\), use \(i=n,j=n+1\). The surviving minor has rows \(n+1,\ldots,2n\), columns \(\{n,n+2,\ldots,2n\}\), and order \(n\). Set these columns of \(B\), in that order, to the standard basis of \(\mathbb F_2^n\). Its submatrix becomes the independent \(n\times n\) block of \(A\), so \(C_{n,n+1}\) restricts to its generic determinant. The known cor:binary-determinant-depth-gap therefore excludes polynomial-size uniformly fixed-depth binary arithmetic circuits for these individual minor registers at growing parameters. This does not extract a determinant from \(Q\), nor show that the chosen masks can be set to one compatibly with this restriction.

Mask control. For any Boolean assignment to the entries of an arbitrary matrix \(E\), every weight \(G_{ij}=D_iW_{ij}C_{ij}\) is zero. If an off-diagonal entry other than \((i,j)\) is one, \(W_{ij}=0\). Otherwise all entries \(E_{ri}\) for \(r>i\) are zero. The minor \(C_{ij}\) contains column \(i\) and exactly those rows, so it has a zero column. This proves the control without assuming rank deficiency. On original Boolean entries of \(A,B\), the entries of \(AB\) are binary, hence \(Q(a,Z)=0\) as a polynomial in the free placeholder tuple. The masked coefficients are not a nonzero Boolean determinant function that can be inverted. This agrees with the recorded normalized-pivot and cube-erasure warnings, and is no lower bound on the formal correction.

Attempt beyond the register. Expanding the exposed column \(i\) gives \(C_{ij}=\sum_{r>i}E_{ri}C_{ij}^{(r)}\), where \(C_{ij}^{(r)}\) deletes row \(r\) and column \(i\). Define \(W_{ij,ri}\) by omitting both distinct factors \((1+E_{ij})\), \((1+E_{ri})\). Then

\[G_{ij}=D_i\sum_{r>i}W_{ij,ri}\bigl(E_{ri}^2+E_{ri}\bigr)C_{ij}^{(r)}.\]

This exact masked Laplace step trades one growing minor for smaller minors and one old Frobenius defect; it keeps the full formula linear in \(Z\). It does not make that next defect vanish formally. When \(r=j\), the remaining row and column sets coincide, leaving a principal minor of order \(m-i-1\); the previously forced zero-column argument no longer applies to that minor. At \(i=n,m=2n\), its order is still \(n-1\). For \(r\ne j\), a mismatch remains. Thus the local step supplies no terminating constant-depth recurrence by itself. I tried using it to replace all tail registers by simple products; the principal-block state remains unpaid. A direct contraction of the combined marked output could still bypass both kinds of register.
