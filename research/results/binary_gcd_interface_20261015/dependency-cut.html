Axiom cut normalization

Lemma. Over any field, let \(C(x,Y)\) be an IPS certificate for a full generator tuple \(F\), including any Boolean generators: \(C(x,0)=0\), \(C(x,F)=1\). For a chosen generator \(F_0\), define \[ D(x,Y)=(1-F_0(x))C(x,Y)+Y_0. \] Then \(D\) is a certificate and \(D\equiv Y_0\pmod{1-F_0}\). Every polynomial substitution \(\rho\) fixing placeholders and satisfying \(\rho(F_0)=1\) gives \(\rho(D)=Y_0\); \(\rho\) need not be a retraction. Hilbert-likeness is preserved. From size \(S\), depth \(d\) for \(C\) and size \(s_0\), depth \(\delta_0\) for \(F_0\), sharing yields size \(O(S+s_0+1)\) and depth at most \(\max(d,\delta_0+1)+2\). If the original Hilbert-like coefficients have degree at most \(L\) and \(\deg F_0\le g\), the new coefficients have degree at most \(L+g\).

Algebraic proof. Substituting zero placeholders gives zero, while substituting \(F\) gives \((1-F_0)+F_0=1\). Divisibility of \(D-Y_0\) is literal. For \(C=\sum_ic_iY_i\), the new coefficients are \((1-F_0)c_i\) for \(i\ne0\) and \((1-F_0)c_0+1\) for \(i=0\). Using \(\sum_ic_iF_i=1\), one also has \[ D-C=\sum_{i>0}c_i(F_iY_0-F_0Y_i). \] Thus it is an explicit Koszul-syzygy change, with all Boolean coefficients retained. The displayed circuit computes the change directly; it does not extract or restrict individual coefficients. The size, depth and degree claims follow. This implies the earlier unit-slice existence statement under its hypotheses with better circuit accounting.
