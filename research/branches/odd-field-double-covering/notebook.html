Working mathematical context

Exact covering model

Fix an odd prime power \(q\). A cover is a multiset of proper affine hyperplanes of \(\mathbb F_q^n\). Every nonzero vector must lie in at least two members, counted with repetition; zero lies in at most one. The minimum multiset size is \(f_q(n,2,1)\). The printed conjectural equality is for sufficiently large \(n\) at fixed \(q\), not an asserted equality in every dimension.

Inherited bounds and positive control

The published elementary lower bound is \((q-1)n+1\), obtained by deleting a plane so the remaining cover avoids zero and covers every other point once. The source construction gives \((q-1)(n+1)\): take \(x_i=c\) for every coordinate and every nonzero \(c\), then \(\sum_i x_i=c\) for each nonzero \(c\). For \(q=3\) this leaves exactly one plane between lower and upper bounds. These are inherited bounds, not new claims.

Formal multiplicity versus values

For a product \(P=\prod_{r=1}^{m}L_r\) of affine hyperplane equations, retain the formal polynomial and factor count \(m\). Hasse multiplicity at a point is the minimum total degree of a nonzero term after translation; each incident affine factor contributes one. The two origin cases impose multiplicity zero or one. Factors through the origin cannot be normalized to have constant term one, so the zero-avoiding selector representation does not cover both cases automatically.

Reduction modulo \(x_i^q-x_i\) preserves values but can erase multiplicity. In the binary example already recorded in the parent assessment, \(1-x\) and \((1-x)^r\) have the same reduced value class but different vanishing orders. A thicker local algebra or incidence ledger is only a proposed mechanism. A low-degree arbitrary polynomial need not split into comparably few affine factors.

Frontier scope and dependencies

The inherited frontier uses independent indicator groups \(a_{j,\lambda}\), with Booleanity and within-group exclusivity, scalar inputs \(g_j=\sum_\lambda\lambda a_{j,\lambda}\), and coefficient degree \(T\) measured in these indicators. For alphabet sizes \(k_j\), its exact condition is \(\sum_{i=1}^{\max(0,N-hT)}k_{(i)}\le h\). The \(T=0\) specialization counts a single covering; \(T>0\) permits polynomial coefficients that are not ordinary affine hyperplane equations. Neither specialization has a proved multiplicity-preserving bridge here.
