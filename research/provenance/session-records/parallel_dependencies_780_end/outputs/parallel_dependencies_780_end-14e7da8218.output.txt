
###hw-ns-criterion
1. Setting, a no-fall criterion and the forced fall
Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with the base \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\) (rows \(i\le N+1\), holes \(j\le N\), \(\rho_i=\sum_jx_{ij}\)) in matching normal form, as in the stability theorem: \(\mathcal P_{\le e}\) is the span of the matching monomials of size at most \(e\), \(m_e\) is the number of matchings of size \(e\), and the normal form of the degree-\(e\) Nullstellensatz space of the base is \(\mathcal I_e=\operatorname{span}\{x_T(1+\rho_i):|T|\le e-1,\ i\notin\operatorname{row}(T)\}\). For affine forms \(\lambda_{b,u}\) put \(Q_b=\prod_{u\le h}(1+\lambda_{b,u})\) (in normal form), \(b\le M\), and
\[ G_e=\mathcal I_e+\sum_{b\le M}Q_b\,\mathcal P_{\le e-h}\qquad(G_e=\mathcal I_e\text{ for }e<h), \]
the degree-\(e\) Nullstellensatz space of the base with the clauses. Let \(\mathcal C_B\) be the normal-form closure: the least subspace of \(\mathcal P_{\le B}\) that contains the normal forms of the axioms of degree at most \(B\) (the \(1+\rho_i\) and the \(Q_b\)) and is closed under \(f\mapsto\operatorname{nf}(xf)\) for \(f\) of degree at most \(B-1\). It contains the normal form of every line of a degree-\(B\) polynomial-calculus derivation, so \(1\notin\mathcal C_B\) implies that there is no refutation of degree \(B\); it may be larger than the set of those normal forms. A degree fall at level \(e\) is an element of \(G_e\cap\mathcal P_{\le e-1}\) outside \(G_{e-1}\).
Lemma E (no-fall criterion). (i) If \(G_B\cap\mathcal P_{\le B-1}=G_{B-1}\), then \(\mathcal C_B=G_B\). (ii) If \(G_e\cap\mathcal P_{\le e-1}=G_{e-1}\) for every \(1\le e\le B\), then \(1\notin G_B=\mathcal C_B\), and the base with the clauses \(Q_b\) (hence with the weaker axioms \(g_{b,i}Q_b\) of Lemma K (i)) has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree \(B\).
Proof. \(G_B\subseteq\mathcal C_B\) because every Nullstellensatz consequence is a consequence within the same ceiling. Conversely \(G_B\) is a subspace that contains the axioms, and for \(f\in G_B\) of degree at most \(B-1\) the hypothesis puts \(f\) in \(G_{B-1}\); since \(\operatorname{nf}(x\cdot x_T(1+\rho_i))\) is zero or \(x_{T'}(1+\rho_i)\) with \(|T'|\le B-1\) and \(i\notin\operatorname{row}(T')\), and \(\operatorname{nf}(xQ_bm)=Q_b\operatorname{nf}(xm)\), one has \(\operatorname{nf}(xG_{B-1})\subseteq G_B\). This is (i). For (ii), the chain of hypotheses gives \(G_B\cap\mathcal P_{\le0}=G_{B-1}\cap\mathcal P_{\le0}=\dots=G_0=\mathcal I_0=0\), as \(h\ge1\). \(\square\)
This is the general one-level form of the truncated-closure induction that the record carries out three times for specific classes (unary base, transported bit class, conditioned class); the stability entry explicitly does not assert equality for an augmented system, and Lemma E says what would have to be shown there. No relation between \(N\) and \(B\) is used; for \(M=0\) the hypothesis is the recorded stability, valid for \(N\ge2B-1\). Part (i) uses only level \(B\); the lower levels are used for \(1\notin G_B\) alone. The proof uses neither the field nor the PHP, only \(\operatorname{nf}(xG_{B-1})\subseteq G_B\) and \(G_0=0\).
Observation F (forced fall). Let \(X\subseteq Y\subseteq\mathcal P_{\le e}\) be subspaces and \(\operatorname{top}_e(Y)=Y/(Y\cap\mathcal P_{\le e-1})\). Then \(\dim X-\dim(X\cap\mathcal P_{\le e-1})\le\dim\operatorname{top}_e(Y)\). Hence, if \(X\subseteq G_e\) and \(\dim X-\dim(X\cap G_{e-1})>\dim\operatorname{top}_e(Y)\), there is a degree fall at level \(e\). (a) With \(X=G_e\), \(Y=\mathcal P_{\le e}\): a fall is forced once \(\dim G_e-\dim G_{e-1}>m_e\). (b) If all forms lie in a space \(L\) of affine forms containing the constants, \(e=h+1\), \(X=\mathcal I_e+\sum_bQ_bL\) and \(Y=\mathcal I_e+L^{h+1}\) (products of \(h+1\) elements of \(L\)): a fall is forced once \(\dim X-\dim G_h>\dim\operatorname{top}_{h+1}(\mathcal I_{h+1}+L^{h+1})\). The proof is the rank inequality for the projection to the top-degree component. The recorded reading of the jump as "a saturation of the space reachable with the available multipliers" (term values) and the generic Hilbert-series count in the bit ring (cycle 223) are instances without the count; that yardstick, \((1+t)^k(1+t^\ell)^{-m}\), treats the collision flats as generic polynomials of degree \(\ell\), and the clause series \((1+t)^k(1-t^\ell/(1+t)^\ell)^m\) has a later first nonpositive coefficient (on sixteen holes, \(m=136\): degree 8 against 7 at \(k=20\), 14 against 11 at \(k=40\); noted by the reviewer, recomputed in series_compare.py).
The count under test. The relations \(\lambda_{b,u}Q_b=0\) and \(Q_bQ_{b'}=Q_{b'}Q_b\) hold identically. If they generate all relations among the multiples, then with \(H_R(t)=\sum_k\gamma_kt^k\), \(\gamma_k=\dim\mathcal P_{\le k}/(\mathcal I_k+\mathcal P_{\le k-1})\) (in the stable range \(\gamma_k=c_k-c_{k-1}\) for \(c_k=\dim\mathcal P_{\le k}/\mathcal I_k\)), the graded dimensions of the quotient by \(M\) clauses are the coefficients of
\[ H_R(t)\Bigl(1-\frac{t^h}{(1+t)^h}\Bigr)^{M} \tag{SR-series} \]
up to the first coefficient that is not positive: the multiples of one clause are a copy of the quotient by its \(h\) forms, shifted by \(h\). For \(h=1\) this is \(H_R(t)/(1+t)^M\). It is the series of a semi-regular sequence over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the sense of Bardet, Faugère and Salvy (cited from memory, not read in this cycle), taken relative to the base ring instead of the Boolean ring. Level by level: a clause of \(h\) forms adds \(c_1-h\) at degree \(h+1\); at degree \(h+2\) the \(b\)-th clause of two forms adds \(c_2-2c_1+3-(b-1)\). At level one the count is an upper bound for \(\dim G_B\) when the forms of a clause are independent modulo \(\mathcal I_1\) and the constants; at level two it is an upper bound if moreover \(\lambda_1R_{\le1}\cap\lambda_2R_{\le1}\) is spanned by \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\) and each \(\lambda_uR_{\le1}\) has dimension \(c_1-1\) (\(R_{\le k}=\mathcal P_{\le k}/\mathcal I_k\)), as the review noted: a rank below the count refutes the count, a rank above it refutes that claim. If the count holds for the dimensions, a negative coefficient in degree \(B\) forces a fall by Observation F (a).


###hw-ns-reading
3. Reading, conjecture, and what it would give
Conjecture SR (finite evidence only). Let the forms be uniform in a space \(L\) that is either all affine forms or the unary images of bit-affine forms. Then with probability \(1-o(1)\): \(\dim G_B\) follows (SR-series); no degree fall occurs at any level \(e\le B\) before the first clause at which Observation F forces one for a pair of the fixed family \(X=\mathcal I_e+\sum_bQ_bL^{d}\), \(Y=\mathcal I_e+L^{d+h}\) with \(d+h\le e\) (for all affine forms this is count (a)), up to a bounded number of clauses; and 1 does not enter \(G_B\) before \(\mathcal I_B+L^B\subseteq G_B\). All degrees are unary degrees: the top part of \(L^3\) for the label forms is spanned by the products of three label bits on three distinct rows, \(\binom93\cdot3^3=2268\), and the bit degree plays no role (in pure bit degree the base itself falls early, as the reviewer notes from the record). Evidence: every run above and every recorded jump; the deviation for general forms is a fall of amount one at slack 0 or 1, in four of the six boundary events, the behaviour of a square or nearly square uniform matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) (singular with probability about 0.71, rank deficient at slack one with probability about 0.42); for label parity constraints at degree two the fall comes at slack one in two of three seeds with amounts 1 and 2 (a corank of two has probability about 0.04 for a uniform matrix, so this comparison is less apt there). The third clause was checked directly for label forms only, where it has content: after clauses 123 to 126 of width two (70, 44, 18, 0) and after clauses 16, 17 of label parity constraints at degree three (88, 0); at degree two the reviewer's scratch check gives 10, 11, 2 before and 0 at the clause where 1 enters (not rerun here). All statistics except those boundary events are the same for all seeds (generic ranks), so the seeds are not independent evidence, and the evidence is at degrees two to four with \(h\le3\).
What it would give. With Lemma E, Conjecture SR implies polynomial-calculus hardness of the base with generic clauses up to the forced clause. For general forms the fall at degree \(B\) is forced, to first order in (SR-series), at \(M^*\approx\gamma_B/\gamma_{B-h}\) clauses, which is of order \((N^2/B)^h\) for \(B\ll\sqrt N\) (\(\gamma_1=N^2-1\), \(\gamma_2=\binom{N+1}2((N-1)(N-2)-1)\), exact in the three computed cases, and \(\gamma_k\) is of order \(N^{2k}/k!\) for \(k\ll\sqrt N\)), and for label forms the pair with multipliers from \(L^{B-h}\) gives, heuristically, about \(((N+1)\log_2N/B)^h\); this is the count of one pair, another pair may force a fall earlier, so it is an upper estimate. Both are superpolynomial for \(h\ge c'\log_2N\) and \(B\) polylogarithmic, so under Conjecture SR, and by extrapolation from \(h\le3\), \(B\le4\), the statement (HW) would hold for generic clauses with room to spare, also at total density above one. For the decoded forms the count has to be made in the subalgebra generated by the forms: count (a) is off by a factor of three there (91 against 264).
What suffices, and what is a convenience. (HW) needs \(1\notin\mathcal C_B\) only; the absence of every fall is stronger, and it is the route that Lemma E offers, not a necessity. It is also the kind of statement that is open in simpler settings: semi-regularity of generic sequences is a conjecture already over the Boolean ring (recollection of the literature, not checked here). What the goal needs is more than the generic case: clauses chosen by a proof. One precision from the falsification attempts below belongs to the statement of (HW): the codimension \(h\) of a clause has to be counted modulo the row sums.
Falsification attempts. (1) The standing reviewer pointed to the recorded jump at axiom 91 with decoded forms as a counter-instance to "falls only when forced": count (a) forces a fall only from clause 264. The subalgebra count (b) explains it exactly (2268, amount 7), and also the entry of 1 at clause 126. (2) Wide forms inside a small subalgebra: the column sums \(\kappa_j=\sum_ix_{ij}\) satisfy \(\sum_i(1+\rho_i)+\sum_j(1+\kappa_j)=1\) identically, because \(\sum_i\rho_i=\sum_j\kappa_j\) and \(2N+1\) is odd: the \(N\) onto axioms \(1+\kappa_j\), which are avoidance clauses of width one with wide forms and total density \(N/2\), give a Nullstellensatz refutation of degree one over \(\mathbb F_2\) (the classical mod 2 counting argument; \(N\) literal clauses of width one cost nothing). This agrees with the pattern of the data: the subalgebra generated by the \(\kappa_j\) has dimension \(N\) in degree one modulo the base, and 1 enters when it is exhausted. In the regime of (HW) the attack needs a cover: the same identity makes the form \(\sum_j\kappa_j\) constant modulo \(\mathcal I_1\), so a parity decision tree that first queries it has \(2^{h-1}\) leaves of nominal codimension \(h\) whose clauses sum to 1 at degree \(h\), at nominal density one half; modulo the row sums the clauses have codimension \(h-1\) and density one, outside the regime. (3) A tempting shortcut fails, as the reviewer showed: if all forms span \(\ell_0\) dimensions modulo the row sums and the clauses are satisfiable as a formula in these parities, one may fix a satisfying value \(y^*\) and replace the clauses by \(\ell_0\) linear equations, from which every \(Q_b\) follows within degree \(h+1\); but the record has no hardness of the base with linear equations at a chosen right-hand side outside the safe bit-linear systems of Corollary SL (the case split keeps the base hard for some sign vector only, and a fresh block never imposes a value), and the clauses \(1+x_{11}\), \(1+x_{21}\) are satisfiable in their two parities while the base with \(x_{11}=x_{21}=1\) is refuted at degree two. So no bound in the dimension of the form space is claimed. Attempts (1) and (2) do not contradict Conjecture SR or (HW) with the precision on the codimension.
Remark S (the recorded support bound, applied to clauses). By the recorded point-support lemma, every set \(T\subseteq\mathbb F_2^v\) of Boolean points that realizes a normalized degree-\(B\) design of the base has at least \(2^{B-1}\) points (axioms of degree at most two). The exact condition for the design to annihilate the multiples of \(Q_b\) is that the evaluations at \(T\cap A_b\) (\(A_b\) the flat of the clause) vanish on all polynomials of degree at most \(B-h\), so \(T\cap A_b\) is empty or a nonzero word of the dual Reed and Muller code on the flat, with at least \(2^{B-h+1}\) points. Heuristically, a fixed \(T\) meets a random flat in about \(|T|2^{-h}\ge2^{B-h-1}\) points that are not of this form, so a design cannot be chosen before the clauses; \(T\) is not unique (it can be changed by any codeword of the dual Reed and Muller code), so this is a heuristic and not a statement about the design.


###parity-two-criterion
1. The two-form criterion
Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\) in matching normal form, \(N\ge3\). Let \(V'\) be the span of the variables \(x_c\), \(c\) a cell, \(\Theta=\operatorname{span}\{\theta_i\}\) with \(\theta_i=\sum_jx_{ij}\) the linear part of \(1+\rho_i\), and \(V=V'/\Theta\), \(n=\dim V=N^2-1\). Elements of \(\Lambda^2V\) are alternating forms on \(V^*=\{\varphi\in\mathbb F_2^{\rm cells}:\sum_j\varphi_{ij}=0\text{ for all }i\}\). Let \(K_0\subseteq\Lambda^2V'\) be the span of the \(x_c\wedge x_{c'}\) over conflicting cells (same row or same column) and \(\bar K\) its image in \(\Lambda^2V\). The degree-two component of the normal form of a product of two affine polynomials with linear parts \(\alpha,\beta\) is the class of \(\alpha\wedge\beta\) modulo \(K_0\). One has \(\dim\bar K=(N+1)(2N^2-3N+2)/2\) (196, 316, 477 on six, seven, eight holes, equal to \(\binom n2-\gamma_2\) with \(\gamma_2\) from the previous entry): \(K_0\) has the \(N(N+1)(2N-1)/2\) conflicting pairs as a basis, and \(K_0\cap(\Theta\wedge V')=\operatorname{span}\{\theta_i\wedge x_{ij}\}\), of dimension \(N^2-1\). Indeed, if \(\sum_i\theta_i\wedge\alpha_i\in K_0\), its coefficient on a compatible pair \((i,j),(i',j')\) is \(\alpha_i(i',j')+\alpha_{i'}(i,j)=0\); for \(N\ge3\) this makes \(\alpha_i(i',j')\) independent of \(j'\) and symmetric in \(i,i'\), so \(\alpha_i\) is its own-row part plus \(\sum_{i'}e_{ii'}\theta_{i'}\) with \(e\) symmetric, and the second part cancels in \(\sum_i\theta_i\wedge\alpha_i\). Parity constraints are axioms \(a_b=\ell_b+c_b\), \(\ell_b\in V'\), \(c_b\in\mathbb F_2\), \(b\le M\); \(G_1=\mathcal I_1+\operatorname{span}\{a_b\}\), \(G_2=\mathcal I_2+\sum_ba_b\mathcal P_{\le1}\). Let \(\bar W\subseteq V\) be the span of the images \(\bar\ell_b\), \(U=\bar W^\perp\subseteq V^*\), \(d=\dim U\).
Theorem T2 (criterion). (The constants \(c_b\) play no role below.) Suppose the \(\bar\ell_b\) are linearly independent in \(V\) (so \(d=n-M\)) and
\[ \text{no nonzero element of }\bar K\text{ vanishes on }U. \tag{*} \]
Then \(G_2\cap\mathcal P_{\le1}=G_1\) and \(G_1\cap\mathcal P_{\le0}=0\); hence \(1\notin\mathcal C_2=G_2\), and the base with the parity constraints has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree two.
Proof. If \(\sum_ic_i(1+\rho_i)+\sum_be_ba_b\) is a constant, its linear part \(\sum c_i\theta_i+\sum e_b\ell_b\) vanishes, so \(e=0\) by independence in \(V\), then \(c=0\), and the constant is 0. Let \(f\in G_2\) have degree at most one, \(f=\operatorname{nf}(\sum_ip_i(1+\rho_i)+\sum_bg_ba_b)\) with \(p_i,g_b\in\mathcal P_{\le1}\) and linear parts \(\alpha_i,\beta_b\). The degree-two component of \(f\) is the class of \(\kappa=\sum_i\alpha_i\wedge\theta_i+\sum_b\beta_b\wedge\ell_b\), so \(\kappa\in K_0\). Its image \(\bar\kappa=\sum_b\bar\beta_b\wedge\bar\ell_b\) lies in \(\bar K\) and vanishes on \(U\), because every term has a factor in \(\bar W=U^\perp\). By \((*)\), \(\bar\kappa=0\). Extending the independent \(\bar\ell_b\) to a basis of \(V\) and comparing coefficients (Cartan's lemma), \(\bar\beta_b=\sum_{b'}s_{bb'}\bar\ell_{b'}\) with \(s\) symmetric, so \(\beta_b=\sum_{b'}s_{bb'}\ell_{b'}+\tau_b\) with \(\tau_b=\sum_it_{bi}\theta_i\in\Theta\). The normal-form ring is a quotient of a Boolean ring, so every element is idempotent; with \(\ell_{b'}=a_{b'}+c_{b'}\),
\[ \sum_{b,b'}s_{bb'}\ell_{b'}a_b=\sum_b\Bigl(s_{bb}+\sum_{b'}s_{bb'}c_{b'}\Bigr)a_b, \]
the terms with \(b\ne b'\) cancelling in pairs. With \(\theta_i=(1+\rho_i)+1\), \(\tau_ba_b=\sum_it_{bi}(1+\rho_i)a_b+(\sum_it_{bi})a_b\). Hence \(f=\operatorname{nf}(\sum_ip_i'(1+\rho_i))+a'\) with \(p_i'=p_i+\sum_bt_{bi}a_b\in\mathcal P_{\le1}\) and \(a'\in\operatorname{span}\{a_b\}\). Then \(f-a'\in\mathcal I_2\cap\mathcal P_{\le1}=\mathcal I_1\) by the stability theorem (\(B=2\), \(N\ge3\)), so \(f\in G_1\). The last assertion is Lemma E (ii). \(\square\)
Remarks. (a) The criterion is sufficient, not necessary: in the finite check below \((*)\) fails without a fall in a few instances on four holes (a relation among the multiples instead of a fall). (b) If \(\binom d2<\dim\bar K\), the restriction \(\bar K\to\Lambda^2U^*\) has a kernel and \((*)\) fails; reading the proof backwards, a nonzero \(\bar\kappa\in\bar K\cap(\bar W\wedge V)\) lifts to multipliers whose combination has degree at most one, which is a fall or, if it lies in \(G_1\), a relation among the multiples beyond the forced ones. This is the forced count of the previous entry in the form \(\binom d2<\dim\bar K\) (\(M=15\) on six holes, \(190<196\le210\); \(M=32\) on eight, \(465<477\le496\)). (c) \((*)\) is a condition on the subspace \(\bar W\) alone. It fails for the structured families on record: for a form \(\ell\) supported in one row and a cell \(x_{ij}\) of that row, \(\bar\ell\wedge\bar x_{ij}\in\bar K\) vanishes on \(U\); for a column sum \(\kappa_j=\sum_ix_{ij}\), \(\kappa_j\wedge x_{ij}\) is a sum of column conflicts, which is how the recorded column-occupancy equations bring the degree down to two; and all \(N\) column sums are dependent in \(V\) (the onto axioms of the previous entry). (d) Theorem T2 is the analogue, for arbitrary multipliers (\(\bar W\wedge V\)), of the recorded quadratic-faithfulness statements, which treat relations whose two factors are both inputs (\(\bar W\wedge\bar W\)) by the same top-coefficient argument and a deterministic localization (rectangle test, localized directions); as the reviewer notes, for uniform forms that line would cover the \(\bar W\wedge\bar W\) part down to \(d\) of about \(3N\), and the forced fall at \(\binom d2<\dim\bar K\) shows that \(\bar W\wedge V\) is the real constraint.


###parity-two-lemmas
2. Three lemmas on alternating forms over \(\mathbb F_2\)
Lemma R1. Let \(\kappa\) be an alternating form of rank \(2\rho\) on \(X=\mathbb F_2^n\) and \(U\) a uniform subspace of dimension \(d\). Then \(\Pr[\kappa|_U=0]\le2.39\cdot2^{\rho(\rho+1)/2-\rho d}\).
Proof. Let \(\pi:X\to S\) be the quotient by the radical; \(S\) is a nondegenerate symplectic space of dimension \(2\rho\). \(\kappa|_U=0\) means that \(\pi(U)\) is totally isotropic, hence contained in a Lagrangian \(\Lambda\) (dimension \(\rho\)). There are \(\prod_{i=1}^\rho(2^i+1)\le2.39\cdot2^{\rho(\rho+1)/2}\) Lagrangians, and \(\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)\) has codimension \(\rho\), so \(\Pr[U\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)]=\prod_{i<d}(2^{n-\rho-i}-1)/(2^{n-i}-1)\le2^{-\rho d}\). \(\square\)
Lemma R2. If moreover \(\rho\ge d\) and \(d\le n-1\), then \(\Pr[\kappa|_U=0]\le6\cdot2^{(\log_2d)^2/6}\,2^{-\binom d2}\).
Proof. Let \(u_1,\dots,u_d\) be independent uniform vectors of \(X\); conditioned on their independence, which has probability at least \(1-2^{d-n}\ge1/2\), their span is a uniform \(d\)-subspace, so it suffices to bound the probability of \(E=\{\kappa(u_k,u_l)=0\ \forall k<l\}\) by \(3\cdot2^{(\log_2d)^2/6-\binom d2}\). Expose the vectors in order and let \(t\) be the dimension of \(T=\pi(\operatorname{span}(u_1,\dots,u_k))\); while \(E\) holds, \(T\) is isotropic. Given the past, \(u_{k+1}\) is orthogonal to the earlier vectors with probability \(2^{-t}\), and then \(\pi(u_{k+1})\) is uniform on \(T^\perp\supseteq T\), of dimension \(2\rho-t\), so it lies in \(T\) with probability \(2^{2t-2\rho}\). Thus a step either raises \(t\) (probability at most \(2^{-t}\)) or keeps it (probability \(2^{t-2\rho}\)), or \(E\) fails. A history with \(j\) stays ends at \(t=d-j\), its raises contribute \(2^{-\binom{d-j}2}\), each stay at most \(2^{(d-j)-2\rho}\le2^{-(d+j)}\), and there are at most \(\binom dj\) such histories:
\[ \Pr[E]\le\sum_j\binom dj2^{-\binom{d-j}2-j(d+j)}=2^{-\binom d2}\sum_j\binom dj2^{-j(3j+1)/2}\le2^{-\binom d2}\sum_j2^{j\log_2d-3j^2/2}, \]
and the last sum is at most \(2^{(\log_2d)^2/6}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}2^{-\frac32(j-j^*)^2}\le3\cdot2^{(\log_2d)^2/6}\). \(\square\)
Lemma R3 (few conflict two-forms of low rank). \(\bar K\) has at most \(\binom n{2\rho}2^{2\rho(3N-2)}\) elements of rank \(2\rho\).
Proof. Use the basis \(\bar x_{ij}\), \(j\le N-1\), of \(V\) (eliminating \(x_{iN}=\theta_i+\sum_{j<N}x_{ij}\)). A lift in \(K_0\) has same-row coefficients \(r_{i,jj'}\) and same-column coefficients \(c_{ii',j}\), \(j\le N\). After the elimination the matrix \(A\) of the form has arbitrary entries inside a row of the board, and between rows \(i\ne i'\) the entries \(A_{(i,j),(i',j')}=\delta_{jj'}c_{ii',j}+c_{ii',N}\). So the row of \(A\) indexed by \((i,j)\) is determined by \(N-2\) bits inside the board row and by the two bits \(c_{ii',j},c_{ii',N}\) for each of the \(N\) other board rows: at most \(2^{3N-2}\) possibilities. A symmetric matrix of rank \(r\) whose rows indexed by \(C\), \(|C|=r\), span its row space satisfies \(A=YA_{C\cdot}\) with \(Y_{C\cdot}=I\), hence \(A=A_{\cdot C}Y^{\mathsf T}\) and \(A_{C\cdot}=A_{CC}Y^{\mathsf T}\), so \(A_{CC}\) is nonsingular and \(A=A_{\cdot C}A_{CC}^{-1}A_{C\cdot}\) is determined by \(C\) and the rows in \(C\). \(\square\)


###parity-two-uniform
3. Uniform forms
Theorem U. Let \(a_1,\dots,a_M\) be independent uniform affine forms in the variables of \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\), \(d=N^2-1-M\ge1\), \(s\ge1\). If
\[ \binom d2\ge\dim\bar K+\tfrac16(\log_2d)^2+s\qquad\text{and}\qquad d\ge12N+8\log_2N+2s, \]
then with probability at least \(1-2^{-d}-7\cdot2^{-s}\) the spaces \(G_1,G_2\) have no degree fall and \(\mathcal F_N^{\rm fun}\cup\{a_b\}\) has no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree two. For large \(N\) this is the range \(M\le N^2-\sqrt2N^{3/2}(1+o(1))\), and by Remark (b) a fall or a relation is forced when \(\binom d2<\dim\bar K\), that is from \(M\ge N^2-\sqrt2N^{3/2}(1-o(1))\).
Proof. The images \(\bar\ell_b\) are independent uniform vectors of \(V\); they are dependent with probability at most \(2^{M-n}=2^{-d}\), and conditioned on independence \(U\) is a uniform \(d\)-subspace of \(V^*\). By Theorem T2 it suffices to bound the expected number of nonzero \(\kappa\in\bar K\) with \(\kappa|_U=0\). Elements of rank \(2\rho\), \(\rho\le d-1\): by R3, \(\binom n{2\rho}\le2^{4\rho\log_2N}\), R1 and \((\rho+1)/2\le d/2\), their contribution is at most \(2.39\sum_{\rho\ge1}2^{-\rho(d/2-6N-4\log_2N+4)}\le2.39\cdot2\cdot2^{-(s+4)}\). Elements of rank at least \(2d\): at most \(2^{\dim\bar K}\) elements, each with probability at most \(6\cdot2^{(\log_2d)^2/6-\binom d2}\) by R2, together at most \(6\cdot2^{-s}\). \(\square\)
The second hypothesis needs \(\sqrt2N^{3/2}>12N\), so the theorem is asymptotic (\(N\) above about 90 for \(s=5\)); on small boards the low-rank elements have visible probability, as the check shows (six holes, \(M=13\): 2 falls in 40 trials with slack 35 in the count).


###parity-two-label
4. Decoded bit-affine forms
Let \(N=2^\ell\), \(S_{i,t}=\sum_{j:\,{\rm bit}_t(j)=1}x_{ij}\), \(\bar L_i\subseteq V\) the span of the images of \(S_{i,t}\), \(t<\ell\) (dimension \(\ell\)), \(\bar L=\bigoplus_i\bar L_i\) (dimension \((N+1)\ell\)), and \(V_i\subseteq V\) the image of the cells of row \(i\) (dimension \(N-1\)). The unary image of a bit-affine form has its linear part in \(\bar L\) modulo \(\Theta\), and a multiple of \(\theta_i\) changes \(a_b\) by an element of \(\mathcal I_1\) and a constant, which changes neither \(G_1\) nor \(G_2\).
Structure lemma. For \(\ell\ge4\), \(\bar K\cap(\bar L\wedge V)=\bigoplus_i\bar L_i\wedge V_i\).
Proof. \(\bar L\wedge V\) is the set of forms that vanish on \(\bar L^\perp=\bigoplus_i{\rm RM}(\ell-2,\ell)\) (per row, the vectors orthogonal to all affine functions of the label). Take a lift of \(\kappa\in\bar K\) with same-row parts \(R_i\) and same-column coefficients \(c_{ii',j}\). For \(\varphi\) on row \(i\) and \(\psi\) on row \(i'\ne i\), both of degree at most \(\ell-2\), \(\kappa(\varphi,\psi)=\sum_jc_{ii',j}\varphi_j\psi_j\). Every monomial of degree at most \(\ell\) is a product of two monomials of degree at most \(\ell-2\) when \(\ell\ge4\), so the products \(\varphi\psi\) span all functions and \(c_{ii'}=0\). The remaining same-row parts vanish on \({\rm RM}(\ell-2,\ell)=\bar L_i^\perp\cap V_i^*\) exactly when \(\bar R_i\in\bar L_i\wedge V_i\); conversely \(\Lambda^2V_i\subseteq\bar K\) because all pairs in a row conflict. \(\square\)
The part of the statement inside \(\Lambda^2\bar L\) is on record: bit polynomials of degree at most two inject into \(\mathcal P_{\le2}/\mathcal I_2\) (low-degree rigidity, first relation degree, exact ranks in the quadratic quotient checks), so \(\bar K\cap\Lambda^2\bar L\) is spanned by the same-row bit products; the new part is \(\bar L\wedge V\). The checker gives \(\dim\bar K\cap(\bar L\wedge V)=850=17\cdot50\) on sixteen holes, as the lemma says, and \(171=135+36\) on eight holes, where \(\ell=3\) leaves the \(\binom92\) constant cross terms \(\sum_jx_{ij}\wedge x_{i'j}\) (\({\rm RM}(1,3)\) is self-dual).
Theorem Lb. Let \(N=2^\ell\ge16\), let \(a_1,\dots,a_M\) be unary images of independent uniform bit-affine forms, \(d_L=(N+1)\ell-M\ge1\), \(s\ge1\). If
\[ \binom{d_L}2\ge(N+1)\binom\ell2+\tfrac16(\log_2d_L)^2+s\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_L\ge\ell^2+\ell+4+2s, \]
then with probability at least \(1-N^22^{-d_L}-9\cdot2^{-s}\) there is no degree fall and no polynomial-calculus refutation of degree two. In the language of Conjecture H1: for a uniform affine subspace \(W\) of the bit space of dimension \(d_L\ge\ell\sqrt{N+1}\,(1+o(1))\), the base with the decoded equations of \(W\) has no refutation of unary degree two. The statement is about unary degree and says nothing about bit degree (a unary monomial of degree two has bit degree up to \(2\ell\) on two rows; the recorded refutation of a sixteen-dimensional subspace on sixteen holes at bit degree six is compatible with it).
Proof. The linear parts are independent uniform vectors of \(\bar L\), dependent with probability at most \(2^{-d_L}\); otherwise \(\bar W\) is a uniform \(M\)-subspace of \(\bar L\) and \(U\supseteq\bar L^\perp\). Let \(\kappa\in\bar K\) vanish on \(U\). Then \(\kappa\in\bar L\wedge V\), so by the structure lemma \(\kappa=\sum_i(\pi_i+\mu_i)\) with \(\pi_i\in\Lambda^2\bar L_i\) and \(\mu_i=\sum_kS_{i,k}\wedge y_{i,k}\), where the \(y_{i,k}\) belong to a fixed complement of \(\bar L_i\) in \(V_i\) and are independent, and \(S_{i,k}\in\bar L_i\) is nonzero. For \(\varphi\in\bar L^\perp\) and \(\psi\in U\), \(\kappa(\varphi,\psi)=\sum_{i,k}S_{i,k}(\psi)y_{i,k}(\varphi)\); the functionals \(y_{i,k}\) are independent on \(\bar L^\perp\), so \(S_{i,k}(\psi)=0\) for all \(\psi\in U\), that is \(S_{i,k}\in\bar W\). A fixed nonzero vector lies in \(\bar W\) with probability at most \(2^{-d_L}\), and there are \((N+1)(N-1)\) nonzero single-row forms; outside this event \(\mu=0\) and \(\kappa=\pi=\sum_{i\in I}\pi_i\) is a form on \(\bar L^*\), block diagonal of rank \(2\rho\ge2|I|\), that vanishes on the annihilator of \(\bar W\) in \(\bar L^*\), a uniform \(d_L\)-subspace. There are at most \(\binom{N+1}t2^{t\binom\ell2}\) such forms with \(|I|=t\). The exponent in R1 does not increase in \(\rho\) on \([t,d_L-1]\), so the forms with \(\rho\le d_L-1\) contribute at most \(2.39\sum_t2^{-t(d_L/2-\binom\ell2-\ell-1)}\le2.39\cdot2^{-s}\), and those with \(\rho\ge d_L\) at most \(6\cdot2^{-s}\) by R2. Theorem T2 applies. \(\square\)
Not sharp for refutations. The recorded closures tip at degree two only at \(\dim W=6\) and 7 on eight and sixteen holes (data of Conjecture H1), while falls start near \(\binom{d_L}2=(N+1)\binom\ell2\), that is \(d_L\le7\) on eight holes and about \(\ell\sqrt N\) in general: for label forms the absence of falls is much stronger than the absence of a refutation, and Lemma E is a convenience there, as the previous entry warned. The case of Conjecture H1 that the record needs, \(\dim W=0.3v\), is inside the range of Theorem Lb, at degree two only.


###parity-two-check
5. Finite check of the criterion
research/tools/two_form_criterion_check.py computes for random instances, independently of the proofs, whether \((*)\) holds (rank of the restriction of the conflict two-forms to \(U\)), whether \(G_2\cap\mathcal P_{\le1}\ne G_1\), and whether \(\dim G_2\) equals the count. Instances: four holes (uniform and label forms, 200 per \(M\)), six holes uniform (40 per \(M\), \(M=11..17\)), eight holes uniform (20 per \(M\), \(M=29..34\)) and label (40 per \(M\), \(M=15..22\)), and sixteen holes label, the smallest board inside the range of the structure lemma (5 per \(M\), \(M=46,50,52,53,54,55\)); 3750 instances. In no instance with independent forms (all but 1 to 36 of 200 on four holes with label forms, all elsewhere) does \((*)\) hold together with a fall. On six, eight and sixteen holes \((*)\) fails exactly in the instances with a fall; on four holes it also fails without a fall in 2 to 23 of 200 instances per \(M\le4\). On sixteen holes no instance falls for \(M\le53\) (\(d_L\ge15\), \(\binom{15}2=105\ge102\)) and every instance falls for \(M\ge54\) (\(\binom{14}2=91<102\)), the forced count of Theorem Lb. With uniform forms every instance falls from the forced \(M\) (5, 15, 32 on four, six, eight holes) and none on eight holes before it; with label forms on eight holes 1, 2, 2, 7, 32 of 40 fall at \(M=15,\dots,19\) and all from 20. The falls before the forced count are the low-rank events of the first moment, as the reviewer computed: on eight holes the term \((N^2-1)2^{-d_L}\) of Theorem Lb (a single-row label form in \(\bar W\)) is 1.5 to 12 percent for \(M=15,\dots,18\), and on six holes at \(M=13\) the roughly \(2\cdot10^4\) elements of \(\bar K\) of rank two, each with probability about \(3\cdot2^{-22}\), account for 2 falls in 40; this is compatible with the conjecture of the previous entry, whose \(O(1)\) allowance concerns the forced clause, but it shows that on small boards falls before it are not rare. The power of the check is for Theorem T2 only; Theorems U and Lb are asymptotic and rest on their proofs. Lemmas R1 and R2 were compared with exact probabilities obtained by enumerating all \(d\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) for \(4\le n\le8\), \(2\le d\le4\) and every rank (research/tools/isotropy_bounds_check.py, 40 cases): both bounds hold, with largest ratios 0.59 and 0.16.


###parity-two-obstruction
6. Degree three and above: the named obstruction; decision
At level \(B\ge3\), with no fall below, the expected form of the statement (not proved here) is: a fall is an element \(\xi\) of the degree-\(B\) part \(\bar K_B=\bar K\wedge\Lambda^{B-2}V\) of the conflict ideal that vanishes on \(U\) and does not lie in \(\bar W\wedge\bar K_{B-1}\), the part that vanishes for the trivial reason. A first moment over \(\bar K_B\) cannot treat the event "\(\xi|_U=0\)" alone, because that event is dominated by the trivial one. For decomposable \(\xi=x\wedge a\wedge b\) with \(a\wedge b\in\bar K\) the two coincide under \((*)\): \(\xi|_U=0\) means that a nonzero combination \(\alpha x+\beta a+\gamma b\) lies in \(\bar W\); if \(\alpha=1\) then \(\xi=w\wedge a\wedge b\in\bar W\wedge\bar K\), and if \(\alpha=0\) then \(a\wedge b\in\bar K\) vanishes on \(U\), against \((*)\). For sums of decomposables one needs a bound on \(\Pr[\xi|_U=0\text{ and }\xi\notin\bar W\wedge\bar K_{B-1}]\) summable over \(\bar K_B\), which asks for a notion of rank of a \(B\)-form relative to the ideal and a count of the elements of low rank; there is no analogue of the principal-minor argument of R3 for \(B\ge3\) (a form of degree three is not determined by boundedly many of its slices), and I have none. This is the obstruction, named: the relative rank count for the conflict ideal in degree three.
Decision. The two entries allotted to (HW) have given: exact evidence that generic clauses do no harm before a count forces it, a criterion (Lemma E), and a proof of that pattern at degree two for parity constraints, with a method whose cost is not additive in the number of forms; they have not given a proof at any degree above two, nor anything for clauses of width above one or for forms chosen by a proof. (HW) is parked next to Question H, with the relative rank count as the recorded way back in. The next entry is the review of the forms outside the type (source-module moment designs, proof-specific forms), as the route review prescribed.
