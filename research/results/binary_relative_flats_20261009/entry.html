The strongest certificate being tested

Use the canonical binary charged graph quotient, with fixed expansion \(\varepsilon>2\), bounded maximum degree \(\Delta\), and its original proper degree window. Let \(n\) and \(m\) be its vertex and edge counts. Then \(n\ge2m/\Delta\), and every allowed \(1\le K\le D\) has \(K<m/4\). Put \(\rho=K/m\), \(a=\varepsilon/2>1\), \[ \eta=(1+2^{\varepsilon+1}\rho^a)^n-1,\qquad C(K)=\min\left\{\frac1{1-\eta},\ 1+2\eta\right\}, \tag{1} \] where the first option is omitted if \(\eta\ge1\). The no-collision estimate and collision estimate together say \(h(K)\ge B_m(K)/C(K)\), with \(B_m(K)=\sum_{j\le K}2^j\binom mj\).

Let \(F=\prod_{i=1}^r(1-\ell_i)\) be a binary affine zero-flat indicator, with independent affine constraints not spanning one; use two independent old indicators per edge after the degree-preserving local normalization. Retain \(K+r\le D\). Gaussian elimination removes \(r\) pivot variables. If \(p_j\) edges have \(j\) pivots, then \(p_0+p_1+p_2=m\), \(p_1+2p_2=r\), and the exact retained local count is \[ B_{\boldsymbol p}(K)=[z^{\le K}](1+2z)^{p_0}(1+z)^{p_1}. \] The affine image proof bounds the multiplication rank by this count before replacing it by its worst pivot pattern. It also factors through the old quotient. Thus the certificate under test is \[ \frac{\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_K\mapsto[Ff])}{h(K)} \le U_{\boldsymbol p}(K):=\min\left\{1,\ C(K)\frac{B_{\boldsymbol p}(K)}{B_m(K)}\right\}. \tag{2} \] Additional old relations can make the actual rank smaller. An independently proved zero image can of course be removed; (2) describes what the retained pivot and expansion data alone guarantee.

A uniform obstruction to this certificate

Proposition (affine collision budget). There is a constant \(C_{\varepsilon,\Delta}>0\) such that for every \(1\le r\le m\), every pivot pattern above and every allowed \(K\), \[ U_{\boldsymbol p}(K)\ge \exp\!\left[-C_{\varepsilon,\Delta} \left(\frac{r^{\varepsilon/2}}m\right)^{1/(\varepsilon/2-1)}\right]. \tag{3} \] In particular, if \(r=o(m^{2/\varepsilon})\), this certified upper bound is at least \(1-o(1)\), uniformly over cutoffs and pivots. It cannot certify a strict summed-rank deficit for even two images whose affine-constraint ranks \(r\) obey this regime using only their individual bounds (2). Every fixed polylogarithmic rank lies in this regime. No corresponding lower bound for the actual error rank is asserted.

Proof: removing pivots does not shrink the local count enough. A degree-\(j\) local normal monomial is obtained by choosing \(j\) distinct edges uniformly and one of their two indicators uniformly. The ratio of its retained count to \(2^j\binom mj\) is the probability that none of its indicator variables is among the \(r\) pivots. Expose the edge/indicator choices sequentially. After any successful prefix, at most \(r\) forbidden choices remain among the \(2(m-i)\) choices at step \(i\). Therefore for \(j\le K\), \[ \Pr(\text{avoid pivots})\ge\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(1-\frac r{2(m-i)}\right) \ge\exp(-2rj/m). \] For the last inequality, \(i<K<m/4\), \(r\le m\), and \(\log(1-x)\ge-x/(1-x)\) give a per-step logarithmic loss at most \(r/[2(m-i)-r]\le2r/m\). Averaging the degree levels in the cumulative count gives \[ \frac{B_{\boldsymbol p}(K)}{B_m(K)}\ge e^{-2r\rho}. \tag{4} \] This proof permits double-pivot edges; it is not restricted to the coarser single-pivot majorant.

Proof: both denominator estimates have the same relevant loss. Both options in (1) are at least \(1+\eta\): for the first, \((1-\eta)^{-1}\ge1+\eta\) when defined; the second is immediate. Since \(x=2^{\varepsilon+1}\rho^a=2\beta^\varepsilon<2\), \[ \log C(K)\ge n\log(1+x)\ge nx/3 \ge c_{\varepsilon,\Delta}\,m\rho^a, \qquad c_{\varepsilon,\Delta}=\frac{2^{\varepsilon+2}}{3\Delta}. \tag{5} \] Combine (4) and (5). The logarithm of the untruncated second option in (2) is at least \(c m\rho^a-2r\rho\), where \(c=c_{\varepsilon,\Delta}\). Over all real \(\rho\ge0\), the maximum of \(2r\rho-cm\rho^a\) occurs at \[ \rho_*=(2r/(acm))^{1/(a-1)} \] and equals \[ \frac{a-1}{a}\,2r\left(\frac{2r}{acm}\right)^{1/(a-1)}. \] This has the form in (3). Allowing all real cutoffs only weakens the lower bound, so integer and original-degree restrictions remain covered. Taking the minimum with one preserves the lower bound because the displayed exponential is at most one. If \(r=o(m^{1/a})\), its exponent tends to zero. For two or more such certified bounds their sum is at least \(2-o(1)>1\), proving the conclusion. \(\square\)
