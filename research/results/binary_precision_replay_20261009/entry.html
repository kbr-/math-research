Finite precision and corrected proof operations

Let \(s\ge1\) be an integer, \(R_s=\mathbb Z/2^s\mathbb Z\), \(q=\lceil\log_2s\rceil\), \(K=3^q\), and define the integer polynomial \(\eta_s=N^{\circ q}\), where \(N(z)=3z^2-2z^3\); \(q=0\) means the identity. The standard idempotent correction obeys \[ N(z)^2-N(z)=(4z^2-4z-3)(z^2-z)^2. \tag{1} \] For every integer \(a\), \(a^2-a\) is even, so iteration makes the defect divisible by \(2^{2^q}\). Each iteration preserves parity. The only idempotents of \(R_s\) are zero and one: one of consecutive integers \(e,e-1\) is odd, so if their product is divisible by \(2^s\), the even factor is divisible by \(2^s\). Therefore \[ \eta_s(a)\equiv a\bmod2\quad\text{as the representative }0\text{ or }1\text{ modulo }2^s,\qquad \deg\eta_s=K. \tag{2} \] This is a finite-precision function construction, not an additive embedding of \(\mathbb F_2\).

A sharper Boolean lift. Before paying the Newton degree, use the special Boolean input structure. Write a binary polynomial \(f\) as the sum of its distinct ordinary monomials \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), and define \[ \mathcal B_s(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(s,r)}(-2)^{j-1}e_j(m_1,\ldots,m_r). \tag{2a} \] The empty sum is zero. On a Boolean point each monomial is zero or one, and the full integer expression \((1-\prod_i(1-2m_i))/2\) is their parity. This quotient is expanded over the integers before reducing; no division by two in \(R_s\) is used. Terms with \(j>s\) are divisible by \(2^s\). Thus \(\mathcal B_s(f)\) is the same bit modulo \(2^s\), with degree at most \(s\deg f\), improving \(K\deg f\). This is the elementary parity expansion, not a new interpolation theorem. Its precision dependence is sharp for full-cube semantic lifting: for \(r\) disjoint squarefree monomials of degree \(\delta\ge1\), the multilinear remainder has exact degree \(\delta\min(s,r)\), since its top coefficient \((-2)^{\min(s,r)-1}\) is nonzero modulo \(2^s\). Uniqueness of Boolean interpolation prevents a lower-degree polynomial with those same values. This is not a lower bound for a relative charged-quotient normalizer.

Boolean certificate convention. If an integer polynomial \(F(y)\) of degree \(C\) is zero modulo \(2^s\) at every Boolean point, then \[ F=\sum_i A_i(y)(y_i^2-y_i)+2^sH(y), \quad \deg(A_i(y_i^2-y_i)),\deg H\le C. \tag{3} \] Divide successively by the monic Boolean equations; this does not increase degree and leaves a multilinear remainder. Each remainder coefficient is an integer alternating sum of its Boolean evaluations, hence divisible by \(2^s\). This proves (3) over integers and gives an ordinary Boolean certificate over \(R_s\). It uses the full Boolean cube only for a domain identity, not as a solution set of the unsatisfiable old base.

Marker denominators after grouping permutations

Lemma (marker valuation). At one stage of the recorded marker expansion, let there be \(c\ge1\) markers and \(N\ge0\) indexed narrow clauses. Group permutations by the exact clause subset \(A\) appearing before the first marker, with \(|A|=j\). After including the path factor \(1/c\), its coefficient is \[ \lambda_{N,c,j}=\frac{j!(N+c-j-1)!}{(N+c)!} =\frac1{m\binom{m-1}{j}},\qquad m=N+c. \tag{4} \] If the original CNF has at most \(M\ge1\) indexed clauses, then \(m\le M\), because the covered violated clauses and the remaining narrow-clause family use disjoint original indices, and \[ -v_2(\lambda_{N,c,j})\le\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor. \] A path of length \(\ell\) therefore has even-denominator exponent at most \(\ell\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor\), and summing terms cannot worsen the maximum of these exponents. This is an upper bound for this specified representation, not a lower bound for optimal aggregation.

Proof. Exactly \(c\,j!(m-j-1)!\) permutations have the specified subset before the first marker: order that subset, choose the first marker, then order everything else. Divide by \(m!\), and cancel the path's \(1/c\). For the valuation bound, expand the elementary rational integral \[ \frac1{m\binom{m-1}{j}} =\int_0^1 t^j(1-t)^{m-j-1}\,dt =\sum_{u=0}^{m-j-1}\frac{(-1)^u\binom{m-j-1}{u}}{j+u+1}. \] The factorial value follows by repeated integration by parts; the sum follows by the binomial theorem. Every denominator in the final sum is between one and \(m\). Multiplication by \(2^{\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor}\) consequently puts every summand in \(\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), proving the bound. This use of an integral is only a finite rational identity. Along a path the coefficients multiply, so valuations add; a finite sum of rational numbers with this denominator bound stays in the same localized lattice. \(\square\)

The first overlapping-clause identity. With \(H=(x_1\lor x_2)\land(x_2\lor x_3)\), target width one, and all variables live, each of its two paths has one remaining narrow clause and one marker. Put \(d_{12}=(1-x_1)(1-x_2)\), \(d_{23}=(1-x_2)(1-x_3)\). The actual expansion is \[ H=1-\tfrac12d_{12}(1+x_3)-\tfrac12d_{23}(1+x_1). \] Grouping first gives the integer polynomial \(H_{\mathbb Z}=x_2+x_1x_3-x_1x_2x_3\). If \(\widetilde H=x_2+x_1x_3+x_1x_2x_3\) is the binary coefficient lift and \(\mathcal N\) the twice-cleared right side, then \[ \mathcal N=2H_{\mathbb Z},\qquad \mathcal N-2\widetilde H=-4x_1x_2x_3. \] Thus precision modulo four recovers the binary target after grouping and dividing the even aggregate. Reducing \(\mathcal N\) modulo two first loses it. The terms here are genuine width-one tests; their sum, not a termwise inverse of two, is being used. Substituting correlated nonlinear features preserves a polynomial identity but does not preserve the independent restriction law automatically.

The available uniform precision still misses the power budget

Write \(b_{\rm cl}\) for the target CNF width, distinct from the earlier weight degree. A path adds at least \(b_{\rm cl}+1\) live coordinates per step. With \(N_{\rm live}\) coordinates, (4) therefore permits the safe precision \[ s=1+\left\lfloor\frac{N_{\rm live}}{b_{\rm cl}+1}\right\rfloor \lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor. \tag{5} \] This cancels factorial denominators before costing precision; keeping separate permutations would be needlessly worse. For the growing-degree law, in the favorable width regime \(\kappa=\Theta(\Delta)\), \(b_{\rm cl}=\Theta(p\Delta)\), \(N_{\rm live}=\Theta(np\Delta)\), and \(\log M=\Theta(n^\alpha)\), the allowance (5) is of order \(n^{1+\alpha}\). Plugging that certified worst-case choice into even the sharper linear factor \(s\) and \(B=O_{A,d}(n^{\alpha d})\) does not establish a replay ceiling below \(D'=\Theta(n^{1+\alpha-\beta})\), even with \(T=1\) and no weight charge. The power comparison would require \[ \alpha d+(1+\alpha)<1+\alpha-\beta, \] which fails for positive \(\alpha,\beta\) and \(d\ge1\).

This is failure of the available uniform sufficient accounting, not proof that the actual aggregate needs that precision. Expanding and grouping a Boolean function completely gives integral coefficients, but can cost full polynomial degree. The missing implication is an affordable joint degree/valuation control for the actual aggregate source map. The preceding real-mass theorem supplies neither it nor a source-rank estimate. Longer-path numerical examples or higher precision checks would not decide that implication.
