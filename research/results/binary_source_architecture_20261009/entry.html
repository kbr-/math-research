The local coefficient law already exists

The 21 September coefficient-law record was read in full, with no later backlinks found. For a nonzero binary tuple \(g\), choose extension-field coefficients \(a_i\) linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_2\), put \(L=\sum_i a_i g_i\), and use the Boolean product functional \(\nu(r^S)=a_S\). Weighting by \(\ell_g(r)=\sum_i g_i r_i\) and dividing by \(L\) gives a normalized row law supported on \(\ell_g=1\), with \[ \mu_g(r^S)=a_S\frac{L+\sum_{i\in S}(1+a_i)g_i}{L}. \] At \(g=0\), use the zero row; the empty moment remains one. This is a field-valued law, not a positive distribution.

For \(r\) independent input coordinates, its recorded polynomial realization uses the product of the \(r-1\) nontrivial Frobenius conjugates of \(L\). On nonzero inputs that product is \(L^{-1}\); its product with \(L\) is the nonzero-input selector. The complete nonempty moments have the recorded input-degree ceiling \(r\), hence \(r\delta\) after substitution. Mixed local laws add their degree costs. This gives no cheap action on an arbitrary old truncated functional. The proposed direct extension must improve this paid interface or use a genuinely different construction.
