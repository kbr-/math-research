Coupled Boolean packing

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\) with old Boolean variables \(x\). Let \(s\ge1\), and let the actual input tuple be \[ (A,B_1,\ldots,B_s,C),\qquad C=\sum_{j=1}^s E_jF_j \] as Boolean functions, where all displayed objects are binary old polynomials. The equality may be literal or modulo old Booleanity; no fresh auxiliary input is introduced. Suppose \(\deg E_j,\deg F_j\le T\), where \(T\ge1\). Define coefficient-row values \[ q_j=E_jA+B_j\quad(1\le j\le s),\qquad q_0=\left(1+\sum_jE_j\right)A+\sum_j(1+F_j)B_j+C. \] All coefficients in these rows have old degree at most \(T\).

Lemma (coupled Boolean packing). The \(s+1\) rows normalize the complete accuracy-\((s+1)\) block on the actual \(s+2\) inputs: with \(P=\prod_{j=0}^s(1-q_j)\), every input times \(P\) vanishes on the old Boolean cube, and all coefficient-field images do as well. Each original axiom of degree \(e\) has a Boolean NS certificate for its image through \(Te\). Thus these old-only maps, simultaneously for any family of qualifying blocks, transfer a degree-\(B\) ordinary NS or PC proof of an old target to degree at most \(TB\). This does not make an unprovided factorization available.

Proof. The row values satisfy, modulo the supplied Boolean-function equality for \(C\), \[ q_0+\sum_j(1+F_j)q_j =A+C+A\sum_jE_jF_j=A+C+AC. \] For binary values, \(A+C+AC\) is zero exactly when \(A=C=0\). If every \(q_j\) is zero, the identity first gives \(A=C=0\), then \(q_j=B_j\) gives every \(B_j=0\). Conversely, if every actual input is zero, every row value is zero. Therefore \(P\) is precisely the common-zero indicator of the actual tuple, proving all companions vanish. Every coefficient image is a binary old polynomial, so its field image vanishes too.

For completeness, a Boolean polynomial vanishing at every cube point has zero multilinear remainder: the squarefree monomials are an evaluation basis, by induction on the number of variables. Reducing powers by \(x_i^2-x_i\) does not raise ordinary degree and produces an ordinary NS certificate through the original polynomial's degree. Under the displayed degree-\(T\) substitution, an original axiom of degree \(e\) has image degree at most \(Te\); hence its vanishing gives precisely the required certificate, including coefficient fields. For an NS cofactor through \(B-e\), its image adds at most \(T(B-e)\). For PC, replay each line by the same global substitution: a primitive variable multiplication becomes multiplication by its degree-at-most-\(T\) image and fits \(TB\). Old variables are fixed. The maps use no other source coefficients, so families of blocks can be treated at once with no inventory factor; higher accuracies may pad zero rows, retaining their original degrees. \(\square\)

The elementary identity is stronger than a zero-set count: it specifies legal maps in the original input positions. On one row's zero set, the others have a useful relation; no row is required to encode a preassigned bin of input factors.

Balanced triple optimum

Let \(\delta\ge1\) be an integer. Take any binary old inputs \(A,B\) of degree at most \(\delta\), and let \(C\) be a product of \(\delta\) binary affine forms. Split that product into \(C=EF\), with \(\deg E\le\lfloor\delta/2\rfloor\) and \(\deg F\le\lceil\delta/2\rceil\); an empty product is one. Put \(T=\lceil\delta/2\rceil\).

Corollary (balanced triple optimum). Two rows of coefficient degree at most \(T\) normalize \((A,B,C)\), using \[ q_0=(1+E)A+(1+F)B+C,\qquad q_1=EA+B. \] Their product \((1-q_0)(1-q_1)\) equals \((1-A)(1-B)(1-C)\) on the Boolean cube. The specialized product has ordinary degree at most \(3\delta\), companions at most \(4\delta\), and every original generator retains the preceding per-axiom \(Te\) certificate bound. For three monomials of degree exactly \(\delta\) on disjoint old variable sets, the minimum possible coefficient degree for any two-row unweighted Boolean normalizer is exactly \(\lceil\delta/2\rceil\).

Proof. Apply the lemma with \(s=1\). Its triangular identity is simply \[q_0+(1+F)q_1=A+C+AC.\] The first row has degree at most \(\delta+\lceil\delta/2\rceil\), the second at most \(\delta+\lfloor\delta/2\rfloor\); their product consequently has degree at most \(3\delta\). Multiplication by any input adds at most \(\delta\). The per-axiom bound is still the substitution bound, even if an input's actual degree is smaller than this displayed upper bound.

For the lower bound, take three disjoint degree-\(\delta\) monomials. Any successful normalizer product is one when all inputs are zero, because each row is then zero, and is zero whenever an input is one, by its companion. Its unique multilinear representative is therefore \((1-A)(1-B)(1-C)\), whose degree is \(3\delta\): the product \(ABC\) has a unique nonzero top monomial. If all coefficient images have degree at most \(T'\), the ordinary product degree is at most \(2(\delta+T')\). Boolean reduction cannot increase degree, so \(3\delta\le2(\delta+T')\) and \(T'\ge\lceil\delta/2\rceil\). Independent spectator variables cannot help: fix them to constants first. This lower bound is over the pure Boolean domain, with weight one; it is not asserted in an arbitrary hard quotient or after additional weighting. \(\square\)

Quadratic row optimum

Corollary (quadratic row optimum). For \(r\ge1\) inputs, each a product of two binary affine forms, \(\lceil2r/3\rceil\) affine coefficient rows give a complete unweighted normalizer. The coefficient map preserves ordinary NS/PC proof degree, including every companion and fresh coefficient field. For \(r\) quadratic monomials on pairwise disjoint pairs of independent bits, this row count is minimal among all affine coefficient normalizers over the pure Boolean domain.

Proof. Partition the input positions into triples and at most two leftovers. Each triple uses the preceding construction at \(\delta=2,T=1\); each leftover uses one selecting constant row. The total is \(2\lfloor r/3\rfloor+(r\bmod3)=\lceil2r/3\rceil\). A nonzero input lies in a group whose row product is zero, so multiplying all group products kills every original companion. All coefficients remain affine in old variables, so the lemma's degree-one substitution and Boolean certificates preserve every original degree. Zero-padding covers larger source accuracies.

For independent quadratic monomials, the forced zero indicator \(\prod_i(1-g_i)\) has multilinear degree \(2r\). With \(h\) affine rows each factor has ordinary degree at most three, so \(2r\le3h\). This proves the matching lower bound. Dependencies among the affine factors are allowed for the upper bound but can make the lower bound false, so independence is used only in the optimality control. \(\square\)

Why a pair-witness requirement was too strong

For three disjoint quadratic monomials \(g_1,g_2,g_3\), no product of two distinct inputs is an affine-coefficient combination of the three modulo Booleanity: its multilinear degree is four, whereas such a combination has degree at most three. More generally, if \(u,v\) are independent constant combinations of those three inputs, the cross-product coefficients in \(uv\) are the two-by-two minors of their coefficient vectors. Some minor is nonzero; its disjoint degree-four monomial cannot cancel. Thus the product witness proposed in the preceding cycle is unavailable for pairing independent constant row values at weight one.

Nevertheless the coupled construction normalizes all three inputs using two affine rows. It does not contradict the ordinary prescribed-product packing theorem: that theorem fixes literal products involving independent child coefficient variables and uses ordinary factorization, while the current equality is modulo Booleanity and the rows are coupled. Nor does it contradict the mixed-degree obstruction with an extra independent affine input, whose zero indicator has degree seven and cannot fit two degree-three rows. The previously recorded odd-prime column-state triple has a similar row count but different input/domain hypotheses; its frontier does not imply this binary monomial construction.

The broader arbitrary quadratic source theorem already gives a complete old-only exclusion with much larger coefficient maps. Its explicit source corollary also covers complete two-level systems with rank-at-most-two old-affine bottoms and arbitrary parent sums, affine offsets and overlaps. The present optimum is a sharper local degree-preserving normalization, not a new unrestricted quadratic lower bound. Its value here is to refute the necessity of independent pairing; the existing sufficient packing criterion never asserted universal optimality and remains valid.

Actual complete source and the remaining obstacle

The maps can be applied coherently in a charged old quotient, since their image certificates use Booleanity alone. To make the two-layer realization explicit, take three degree-\(\delta\) products of selected old indicators, create each as an accuracy-\(\delta\) bottom with literal inputs \(1-x\), and select those literals once each. The bottom products are shared globally and become \(A,B,C\). A complete accuracy-two parent reading them then takes the two rows above. The global old map degree is \(T=\lceil\delta/2\rceil\), with no new field or auxiliary input.

Original bottom product/companion degrees are \(2\delta\) and \(2\delta+1\); original parent product/companion degrees are \(4\delta+2\) and \(6\delta+2\). They are not replaced by the smaller specialized degrees. With the proper binary charged pseudoideal, original proof window \(B\ge\max(6\delta+2,\Delta)\), and \(TB\le D\), every axiom image and inference fits the degree-\(D\) old space. Weight one survives, so such a source has no refutation through \(B\). Multiple qualifying parents may share the same fixed bottoms. This is a direct scoped application of the complete map, not a hypothesis that every compiled parent has this structure.

For quadratic product inputs the coefficient map is affine and the original proof ceiling is unchanged. For growing-degree products the balanced map still pays \(\lceil\delta/2\rceil\) globally. An arbitrary affine mixture of bottom products need not itself admit a cheap balanced factorization, and parent accuracy need not be comparable to its input rank. Those remain the missing coverage and budget issues; a constant-factor packing gain alone does not establish a superpolynomial bound.

Falsifying test, verification and route decision

The single SAT test used three disjoint quadratic monomials in six bits and two affine coefficient rows: 42 coefficient bits, 37 nonzero-input assignments, 448 CNF variables and 1,661 clauses. The native solver returned a witness, and a separate direct evaluation checked every CNF clause and all 64 old assignments. The search was then stopped. Its raw witness was simplified to the triangular identity above; no larger board or degree was needed.

The extracted rule was independently checked in GAP on all 16 Boolean assignments for one product term and all 128 assignments for two product terms, exercising the cross terms of the sum-of-products construction. All 144 cases passed the identity, zero-indicator and companion checks; omitting the \(\sum E_j\) coefficient term failed in ten cases. The solver instance, raw model, all pointwise values, scripts and full outputs are retained under the cycle evidence. Forward-global warnings in GAP were nonfatal; the final success marker was reached. The proofs above, not these finite counts, establish the general claims and optimality.

Architecture test. Independent pair witnesses are not necessary for useful affine normalization. A coupled row system bypasses their absence on independent quadratics, and the exact source realization preserves coherent bottom maps and all original costs. This is a better local construction than the proposed pair-by-pair interface, but its factorization premise and row count have not been shown on arbitrary nonlinear mixtures. The goal remains unchanged.
