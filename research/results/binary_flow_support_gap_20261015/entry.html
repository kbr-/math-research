Flow support gap

Statement. Let a connected graph have \(n\) vertices and \(m\) edges, write \(r=m-n+1\), and fix \(c\in\mathbb F_3^n\) and \(0\ne\lambda\in\mathbb F_3\). Let \(\mathcal S\) be any collection of distinct vertex sets satisfying \(\lambda|S|=\sum_vc_v\) in \(\mathbb F_3\). Its corrected-flow fibres

\[A_S=\{y\in\mathbb F_3^E:\operatorname{div}y=c-\lambda\mathbf1_S\}\]

are nonempty, disjoint affine torsors of dimension \(r\). On each fibre give a density \(a_S\) which is a linear combination over \(\mathbb F_4\) of restrictions of edge characters \(\chi_\alpha(y)=\omega^{\alpha\cdot y}\), each with \(|\operatorname{supp}\alpha|\le b\). Its coefficients may depend arbitrarily on \(S\). Assume these densities are not all zero. For nonnegative integers \(b,K\), put

\[B_m(j)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(j,m)}\binom mi2^i,\qquad L=\#\{(S,y):a_S(y)\ne0\}.\]

For every positive integer \(H\le B_m(K)\),

\[\frac LH\ge\frac{3^{m-n+1}}{B_m(b)B_m(K)}\ge\frac{3^{m-n+1}}{B_{2m}(b+K)}.\]

In particular,

\[m\ge4n,\quad b+K\le m/8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L/H\ge2^{m/4}.\]

This statement needs no old-annihilation, positivity, normalization or expansion hypothesis. Its application below supplies the proper charged quotient and faithful source matrix separately. It does not assert that every useful dual functional has a bounded-mode flow realization.

Fourier proof. Choose a fibre with nonzero density and identify it with \(\mathbb F_3^r\) by an origin and a cycle-space basis. Restricting an edge character gives a nonzero scalar times a character of this group; collisions only reduce the number of frequencies. Thus the density has at most \(B_m(b)\) nonzero Fourier coefficients. This counts edge support, not ordinary polynomial degree in arbitrarily chosen flow coordinates. The Fourier matrix over \(\mathbb F_4\) is invertible because the group order is odd and all its characters take values there.

For completeness, convolution by a nonzero function has rank equal to its Fourier support size \(s\). Choose \(s\) translates forming a basis of its column space. Their supports cover the whole group: a coordinate zero in every basis translate would be zero in every translate, impossible. If its point support has size \(\ell\), then \(3^r\le s\ell\). This is precisely the translated-ideal argument already written in lem:sparse-mask-survival. Hence this one fibre contributes at least \(3^r/B_m(b)\) points. Disjointness of the fibres gives the first inequality. No enumeration of the exponentially large fibres is needed.

Budget comparison. Counting pairs of subsets of two labelled copies of the edge set gives \(B_m(b)B_m(K)\le B_{2m}(b+K)\). Write \(s=b+K\). If \(s=0\), this last quantity is one. Otherwise, for \(s\le m/8\), take \(t=s/(4m)\le1\). Then

\[B_{2m}(s)\le t^{-s}(1+2t)^{2m}\le(4em/s)^s\le(32e)^{m/8}\lt2^{7m/8}.\]

The middle expression increases with \(s\) on this range, and \(e\lt4\). Also \(m\ge4n\) gives \(r\ge3m/4\), so \(3^r\ge2^{9m/8}\), since \(9>8\). Division proves the stated \(2^{m/4}\) gap, including \(s=0\).
