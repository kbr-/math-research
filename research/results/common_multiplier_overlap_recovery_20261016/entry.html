Exact common-kernel selection

Statement. Let \(S\) be a finite-dimensional space over a field, \(J\le S\) a subspace, \(\rho_a:S\to W_a\) finitely many linear maps, \(\rho=(\rho_a)_a\) the stacked map and \(q:S\to S/J\) the quotient. Then \(\bigcap_a\ker\rho_a\not\subseteq J\) if and only if

\[\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)>\operatorname{rank}\rho.\tag{0}\]

In particular (0) holds whenever \(\operatorname{rank}\rho<\dim S/J\), and so whenever \(\sum_ar_a<\dim S/J\) for upper bounds \(r_a\ge\operatorname{rank}\rho_a\). A basis of \(\ker\rho\) and reduction modulo \(J\) find a vector of \(\ker\rho\setminus J\) when (0) holds.

Proof. \(\ker(\rho,q)=\ker\rho\cap J\). Rank–nullity on \(S\) for both maps gives \(\dim\ker\rho-\dim(\ker\rho\cap J)=\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)-\operatorname{rank}\rho\). The left side is positive exactly when \(\ker\rho\not\subseteq J\). For the sufficient forms, \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)\ge\operatorname{rank}q=\dim S/J\), and \(\operatorname{rank}\rho\le\sum_a\operatorname{rank}\rho_a\le\sum_ar_a\).

Use in the composition. In thm:certified-repair-composition take \(J=S\cap J_D\). Its proof uses the summed bound only to obtain some \(w\in\bigcap_a\ker\rho_a\setminus J_D\); the exact-repair construction, the finite-grid stage, the absorption of earlier errors and replay all start from that chosen \(w\), as its full proof states. So the composition holds verbatim with its hypothesis \(\sum_ar_a<H_0\) replaced by any certificate of (0). This changes the selection hypothesis, not its later costs. A certificate of (0) is itself a cost: an independent bound on each \(\rho_a\) does not bound the stacked rank better than the sum, and for Braun's and Pang's adapters no joint bound is recorded. The curve theorem below supplies one in its own model. It is a linear-algebra refinement; by its registered statement, read in this cycle, lem:weight-compatibility-factorization already uses the predicate “common kernel nonzero in the quotient by the forbidden space” for two families.

Overlapping curve recovery

Setting and statement. Use a smooth projective geometrically integral curve \(X/k\) of genus \(g\) over a finite field, distinct rational received points \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\), a further protected rational point \(R\), and divisors \(A,G\) of nonnegative degrees \(a,m\) supported away from all these points. There are \(L\ge1\) received words

\[y^{(j)}=\operatorname{ev}(f_j)+e^{(j)},\qquad f_j\in L(G).\]

Let \(E_j\) be the exact nonzero support of \(e^{(j)}\), \(E=\bigcup_jE_j\), \(u=|E|\), \(t=\max_j|E_j|\). Assume

\[a\ge u+2g,\qquad n>a+m+t.\tag{1}\]

All ranks below use the same domain \(U=L(A)\). Define \(T_j(\lambda)=(\lambda(P_i)e_i^{(j)})_i\), \(H=\operatorname{ev}L(A+G)\), and the quotient map \(Q:k^n\to k^n/H\). Then

\[\operatorname{rank}T_j=\operatorname{rank}(QT_j)=|E_j|,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(T_1,\ldots,T_L)=\operatorname{rank}(QT_1,\ldots,QT_L)=u.\tag{2}\]

The common kernel is \(L(A-\sum_{i\in E}P_i)\), and evaluation at \(R\) on it is surjective. Thus a common locator \(\lambda(R)=1\) exists. It is found without knowing the supports by solving

\[\lambda\in L(A),\quad N_j\in L(A+G),\quad N_j(P_i)=\lambda(P_i)y_i^{(j)}\quad\forall i,j,\qquad\lambda(R)=1.\tag{3}\]

Every solution satisfies \(N_j=\lambda f_j\) identically and kills every actual error. Separate linear systems with this same locator recover all \(f_j\) and their protected values \(f_j(R)=N_j(R)\). With bases and evaluations, including the \(R\)-evaluation row, supplied, the algorithm uses \(O(Ln^3)\) field operations and \(O(n^2+Ln)\) memory. It may use supplied upper bounds \(u_0\ge u,t_0\ge t\) in (1); the actual supports need not be supplied or guessed. Finiteness of \(k\) is inherited from the dependency's statement and is not used by the new steps; the exact ranks need only \(a\ge u+2g-1\), and a nonzero common locator only \(a\ge u+g\); the full (1) is used for normalization at \(R\).

Proof of the rank identities. Since \(a-u-1\ge2g-1\), Riemann–Roch and the negative-degree dual-section criterion give \(H^1(A-\sum_{i\in E}P_i-R)=0\). The evaluation exact sequence makes

\[L(A)\longrightarrow k^E\oplus k,\qquad \lambda\longmapsto((\lambda(P_i))_{i\in E},\lambda(R))\tag{4}\]

surjective. This is the point-evaluation argument in the full proof of cor:curve-locator-budget, now for the whole finite set. Multiplication by an error factors through evaluation on its support, followed by an injective diagonal map because all its values there are nonzero. This proves the individual ranks. The stacked map factors through evaluation on \(E\), followed by an injective map: each union coordinate has a nonzero error value in at least one component, so an element killed in every component has every coordinate zero. Its rank is therefore \(u\), and its kernel is the stated vanishing space. Surjectivity in (4) supplies the normalized locator. In the language of lem:exact-common-kernel-selection with \(S=U\), \(J=\{\lambda:\lambda(R)=0\}\) and \(\rho=(T_j)_j\), (4) says \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)=u+1>u=\operatorname{rank}\rho\), although \(\dim S/J=1\le u\): the sufficient forms of that lemma fail here and only the exact criterion applies.

A nonzero element of \(H\) has at least \(n-a-m>t\) nonzero coordinates, by the divisor-zero proof in thm:marked-curve-recovery. Thus \(Q\) is injective on vectors supported on any \(E_j\); applying it separately to the components preserves each individual kernel and the common kernel. Rank–nullity proves both observable identities in (2). All comparisons use \(U\), not separate quotients chosen for separate errors.

Proof of recovery and cost. Eliminating \(N_j\), a \(\lambda\in L(A)\) extends to a solution of (3) exactly when \(Q\operatorname{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A\lambda=0\) for every \(j\). Since \(\operatorname{ev}(\lambda f_j)\in H\), \(Q\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{ev}f_j)\operatorname{ev}_A=0\), hence \(Q\operatorname{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A=QT_j\): the observable map of row \(j\) is \(QT_j\). A locator from (4) vanishing on \(E\), together with \(N_j=\lambda f_j\), solves (3), so that system is consistent. For any solution, \(N_j-\lambda f_j\) vanishes at the at least \(n-|E_j|>a+m\) correct positions in row \(j\); the same zero bound makes it identically zero. Since \(\lambda(R)=1\), it is nonzero. Recover each \(f_j\) by the second system of thm:marked-curve-recovery; its uniqueness proof applies verbatim to each row.

For the runtime bound, do not eliminate a single dense matrix with \(L\) separate numerator blocks cubically in its full width. First form \(Q\) once by elimination on the supplied evaluation basis of \(H\). In row \(j\) the observable map \(Q\,\mathrm{diag}(y^{(j)})\operatorname{ev}_A\) has at most \(n\) rows and \(a+1=O(n)\) columns. Form it in \(O(n^3)\) field operations and insert its rows into a maintained echelon system for \(\lambda\), including \(\lambda(R)=1\). Across all rows this costs \(O(Ln^3)\). The consistent affine solution is a locator. For each word solve \(\lambda(P_i)h(P_i)=\lambda(P_i)y_i^{(j)}\) in \(L(G)\), another \(O(n^3)\) operations. Compute \(N_j=\lambda h\), or just its evaluations and protected value, after recovering \(h\). Streaming the constraint rows requires \(O(n^2)\) workspace; retaining the received words uses \(O(Ln)\). Product coordinates, if requested, use the supplied multiplication representation or an additional solve in the injective evaluation basis of \(H\), again cubic per word. No search through supports is used.

One protected quotient

Let \(\mathcal A=k^{\{P_1,\ldots,P_n,R\}}\), with coordinate multiplication, and let \(\operatorname{ev}_{D+R}\) be the evaluation map from functions regular at these points. Its kernel is one fixed ideal. Its restriction to \(U=L(A)\) is injective, because a nonzero element of \(L(A)\) has at most \(a<n\) zeros. This identifies \(U\) with its image in that same algebra for every action in (2). Extend each error by zero at \(R\), and let \(p_R\) be the coordinate idempotent there. The selected multiplier obeys \(\lambda e^{(j)}=0\) for all \(j\) and \(\lambda p_R=p_R\). The \(R\)-coordinate is not discarded in a quotient or changed between rows. The tester quotient \(k^n/H\) is only an observation space, not the protected algebra. This section records the bookkeeping; it adds no constraint beyond (1).

Strict savings at unbounded parameters

Family. On the same Hermitian curves, received points and protected point as cor:hermitian-repair-family, take every \(s=2^r\ge8\) and put

\[n=s^3-1,\quad g=s(s-1)/2,\quad m=s^3/4-1,\quad d=s^3/16-1,\quad a=3d+2g,\quad A=aP_\infty,\ G=mP_\infty.\tag{5}\]

Choose disjoint sets \(I,J,K\) of received points, each of size \(d\), and take two error vectors with nonzero values exactly on \(E_1=I\cup J\) and \(E_2=I\cup K\). Arbitrary nonzero values are permitted. The decoder uses only \(u_0=3d,t_0=2d\). There are enough points since \(3d<n\). The supports and action kernels are distinct and incomparable: surjectivity in (4) gives a multiplier nonzero at a chosen point of \(J\) and zero on \(I\cup K\), and conversely for \(K\). Thus neither individual kernel contains the other.

The ranks are \(2d,2d\) and \(3d\) jointly. This is genuine overlap between different constraints on one domain, not copies of one error map or an invertible change of its output coordinates. It is elementary support geometry: the structural hypothesis, a bound on the union of supports, is in this model the joint-rank bound itself, so the saving over the sum is inclusion–exclusion. It supplies no bound on arbitrary PC error images.

Why one multiplier. Each word alone has a normalized locator at pole order \(2d+2g<a\), cheaper than both budgets below. Separate locators give separate multipliers. The selection step of thm:certified-repair-composition needs one weight \(w\) killing every defect while preserving the protected class, so the comparison below is between certificates for one common multiplier. The curve instance is an instance of lem:exact-common-kernel-selection only and an analogue of that selection step: the composition is stated for polynomial spaces \(S\le k[x]_{\le b}\) and a cutoff space \(J_D\), and no translation carrying \(L(A)\), its forbidden hyperplane \(\lambda(R)=0\) and its observations into that setting is given here.

Budget comparison. Since \(a\ge2g\), Riemann–Roch gives \(\ell(A)=a+1-g=3d+g+1\). Moreover

\[d-g-1=s^3/16-s(s-1)/2-2 =s^2(s-8)/16+s/2-2>0\quad(s\ge8).\tag{6}\]

Thus \(3d<\ell(A)<4d\). For a pole order \(b\), the individual rank of the action of \(e^{(j)}\) on \(L(bP_\infty)\) is \(\ell(bP_\infty)-\ell(bP_\infty-E_j)\), so the summed-rank criterion for a nonzero common multiplier reads \(\ell(bP_\infty)<\ell(bP_\infty-E_1)+\ell(bP_\infty-E_2)\).

  • For \(b\ge2d+2g-1\) both sides are nonspecial: \(\ell(bP_\infty-E_j)=b-2d+1-g\), \(\ell(bP_\infty)=b+1-g\), and the criterion holds exactly when \(b\ge b_{\rm sum}=4d+g\).
  • For \(b<2d\) the right side is zero and the criterion fails.
  • For \(2d\le b\le4d-g-1\), the upper bound \(\ell(D)\le\deg D+1\) for \(\deg D\ge0\) (used in thm:marked-curve-recovery) gives a right side at most \(2b-4d+2\le b+1-g\le\ell(bP_\infty)\), and the criterion fails.

The last two ranges and the first cover every \(b\) when \(4d-g-1\ge2d+2g-2\), that is \(2d\ge3g-1\); here \(2d-3g+1=s^2(s-12)/8+3s/2-1>0\) for \(s\ge16\). So for every \(s\ge16\) and every choice of \(I,J,K\), the exact summed-rank criterion first holds at \(b_{\rm sum}=4d+g\). At \(s=8\) the orders \(96\le b\le116\) are not covered by this argument; there the comparison is with the support-based rank bounds \(2d,2d\), which are exact at every \(b\ge2d+2g-1\) and valid upper bounds below, and the same threshold \(4d+g\) is that criterion's first order.

Two comparisons follow, with the same curve, point at infinity, supports and message divisor.

  • Nonzero against nonzero. The actual common kernel \(L(bP_\infty-E)\) is nonzero once \(b\ge3d+g\), by Riemann–Roch, against \(b_{\rm sum}=4d+g\): a saving of \(d\). At \(s=8\) this is established against the support-based rank bounds only, because of the uncovered orders above.
  • Protected against the summed baseline. The composition needs \(w\) outside the forbidden space, here \(\lambda(R)\ne0\). The summed and joint rank conditions of the composition both ask for a rank below \(\dim S/J=1\) and never hold. The exact criterion (0) holds at \(a=3d+2g\) by (4). Against the summed nonzero threshold, which gives no normalization at all, the saving is \(b_{\rm sum}-a=d-g>0\).

Neither \(3d+g\) nor \(a\) is asserted optimal, and other decoders are not compared. The saving survives detection and message recovery, because

\[n-(a+m+2d)=7s^3/16-s^2+s+5>0.\tag{7}\]

The baseline's larger pole budget also fits the observation window: \(n-(b_{\rm sum}+m+2d)=3s^3/8-s^2/2+s/2+6>0\). Thus the comparison is between two feasible pole windows, not with an impossible baseline. The weighted-message budget drops by the same \(d-g\), from \(b_{\rm sum}+m\) to \(a+m\). Points, bases and evaluation matrices use the explicit construction already proved in cor:hermitian-repair-family, at the new \(O(n)\) pole orders. For two words the complete cost remains \(O(n^3)\) field operations and \(O(n^2)\) memory.

Nonvacuity. The same-quotient protected ledger and full saving and recovery ledger are both realized by (5) for every \(s=2^r\ge8\). At \(s=8\), direct symbolic substitution gives \(n=511,g=28,m=127,d=31,a=149\), ranks \(62,62,93\), domain dimension \(122\), summed-rank threshold \(b_{\rm sum}=152\), and recovery margin \(511-(149+127+62)=173\). At \(s=16\): \(n=4095,g=120,d=255,a=1005,b_{\rm sum}=1140\). One may take both messages equal to one and each error equal to one on its prescribed support; the supports remain distinct. These are exact substitutions, not a numerical run. For lem:exact-common-kernel-selection, the same instance gives \(S=L(A)\) of dimension \(122\), \(\operatorname{rank}\rho=93\), \(\operatorname{rank}(\rho,q)=94\).

Degenerate cases. If every error is zero, the joint rank is zero and a normalized locator exists under (1). With one word there is no rank-sum gain. Disjoint supports give equality between union and summed ranks. Identical supports may have different nonzero values but the same kernel; they are not the claimed nonduplicate family. A zero error value removes that coordinate from its exact support, so it is not counted twice by definition. Repeated evaluation points are excluded. A protected point in the defect support is excluded. In lem:exact-common-kernel-selection, \(J=S\) makes (0) impossible, \(J=0\) reduces it to \(\ker\rho\ne0\), and no maps at all give \(\rho=0\) and the condition \(S\ne J\).

New mathematics. A complete simultaneous marked-recovery consequence with exact joint-action ranks; a pole-budget gain of \(d\) (nonzero) and \(d-g\) (protected) over the summed-rank certificate on distinct incomparable kernels, exact for every support choice when \(s\ge16\); and an exact selection criterion for the composition theorem. The curve instance satisfies that criterion, an instance of the abstract lemma where its summed form cannot certify a protected value; it is not itself an instance of the composition theorem. The support factorization and the decoder are established elementary tools; no historical novelty or improved decoding radius is claimed. Simultaneous decoding of several received words with one error locator for the union of their supports is the setting of interleaved codes; no source on interleaved decoding was read in this cycle, and nothing here relies on one.

Selection gain. lem:exact-common-kernel-selection widens the selection hypothesis of the composition from a summed rank bound to an exact rank criterion, with unchanged later costs. In the explicit family a protected common multiplier is certified at pole budget \(3d+2g\), where the summed criterion first gives even a nonzero multiplier at \(4d+g\). This changes the sufficient common-multiplier cost, not only its representation. Separate per-word locators are cheaper still; the gain concerns one common multiplier.
