One common form for an actual error map

Let \(J\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a proper degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Put \(Q_d=\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le d}/(J\cap\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le d})\), using their natural inclusions. Fix \(s\ge0\), \(e\ge1\), \(L=s+e\), and \[ 2L\le D. \tag{1} \] Let \(0\ne S\subseteq Q_s\) be a binary subspace and let \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\), \(r\ge1\), be the actual fixed binary error polynomials, each of degree at most \(e\). Bounded absorption makes \[ T:S\longrightarrow Q_L^r,\qquad f\longmapsto([E_1f],\ldots,[E_rf]) \tag{2} \] well-defined. The spaces \(S\), \(\ker T\) and the map \(T\) are fixed before choosing \(\mu\); no new coefficient choices are made inside this map.

Lemma (common moment adjoint). There is a finite extension \(\mathbb L/\mathbb F_2\) and a functional \(\mu\) on the old degree-\(D\) space over \(\mathbb L\), annihilating the extended \(J\) and satisfying \(\mu(1)=1\), such that the same pairing \[ B_\mu(f,g)=\mu(fg) \] is nonsingular on \(Q_L\), \(S\), and \(\ker T\), after scalar extension. Use its restriction on \(S\) and its coordinatewise direct sum on \(Q_L^r\). The adjoint \(T^\dagger:Q_L^r\to S\) satisfies \[ T^\dagger(y_1,\ldots,y_r) =\Pi_S\!\left(\sum_i E_i y_i\right), \qquad \operatorname{rank}(TT^\dagger)=\operatorname{rank}T, \tag{3} \] where \(\Pi_S(v)\in S\) is determined by \[ \mu\bigl(f\,\Pi_S(v)\bigr)=\mu(fv)\qquad(f\in S). \tag{4} \] The formula is defined for representatives \(y_i\) through degree \(L\); every tested product fits (1). In particular the adjoint has an old polynomial representative through degree \(s\). If the multiplier sum already represents a class in \(S\), the projection returns that class. A field with \[ |\mathbb L|>1+\dim Q_L+\dim S+\dim\ker T \] suffices; this is a sufficient, not minimal, field-size bound.

Proof: choose the form. The three spaces \(Q_L,S,\ker T\) are scalar binary subspaces of \(Q_L\). Apply the existing finite-family pairing lemma with tuple count one and cutoff \(L\). Its Boolean diagonal/determinant argument gives one normalized \(\mu\) nonsingular on all three, with the stated field bound. A zero kernel needs no determinant. This does not ask one common functional to be nonsingular on every arbitrary tuple subspace: the output is the full direct sum of copies of \(Q_L\), so nonsingularity there follows from nonsingularity on \(Q_L\).

Proof: the multiplier formula. Nonsingularity on \(S\) makes (4) a unique element of \(S\) whenever the right side is a well-defined functional on \(S\). A representative \(v=\sum_iE_i y_i\) has degree at most \(e+L\). Pairing with \(f\in S\) costs at most \(s+e+L=2L\le D\). Changing an input representative by a \(J\)-relation of degree at most \(s\), or an output representative by one through \(L\), gives a product in \(J\) within the same bound, so the expression is independent of representatives. Then \[ \sum_i\mu(E_i f\,y_i)=\mu\!\left(f\sum_iE_i y_i\right) =\mu\bigl(f\,\Pi_S(v)\bigr), \] which is precisely the adjoint identity. The projected state belongs to \(S\subseteq Q_s\), giving its degree bound. If \(v\) already represents an element of \(S\), that element satisfies (4) and uniqueness proves the last assertion. No projection is claimed to be multiplication by a polynomial or a legal coefficient substitution.

Proof: rank. Let \(K=\ker T\) after scalar extension. Adjoint duality gives \(\operatorname{im}T^\dagger=K^\perp\) in \(S\). Nonsingularity on \(K\) gives \(K\cap K^\perp=0\). Therefore restricting \(T\) to \(\operatorname{im}T^\dagger\) is injective, and \[ \operatorname{rank}(TT^\dagger) =\dim K^\perp=\dim S-\dim K=\operatorname{rank}T. \] This is the recorded rank-defect identity with its defect removed by a specifically chosen common form. It does not assert the same equality for \(T^\dagger T\). \(\square\)

Recover the permitted old weight

For the actual weighted source application, let \(w\) be binary of degree at most \(b\), let \(U\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le k}\) be the allowed binary linear subspace encoding any earlier obligations, and take \(S=[wU]\ne0\). Set \(s=b+k\); then the explicit reserve is \[ 2(b+k+e)\le D. \tag{5} \] A linear section of \(q\mapsto[wq]\) on \(U\), extended by scalars, recovers every nonzero adjoint output as \(wq\notin J\), with \(q\in\mathbb L\otimes U\) and \(\deg q\le k\). It preserves membership in the originally allowed domain; it does not silently replace that domain by all polynomials. This is a vector-space section with no claim of a small circuit representation.

The extension field belongs to the analysis forms and adjoint, not to the source coefficient domain. Original binary maps, kernels and degree spaces extend without changing rank. If a later argument actually produces a nonzero binary-system kernel vector over \(\mathbb L\), coefficient components recover a binary kernel vector without increasing degree. The present adjoint output is not automatically such a kernel vector: it lies in the orthogonal complement of the error kernel. Nor does \(\mu(1)=1\) make the recovered state a normalized augmented-source functional.

All original proof-replay costs remain separate. For a fixed complete-source substitution of degree at most \(t\) and original source ceiling \(B\), still require \(b+k+tB\le D\). For actual leaf/copy/MP errors use the recorded \(\deg w+k+2L_{\rm value}\le D\) ledger. Formula (5) is an additional pairing reserve, not a replacement for either one.
