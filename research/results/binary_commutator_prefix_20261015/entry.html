The actual sequent block

Use the exact binary sequent presentation and zero-respecting compiler of the shallow simulation. Keep the recorded corrected odd-parity falsity convention \(t(\bigoplus_i A_i)=1+r+\sum_i t(A_i)\), so the empty parity \(Q=\bigoplus()\) has falsity one. No unary parity or double-negation simplification is made into an inference rule.

Let \(U,V\) be Boolean formulas with falsities \(X,Y\). Put \(A=\bigvee(U,V)\), \(B=\bigvee(V,U)\), \(T=\bigoplus(B)\). First derive \(A\to B\) and \(B\to A\): from each identity \(U\to U,V\to V\), weaken by the other literal, add empty OR on the right and perform the two tail OR-right steps to obtain each target disjunction. The empty-OR axiom, weakened by that target, supplies the empty tail. Two OR-left steps give the source disjunction. Thus no commutation identity is used as an unproved logical rule.

From the axiom \(\to\neg Q\), weakening gives \(B,A\to\neg Q,Q\); from \(A\to B\), weakening gives \(A\to B,Q,Q\). Unary parity-right gives \(A\to T,Q\). From \(B\to A\), weakening gives \(B,\neg Q\to A,Q\); from \(Q\to Q\), weakening gives \(Q\to B,A,Q\). Unary parity-left gives \(T\to A,Q\). Move \(T\) in the former sequent to the left by negation, obtaining \(A,\neg T\to Q\). Together with \(T\to A,Q\), parity-left gives \(\bigoplus(A,B)\to Q\). Negation and OR-right grouping give \[ \to Z,\qquad Z=\bigvee\bigl(\neg\bigoplus(A,B),Q\bigr),\qquad t(Z)=XY+YX. \] Weaken by a second \(Z\), then group the two by the actual tail OR-right rules, giving \(\to W\), \(W=\bigvee(Z,Z)\), with \(t(W)=(XY+YX)^2\). All empty tails are retained syntactically. Every node is a logical consequence without input assumptions.

Complete prefix flags

For one such block, let \(L_1(X,Y),\ldots,L_b(X,Y)\) be its ordered falsity polynomials, including all axioms and the final root, and define \[ E(X,Y)=\prod_{i=1}^{b}(1-L_i(X,Y)). \] For \(X,Y\in M_2(\mathbb F_2)\), \(E(X,Y)^2\) is \(I\) exactly when \(X,Y\) have a common invariant binary line; otherwise \(E(X,Y)=0\). A single prefix need not be central. The three checked ordering/grouping variants have the same asserted squared flag, without an assertion that their formal prefixes are identical.

Proof. On a common invariant line choose a simultaneous upper triangular binary basis. Each diagonal of every \(L_i\) is the falsity of a valid logical sequent at a Boolean assignment of \(U,V\), hence is zero. Each \(1-L_i\) is upper triangular with diagonal ones. Their product has the form \(I+cE_{12}\), whose square is \(I\) in characteristic two.

Suppose no common binary line exists. If \(X^2+X=I\) or \(Y^2+Y=I\), its identity-axiom line has value \(I\), so a factor of the complete prefix is zero. Otherwise both characteristic polynomials split over \(\mathbb F_2\). A scalar \(X\) would share an eigenline of \(Y\), so \(X\) is nonscalar. In a binary basis either \(X=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\), or \(X=\lambda I+E_{12}\) with \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_2\). Write \(Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&e\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). In the diagonal case, \[ [X,Y]=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\c&0\end{pmatrix}. \] If \(bc=0\), one coordinate line is common; thus \(bc=1\) and \([X,Y]^2=I\). In the Jordan case, \[ [X,Y]=\begin{pmatrix}c&e+a\\0&c\end{pmatrix}. \] If \(c=0\), the first coordinate line is common; otherwise its square is \(I\). In either remaining case the block's final root has value \(I\), killing a prefix factor. These cases exhaust binary two-by-two matrices, since the only irreducible quadratic is \(z^2+z+1\). This proves the flag assertion.

This also explains why the root alone is insufficient. With \(X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(Y=0\), the commutator square is zero but the \(U\)-identity line is \(I\). For the original three-clause control \(x\vee y,\neg x,\neg y\), the input mask is \[ P=(I+(I+X)(I+Y))(I+X)(I+Y)=X(I+X)=I. \] The root-only mask \((I+[X,Y]^2)P^2\) is \(I\), whereas the complete prefix times \(P^2\) is zero. The nilpotent pair from the earlier entry is separately covered by its square-commutator root. Singular commutators and commuting irreducible pairs are therefore included in the argument.
