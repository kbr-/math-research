Working mathematical context

Target and conventions

Old variables \(X_{ij}\) are Boolean even when coefficients lie in \(\mathbb F_p\). Ordinary PHP has pigeon clauses \(\bigvee_j X_{ij}\) and hole clauses \(\neg X_{ij}\lor\neg X_{i\prime j}\); functionality additionally forbids two occupied cells in a row. The first target is: for every fixed odd prime \(p\) and real \(K>0\), sufficiently large \(N\) require more than \(N^K\) proof lines in the audited standard DAG calculus. Do not assume the stronger binary semantic rule convention transfers. Extension fields and growing primes are outside the initial target.

Available building blocks

  • All-prime affine exclusion: polynomially many complete one-level ENS blocks, old-affine inputs, arbitrary positive accuracies and coefficient equations \(r^p-r\), with polylogarithmic ordinary PC/NS degree. The graph normalization uses \(T=(p-1)301k\), \(k=\lceil\sqrt{N\ln(16(M+1))}\rceil\), and ceiling \(TD+k=o(N)\). This is a working algebraic theorem, not an odd-prime compiler.
  • Graph-separated matching space, residual graph PC hardness, and signed coefficient detectors: a sixteen-neighbor graph supports a large degree-\(k\) space separated from graph-PC consequences through \(N/10^6\). The detector argument works over every field and does not assume graph-PC equals NS.
  • Affine row-input replacement: eliminate distinguished coordinates inside affine inputs using linear row equations, retaining the original companion degree accounting.
  • Binary generic CNF transfer and finite affine DAG registry: the verified binary model has \(3S+t\) slots and degree \(\max(2h+w,4h+1)\). They are templates to inspect, not established odd-prime results.
  • Same-graph unary composition and quantitative binary refinement: restrict the actual proof before compiling constant-width graph initials; never change graphs afterward. Seek superpolynomial odd-prime bounds before a quantitative refinement.
