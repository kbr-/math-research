General statement. lem:rank-retention-sampling gives a checked Las Vegas choice rule for a finite-field matrix pencil. thm:finite-grid-repair extends the weighted adaptive mechanism from Boolean variables over a prime field to product grids over every finite field, with the appropriate rank profile and original-style degree reserve. thm:certified-repair-composition combines independently bounded exact observations with this adaptive provider while preserving previous repairs at the final cutoff. The core abstraction. A common multiplier repairs a finite algebraic process on a surviving cyclic part of its target. Every approximate relation has a defect; multiplication by the same weight kills those defects while commuting with the permitted paid operations. Survival prevents the repair from being the zero map. The power comes from selecting that one weight by a quantitative dimension certificate, rather than multiplying a separate locator for every defect. Braun obtains the certificate from ordinary restriction geometry and then chooses a weight-dependent substitution; Pang first reduces the defect ranks by adaptive approximation and then selects the weight. The common algebraic structure does not erase this difference in how the certificates are obtained.

Rank retention sampling

Statement. Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) any prime power, and let \(M_1,\ldots,M_s\) be matrices over \(k\). Suppose their generic pencil has rank \(R\), and that \(R\) is known or structurally certified. A uniform independent \(a\in k^s\) satisfies

\[\Pr\!\left[\operatorname{rank}\sum_i a_iM_i\ge R-\lfloor R/q\rfloor\right]\ge\frac1{R+1}.\tag{1}\]

Repeated independent draws, accepting only after an exact rank test, therefore terminate almost surely in at most \(R+1\) expected draws. At \(R=0\), choose the zero vector immediately. This is a bound in matrix-rank tests, not a promise that the input matrices or generic rank are cheaply available.

Proof of the rank expectation. Pang's rank-specialization proof works over every finite field. To make the extension explicit, take a nonzero maximal minor \(H\) of the generic pencil, of total degree at most \(R\). At a point \(a\), constant row operations make at least \(R-\operatorname{rank}M(a)\) rows of that minor vanish, so its multiplicity at \(a\) is at least that deficiency. For a nonzero polynomial of degree \(d\) on \(k^s\), the sum of point multiplicities is at most \(dq^{s-1}\). Here is the induction: write \(H(y,z)=\sum_{j=0}^tH_j(y)z^j\), \(H_t\ne0\); at \(y=b\), a lowest-degree nonzero coefficient of \(H_t(b+y)\), of degree \(\mu\), is the leading coefficient of a degree-\(t\) polynomial in \(z\). Thus each full multiplicity is at most \(\mu\) plus that univariate multiplicity. Summing over \(z\in k\) gives at most \(q\mu+t\); induction on \(H_t\), and \(\deg H_t+t\le d\), finish. The univariate case is the root-multiplicity bound. Averaging the determinant deficiencies gives \(\mathbb E\operatorname{rank}M(a)\ge(1-1/q)R\).

Integer acceptance bound. Write \(R=qv+r\), \(0\le r<q\), and \(b=R-v\). If acceptance has probability \(\theta\), the rank is at most \(b-1\) on failure and at most \(R\) always. Therefore \(\theta\ge(q-r)/(q(v+1))\). This is at least \(1/(R+1)\), since \((q-r)(R+1)-q(v+1)=R(q-r-1)\ge0\). The geometric waiting-time formula proves (1). For \(q=2,R=1,M(a)=a\), acceptance probability is exactly one half, so the bound is attained in a nontrivial case.

Finite-grid repair

Setting. Work over \(k=\mathbb F_q\). Fix nonempty sets \(A_i\subseteq k\), \(a_i=|A_i|\), on variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), and their monic domain polynomials \(F_i(x_i)=\prod_{\alpha\in A_i}(x_i-\alpha)\). Put \(L=\sum_i(a_i-1)\). Let \(J_D\le k[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing all degree-at-most-\(D\) domain consequences. Thus \(u\in J_D,\deg u+\deg v\le D\) implies \(uv\in J_D\). An actual bounded-PC consequence space containing the \(F_i\) is an example. It is not replaced by an unrestricted ideal.

Let \(V_k=k[x]_{\le k}\), and let \(\mathcal N_A(k)\) count exponent vectors \(0\le\alpha_i<a_i\) with \(|\alpha|\le k\). Equivalently it is the coefficient sum through \(k\) of \(\prod_i(1+z+\cdots+z^{a_i-1})\). Fix \(w\notin J_D\) of degree at most \(b\), \(M\ge1\) finite tuples \(g_{\nu,i}\) of degree at most \(d\), and integers \(t\ge1\), \(1\le d\le K\le L\). Assume

\[\Delta=(q-1)dt,\qquad b+K+\Delta+d\le D,\qquad \eta=(1-1/q)\frac{\mathcal N_A(K-d)}{\mathcal N_A(K)},\qquad M(1-\eta)^t<1.\tag{2}\]

Statement. There are \(q_{\nu,j}\in\operatorname{span}_k\{g_{\nu,i}\}_i\), \(P_\nu=\prod_{j=1}^t(1-q_{\nu,j}^{q-1})\), and a grid-reduced polynomial \(f\) of degree at most \(K\), such that

\[wf\notin J_D,\qquad wfP_\nu g_{\nu,i}\in J_D\quad\text{for every }\nu,i.\tag{3}\]

For each tuple, its final actual error rank is at most \(h_w(K)(1-\eta)^t\), where \(h_w(K)=\operatorname{rank}(u\in V_K\mapsto[wu])\). Every previously proved \(wE\in J_D\) persists as \(wfE\in J_D\) when its actual product ceiling \(\deg(wE)+K\le D\) holds. The output is constructed over the original field; no analysis-field extension changes source coefficients.

Grid reduction. Successive division by the monic \(F_i\) expresses every polynomial as its unique reduced representative, with each exponent below \(a_i\), plus domain multiples of no larger total degree. Uniqueness follows by induction from the univariate root bound on the distinct points \(A_i\). In particular a polynomial vanishing on the grid is a domain consequence through its actual degree. For \(g\in k[x]_{\le d}\), \(g^q-g\) vanishes there and is a domain consequence through \(qd\). This replaces Boolean reduction, with its full degree charge retained.

Imported combinatorial input. A product of finite chains has the normalized-matching property: for a subset of a rank level, its upper shadow occupies at least the same fraction of the next level. The full product theorem for normal posets with log-concave rank numbers is read in Madiman–Wang–Woo, arXiv:1712.00913v3, Section 3.2, Proposition 3.1, attributed there to Hsieh–Kleitman and Engel. Each unweighted chain is normal with log-concave rank numbers, so repeated products give the stated input, including singleton factors. This published combinatorial theorem is imported, not reproved here; it concerns integer counts and has no field-characteristic restriction.

Normalized error rank. For a fixed prefactor \(A\) of degree at most \(a\), define \(Z_A(k)=\{u\in V_k:Ag_i u\in J_D\ \forall i\}\) and \(c_A(k)=\dim V_k/Z_A(k)\). In the window \(a+K+d\le D\), these spaces respect filtration intersections and multiplication within degree \(K\). Their standard monomials under a degree-compatible order form a downward-closed subset of the exponent box: domain leading monomials exclude \(x_i^{a_i}\), and bounded absorption makes the excluded set upward closed within the window. A level's excluded monomials have their upper shadow excluded too. Normalized matching therefore makes the fractions of retained monomials nonincreasing by degree. Cumulative weighted averages give

\[c_A(K-d)\ge\frac{\mathcal N_A(K-d)}{\mathcal N_A(K)}c_A(K).\tag{4}\]

This uses the full degree domain, not an arbitrary selected subspace whose rank profile need not be normal.

One adaptive step. Suppose \(a+K+qd\le D\). On \(V_{K-d}\), put \(M_i u=([Ag_\alpha g_i u])_\alpha\). Its stacked kernel equals \(Z_A(K-d)\): one inclusion is absorption; the other follows from \(Ag_i^2u\in J_D\), multiplication by \(g_i^{q-2}\), and subtraction of \(Au(g_i^q-g_i)\). Those operations fit \(a+K+(q-1)d\le D\).

For independent parameters \(\lambda_i\), put \(E=k(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)\). The generic pencil \(M(\lambda)\) has the same kernel as the stacked map after scalar extension. Indeed \(g_\lambda=\sum\lambda_i g_i\) and \(Ag_\alpha g_\lambda u\in J_D^E\) imply \(Ag_\alpha g_\lambda^q u\in J_D^E\); subtracting \(\sum_i\lambda_i^q Ag_\alpha(g_i^q-g_i)u\) gives \(M(\lambda^q)u=0\), within \(a+K+qd\). Scalar extension preserves the degree intersections and bounded absorption: expand a degree-\(j\) consequence in a base-field basis of \(J_D\cap V_j\) before multiplying it. Corresponding minors of \(M(\lambda^q)\) are \(q\)-th powers of those of \(M(\lambda)\), so the inclusion of kernels is equality. The kernel is stable under coordinatewise \(q\)-Frobenius; the unique reduced row-echelon basis must have entries fixed by that map, hence in \(k\). Parameter independence then identifies it with the stacked kernel. This is Pang's descent argument with the field cardinality \(q\), not an assertion that an arbitrary extension-valued polynomial satisfies \(u^q=u\).

Consequently the computable stacked rank \(R=c_A(K-d)\) is the generic rank. By (1), choose an original-field \(q_0\in\operatorname{span}\{g_i\}\) with the rank of \(u\mapsto([Ag_\alpha q_0u])_\alpha\) at least \((1-1/q)c_A(K-d)\). For \(A'=A(1-q_0^{q-1})\), its kernel contains both \(Z_A(K)\) and \(q_0V_{K-d}\): preserve the former by absorption, and use \(q_0-q_0^q\) for the latter. Both cost at most \(a+K+qd\). Counting the gained subspace modulo the old kernel gives

\[c_{A'}(K)\le c_A(K)-(1-1/q)c_A(K-d)\le(1-\eta)c_A(K).\tag{5}\]

Iteration and survival. Start with \(A=w\). The kernel of \(u\mapsto[wu]\) lies in the initial error kernel, so \(c_w(K)\le h_w(K)>0\). At step \(j\le t\), \(a\le b+(j-1)(q-1)d\), and the maximum required degree is \(b+K+j(q-1)d+d\le D\). Thus each final tuple rank is at most \(h_w(K)(1-\eta)^t\). All final maps kill the same forbidden weight kernel. Their joint rank on its quotient is below \(h_w(K)\) by (2), giving a nonzero common kernel class. Choose a representative and reduce it on the grid; the changes to the weighted multiplier and every error product are domain consequences within the same bounds. This proves (3). The earlier-error assertion is direct bounded absorption of the already completed consequence \(wE\); it does not reweight its entire old derivation.

Construction cost. Given explicit dense polynomial coordinates and a basis/reduction map for \(J_D\), all maps above can be formed by bounded polynomial arithmetic and linear algebra. Each round computes its stacked rank once. Exact rank testing of independent coefficient draws finds its retained row in at most \(R+1\le\mathcal N_A(K-d)+1\) expected trials. Conditional on any previous choices this bound still holds, so all tuples and rounds use at most \(Mt(\mathcal N_A(K-d)+1)\) expected sampled rank tests, plus the initial stacked ranks and final kernel computation. Matrix dimensions and coordinate formation are polynomial in the explicit degree-\(D\) dense dimension, tuple lengths and degree parameters. This does not make them polynomial in \(n\), in a circuit description, or in the original proof size. Computing a PC consequence basis is part of the input-preparation cost, not free. Every accepted row and final multiplier is checked exactly, so randomness affects runtime, not correctness.

Certified repair composition

Static certificate. At one fixed final cutoff \(D\), let \(0\le b\le D\) and \(S\le k[x]_{\le b}\) be a supplied finite space, with a certified lower bound \(H_0\le\dim S/(S\cap J_D)\). Let \(\rho_a:S\to W_a\) be explicit linear observations with independently proved rank upper bounds \(r_a\), and \(\sum_a r_a<H_0\). Suppose that any chosen \(w\in\bigcap_a\ker\rho_a\setminus J_D\) admits a specified exact-repair construction: it freezes all earlier coefficient choices and yields actual old errors \(E_j\) with certificates \(wE_j\in J_D\). The observations may be ordinary polynomial restrictions, a faithful coding tester, or another proved provider; their implication to the exact errors must be established, not stipulated from the desired answer.

Statement. Such a \(w\) can be selected by linear algebra. If there is no residual family, take \(W=w\). Otherwise, when \(k=\mathbb F_q\) and the grid hypotheses and (2) hold for the frozen residual tuples, thm:finite-grid-repair constructs \(W=wf\) of degree at most \(b+K\), outside \(J_D\), killing every adaptive residual error. It also kills every earlier \(E_j\) whose certificate has \(\deg(wE_j)+K\le D\). If a fixed polynomial substitution \(\Phi\), possibly chosen after \(w\), has variable degree at most \(T\), a source derivation ceiling \(B\) replays under multiplication by \(W\) whenever all weighted image axioms have their certificates and \(b+K+TB\le D\). With no adaptive stage use \(K=0\). Construction cost is the static linear algebra and exact-repair algorithm, plus the explicitly represented adaptive cost above.

Proof. Rank-nullity gives \(\dim\bigcap_a\ker\rho_a\ge\dim S-\sum_a r_a>\dim(S\cap J_D)\). Therefore the common kernel contains \(w\notin J_D\), found by a kernel basis and reduction modulo \(J_D\), or by a separate injectivity certificate making every nonzero kernel vector safe. Freeze its exact-repair output before forming the residual maps. The preceding theorem supplies \(f\), and bounded absorption preserves the earlier errors at the displayed ceilings. For replay, linear combinations commute with \(F\mapsto W\Phi(F)\). A source variable-multiplication step becomes multiplication by \(\Phi(x)\); expand that polynomial into monomials and use the completed previous image line. Every intermediate product fits the original \(b+K+TB\) ceiling, using the actual degree of the source line before that multiplication. Constant or zero images only lower the degree. This gives the weighted derivation with the same fixed \(W,\Phi\), without assigning separate weights to its lines. The terminal image is \(W\) if the source terminal polynomial is one. This is the costed cyclic-repair principle, not a claim that every approximate interpretation is a fixed substitution.

Source adapters and a new domain

Braun. Use the published binary row-linear space \(S=\mathcal L_k\), its separation from the old \(J_D\) at the final \(D=k(B+1)\), and the ordinary high-flat restriction maps. The matched result cor:ordinary-restriction-moments supplies \(\dim S=\sum_{j\le k}\binom mj\ell^j\) and total restriction rank at most one quarter of \(\binom mk\ell^k\), under its full hypotheses \(m>0,\ell\ge2,M,k\ge1\), at most \(M\) proper blocks of input-span rank greater than \(h(k+1)\), \(h=3\ell\), \(m\log(4M)\le k^2\), \(k\le m\), \(B\ge2h+1\), \(4(k-1)<2^\ell\), \(2k(B+1)-1\le2^\ell\). The source is one complete old-affine level with disjoint fresh coefficient variables, all companions and all Boolean fields. Ordinary vanishing yields literal cofactors of degree at most \(k-1\); the existing low-rank packing, high-unit cleanup and high-proper cofactor construction produce the simultaneous substitution of degree at most \(k\), with weighted image certificates and replay through \(k(B+1)\). This is the static stage with no residual family. Its substitution depends on the selected weight, which is allowed because it is fixed before replay. No finite-point replacement is made.

Pang. Take \(S=\langle1\rangle\), no static observations, \(H_0=1\), \(w=1\), and Boolean coordinate grids \(A_i=\{0,1\}\) over the original prime field \(\mathbb F_p\). Then \(\mathcal N_A(K)=N_n(K)\), \(L=n\), and (2) is precisely \((p-1)dt+K+d\le D\) and \(M(1-\eta)^t<1\), with Pang's original \(\eta\). The one-step and final error statements reproduce Theorem 4.3. Its inference simulation is an approximate-map adapter rather than the fixed-substitution clause: Proposition 5.1 uses the same actual error identities at \(K+\max\{2\Delta+2d,\Delta+w(F)\}\le D\), giving the terminal \(f\in J_D\). That separate ledger is unchanged. The previously checked binary leveled choice order also remains available once earlier levels are frozen; no new all-depth source coverage is asserted.

Finite-grid application. The adaptive statement now applies directly to systems over \(\mathbb F_q\) whose coordinates have specified finite sets \(A_i\), using the product-of-chains profile rather than the Boolean binomial profile. For example, on \(\mathbb F_3^n\) it uses coefficient sums of \((1+z+z^2)^n\), with factors \(1-q_{\nu,j}^2\) and reserve \(b+K+2dt+d\le D\). This is a distinct native finite-domain repair theorem; it is not a claim of a new lower bound for such systems or a comparison with an unanalysed Boolean encoding.

Nonvacuity. A mixed instance of the composition budget uses the Boolean-only \(J_4\), \(n\ge3\), \(S=\operatorname{span}\{1,x_1\}\), and ordinary restriction \(x_1=0\). Its rank is one and \(S\cap J_4=0\), so choose \(w=x_1\), which kills the exact error \(E_0=1-x_1\) through degree two. This is a concrete use of the known weighted composition, not a lower bound or a claim that every unweighted strategy fails.

Degenerate cases. Zero error tuples need no sampled row and have zero rank. At generic rank zero no random search is required. A zero protected image cannot meet a strict survival condition. The adaptive theorem assumes \(L\ge K\ge d\ge1\); an all-singleton grid is outside that stage and reduces to constant data. Singleton factors in a larger grid cause no problem. Static observations may be empty, as in Pang, and no adaptive stage is required, as in Braun. Two separately nonzero weights can multiply to zero; only survival of \(wf\) in the same final quotient is asserted. No basis of an inconsistent full quotient is substituted for the proper bounded consequence space.
