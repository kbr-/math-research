\section{The bit decoder and a separating functional}\label{sec:decoder}

Continue with \(n=2^\ell\), \(\ell\ge2\), and \(m=n+1\).
The old compact bit system is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-base}
 \mathcal Q_n
  =\{E_{ii'}:i<i'\in[m]\}\cup\Bool_b,
 \qquad
 E_{ii'}=\prod_{t=1}^{\ell}(1-b_{it}-b_{i't}).
\end{equation}
The degree of each collision generator is \(\ell\).
Its vanishing asserts that the two bit rows encode different labels.

Identify unary columns with labels \(z\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), and define the
ordinary ring map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decoder}
 \tau(b_{it})=\sum_{z:z_t=1}X_{iz}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Two-row interpolation]\label{lem:interpolation}
Let \(\mathcal U_{i,i'}\) consist of Booleanity, same-row exclusions,
and the two row-sum equations for distinct rows \(i,i'\).
For a polynomial \(R\) supported on these rows and of degree at most \(d\),
\[
 R-\sum_{z,w}R(e_z,e_w)X_{iz}X_{i'w}
     \in\Ideal_{\max\{d,2\}}(\mathcal U_{i,i'}).
\]
Here \(e_z,e_w\) are the corresponding one-hot assignments.
Column exclusions are not used in this interpolation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Reduce powers and same-row collisions. The remainder is a linear
combination of \(1\), individual cells of the two rows, and products
containing one cell from each row. This reduction costs at most \(d\).
Replace the constant by \(\rho_i\rho_{i'}\), using
\[
 1-\rho_i\rho_{i'}=(1-\rho_i)+\rho_i(1-\rho_{i'}),
\]
and replace each remaining single cell by its product with the other
row sum. These changes have certificates of degree at most two.
The result is bilinear, and its coefficient at \(X_{iz}X_{i'w}\)
is its value at \((e_z,e_w)\), namely \(R(e_z,e_w)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Degree-preserving old-base transfer]\label{lem:decoder}
If \(q\in\Cons_B(\mathcal Q_n)\), then
\[
 \tau q\in\Cons_B(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{fun}}_{m,n}).
\]
The map \(\tau\) is injective and preserves ordinary degree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The bit Booleanity images have explicit degree-two certificates:
\[
 \tau(b_{it}^2-b_{it})
    =\sum_{z:z_t=1}(X_{iz}^2-X_{iz}).
\]
For a collision image, use Lemma~\ref{lem:interpolation} with
\(R=\tau(E_{ii'})\). At \((e_z,e_w)\) its value is one exactly when
\(z=w\). Since \(\ell\ge2\), the lemma gives
\[
 \tau(E_{ii'})-\sum_z X_{iz}X_{i'z}
     \in\Ideal_\ell(\mathcal U_{i,i'}).
\]
The remaining sum consists of degree-two column exclusions.
Thus every old bit axiom image has an NS, hence PC, certificate through
its original degree.

For each row, the coefficient vectors of the \(\ell\) forms
\(\tau(b_{it})\) are linearly independent: the column labeled by the
\(t\)-th unit bit vector isolates the \(t\)-th coefficient.
Complete these forms to a linear coordinate system in the unary
variables. This proves injectivity and degree preservation.
Finally, replay a PC derivation under \(\tau\). A variable multiplier
becomes a linear polynomial, which is implemented by variable
multiplications and a linear combination at the same degree.
The certified axiom images initialize this replay.
\end{proof}

Let \(\mathcal L_k\) be the space spanned by monomials of degree at most
\(k\) containing at most one bit variable from each row. Its dimension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-linear-dimension}
 \dim\mathcal L_k=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom mj\ell^j
 \quad\text{when }k\le m.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Row-linear separation through a larger PC degree]
\label{thm:row-separation}
Suppose \(1\le k\le B\), \(4(k-1)<n\), and \(n\ge2B-1\).
For every nonzero \(f\in\mathcal L_k\),
\[
 \tau f\notin\Cons_B(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{fun}}_{m,n}),
 \qquad
 f\notin\Cons_B(\mathcal Q_n).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(f\) is constant, it is one, and a normalized functional from
Theorem~\ref{thm:moments} separates it.
Otherwise choose a degree-\(t\) monomial with nonzero coefficient, where
\(t=\deg f\le k\). Let \(S\) be its row set and let \(a_i\) be its bit
coordinate in row \(i\in S\).

Choose disjoint two-point coordinate lines
\[
 C_i=\{c_i,c_i+e_{a_i}\}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\ell},\qquad i\in S.
\]
For any required direction there are \(n/2\) possible lines.
The points in previously chosen lines forbid at most \(2(t-1)\) of them.
The inequality \(4(k-1)<n\) therefore permits greedy selection.
Let \(F=\bigcup_{i\in S}C_i\), so \(|F|=2t\).

On the \(m-t\) remaining rows and the \(n-2t\) labels outside \(F\),
choose a normalized matching functional \(\lambda\) through degree
\(B-t\). Theorem~\ref{thm:moments} applies because
\[
 m-t\ge B-t,\qquad n-2t\ge2(B-t)-1.
\]
There are residual labels: since \(n\) is divisible by four, the strict
packing inequality implies \(k\le n/4\).
At degree zero, \(\lambda\) is simply the normalized functional on
constants.

Define moments \(\mu(X_T)\) for all matchings of size at most \(B\).
Give a matching value zero unless it occupies every row \(i\in S\)
at a label in \(C_i\), and every other occupied row uses a label outside
all of \(F\). In the remaining case set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cube-moment}
 \mu(X_T)=\lambda(X_{T|_{[m]\setminus S}}).
\end{equation}
Extend this to ordinary monomials by Boolean power reduction and value
zero on row or column collisions.

We verify all equations of Lemma~\ref{lem:marginals}.
An already incorrect selected-row label or an outside row using a label
in \(F\) cannot be repaired by adding a cell.
If a matching misses at least two selected rows, one extension leaves
its value zero. If it misses exactly one selected row and the marginal
adds that row, its two allowed endpoints give equal residual values
whose sum is zero in \(\F\). A marginal adding a different row still
has value zero. Finally, if every selected row is present, a marginal
in another row is a marginal of \(\lambda\), since all added labels in
\(F\) have value zero. These cases exhaust the equations.
Consequently \(\mu\) annihilates \(\Ideal_B\), and hence \(\Cons_B\) by
Corollary~\ref{cor:stability}.

Only monomials of \(f\) with row set \(S\) can contribute to
\(\mu(\tau f)\). They have degree \(t\) and use one bit in each such row.
Equation~\eqref{eq:cube-moment} evaluates them by summing over
\(\prod_{i\in S}C_i\), with residual value \(\lambda(1)=1\).
In a selected row the sum of bit \(a_i\) over its two endpoints is one,
whereas the sum of every other bit is zero. The product of these
one-bit differences isolates exactly the chosen monomial's coefficient.
Thus \(\mu(\tau f)=1\), proving the first exclusion.
The second follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:decoder}.
\end{proof}

The functional \(\mu\) in the nonconstant case has value zero on one.
It is a separating direction in a finite-dimensional dual space,
not a probability distribution. Its role is to exclude the particular
nonzero \(f\), rather than merely to exclude a refutation of the old base.
