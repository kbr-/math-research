Harris kernel certificate

Statement. In Braun's setting (\(\mathbb F_2\), \(m\) rows of \(\ell\) bits, \(v=m\ell\), row-linear \(\mathcal L_k\)), let \(M\ge1\) proper coordinate blocks pin sets \(P_1,\ldots,P_M\) of bits to constants, each of size at least \(r\), and suppose that every bit pinned by several blocks is pinned by all of them to the same constant (consistent pins). Let \(0<\mu<k\), \(\pi=\mu/m\), \(0<\varepsilon<2\), \(J=\lfloor\varepsilon/\pi\rfloor\ge1\), \(c_0=\lceil r/(2\ell)\rceil\), \(y=(1-\varepsilon/2)\mu r/v\), \(g(y)=-\ln(1-e^{-y})\), \(\tau=e^{-\varepsilon c_0}/(J(1-e^{-\pi c_0}))\) and \(h(t)=t\ln t-t+1\). If

\[Me^{-r/(8\ell)}\le\tfrac12\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2M\bigl(g(y)+\tau\bigr)<\mu\,h(k/\mu),\tag{1}\]

then a nonzero \(f\in\mathcal L_k\) has zero ordinary restriction to every flat. Without consistent pins the same holds with \(\ell\) and \(v\) replaced by \(2\ell\) and \(2v\) in \(c_0\), \(y\) and (1).

Proof. Call a pair \((b,c)\) of a bit and a constant a literal, and let \(\Lambda_a\) be the literals block \(a\) pins, at least \(r\) of them and at most \(\ell\) in each row. For a set \(X\) of literals in distinct rows with \(|X|\le k\), the product \(f_X=\prod_{(b,c)\in X}(x_b+c)\) expands into row-linear monomials of degree at most \(|X|\), so lies in \(\mathcal L_k\), and is nonzero; if \(X\) meets \(\Lambda_a\) its restriction to block \(a\)'s flat has a zero factor. So it suffices to find such an \(X\), nonempty, meeting every \(\Lambda_a\). With consistent pins, give each bit the constant its pins share (zero if unpinned); this is one literal per bit, and \(\Lambda_a\) is identified with \(P_a\). Choose a bit \(b_i\) uniformly in each row \(i\), independently; let \(s_{a,i}=|P_a\cap\text{row }i|/\ell\) and \(c_a=\#\{i:b_i\in P_a\}\), a sum of independent Bernoulli\((s_{a,i})\) variables with mean \(\sigma_a=|P_a|/\ell\ge r/\ell\). Given \(b\), select each row independently with probability \(\pi\) and let \(X\) be the literals of the chosen bits of the selected rows. The event “\(X\) meets \(P_a\)” is that some row with \(b_i\in P_a\) is selected, an increasing event of the selection vector, with probability \(1-(1-\pi)^{c_a}\). Harris's inequality for increasing events under a product measure (the form used in lem:avoiding-sets-fraction) gives \(\Pr[X\text{ meets all }P_a\mid b]\ge\exp(-\sum_a\varphi(c_a))\), where \(\varphi(c)=-\ln(1-(1-\pi)^c)=\sum_{j\ge1}(1-\pi)^{cj}/j\). The Chernoff bound for the binomial number of selected rows gives \(\Pr[|X|>k]\le e^{-\mu h(k/\mu)}\), since \(k>\mu\). So a suitable \(X\) exists for this \(b\) when \(\sum_a\varphi(c_a(b))<\mu h(k/\mu)\); it is nonempty because \(M\ge1\).

Let \(G\) be the event that \(c_a\ge c_0\) for every \(a\). Since \(c_0-1<\sigma_a/2\), the Chernoff lower tail gives \(\Pr[c_a<c_0]\le e^{-\sigma_a/8}\le e^{-r/(8\ell)}\), so \(\Pr[G]\ge1-Me^{-r/(8\ell)}\ge\frac12\) by (1). Some \(b\in G\) has \(\sum_a\varphi(c_a(b))\le E[\sum_a\varphi(c_a)\mathbf 1_G]/\Pr[G]\le2\sum_aE[\varphi(\max(c_a,c_0))]\), as \(\varphi\) decreases. Split the series at \(J\). For \(j\le J\), \(E(1-\pi)^{jc_a}=\prod_i(1-s_{a,i}(1-(1-\pi)^j))\le\exp(-\sigma_a(1-(1-\pi)^j))\), and \(1-(1-\pi)^j\ge1-e^{-j\pi}\ge j\pi(1-j\pi/2)\ge j\pi(1-\varepsilon/2)\); so these terms are at most \(e^{-jy}/j\), whose sum over all \(j\ge1\) is \(g(y)\). For \(j>J\), \((1-\pi)^{j\max(c_a,c_0)}\le e^{-j\pi c_0}\), and \(\sum_{j>J}e^{-j\pi c_0}/j\le e^{-(J+1)\pi c_0}/((J+1)(1-e^{-\pi c_0}))\le\tau\), since \((J+1)\pi\ge\varepsilon\). Hence \(\sum_a\varphi(c_a(b))\le2M(g(y)+\tau)<\mu h(k/\mu)\). Without consistent pins, choose a literal uniformly among the \(2\ell\) of each row instead of a bit: then \(s_{a,i}=|\Lambda_a\cap\text{row }i|/(2\ell)\), \(\sigma_a\ge r/(2\ell)\), and the same argument runs with \(2\ell\) and \(2v\) in place of \(\ell\) and \(v\).

A correction to the first draft. The first draft reduced to pins to zero by translating each pinned-to-one bit. That is valid for one block at a time, as in the coordinate-count and ceiling proofs, which bound single restriction ranks, but not for a common kernel when two blocks pin one bit to different constants; the literal form above replaces it, and the author found this while rewriting after the review. Correction to the route review. The route review's Obstacle says that for coordinate blocks the common kernel exists exactly when a row-linear monomial meets every pinned set. That characterization is proved for pins to zero, which is the case prop:common-kernel-ceiling uses (its family pins \(x_P=0\), so the ceiling stands); for blocks pinning one bit to different constants it is not proved, and the lemmas here give only sufficient conditions through products of literal factors.

Greedy transversal certificate

Statement. In the same setting, let \(k\ge1\), let every block pin at least \(r>\ell(k-1)\) bits, and let \(V=v\) with consistent pins and \(V=2v\) otherwise. If for some integer \(0\le t\le k-1\)

\[M\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\Bigl(1-\frac{r-\ell i}{V}\Bigr)\le k-t,\tag{2}\]

then a nonzero \(f\in\mathcal L_k\) has zero ordinary restriction to every flat. At \(t=0\) this is: any \(M\le k\) blocks have a common kernel. It refines the greedy transversal bound of the route review, about \(\ln M<2k^2/m\), by a finishing step that adds \(\ln(k-t)\).

Proof. As in the previous proof it suffices to find a set \(X\) of at most \(k\) literals in distinct rows meeting every \(\Lambda_a\); for \(M\ge1\) it is nonempty, and for \(M=0\) take \(f=1\). Build \(X\) greedily. Let \(R_0\) be all blocks, and suppose \(i<t\) literals are chosen, in \(i\) rows, and \(R_i\) is the set of blocks not yet met. Each block of \(R_i\) pins at least \(r-\ell i>0\) literals in unused rows, since it pins at most \(\ell\) per row; there are at most \(V\) literals in unused rows that some block pins (one per bit with consistent pins, two otherwise). By double counting some such literal lies in at least \(|R_i|(r-\ell i)/V\) of the sets \(\Lambda_a\), \(a\in R_i\); add it, so \(|R_{i+1}|\le|R_i|(1-(r-\ell i)/V)\). After \(t\) steps (2) gives \(|R_t|\le k-t\). For each remaining block in turn add one of its literals in a row not yet used; one exists, since at most \(k-1\) rows are used and the block pins at least \(r-\ell(k-1)>0\) literals outside them. In total \(X\) has at most \(t+(k-t)=k\) literals in distinct rows and meets every block.

Degenerate cases. \(M=0\): \(f=1\). \(M\le k\): the greedy certificate at \(t=0\). \(r\le\ell(k-1)\): the greedy lemma says nothing; a block may then be pinned inside \(k-1\) rows. \(\mu\ge k\) or \(J=0\): the Harris lemma says nothing. Duplicated blocks change no transversal.

Goal progress. Unchanged. The route review's restated non-domination requirement asks for a certificate whose hypothesis needs no given cover and does not follow from the cover bound for a cover it supplies. The trivial bound \(M\le k\) meets those words, so they do not separate a structural saving from an elementary one, and meeting them is not evidence of progress; this goes to the next review, which must restate the requirement. Bearing. None. The goal's own parameters are a polynomial inventory, \(\ln M=O(\ell)\), at polylogarithmic degree. There \(K_D\) is about \(n/(2D)\), and the summed count, like the bound behind Braun's corollary on polynomial inventory and polylogarithmic degree, certifies \(\ln M\) up to order \(K_D^2/m\), far beyond \(O(\ell)\) (\(593\) at \(\ell=24\), \(D=145\) in the saved table), so no saving is needed there. The certificates of this entry gain only at degrees polynomial in \(n\), such as \(D\approx\sqrt n/2\), outside the goal's range. The cycle was spent because a correlation certificate could have given a saving that transfers to non-coordinate flats, where the single-flat estimate is not shown tight; it gives none on coordinate flats.
