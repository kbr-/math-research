<h4 id="weighted-coefficient-grid">Weighted coefficient grid</h4>
<p>Let \(J_2\) be a degree-\(D\) binary pseudoideal containing old Booleanity, \(E=\mathbb F_{2^e}\), \(J_E=E\otimes J_2\), and \(W\in E[x]_{\le b}\setminus J_E\). Fix \(T\ge1\) with \(b+2T\le D\). Define
\[
K_W=\{a\in E[x]_{\le T}:Wa\in J_E\},\qquad
Q_W=E[x]_{\le T}/K_W,\qquad r=\dim_E Q_W.
\tag{1}
\]
This quotient describes coefficient polynomials indistinguishable after weighting; it is not a quotient of the weight space. Its dimension is the multiplication rank \(h_W(T)\), and \(r\ge1\), since the class of one is nonzero.</p>
<p><strong>Lemma (weighted coefficient grid).</strong> The kernel \(K_W\) has a basis with binary coefficients, even when \(W\) is not binary. Choose binary polynomials \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\) representing a basis of \(Q_W\). Then, over \(E\) or its algebraic closure, a class \(a=\sum_j\lambda_j e_j\) satisfies the weighted field equation \(W(a^2-a)\in J\) exactly when every \(\lambda_j\in\mathbb F_2\). For \(s\ge1\) fresh coefficient variables there are exactly \(2^{sr}\) legal class tuples.</p>
<p>In homogeneous coordinates \([t:(\lambda_{u,j})_{1\le u\le s,1\le j\le r}]\), the field equations are precisely
\[
\lambda_{u,j}^2-t\lambda_{u,j}=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Their projective scheme is the reduced set of \(2^{sr}\) points in the chart \(t\ne0\); it has no point at infinity. Finally, if a binary source polynomial \(F(x,y)\) has original joint degree at most \(B\ge2\) and \(b+TB\le D\), its weighted image under degree-\(T\) maps depends only on their classes in (1). Thus every complete source membership condition is a well-defined additional equation on this exact finite grid.</p>
<p><strong>Proof: invariant kernel.</strong> Let \(\sigma a\) square the coefficients of \(a\) and fix the old indeterminates. The old Boolean equations give \(a^2-\sigma a\) through degree \(2T\). If \(Wa\in J_E\), multiply that membership by \(a\), within \(b+2T\), and subtract \(W(a^2-\sigma a)\). This gives \(W\sigma a\in J_E\). Therefore \(\sigma K_W\subseteq K_W\); iterating the finite-order Frobenius makes this equality. In a fixed monomial basis of the degree-\(T\) space, the unique reduced row-echelon basis of \(K_W\) is preserved by coefficientwise Frobenius. Every entry is consequently binary. Extend that binary basis to a binary basis of the whole polynomial space; the added vectors give the \(e_j\)'s. This is the same paid Frobenius reuse as in weighted map descent, now applied to the annihilator kernel.</p>
<p><strong>Proof: field fiber.</strong> Again through \(b+2T\),
\[
W(a^2-a)\equiv W(\sigma a-a).
\]
In the binary quotient basis, \(\sigma a-a\) has coordinates \(\lambda_j^2-\lambda_j\), so the weighted field membership is exactly the coordinatewise Boolean equation. The argument also shows representative independence. More generally \(W(a^2-ta)\) has coordinates \(\lambda_j^2-t\lambda_j\) in the weighted image, giving (2). At \(t=0\) these equations force all \(\lambda_{u,j}=0\), which is not a projective point. In \(t=1\), the coordinate ring is the tensor product of the rings \(E[\lambda]/(\lambda^2-\lambda)\); each factor is reduced with its two simple binary roots. Hence the entire projective scheme is the stated finite reduced grid. For algebraic-closure coefficients, choose a finite extension containing the finitely many coefficients. The binary kernel basis and rank are preserved by scalar extension, so no new classes appear.</p>
<p><strong>Proof: full source equations.</strong> If two coefficient images differ by an element of \(K_W\), their weighted difference lies in \(J_E\). Telescope each monomial of \(F\) between the two image tuples. Each term contains one such difference and at most \(B-1\) other old or substituted factors. Its multiplication ceiling is at most \(b+TB\). Thus the weighted images differ by an element of \(J_E\), proving that the source equations descend to the quotient. After a basis is chosen they are polynomial equations on its coordinates, with parameter degree no larger than the original degree of \(F\). No multiplication algebra on the entire truncated quotient is assumed. \(\square\)</p>
<h4>What happened to the earlier point at infinity?</h4>
<p>In the earlier <a href="#coefficient-chart-boundary">chart control</a>, a boundary solution used \(W=x\), \(a=1-x\), \(t=0\). Its coefficient image is invisible to the weight: \(W(1-x)=0\). In the present quotient it is the zero class. Hence its homogeneous coordinates would all be zero and it is correctly excluded. The construction repairs precisely the spurious-boundary inference rather than declaring the old projective method impossible from one example.</p>
<p>The repair does not create an affine solution for that source. The same control already proved the required weight threshold, and below it every complete source fiber is empty. The new projective model of that complete fiber is then simply empty as well. Field equations have used up all \(sr\) coefficient dimensions before companions are imposed. Intersecting the remaining equations with a finite grid may discard every point; ambient parameter count does not prevent that. For an unfixed weight the kernels \(K_W\) can change, so no single uniform quotient basis or trivial bundle over all weights is asserted.</p>
<p>The <a href="#binary-map-weight-locus">finite union of weight kernels</a> remains the global statement. Its projective weight locus can be proper or empty. The present coefficient reduction is compatible with it and does not replace its nonemptiness premise. Generic weights would still demand the stronger unweighted-map property already identified there.</p>
