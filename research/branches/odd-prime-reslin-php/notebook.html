21 September 2026 — Standard equation clauses compile at constant degree into power-selector inputs

Status: working proof of a syntactic Res(Lin) transfer; no PHP lower bound. Its one-level inputs are degree-\(p-1\) selectors, generally not affine. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Missing implication tested. Can arbitrary-width standard equation clauses be handled without exponential residue splitting, and with a degree bound independent of DAG height? We allow polynomial selector inputs temporarily but retain their exact form and degree; a successful translation must expose, rather than hide, the remaining exclusion obligation.

Statement and source format

Fix a prime \(p\), put \(\delta=p-1\), and let \(v\) old variables be Boolean. Let a standard syntactic \(\mathrm{Res}(\mathrm{lin}_{\mathbb F_p})\) refutation have \(S\) lines, with input CNF clauses of width at most \(w\). Include Boolean axioms among the initials and set \(w_*=\max(w,2)\). The rules are the resolution, literal weakening, simplification and contraction audited in the preceding entry; arbitrary semantic inference is not assumed.

For an affine equation \(L=0\), set \(t_L=1-L^\delta\). There is a complete one-level ENS family with at most \(S\) blocks, each at accuracy one and having at most \(\binom{v+\delta}{\delta}\) inputs, each input literally some \(t_L\) from its source line. With coefficient equations \(r^p-r\), this family and the ordinary CNF polynomial axioms have an ordinary PC refutation through

\[D=\max\{4p-1,\ p+w_*\delta\}.\]

No proof-height factor occurs. The initial polynomial for \(\bigvee_i(L_i=0)\) is \(F_A=\prod_iL_i\); for Boolean CNF literals these are affine coordinate forms, up to nonzero signs. For fixed \(p\), both the registry inventory and the coefficient-variable count are polynomial in \(S,v\), even if the original intermediate clauses have enormous width. This is an existence statement about a PC refutation and registry, not a bound on monomial-expanded PC length.

Compression and complete blocks

In each line choose a basis subset of its selectors in the vector space of ordinary polynomials of degree at most \(\delta\). Every omitted selector is a constant linear combination of this subset. The bound follows from the displayed ambient dimension. No Boolean quotient is needed for this compression. Empty spans have an empty basis. For the chosen basis \(B_C\), introduce fresh coefficients and set

\[P_C=1-\sum_{j\in B_C}r_{C,j}t_j,\qquad E_{C,j}=t_jP_C.\]

The complete block contains every companion \(E_{C,j}\) and every coefficient field equation. Thus \(\deg P_C\le p\), \(\deg E_{C,j}\le2p-1\). Any original selector in line \(C\) times \(P_C\) is a constant combination of these companions at the same ceiling. No derived line \(P_C\) is added as an axiom. For an empty basis \(P_C=1\), no coefficient variables or companions are required.

Boolean vanishing certificates used below

A polynomial in old Boolean variables that vanishes on the whole Boolean cube has an ordinary Boolean-ideal certificate through its own total degree: reduce each power using \(x_i^2-x_i\) without increasing degree; the remaining multilinear polynomial is zero by evaluation on the cube. This argument works over every field. It will be used only for an explicit local selector identity, not for any PHP consequence.

Initial values and one-premise rules

For an initial clause \(A\), its original selectors satisfy

\[1-\prod_{i=1}^{a}(1-t_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}t_i\prod_{j<i}(1-t_j),\qquad \prod_i(1-t_i)=F_A^\delta.\]

Multiply by \(P_A\). Each summand on the right is obtained from \(t_iP_A\), a constant combination of complete companions, by multiplying by the preceding factors. The term \(F_A^\delta P_A\) follows from the initial axiom \(F_A\). All terms have degree at most \(p+a\delta\); for Boolean axioms, \(F_A=x_i(x_i-1)\) is already an old Boolean equation. Hence the initial \(P_A\) is derived within \(D\).

For weakening from \(A\) to \(T\), every basis selector of \(A\) lies in the selector span of \(T\). The identity \(P_T=P_AP_T+\sum_jr_{A,j}t_jP_T\) derives \(P_T\) from the completed line \(P_A\) and the \(T\)-companions through \(\max(D,2p)\). Simplification deletes only \(a=0\) with \(a\ne0\), whose selector is identically zero, so the same argument applies. Contraction leaves the span unchanged.

Resolution and exact degree ledger

Consider premises \(A=C\vee(f=0)\), \(B=E\vee(g=0)\), and conclusion \(T=C\vee E\vee(h=0)\), where \(h=\alpha f+\beta g\). In the basis subset of \(A\), all selectors except possibly \(t_f\) come from \(C\); let \(r_f\) be its coefficient if selected, or zero otherwise. Likewise define \(r_g\). A selector that also occurs in the context can still be designated as pivot; the remaining ones are context selectors. Using the completed premise values and the \(T\)-companions derives

\[R_A=P_T-r_ft_fP_T=0,\qquad R_B=P_T-r_gt_gP_T=0\]

through \(\max(D,2p)\). For example \(R_A=P_AP_T+\sum_{j\text{ context}}r_{A,j}t_jP_T\). Each context selector is in the selector span of \(T\).

At every old Boolean assignment, \(t_ft_g(1-t_h)=0\): if both first selectors are one then \(f=g=0\), hence \(h=0\); otherwise their product is zero. Let \(I=t_ft_g(1-t_h)\). Its degree is at most \(3\delta\), so it has the Boolean certificate just proved. Also \(t_hP_T\) is a constant combination of \(T\)-companions. Consequently

\[Q=t_ft_gP_T=t_ft_g(t_hP_T)+IP_T=0\]

has a PC derivation through \(\max(D,4p-3)\). The last local step is the ordinary polynomial identity

\[P_T=R_A+r_ft_fR_B+r_fr_gQ.\]

The middle product has degree at most \(3p\). Constructing the last term by multiplying the completed \(Q\) line by its two coefficient variables costs at most \(4p-1\) even if its derivation previously reached \(4p-3\). Thus the entire inference fits \(D\). Multiplication by a polynomial means its expansion into monomials, each implemented by variable multiplications followed by field-linear combinations. Crucially, multiply the completed premise lines rather than their whole derivations; the degree does not accumulate along the DAG.

Induct through the original topological line order, in one fixed registry. The empty final clause has \(P_\varnothing=1\), so this yields the asserted PC refutation. Coefficient field equations remain in the source although this forward simulation does not need them. The empty-initial-clause case already has axiom one and is immediate.

What this does and does not settle

This proves a working translation for the actual standard equation-clause rules, with polynomial inventory and constant degree at constant initial width. After the usual graph restriction, the missing implication becomes exclusion of complete one-level families whose inputs are these specific shifted powers of affine forms. The all-prime affine theorem does not cover them. The earlier odd-prime nonlinearity control already shows that Boolean reduction does not generally make them affine. We therefore obtain no Res(Lin) lower bound here.

For \(p=3\) the precise target inputs are \(1-L^2\), not arbitrary quadratic polynomials. The next cycle should test a common-weight exclusion for that family, preserving its structure before demanding a theorem for all bounded-degree inputs. The known full-field conversion obstruction is respected: this transfer uses nonlinear inputs and does not assert an equivalent affine-disequality CNF.

Checks, dependencies and assessment

The proof above is self-contained given the audited syntactic rules, elementary polynomial linear algebra and ordinary PC definitions. It adapts the existing complete-block replay pattern; no semantic weakening theorem or binary compression theorem is invoked at odd primes. Exact finite tests over \(\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\) cover every \((f,g,\alpha,\beta)\), and removing the second-premise selector produces counterexamples in each field. The saved results check that local relation only; they are not a full primitive-PC or Lean verification.

Focused review. Basis compression uses literal polynomial spans, not unproved semantic implications. Initials retain their product axioms. Zero coefficients, absent pivots, duplicate selectors and empty bases are allowed. Both premise selectors are essential, as the negative controls demonstrate. Original input degree \(p-1\) is charged before any reduction; no auxiliary names or omitted defining equations are introduced. The remaining lower-bound obligation is untouched by simply observing that this degree is constant.

Contribution and process. This cycle discharges the forward syntactic transfer into a precisely restricted nonlinear source; it does not discharge source exclusion. That makes the next mathematical test concrete. Reusing the earlier disequality ledger avoided redundant rule development, and no framework change is warranted.

21 September 2026 — Full-field selector zero sets need not shrink the graph weight space

Status: working obstruction to one proposed restriction map, not to the selector compiler or to Boolean-domain normalization. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Test. The affine exclusion proof selects a common weight from kernels of restrictions to high-rank zero flats. Can the same step use the full-field common-zero set of many independent power selectors instead? The required implication is a small image of the actual graph-separated weight space, not merely a small geometric zero set. Stop if even one allowed selector tuple has injective restriction on that weight space.

A maximal-rank tuple with no multilinear restriction kernel

Fix an odd prime \(p\), old coordinates \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\), and the legal selector inputs \(g_i=1-Y_i^{p-1}\). Their underlying affine forms have rank \(s\), and the \(g_i\) are linearly independent ordinary polynomials. Their full-field common-zero set is \((\mathbb F_p^\times)^s\). Restriction to this set is injective on the whole space of multilinear polynomials, hence on every graph weight space spanned by squarefree matching monomials.

Proof. Choose distinct nonzero \(a,b\in\mathbb F_p\). A multilinear polynomial vanishing on \(\{a,b\}^s\) is zero. Induct on \(s\): write it as \(A(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{s-1})+Y_sB(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{s-1})\). Evaluating at \(a,b\) and subtracting gives \((a-b)B=0\) on the smaller grid, so the induction hypothesis gives \(B=0\) and then \(A=0\). The base case is constant polynomials. Since the two-point grid lies in the common-zero set, injectivity follows. In particular no nonzero graph weight can lie in the ordinary ideal generated by these selectors: membership would force vanishing on that set. Linear independence of the inputs follows by comparing the distinct monomials \(Y_i^{p-1}\). This proves the assertion.

The failure already occurs for \(p=3\) and a single accuracy-one block. An arbitrary equation clause listing the coordinate equations supplies precisely this kind of selector tuple, so the compiler does not prohibit it. This is a countercontrol to a universal normalization argument for its source class, not an assertion that a hard PHP refutation necessarily contains this line.

Why this does not refute the Boolean route

On the old Boolean cube, \(g_i\) equals \(1-Y_i\). Their common-zero set there is just the all-one assignment. For positive degree, a space spanned by degree-\(k\) matching monomials restricts to constants on that point and has image dimension at most one. For \(p=3\), the identity is explicitly \(g_i-(1-Y_i)=-(Y_i^2-Y_i)\); for general \(p\), the difference is a Boolean-ideal consequence through degree \(p-1\). Thus the full-field injectivity and the Boolean-domain collapse are compatible. The normalization must preserve Boolean relations rather than demand literal ideal membership in the selector ideal alone.

This control is not a proof that every selector tuple has a large affine part after Boolean reduction. The existing two-bit control gives \(1-(x+y)^{p-1}=(1-x)(1-y)\) on Boolean inputs for odd \(p\), whose quadratic term does not vanish. Coordinate selectors are therefore a falsification control, not a general solution.

Comparison with available nonlinear machinery

The binary quadratic rank-or-leading-pairs criterion retains the actual input span modulo Boolean equations and separates its affine part. Its high case additionally requires disjoint squarefree leading pairs and its dimension budget uses the compact binary cube-volume multiplier space. Its proof cannot simply be applied to a ternary graph-PHP weight space, or to arbitrary odd-prime affine coordinate changes, which need not preserve the Boolean cube. The later binary cubic endpoint is likewise not an all-prime theorem.

Outcome and next test. The full-field restriction variant is refuted, while the actual lower-bound implication remains open. Next examine the Boolean-reduced span of ternary selector inputs: separate its affine part and determine whether the remaining quadratic span admits an affordable common-weight map on the graph base. Check both coordinate selectors and selectors of sums of two bits. A successful theorem must work for arbitrary compiled tuples and must budget image loss against the actual graph weight dimension; a binary cube estimate is not a substitute.

Significance and checks. This elementary interpolation argument is a route-specific negative result, with no novelty claim. It uses no computation or new imported theorem, and no lower bound is asserted. The self-contained proof checks both the full-field kernel and the Boolean-domain countercontrol.

Process assessment. Testing the proposed map on a maximal-rank coordinate tuple ruled it out before developing a general Hilbert-function estimate; the existing search and excerpt tools were sufficient.

21 September 2026 — Existing all-prime quadratic maps do not yet meet the graph weight budget

Status: parameter audit of inherited bounds. The raw estimates do not prove a common kernel; no impossibility of a better relative map is claimed. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

General claim under test. Combine a Boolean-domain quadratic reduction with the graph-separated multiplier space to exclude the ternary selector source. The goal needs contraction of the actual graph weight space sufficient for polynomially many blocks, plus affordable original-degree certificates. Merely recognizing selectors as quadratics does not give this.

Recovered positive ingredient. The leading-pair reduction lemma works over every prime, provided the original Boolean-coordinate input span supplies disjoint squarefree leading pairs. Its kernel has ordinary membership certificates modulo old Booleanity. The all-prime application already combines it with a compact bit-PHP row-cube separator; it is not restricted to characteristic two. However, that application does not use our graph unary base. This distinction sharpens the previous cycle’s comparison with the binary quadratic and cubic endpoints.

Budget test. Write \(s=15(N+1)\). The current graph construction guarantees a degree-\(k\) separated space of dimension at least \(\tfrac12e^{-5k^2/N}\binom sk\), in its stated range. The inherited raw leading-pair image estimate is \((s+1)e^{-Rk^2/s^2}\binom sk\), with \(R\le s/2\). Their ratio (image upper bound divided by available dimension lower bound) is

\[2(s+1)\exp\left(\left(\frac5N-\frac R{s^2}\right)k^2\right)>1,\]

because \(R/s^2\le1/(2s)<5/N\). Thus these two displayed bounds cannot certify even one image smaller than the guaranteed weight space; multiplying by the number of blocks only worsens the comparison. This holds even if disjoint leading pairs are supplied for free. It does not compare the actual image and actual weight dimensions, which may be substantially better. In particular, it is not a proof that no useful kernel exists.

Why the compact cube proof does not remove this loss automatically. The compact multiplier construction has nearly the full degree-\(k\) monomial dimension because it may choose disjoint label cubes for each target monomial from the full label set. Sparse graph rows are confined to their prescribed neighbors; that cube-choice proof does not supply the same freedom. Conversely, a direct unary-to-bit substitution turns cells into label indicators of logarithmic degree and must be charged in every original input and initial axiom. Neither conversion has been proved here to preserve the needed budget.

Route assessment and alternative. The local all-prime map is a useful existing tool, but the present composition lacks both a structure theorem for arbitrary selector spans and a compatible graph-relative dimension estimate. The numerical mismatch means that parameter tuning of these raw bounds is not the next task. The next test should count reduction images relative to the graph matching space, with old row/hole equations explicitly included, and distinguish genuinely new leading constraints from old zero monomials. Such a theorem remains unproved.

Checks and process. Read the exact graph bound, local reduction lemma, all-prime compact application and cube-volume proof, and compare their hypotheses and displayed inequalities. No computational or formal verification is claimed. This audit introduces no new structural claim; bounded dependency retrieval found a stronger all-prime ingredient and prevented an unnecessary field-generalization exercise.
