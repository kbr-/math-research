9 October 2026 — Proper kernels need a large free coordinate set

Working dimension obstruction and structural ceiling; a non-vacuous shattering refinement. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Question. On near-half-family, can the last cycle's polynomial-dimensional compatible coefficient space support any proper degree-D PC kernel, even after its operators are repaired? Test the dimension budget before making more local corrections. Then derive any inexpensive necessary condition on the formula and check its non-vacuity.

Route comparison. Direct control of the large fixed-cutoff quotient remains possible. Repairing the small selector space is worthwhile only if that space can hold the required kernel. The dimension argument below rules out that entire small-space endpoint, while its free-coordinate consequence supplies a structural test. The test is weaker than hardness and has both a fixed-width witness and a verified application to the current central source.

New mathematics. Every proper Boolean truncated kernel for an unsatisfiable input has a standard monomial of degree at least \(D-1\). Its divisors give a free coordinate set of that size and at least \(2^{D-1}\) quotient dimensions. Thus the small selector space cannot certify the near-half barrier. The same fact bounds ordinary PC degree by the largest set simultaneously shattered by all individual rows, plus two and the original-width floor. A guarded witness validates the resulting specification condition, and deleting the variables of rare bad rows makes the central source pass it with a deficit smaller than every fixed inverse-logarithmic fraction.

Goal progress. Unchanged. A proposed small representation is excluded and the specification is strengthened with witnesses. No proper near-half kernel, surviving full coordinate algebra modulo PC, or complete Frege transfer is constructed. Rowwise shattering does not imply that joint row consequences vanish.

A proper truncated quotient cannot be small

Let \(F\) be any field and \(B=F[x_1,\ldots,x_v]/(x_i^2-x_i)\), with its multilinear total-degree filtration. Fix an integer \(D\ge2\). Let \(J\subseteq B_{\le D}\) be a linear subspace with \[1\notin J,\qquad x_i(J\cap B_{\le D-1})\subseteq J\quad(i\in[v]).\tag{1}\] Assume \(J\) contains all members of an unsatisfiable Boolean input family \(\Gamma\subseteq B_{\le D}\). Multiplication in (1) is Boolean multiplication; the predecessor degree restriction is part of the hypothesis. Set \(Q=B_{\le D}/J\), and let \(Q_{\le j}\) be the image of \(B_{\le j}\), with \(Q_{\le-1}=0\).

Theorem (truncated kernel dimension). There is a set \(S\subseteq[v]\) of size \(D-1\) such that the entire Boolean coordinate algebra \(F[x_i:i\in S]/(x_i^2-x_i)\) injects linearly into \(Q\). In particular, for \(0\le j\le D-1\), \[\dim(Q_{\le j}/Q_{\le j-1})\ge\binom{D-1}{j},\qquad \dim Q_{\le j}\ge\sum_{r=0}^j\binom{D-1}{r},\qquad \dim Q\ge2^{D-1}.\tag{2}\] The assertion implies nonexistence of such a kernel if its required set size exceeds \(v\). It asserts linear independence of all monomials on \(S\), not closure of their image under every outside multiplication.

Two degrees of slack force a solution. Suppose first that \(Q_{\le D-2}=Q\). Choose a linear representative map into \(B_{\le D-2}\). Define \(T_i[p]=[x_ip]\) using such a low-degree representative. This is independent of its choice because the difference of two representatives lies in \(J\cap B_{\le D-2}\), and (1) permits its multiplication. If \(q_j\) is a low-degree representative of \([x_jp]\), then \(q_j-x_jp\in J\cap B_{\le D-1}\). Its multiplication by \(x_i\) is legal, so \[T_iT_j[p]=[x_iq_j]=[x_ix_jp].\tag{3}\] The same calculation with the indices exchanged, and with equal indices using Booleanity, proves \(T_iT_j=T_jT_i\) and \(T_i^2=T_i\).

Write \(e=[1]\ne0\). For every monomial \(M\) of degree at most \(D\), induction gives \(M(T)e=[M]\). At the last step the preceding monomial has degree at most \(D-1\), and its difference from the chosen representative also has degree at most \(D-1\); thus (1) justifies that step even when the input degree equals \(D\). Hence every \(g\in\Gamma\) satisfies \(g(T)e=0\). The joint Boolean projectors \(P_a=\prod_{i:a_i=1}T_i\prod_{i:a_i=0}(I-T_i)\) sum to the identity. Some \(P_ae\) is nonzero. Commutativity gives \(g(a)P_ae=P_ag(T)e=0\), so every input vanishes at the Boolean assignment \(a\), contradicting unsatisfiability. Therefore \(Q_{\le D-2}\ne Q\).

Standard monomials and their divisors. Choose any degree-compatible monomial order and row-reduce \(J\) with largest monomials first. The squarefree monomials not occurring as leading monomials of \(J\) form a filtered basis of \(Q\). They are closed under divisors through degree \(D\). Indeed, if \(x_A\) is the leading monomial of \(f\in J\) and \(i\notin A\), with \(|A|+1\le D\), then \(\deg f=|A|\) and (1) puts \(x_if\) in \(J\). Its leading squarefree term is \(x_{A\cup\{i\}}\): ordinary monomial-order multiplication preserves the leading order, and reduction of a repeated variable only lowers degree. Iterating proves the assertion.

Since the quotient is not spanned through degree \(D-2\), its filtered monomial basis has a member of degree at least \(D-1\). Choose a divisor \(x_S\) of degree exactly \(D-1\). Every divisor of this monomial remains standard, so all \(2^{D-1}\) monomials on \(S\) are independent. Counting them by degree proves (2). \(\square\)

The mechanism combines the already used quotient-stabilization argument with downward closure of standard monomials. The older design support bound also gives an exponential count, but for the point support of a scalar design with quadratic inputs. Here no actual point-support representation is assumed, the quantity is truncated quotient dimension, and all active input degrees up to \(D\) are allowed. This is a route-specific extension of those mechanisms, not a claim of independent novelty.

The selector space fails the necessary dimension budget

Corollary (local state dimension obstruction). Suppose a linear map \(\Phi:B_{\le D}\to V\) has \(\Phi(1)\ne0\), kills all the unsatisfiable active inputs, and its kernel satisfies (1). Then \(\dim V\ge2^{D-1}\). If \(V\) is a subspace or quotient of a direct sum of \(M\) scalar-valued Boolean coefficient spaces, each on at most \(b\) Boolean bits, necessarily \[D\le1+\log_2\dim V\le1+b+\log_2M.\tag{4}\] For the preceding central-source selector space, \(\dim V\le m2^k=5\cdot2^{9l-3}\), so an integer cutoff must satisfy \(D\le9l\). It cannot support a kernel of this kind through a near-half cutoff.

Proof. Apply (2) to \(J=\ker\Phi\) and identify the quotient with \(\operatorname{im}\Phi\). A direct sum of \(M\) spaces of dimensions at most \(2^b\) has dimension at most \(M2^b\); taking a subspace or quotient cannot increase it. At the central parameters \(\lfloor\log_2(m2^k)\rfloor+1=9l\). \(\square\)

This rules out every repair inside the same polynomial-dimensional scalar state space that has the stated kernel property, not only the zero-fill operators. It also applies to polynomially many bounded-size joint-context coefficient lists. It does not rule out polynomial-valued coefficients carrying all spectator monomials, implicit exponential-dimensional tensor spaces, or the existing full fixed-cutoff quotient. A scalar PC annihilator may have a one-dimensional target because its entire kernel need not be multiplication-closed; a proof that uses additional state has to count that state. Satisfiable examples, including the repaired path, can have one-dimensional proper evaluation quotients and are outside the unsatisfiability hypothesis.

A cheap structural consequence

For a canonical Boolean relation system with scopes \(X_a\) and allowed sets \(R_a\), call \(S\subseteq[v]\) locally shattered when \[\pi_{S\cap X_a}R_a=\{0,1\}^{S\cap X_a}\quad\text{for every row }a.\tag{5}\] Thus every assignment on the selected coordinates extends within each row separately. Let \(\sigma\) be the maximum size of such a set; put \(\sigma=-1\) if some row is empty, so that even the empty set fails (5). This is a property of the chosen relation representation, not of a presumed PC proof.

Theorem (local shattering ceiling). For an unsatisfiable canonical relation system of maximum actual width \(w\), over every field, \[d_F\le\max\{w,2,\sigma+2\}.\tag{6}\] Consequently a barrier through \(D\ge\max\{w,2\}\) requires \(\sigma\ge D-1\).

Proof. The multilinear part of ordinary PC closure \(C_D\), identified with a subspace of \(B_{\le D}\), satisfies (1) if it is proper. Indeed multilinearizing an ordinary degree-D line uses Boolean-axiom multiples through that same degree; a multilinear predecessor of degree at most \(D-1\) may then be multiplied and reduced through degree \(D\). All canonical inputs of width at most \(D\) are present. Apply the preceding theorem and take its free set \(S\) of size \(D-1\).

If (5) fails for some row on \(U=S\cap X_a\), a missing assignment on \(U\) has a nonzero indicator \(f_U\) that vanishes on \(R_a\). The literal Boolean indicator expansion writes \(f_U\) as the scalar sum of its values times the forbidden full-row indicators, which are original axioms of degree at most \(w\le D\). Thus \(f_U\in C_D\). But it is a nonzero polynomial on \(S\), contradicting the free-coordinate conclusion. So \(\sigma\ge D-1\) whenever \(1\notin C_D\). Taking \(D=\max\{w,2,\sigma+2\}\) proves (6), including the empty-row case. \(\square\)

The condition learns from the upper bound: rows that collectively prevent every large set from being locally shattered rule out the proposed near-half barrier. It is a local-projection version of the existing degree-lowering coverage mechanism; absence of that coverage alone never supplies hardness. The accepted fixed-width specification may now require \(\sigma\ge D_v-1\) for its intended cutoff. The following witness verifies that this structural revision is non-vacuous while preserving all previously accepted conditions.

An unbounded witness for the refined specification

Proposition (shattering specification witness). There is an unbounded minimal unsatisfiable width-at-most-five CNF family of linear size, with essential scopes, at least two occurrences per variable, branching fraction at least \(2/5\), no common Horn polarity and full binary affine hull in each row, such that \(\sigma=v-1\). Its ordinary PC degree is at least a fixed positive fraction of its actual variables over every field. It is a non-vacuity witness, not a near-half-hard family.

Construction. Take two disjoint identical copies \(F(x),F(y)\) of the proved branching witness, each with \(N\) variables, \(M=O(N)\) clauses of widths three or four, branching count \(h\ge2N/5\) and degree at least \(cN\). Add a single bit \(t\) and use \[\{t\vee C(x):C\in F\}\ \cup\ \{\neg t\vee C(y):C\in F\}.\tag{7}\] There are \(v=2N+1\) actual variables and \(2M\) clauses, of width at most five. When \(t=0\) the first copy is active; when \(t=1\) the second is active, so the formula is unsatisfiable. Deleting any guarded clause permits a satisfying assignment of the corresponding old formula with that one clause deleted, while the other copy is disabled by the chosen guard. Hence minimality survives.

Every clause is proper and each of its variables essential. Old occurrences are unchanged, and \(t\) occurs \(2M\ge4\) times. The branching fraction is \((2h+1)/(2N+1)\ge2/5\), more than the width-five requirement \(1/10\). A proper clause of width at least two has full binary affine hull: after moving its unique forbidden tuple to zero, its allowed nonzero cube contains all unit vectors and a sum of two of them, forcing every affine vanishing form to be zero. Thus all local binary affine consequences are jointly consistent.

No common polarity can make the formula Horn. In either polarity of \(t\), one copy's guard is a positive literal. Hornness would force every literal of every old clause in that copy to be negative. All those nonempty clauses would then be satisfied by setting its renamed variables to zero, contradicting the copy's unsatisfiability. For single-clause relations Hornness is equivalent to intersection closure: two distinct positive literals give two satisfying assignments whose meet violates the clause, while clauses with at most one positive literal are intersection-closed.

Take \(S\) to be all variables except \(t\). Every row projects fully onto its selected coordinates because its guard can satisfy that row independently, so \(\sigma\ge v-1\). The full set fails for any non-universal row, giving equality. Finally restrict \(t=0\) and all unused \(y\)-variables to constants in a PC refutation. The result is a refutation of \(F(x)\) with no degree increase. The new degree is therefore at least \(cN=c(v-1)/2\). This pays the duplicated variables and loses a constant factor, while the width rises from four to five. Linear size and every other accepted property survive. \(\square\)

The witness also refutes the converse from local shattering to a free PC coordinate algebra. Write the old clause polynomials as \(f(x),g(y)\). From guarded inputs \((1-t)f\) and \(tg\), multiply by \(g\) and \(f\), respectively, and add to derive the nonzero polynomial \(fg\) on \(S\) through degree at most \(\deg f+\deg g+1\le9\). It has final degree at most eight. Thus a large rowwise shattered set can already support a low-degree joint consequence. This all-parameter control prevents using (5) as a hardness assumption in disguise.

The central source passes the new screen

Theorem (central local shattering). At the unchanged central parameters \(v=l2^l,k=8l,m=5v/k\), with probability \(1-o(1)\), there is a locally shattered set of size at least \[v/2-5v\exp(-9l/512).\tag{8}\] This deficit is \(o(v/(\log v)^A)\) for every fixed \(A\). The event can be intersected with all the already proved central-source properties. It gives rowwise extensions for every pin on this one set, not a global solution or a free coordinate algebra modulo PC.

Random subset sums cover the modulus. Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_h\) be independent uniform elements of the odd field \(\mathbb F_q\). For a uniform independent target, apply the proved subset fourth-moment bound to the entire \(h\)-cube, of size \(2^h\). All nonzero fixed targets have the same omission probability by scaling the coefficients, and target zero is always attained. The union over all targets is therefore bounded by exactly \(q\) times the uniform-target omission probability. Hence \[\Pr\left[\{\textstyle\sum_i a_ix_i:x\in\{0,1\}^h\}\ne\mathbb F_q\right] \le q^4/2^{3h}+3q^3/2^{2h}+3q^2(3/8)^h.\tag{9}\] This is a probability estimate in the sampling space, not a reduction of real moments into the proof field.

A fixed half set and its rare bad rows. Fix \(S_0\subseteq[v]\) of size \(v/2\), and put \(r=\lceil29k/64\rceil\). Call a row bad if \(H_a=|X_a\setminus S_0|<r\). The complement count \(Y_a=k-H_a\) has hypergeometric law with half the population marked. Its moment generating function obeys, for \(u\ge0\), \[\mathbb E e^{uY_a}=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom kj(e^u-1)^j\frac{(v/2)_j}{(v)_j} \le\left(\frac{1+e^u}{2}\right)^k\le\exp(ku/2+ku^2/8).\tag{10}\] The first expansion selects marked positions in the sample; the falling-factorial ratio is at most \(2^{-j}\). The last bound follows from \(\cosh z\le e^{z^2/2}\), obtained by integrating \(\tanh z\le z\). Badness implies \(Y_a>35k/64\). Chernoff optimization at the deviation \(3k/64\) gives \[\Pr[a\text{ bad}]\le e^{-9k/2048}=e^{-9l/256}=:\eta.\tag{11}\] The expected number of bad rows is at most \(m\eta\), regardless of dependence among rows in a partition. Markov's inequality makes that number at most \(m\sqrt\eta\) with probability at least \(1-\sqrt\eta\).

All good rows extend all selected assignments. Conditional on the incidence scopes, the coefficients in \(X_a\setminus S_0\) are independent uniform field elements. For a good row use (9) with \(h\ge r\), and union over at most \(m\) rows. At \(q<2v^2\), the three terms have, apart from polynomial factors in \(l\), exponential factors \[2^{-15l/8},\qquad 2^{-l/4},\qquad \left[2^5(3/8)^{29/8}\right]^l.\tag{12}\] The last base is less than one because its eighth power is \(3^{29}/2^{47}<1\). Thus with probability \(1-o(1)\) every good row's free-coordinate subset sums cover all of \(\mathbb F_q\). This implies extension of every assignment on \(S_0\cap X_a\), whatever its central target or the coefficients of the pinned variables.

Delete from \(S_0\) every coordinate lying in a bad row, and call the result \(S\). At most \(km\sqrt\eta=5v e^{-9l/512}\) coordinates are removed. For a good row, any assignment on the smaller selected set extends: set other formerly selected variables arbitrarily and use the old free coordinates to meet its target. For a bad row, \(S\cap X_a=\varnothing\), and the previously proved nonemptiness of every central row suffices. Hence (5) holds and (8) follows. All events are combined by a union bound, with no conditioning on the nonzero-coefficient event and no independence claim among final events. \(\square\)
