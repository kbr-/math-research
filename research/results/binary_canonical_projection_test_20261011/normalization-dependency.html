Clause coefficient normalization

Lemma. Work over any field \(k\). Let \(F_i=\prod_{u=1}^{w_i}\ell_{iu}\), \(1\le i\le m\), be the violation polynomials of a literal CNF on \(n\) variables, where each \(\ell_{iu}\) is \(x_j\) or \(1-x_j\). Include every \(B_j=x_j^2-x_j\). Repeated literals, tautological clauses and empty clauses are permitted; an empty product is one. Set \(L=\sum_iw_i\), \(s=m+n+L+1\), and \[P=\prod_i(1-F_i),\qquad P_i=\prod_{k\ne i}(1-F_k),\qquad d_i=\prod_{k

Proof. For each clause construct a linear-placeholder polynomial \[T_i(x,Z)=\sum_{u=1}^{w_i} Z_{j(i,u)} \prod_{v<u}\ell_{iv}^2\prod_{v>u}\ell_{iv}.\] Both possible literals satisfy \(\ell_{iu}^2-\ell_{iu}=B_{j(i,u)}\), even if variables repeat. Product telescoping therefore gives the formal identity \[T_i(x,B)=F_i^2-F_i.\] For an empty clause the sum is zero and the identity remains valid. Write \(T_i=\sum_j t_{ij}Z_j\). Define, by substitution at the placeholders of the given circuit, \[Q(x,Z)=C\bigl(x,(-P_iT_i(x,Z))_{i=1}^m,(PZ_j)_{j=1}^n\bigr).\] This polynomial is linear in \(Z\). Upon substituting \(Z=B\), the replacement for \(Y_i\) becomes \[-P_i(F_i^2-F_i)=F_i(1-F_i)P_i=PF_i.\] The replacement for \(Z_j\) becomes \(PB_j\). Hence Hilbert-likeness and the given formal identity imply \[Q(x,B)=C(x,PF,PB)=P C(x,F,B)=P.\] Equivalently its Boolean coefficients are \(b'_j=Pb_j-\sum_i c_iP_i t_{ij}\). No Boolean term has been omitted or identified with zero as a formal polynomial.

For the converse, product telescoping gives \(\sum_i d_iF_i=1-P\) in the polynomial ring. Thus \[C'(x,Y,Z)=\sum_i d_iY_i+Q(x,Z)\] satisfies \(C'(x,F,B)=1\) and is Hilbert-like. Its difference from the original \(C\), when supplied, is a syzygy of the complete generator list, since both evaluate to one; no assertion that individual coefficient functions are invariant is used.

For complexity, construct all literals at depth one, every \(F_i\) at depth two, \(1-F_i\) at depth three, and all \(P,P_i,d_i\) by separate unbounded-fanin product gates at depth four. This uses \(O(L+m^2+n+1)\) wires. Each summand of \(T_i\) is one product of \(Z_{j(i,u)}\) and at most \(2w_i\) literal factors; squaring is represented by two input wires, not an extra arithmetic layer. Then every \(T_i\) has depth at most three, and all such circuits use \(O(\sum_iw_i^2+L+n+1)\) wires. The placeholder replacements have depth at most six, including signs. Grafting them to the original circuit adds only that constant to its depth, and reuses their outputs at repeated placeholder occurrences. The stated cubic size and ten-layer bounds are conservative. The converse adds the selector products, one product layer with placeholders and a final sum, giving the displayed bound. No expansion into monomials, division, or derivative extraction of the original circuit is used. \(\square\)

What changed and what did not

For a fixed CNF family, polynomial-size constant-depth Hilbert-like refutations exist if and only if polynomial-size constant-depth Boolean-ideal certificates for the explicitly small polynomial \(P\) exist, allowing the stated additive depth overhead. For a fixed exact depth the overhead must still be charged. A lower bound for every such Boolean correction would therefore suffice through the matching simulation. This is a stronger target than excluding only its exact simulation image, and no equivalence of that image under normalization is claimed.

Even when the CNF is unsatisfiable, \(P\) generally is not the zero formal polynomial. On the Boolean cube it is zero. The formal circuit identity \(P=\sum_j b'_j B_j\) is precisely the remaining task; neither its semantic truth nor the fact that \(P\) itself has a small circuit supplies small circuits for \(b'_j\). For the inconsistent units \(F_1=x,F_2=1-x\), \(P=x(1-x)=-B_1\), so \(Q=-Z_1\) is a small positive control in every characteristic. This example is not a candidate hard family. The finite-jet construction already warns that bounded-order data cannot replace a full formal identity.

Coefficient cube traces

Corollary. For every formal identity \(P=\sum_jb_jB_j\) over any field and every Boolean point \(a\), the complete vector of coefficient values is forced: \[b_j(a)=(2a_j-1)\partial_jP(a).\] For the satisfaction product above these functions have polynomial representatives \(r_j=(2x_j-1)\partial_jP\) jointly computable in size \(O(s^3)\) and depth at most eight. The representatives need not supply a formal certificate.

Proof. Differentiate the identity in \(x_j\) and evaluate at \(a\). All terms involving a factor \(B_i(a)\) vanish, leaving \(\partial_jP(a)=(2a_j-1)b_j(a)\). The scalar \(2a_j-1\) squares to one, including in characteristic two. The same calculation for any syzygy \(\sum_i h_iB_i=0\) gives \(h_j(a)=0\), explicitly checking invariance under all coefficient changes. For complexity use \[\partial_jP=-\sum_iP_i\partial_jF_i,\qquad \partial_jF_i=\sum_{u:j(i,u)=j}\epsilon_{iu}\prod_{v\ne u}\ell_{iv},\] where \(\epsilon_{iu}=1\) or \(-1\) is the literal derivative. Products with repeated variables are differentiated by occurrences. The product-rule circuits have shared size \(O(s^3)\), depth at most six before multiplying by \(2x_j-1\), hence the conservative bound eight. No differentiation of an arbitrary certificate circuit is needed. For duplicated inconsistent units, take \(F_1=F_2=x\), \(F_3=F_4=1-x\). Then \(P=x^2(1-x)^2=B_1^2\), whose coefficient \(b_1=B_1\) and first derivative both vanish on the Boolean cube, although \(P\ne0\) formally. This is also the square case of the earlier finite-jet warning. \(\square\)

Triangular satisfaction projection

Lemma. Let \(H(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\) be the satisfaction product of any literal CNF, including a satisfiable one. Append the inconsistent unit clauses on a fresh variable \(z\). The resulting unsatisfiable CNF has satisfaction product \(P=-B_zH\) and the explicit small constant-depth Boolean-ideal certificate \(Q=-HZ_z\). Successive monic division of \(P\) by \(B_1,\ldots,B_r,B_z\), retaining each quotient and passing only the remainder to the next division, has last coefficient \(-\operatorname{ML}(H)\), where \(\operatorname{ML}\) is the unique multilinear polynomial agreeing with \(H\) on the Boolean cube.

Proof. The two unit-clause satisfaction factors are \((1-z)z=-B_z\). For each of the first \(r\) divisions, the independent factor \(-B_z\) passes through polynomial division unchanged. After these divisions the remainder is \(-B_z\operatorname{ML}(H)\); division by \(B_z\) gives the claimed last quotient and zero remainder. Existence and uniqueness of \(\operatorname{ML}\) follow from univariate monic division and induction on the Boolean grid, as in the historical field-reduction proof, read here in full. The input certificate has the written product circuit for \(H\) followed by one placeholder multiplication. Thus efficient universal extraction of the last canonical coefficient, even restricted to satisfaction products with supplied small certificates, would efficiently multilinearize every CNF satisfaction product. This is an implication, not an unconditional lower bound on that operation. A difficult canonical coefficient alone cannot prove certificate hardness: these very instances have the explicit cheap certificate. \(\square\)
