The exact rank defect

Lemma (backward rank defect). On a finite-dimensional vector space with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear or Hermitian form, let \(A^*\) denote the adjoint of \(A\). Then \[\operatorname{rank}A=\operatorname{rank}(AA^*)+\kappa(A),\qquad \kappa(A)=\dim(\operatorname{im}A^*\cap\ker A).\tag{1}\] Let \(H,P,Q\) be self-adjoint idempotents, put \(A=HQP\), and let \(F\) be any endomorphism. Let \(E_0+E_1=I\) be complementary idempotents commuting with \(Q-F\). Then \[\operatorname{rank}A\le\operatorname{rank}(HFP)+\operatorname{rank}((Q-F)E_0)+\operatorname{rank}(E_1PQ)+\kappa(A).\tag{2}\] No positivity is assumed or concluded. Idempotence of \(H,Q,E_0,E_1\) is retained for the projection application; the proof only needs their stated adjoint, sum and commutation identities, together with \(P^2=P\).

Proof. Restrict \(A\) to \(\operatorname{im}A^*\). Its kernel is the intersection in (1), and its image is \(\operatorname{im}AA^*\); \(\operatorname{rank}A^*=\operatorname{rank}A\). This proves (1). Since \(P^2=P\), \(AA^*=HQPQH\). Write this as \(HFPQH+H(Q-F)PQH\), apply rank subadditivity, and remove outer factors to bound by \(\operatorname{rank}(HFP)+\operatorname{rank}((Q-F)PQ)\). Split the latter using \(E_0+E_1\); commutation bounds the first part by \(\operatorname{rank}((Q-F)E_0)\), the second by \(\operatorname{rank}(E_1PQ)\). Add (1). If \(Q=UQ_0U^{-1}\) and \(E_1=UD_1U^{-1}\), the last rank is exactly that of \(D_1U^{-1}PUQ_0\), by conjugation. This is the algebraic exchange of roles; it gives no bound on that reverse transition. \(\square\)

Unitary control at every size. Over \(\mathbb F_4\), with standard Hermitian adjoint and \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), take \[U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&\omega&\omega^2\\1&\omega^2&\omega\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0),\quad P=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0),\quad H=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1).\] Distinct columns have Hermitian inner product zero and each has norm one, so \(UU^*=I\). Thus \(Q=UQ_0U^*\) is the all-ones matrix, a self-adjoint idempotent, with \(HP=0\). The only nonzero row of \(A=HQP\) is \((1,1,0)\); it has rank one but \(AA^*=0\). Direct sums of \(r\ge1\) copies give rank \(r\) and squared rank zero. Adding idle coordinates embeds the example in any sufficiently large power-of-two dimension. This is a finite-field algebraic control, not a claimed realization by the paper's complex gate class or the PC compiler. No change of form can both keep these fixed \(H,P,Q\) self-adjoint and remove their defect: the literal product \(HQPQH=0\) is independent of the form.

An existing pairing tool prevents an overstrong negative conclusion

Lemma (finite family pairing). Let \(J_2\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a proper degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity. Fix \(T\ge1\), \(2T\le D\), \(q\ge1\), and let \(Q_T=\mathcal P_{\le T}/(J_2\cap\mathcal P_{\le T})\). For a finite collection of binary subspaces \(W_\alpha\subseteq Q_T^q\), there exist functionals \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_q\) annihilating \(J_2\) over a finite extension \(K/\mathbb F_2\), with \(\mu_1(1)=1\), such that \[B_\mu(f,g)=\sum_{i=1}^q\mu_i(f_i g_i)\tag{3}\] is nonsingular on every \(K\otimes W_\alpha\). Any extension with size greater than \(1+\sum_\alpha\dim W_\alpha\) suffices. No source coefficient domain is enlarged. Zero subspaces require no determinant.

Proof. Bounded absorption through \(2T\le D\) makes (3) independent of representatives. Annihilators of \(J_2\) restrict surjectively onto \(Q_T^*\): extend a functional on \(\mathcal P_{\le T}+J_2\) to the degree-\(D\) space. Hence independent choices of the \(\mu_i\) allow arbitrary linear values on \(Q_T^q\).

For a binary basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\) of one \(W\), each coordinate obeys \(e_{ji}^2-e_{ji}\in J_2\) through \(2T\). The Gram diagonal is therefore \(L_j=\sum_i\mu_i(e_{ji})\). These are independent linear coordinate functions of the unrestricted annihilator parameters, by basis independence and the preceding surjection. Choose them as the first \(d\) coordinates. Every Gram entry is linear in those and the remaining parameters. In characteristic two, determinant terms with a cycle of length at least three cancel in reversed-cycle pairs. A nonidentity involution has a transposed index, loses its diagonal variable, and its squared off-diagonal factors cannot restore an exponent one in that variable. Thus the coefficient of \(L_1\cdots L_d\), with no other parameters, is one. Each restricted determinant is nonzero of degree \(d\).

The product of these finitely many determinants and the nonzero linear form \(\mu_1(1)\) is nonzero; properness ensures that this last form is available. Its degree is at most \(1+\sum_\alpha\dim W_\alpha\), so the usual finite-grid induction gives a nonvanishing specialization over the stated field. Divide all functionals by the common nonzero value \(\mu_1(1)\). This normalizes the first functional and scales all Gram matrices without changing their ranks. \(\square\)

Rank repair consequence. For a fixed finite family of binary linear maps between fixed binary tuple subspaces, apply the lemma separately to each object over one common sufficiently large finite extension, including that object and every outgoing map's kernel in its family. All ambient forms and these kernel restrictions are nonsingular. Define adjoints using these forms. Then \(\operatorname{im}A^*=(\ker A)^\perp\) and its intersection with \(\ker A\) is zero; hence every map has \(\operatorname{rank}AA^*=\operatorname{rank}A\). Hermitian versions are also available: pass from \(K\) to its quadratic extension and conjugate the second argument in (3). The Gram matrices remain the same nonsingular matrices over \(K\), and the extended binary subspaces have the same determinants. This is a finite-family linear-algebra repair, not simultaneous satisfaction of prescribed self-adjointness, inverse-closure or source equations. The projection control proves that imposing all those extra identities can conflict with the repair.

A repaired adjoint can still be expensive

Proposition (adjoint degree loss). For every \(N\ge1\), in the full Boolean function algebra on \(N\) bits there is a nondegenerate normalized characteristic-two moment pairing for which the degree-preserving map \(Sf=f(0)1\) has nonsingular kernel restriction and \(\operatorname{rank}SS^*=\operatorname{rank}S=1\), but \(S^*1\) has exact multilinear degree \(N\).

Proof. Assign nonzero weights \(m_x\) to all Boolean points with \(\sum_xm_x=1\), and let \(\lambda(f)=\sum_xm_xf(x)\). Such weights exist over \(\mathbb F_4\): give the origin weight \(\omega\), one other point weight \(\omega^2\), and all remaining points weight one. Their even remaining count contributes zero. The pairing \(B(f,g)=\lambda(fg)\) is diagonal and nonsingular in the point-indicator basis; its restriction to \(\ker S=\{f:f(0)=0\}\) is also nonsingular. Its adjoint satisfies \[S^*g=\frac{\lambda(g)}{m_0}\,\mathbf1_{\{0\}},\qquad SS^*g=\frac{\lambda(g)}{m_0}\,1.\] Indeed pairing the first expression with \(f\) gives \(f(0)\lambda(g)=B(Sf,g)\). Both maps have rank one, while normalization gives \(S^*1=m_0^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^N(1-x_j)\), of exact degree \(N\) by multilinear uniqueness. This is also the recorded localization support cost after complementing coordinates. A Hermitian version follows by keeping the weights in \(\mathbb F_4\) and extending to \(\mathbb F_{16}\) with its conjugation fixing that subfield. The displayed adjoint expression still holds, with the pairing conjugate-linear in the second argument. \(\square\)

Thus even an algebraically supplied dual map can change the polynomial degree by an unbounded amount. This does not rule out a costed adjoint for a special source. The degree loss is not avoided by a free square dilation: adjoining a formal dual register changes the state space, and one must still map it back to a nonzero old weight with a paid degree. Ordinary transpose preserves rank, but simply transposing \(HQP\) gives \(PQH\), not the extra \(Q\) that the squared-error localization step needs.

Count filters and the remaining interface

A literal finite-field count \(C=\sum_j e_j\) also loses the integer spectrum. In characteristic two, zero and two violations have the same scalar value, so no univariate polynomial of that count can distinguish them. Using a weighted count in a larger extension can separate some collisions, but an exact polynomial zero-test for \(N\) independent Boolean violations still has degree at least \(N\): its multilinear representative is \(\prod_j(1-e_j)\), whereas a degree-\(k\) polynomial of any affine weighted count has multilinear degree at most \(k\). This is an application of the already proved support-cost lemma, not a new zero-test theorem. Neither argument forbids approximate or low-rank-error filters. Those require the missing quantitative estimate; small real coefficients do not themselves bound finite-field rank.

Three alternatives have therefore been examined after reading the source proof: direct Hermitian squaring has a precisely measured radical loss; flexible moment pairings can repair rank but do not preserve all source operator identities; keeping primal and dual registers separate avoids positivity but still requires a costed map back to the old polynomial domain. Integer grading can retain counts as external labels, but supplies neither the source's real polynomial approximation nor a finite-field error-rank bound. The original finite exponent chain is not the obstructed part.
