Quadratic separated-family kernels

Statement. Corollary (quadratic separated-family kernels). Over any field, let \(N\ge8\), \(m\ge4\), \(K=2\) and \(4\le D\le\lceil N/2\rceil\). Then (a) each error of the separated family has \(\operatorname{Ann}_C(E_e)\cap V_2=A_e+C\cap V_2\); (b) writing \(\rho_e(u)=u_0+x_\alpha u_\alpha+x_\beta u_\beta+\gamma x_\alpha x_\beta\) with \(u_0\) free of \(x_\alpha,x_\beta\) and \(u_\alpha=s_\alpha+\sum_gr_{\alpha g}x_g\), \(u_\beta=s_\beta+\sum_gr_{\beta g}x_g\) linear and free of both, \(u\in A_e\) exactly when \(u_0=0\) and: in characteristic \(2\), \(s_\alpha+s_\beta+\gamma=0\) and \(r_{\alpha g}=r_{\beta g}\) for every edge \(g\) sharing no pigeon or hole with \(e\), \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\); in characteristic \(3\), \(u_\alpha=u_\beta=0\); in every other characteristic, \(\rho_e(u)=0\); (c) when \(m\ge5\), or \(m=4\) in a characteristic other than \(2\) and \(3\), the common kernel in \(V_2\) of the separated family is \((C\cap V_2)\oplus\operatorname{span}\{q_1q_2\}\) in characteristic \(2\), and \(C\cap V_2\) in every other characteristic; (d) in characteristic \(3\) with \(m=4\), \(x_{00}x_{11}\) kills every error of the separated family and lies outside \(C\), so (c) fails there.

Proof. (a) By lem:polynomial-numbering-transfer with \(\mathcal E=\{E_e\}\) (errors of degree \(2=D-K\) at most), it suffices to number holes and pigeons so that for each matching monomial \(x_{E'}\) of \(E_e\), namely \(x_e,x_ex_\alpha,x_ex_\beta\), and each matching \(t\) of size at most \(2\) with a dance and \(t\cup E'\) a matching, \(t\cup E'\) has a dance. Rank the hole \(j\) of \(e\) at \(0\) and the holes of \(\alpha,\beta\) at \(1,2\); the pigeon order is arbitrary. The holes of \(t\cup E'\) are at most two of ranks \(0,1,2\) from \(E'\) and at most two of \(t\), none at the top rank \(N-1\), since a pigeon there cannot move up and \(t\) has a dance. lem:ballot-dance-maps gives a dance in every pigeon order when every top segment from rank \(r\) holds at most \((N-r)/2\) of these holes. From rank \(0\): at most \(4\le N/2\); from rank \(1\): at most \(3\le(N-1)/2\); from rank \(2\): at most \(3\le(N-2)/2\); from rank \(r\ge3\) only \(t\)'s holes count, one needs \(r\le N-2\) and two need \(r\le N-4\). So the condition fails only when \(t\)'s two holes lie in ranks \(\ge N-3\) other than the top, that is at ranks \(\{N-3,N-2\}\). Such a \(t=\{(p,N-2),(p',N-3)\}\) has a dance only when \(p\) precedes \(p'\): if \(p'\) moved first its only free hole above is \(N-1\), and then \(p\) has none. For it, \(t\cup E'\) has this dance: at its turn each pigeon of \(E'\), at rank at most \(2\), moves to a hole of rank in \([3,N-4]\) not yet taken, of which there are \(N-6\ge2\), enough for its at most two pigeons; \(p\) moves to \(N-1\), which no pigeon of \(E'\) takes; \(p'\), after \(p\), moves to the vacated \(N-2\). Every move goes up into a free hole. So the property holds and (a) follows, with \(C\cap V_2\) as in the Setting. (b) \(uE_e\in H\) exactly when \(\rho_e(u)(1+x_\alpha+x_\beta)\equiv0\) modulo the hole and functionality axioms on the edges sharing no pigeon or hole with \(e\), since \(x_e\) times a polynomial in those edges lies in \(H\) only when the polynomial does. A polynomial of degree at most \(4\) in the matching ring vanishes there exactly when it vanishes at every matching \(\sigma\) of size at most \(4\) (the matching monomials are triangular against these evaluations), so the condition is \(\rho_e(u)(\sigma)(1+q(\sigma))=0\) for every matching \(\sigma\) of those edges, with \(q(\sigma)\in\{0,1,2\}\) the number of \(\alpha,\beta\) in \(\sigma\). On \(\sigma\) avoiding both, \(1+q=1\): \(u_0\) vanishes on every matching avoiding \(\alpha,\beta\), so \(u_0=0\). On \(\sigma\) with exactly one, \(1+q=2\): no condition in characteristic \(2\); otherwise \(u_\alpha\) vanishes on every matching sharing nothing with \(\alpha\) and avoiding \(\beta\), so \(u_\alpha=0\), and likewise \(u_\beta=0\). On \(\sigma\supseteq\{\alpha,\beta\}\), \(1+q=3\): no condition in characteristic \(3\); otherwise \(\gamma+u_\alpha+u_\beta\) vanishes on every matching sharing nothing with \(e,\alpha,\beta\), which in characteristic \(2\) is \(s_\alpha+s_\beta+\gamma=0\) and \(r_{\alpha g}=r_{\beta g}\) on those edges, and in the remaining characteristics, where \(u_\alpha=u_\beta=0\), is \(\gamma=0\). In the coordinates of \(u\), \(r_{\alpha g}=b_{\alpha g}\), \(s_\alpha=a_\alpha+b_{e\alpha}\), \(\gamma=b_{\alpha\beta}\), and \(u_0=0\) says \(c+a_e=0\), \(a_g+b_{eg}=0\) and \(b_{gg'}=0\) for edges \(g,g'\ne\alpha,\beta\) sharing no pigeon or hole with \(e\). (c) \(C\cap V_2\) lies in every kernel, since \(D\ge4\); in characteristic \(2\), \(q_1q_2E_e=0\) for every error and \(q_1q_2\notin C\) (cor:separated-family-kernels (d)). Let \(u\) lie in the common kernel. By (a) and the Setting, for each error \(e\) there is \(w_e\in W_2\) with \(u-w_e\in A_e\). The form of \(W_2\). From \(Q_i\) and \(x_fQ_i=x_f-\sum_{h\ne h_f}x_fx_{ih}\) (\(h_f\) the hole of \(f\)), an element \(\sum_i\lambda_iQ_i+\sum\mu_{i,f}x_fQ_i\) has \(b_{gg'}=\nu(g,k')+\nu(g',k)\) for \(g\) in row \(k\) and \(g'\) in row \(k'\), with \(\nu(g,k')=-\mu_{k',g}\). For two rows \(k\ne k'\), write \(b_{kk'}(h,h')=b_{(k,h),(k',h')}\) for \(h\ne h'\); it is separable on a set \(P\) of such pairs when \(b_{kk'}=F(h)+G(h')\) there. Conversely any choice of separable \(b_{kk'}\), one per pair of rows, is the quadratic part of an element of \(W_2\): put \(\mu_{k',(k,h)}=-F(h)\) and \(\mu_{k,(k',h')}=-G(h')\). On a set \(P\) whose graph, holes \(h\) of row \(k\) joined to holes \(h'\) of row \(k'\) for \((h,h')\in P\), is connected, \(F\) and \(G\) are unique up to \(F+t\), \(G-t\). The graph of all pairs \(h\ne h'\) with \(h\in L\), \(h'\in R\) is connected when \(|L|,|R|\ge3\). Gluing. If \(b_{kk'}\) is separable on \(P_j=\{(h,h'):h,h'\ne j\}\cap P\) for each \(j\) in a set \(S\) of at least three holes, each \(P_j\) and each \(P_j\cap P_{j'}\) having connected graph on the holes they use, and each \(P_j\cap P_{j'}\cap P_{j''}\) nonempty, then \(b_{kk'}\) is separable on \(\bigcup_jP_j\): fix \(j_0\in S\) and shift each \(F_j,G_j\) by a constant so that they agree with \(F_{j_0},G_{j_0}\) on the holes of \(P_j\cap P_{j_0}\); by uniqueness on \(P_j\cap P_{j'}\), \(F_j-F_{j'}\) is a constant there, which vanishes at a pair of \(P_j\cap P_{j'}\cap P_{j_0}\), where both agree with \(F_{j_0}\), so any two agree on the holes both use (in every use below a pair avoiding three given holes exists, \(N\ge5\)), so \(F,G\) are well defined and \(b_{kk'}=F+G\) on each \(P_j\). Every pair \((h,h')\) of \(P\) avoids some \(j\in S\), so \(\bigcup_jP_j=P\). Step 1: the quadratic part. For an error \(e=(i,j)\) of family \(\nu\) and rows \(k,k'\ne i\), \(u-w_e\in A_e\) gives \(b^u_{gg'}=b^{w_e}_{gg'}\) for all pairs \(g=(k,h)\), \(g'=(k',h')\) with \(h,h'\ne j\) and \(g,g'\notin\{\alpha_\nu,\beta_\nu\}\), so \(b^u_{kk'}\) is separable there. For each pair of rows: if it misses \(\{0,1\}\), family \(1\) with a row \(i\in P_1\setminus\{k,k'\}\) (one exists at \(m\ge5\), \(|P_1|=m-2\ge3\); for \(m=4\) see the paragraph on \(m=4\) below) and \(j\) over \(J_1\) has no excluded edge in rows \(k,k'\), and gluing over \(S=J_1\) (\(|J_1|=N-2\ge3\); the graphs on \(N-1\) and \(N-2\) holes a side are connected) makes \(b^u_{kk'}\) separable on all pairs; if it misses \(\{2,3\}\), family \(2\) likewise, with \(i\in P_2\setminus\{k,k'\}\), which exists since \(m\ge5\). In characteristic other than \(2\) the excluded edges impose nothing, since by (b) \(u_\alpha=u_\beta=0\) gives \(b_{\alpha g}\) and \(b_{\beta g}\) too; in characteristic \(3\) only the one pair \(\{\alpha_\nu,\beta_\nu\}\), in rows \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{2,3\}\), is left, which the other family covers, and in the others nothing is left; so every pair of rows is separable on all pairs. In characteristic \(2\) the remaining pairs of rows are \(\{k,k'\}\) with \(k\in\{0,1\}\), \(k'\in\{2,3\}\). Family \(1\) with \(i\in P_1\setminus\{k'\}\) makes \(b^u_{kk'}\) separable on the pairs with \((k,h)\ne\alpha_1,\beta_1\), that is \(h\ne k\); family \(2\) with \(i\in P_2\setminus\{k\}\) on the pairs with \(h'\ne k'\); these two sets of pairs meet in a set with connected graph (holes \(h\ne k\) against \(h'\ne k'\)), so the glued \(F,G\) agree and \(b^u_{kk'}=F+G\) except possibly at the one pair \((h,h')=(k,k')\), the cell \(x_{kk}x_{k'k'}\). Taking the element \(w\in W_2\) with these separable parts, \(u_1=u-w\) lies in the common kernel and has \(b^{u_1}=0\) except, in characteristic \(2\), \(b^{u_1}=s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\) on the cells \(x_{00}x_{22},x_{00}x_{33},x_{11}x_{22},x_{11}x_{33}\), the monomials of \(q_1q_2\).
