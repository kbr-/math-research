ATTEMPT, made before the test was written. In Turan's setting a zero rho = beta + i gamma contributes to the
sum over (y, 2y] of Lambda(n) n^(-i tau) a term c_rho y^(rho - i tau), c_rho = -(2^(rho - i tau) - 1)/(rho - i tau),
by the classical explicit formula (recalled; the record's lemma is for the Riesz mean). The premise allows
y/tau. A zero with beta > 1/2 exceeds it for tau^2 <= y < tau^(1/(1 - beta)): in u = log y a window of length
R = (1/(1 - beta) - 2) log tau, which is about 4 (beta - 1/2) log tau near the line. So the premise is a bound
for an exponential sum in u with frequencies gamma - tau and amplitudes |c_rho| e^(beta u) on a window of
length R. If the off-line frequencies in play are separated by g > 2 pi/R, a mean-value inequality of Ingham's
type on the window bounds the sum of squares of the amplitudes by the mean square (recalled; the record's
thm:resonance-free-bandwidth is a statement of this kind with a longer window, 4M^2/(g m^2)). It stops at two
places: frequencies closer than 2 pi/R, where the record's power-sum bounds lose a factor exponential in the
number of points; and the zeros at other heights, whose amplitudes decay only like 1/|gamma - tau|.
