Bounded phases and the canonical minimizer

Lemma (input phase growth). Let \(J_2\subseteq\mathbb F_2[x]_{\le D}\) be a degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing Booleanity, and put \(J_E=E\otimes J_2\) for \(E=\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2(\omega)\), \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). Let binary polynomials \(g_i\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\). Let \(0\ne Y=E\otimes Y_2\) be a binary-defined subspace of finite tuples of quotient classes, with coordinate representatives through degree \(d_0\). For \(\ell\ge1\), assume \(d_0+2\ell\delta\le D\). Write \(\mathcal B X=X+\sum_i g_iX\) and \[\rho=\min_{0\ne X\subseteq Y}\frac{\dim\mathcal B X}{\dim X}.\] There is a unique largest minimizing subspace \(X_*\). It is binary-defined, the minimum equals the binary minimum, and \(\dim\mathcal B^\ell X_*\le\rho^\ell\dim X_*\). Products here act only in the displayed degree window.

Proof: bounded inverses. Put \(u_i=1+\omega g_i\), \(v_i=1+\omega^2g_i\). Their product is \(1+g_i+g_i^2\), equal to one modulo the Boolean equations through degree \(2\delta\). Indeed squaring a binary polynomial termwise and reducing repeated old variables proves \(g_i^2-g_i\) at that ceiling. The span of the identity and the \(u_i\) is exactly the span of the identity and the \(g_i\). A word of \(r\) phases and its reversed inverse both have degree at most \(r\delta\); telescoping their identities costs at most \(2r\delta\). Thus multiplication by such a word is injective on classes represented through \(e\) when \(e+2r\delta\le D\). This is a bounded assertion, not a global quotient algebra. The phases are analysis operators, not source coefficients.

Proof: magnification. The dimension ratios have a finite set of possible values, so a minimizer exists. For any minimizer \(X\) and a list of phase words \(c_j\) of length at most \(\ell-1\), put \(T_j=\sum_{i\le j}c_iX\) and \(Z_j=\{x\in X:c_jx\in T_{j-1}\}\). Injection on \(X\) gives \(\dim T_j-\dim T_{j-1}=\dim X-\dim Z_j\). Multiplying the relations defining \(Z_j\) by \(\mathcal B\) gives \(c_j\mathcal B Z_j\subseteq\mathcal B T_{j-1}\), and injection on \(\mathcal B X\) gives \[\dim\mathcal B T_j-\dim\mathcal B T_{j-1}\le\dim\mathcal B X-\dim\mathcal B Z_j\le\rho(\dim X-\dim Z_j).\] The last inequality is minimality, also when \(Z_j=0\). Injection costs at most \(d_0+\delta+2(\ell-1)\delta\); relation propagation costs at most \(d_0+\ell\delta\), both covered by the stated reserve. Summing and listing words spanning \(\mathcal B^{r-1}\) yields \(\dim\mathcal B^rX\le\rho\dim\mathcal B^{r-1}X\) for \(r\le\ell\), proving the power bound.

Proof: binary descent. Put \(f(X)=\dim\mathcal B X\), with \(f(0)=0\). Since \(\mathcal B(X+Z)=\mathcal B X+\mathcal B Z\) and \(\mathcal B(X\cap Z)\subseteq\mathcal B X\cap\mathcal B Z\), \[f(X+Z)+f(X\cap Z)\le f(X)+f(Z).\] For two minimizers, the lower bounds \(f(W)\ge\rho\dim W\), together with the dimension formula, force their sum and every nonzero intersection to be minimizers. A finite sum spanning the sum of all minimizers is therefore the unique largest one \(X_*\). Coefficient Frobenius \(\sigma\) preserves \(Y\) and every binary input map, hence carries minimizers to minimizers; uniqueness gives \(\sigma X_*=X_*\). In binary coordinates of the ambient quotient, write \(v=A+\omega B\). Then \(B=v+\sigma v\) and \(A=v+\omega B\) both belong to \(X_*\) and are binary. These components span \(X_*\) over \(E\). Scalar extension preserves their dimensions and images, proving descent and equality of minima. An arbitrary individual minimizer need not be stable. The restriction \(\delta\ge1\) is retained for compatibility with partial-image contraction; the phase lemma itself also works at \(\delta=0\). \(\square\)

What a coherent preimage buys

Corollary (small growth removal). Work in the binary setting above. Fix a binary weight \(w\), prefix \(P\), and \(A=wP\), with the safe charge \(a=\deg w+\deg P\). Let \(U_0\subseteq V_K\) be a binary subspace preserving all earlier obligations, and let \[T f=([Ag_if])_i,\quad Y_2=T(U_0)\ne0,\quad a+K+3\delta\le D.\] For its canonical minimizer \(X_2\), put \(c=\dim X_2\), \(U_X=T^{-1}(X_2)\subseteq U_0\), and \(H_X=\operatorname{rank}(f\in U_X\mapsto[wf])\). If its growth ratio satisfies \(\rho<3/2\), there are a binary row \(q=\sum_i t_i g_i\) and \(f\in U_X\) with \[wf\notin J_2,\qquad A(1-q)g_i f\in J_2\quad\hbox{for all }i.\] More precisely the new error rank on \(U_X\) is at most \(\min\{c,(\rho-1/2)c\}\), and its weighted common kernel has dimension at least \(H_X-c'\). No bound on \(H_X/H_0\), where \(H_0=\operatorname{rank}(wU_0)\), is asserted.

Proof. Apply the lemma with \(d_0=a+\delta+K\), \(\ell=1\). Binary descent gives a binary preimage without increasing \(K\). Absorption gives \(\ker(w|_{U_X})\subseteq\ker(T|_{U_X})\), so \(H_X\ge c\). The generic multiplied image modulo \(X_2\) lies in \(\mathcal B X_2/X_2\), of dimension \((\rho-1)c\). The partial-image lemma, at exactly the displayed \(a+K+3\delta\) ceiling, therefore gives \(c'\le\min(c,c/2+(\rho-1)c)\). For \(\rho<3/2\), this is strictly less than \(c\le H_X\). The new map also factors through the weight image by absorption, so its kernel contains a nonzero weighted class. Choose a binary representative. Membership in \(U_0\) preserves earlier constraints; any full source replay must separately pay the original product and coefficient-field costs. No smaller ordinary NS certificate follows. \(\square\)

Every useful low-growth space can be tiny

Proposition (small growth loss). For every integer \(v\ge4\), there is a satisfiable binary Boolean base, two inputs of degree at most four and the full degree-one multiplier space of dimension \(v+6\), such that every nonzero input-image subspace of growth below \(3/2\) has dimension one. Its canonical minimizer has weighted preimage rank one. The tuple has no affine unweighted one-row normalizer but has a degree-two coefficient normalizer.

Construction. Use variables \(a,x_1,\ldots,x_v,z_1,\ldots,z_4\), Booleanity and the equations \(ax_i=az_j=0\). Their solution set is the full \((v+4)\)-cube at \(a=0\), together with the one point \(a=1,x=z=0\). Work in its function algebra, or the vanishing pseudoideal through \(D\ge\max(v+5,18)\). Put \[Q=x_1x_2+x_3x_4,\quad H=z_1z_2+z_3z_4,\quad g_1=Q,\quad g_2=1+QH.\] Take \(w=P=1,K=1,U_0=V_1\) restricted to this set. The affine functions have dimension \(v+6\). Since \(Hg_1+g_2=1\) literally, the tuple map \(Tf=(g_1f,g_2f)\) is injective on the whole function algebra. Its degree-one image \(Y\) thus has dimension \(v+6\); \(d_0=5,\delta=4\) fit the phase reserve. On the anchor line \(\langle a\rangle\), multiplication by \(g_1,g_2\) is respectively zero and identity, giving growth one.

Proof of uniqueness below the threshold. On the main cube, no nonzero affine function \(L\) has \(QL\) affine. The coefficients of \(x_1x_3x_4,x_2x_3x_4,x_1x_2x_3,x_1x_2x_4\) force the coefficients of \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) in \(L\) to vanish. For each other variable, its products with \(x_1x_2\) and \(x_3x_4\) give distinct cubic monomials, forcing its coefficient to vanish. The remaining constant multiple of the nonzero quadratic \(Q\) is affine only when that constant is zero. This coefficient argument also holds over \(\mathbb F_4\).

Identify any \(X\subseteq Y\) with \(Z=T^{-1}X\subseteq U_0\), using global injectivity to compare products. Projection of \(Z\) to main-cube affine functions has rank at least \(\dim Z-1\), its kernel being the anchor line. Multiplication by \(Q\) is injective on that projection by the preceding argument, and \(QZ\cap Z=0\): equality would make a main-cube product affine, hence zero; it is also zero at the anchor. Therefore \[\dim(X+g_1X)\ge2\dim X-1.\] For \(\dim X\ge2\), the growth is at least \(3/2\). For a nonanchor line, the main projection has rank one, giving growth at least two. The only nonzero subspace with growth below \(3/2\) is consequently \(T\langle a\rangle\). Its preimage and weighted rank are one, while the starting rank is \(v+6\).

Alternative-map control. Because the inputs never vanish together, an unweighted accuracy-one affine-coefficient normalizer would satisfy \(\alpha Q+\beta(1+QH)=1\) as a function, with affine \(\alpha,\beta\). On \(Q=0\), this forces \(\beta=1\). That main-cube locus contains the zero vector and every coordinate unit vector, so all main-cube affine coefficients of \(\beta-1\) vanish; the anchor forces its remaining coefficient to vanish. Thus \(\beta=1\) on the full domain. Fix an \(x\) with \(Q=1\) and let all \(z\)'s vary. The identity then forces the affine \(\alpha\) to equal the genuinely quadratic \(H\), impossible. In contrast \((\alpha,\beta)=(H,1)\) is a literal degree-two solution. Its prefix is zero; its coefficient Boolean equation follows through degree four. Explicitly, the complete accuracy-one source has \(P=1-r_1Q-r_2(1+QH)\), companions \(QP,(1+QH)P\), and fresh equations \(r_1^2-r_1,r_2^2-r_2\). Their original ceilings are respectively seven, nine and two, with product ceiling five. Thus the safe degree-two substitution ceiling eighteen pays all original costs. This example does not obstruct other maps, other multiplier strategies, or the actual hard family. \(\square\)
