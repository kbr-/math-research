Affine union coded bound

Work in the binary local function algebra on \(m\) three-state edge pairs, with the ordinary filtration \(V_K\) and dimension \(B_m(K)=\sum_{j\le K}2^j\binom mj\). Let \(q\ge0\) features \(z_i\) be indicators of affine zero flats on the old binary coordinates. Their defining equations have independent homogeneous parts within each feature. No support disjointness is assumed. For \(S\subseteq[q]\), let \(R_S\) be the rank of the union of those homogeneous direction spaces; \(R_\varnothing=0\). If the affine equations for \(S\) are inconsistent, its contribution in every sum below may be omitted.

Actual input polynomials \(g_j\) of degree at most \(\delta\ge1\) have local function values \(\ell_j+F_j(z)\), where \(\ell_j\) is homogeneous old-linear and \(F_j\) is any supplied Boolean feature function, including constants. Fix integers \(1\le K<m/2\), \(s,H\ge1\), and put \[a=1-\frac K{2m},\qquad t=s\lceil\log_2(q+1)\rceil+1,\] \[L_s=\sum_{|S|>s}a^{R_S},\qquad U_H=\sum_{S\subseteq[q]}2^{|S|}a^{\max(H,R_S)}.\tag{1}\]

Theorem. Some at most \(H+t\) constant binary rows in the actual input positions give a product \(P\) whose stacked companion map satisfies \[\operatorname{rank}(f\in V_K\mapsto(Pg_jf)_j)\le (m+1)B_m(K)(L_s+U_H).\tag{2}\] If a degree-\(D\) binary PC pseudoideal contains the local equations, \(\deg w\le b\), and \(b+K+(H+t+1)\delta\le D\), the weighted quotient map has at most this rank. Its common-kernel comparison must use the actual dimension \(\dim[wV_K]\).

One local flat count. Put \(\theta=K/[2(m-K)]\). For any affine zero flat of homogeneous rank \(R\), its indicator \(I\) has multiplication rank on \(V_K\) at most \[(m+1)B_m(K)a^R.\tag{3}\] Indeed the local pivot count is bounded at \(\theta\) by \((1+2\theta)^m a^R\), since deleting one indicator costs \(a=(1+\theta)/(1+2\theta)\) and deleting both costs at most \(a^2\). A coefficient sum through \(K\) is at most \(\theta^{-K}\) times that value, and the binomial-mode estimate gives \(\theta^{-K}(1+2\theta)^m\le(m+1)B_m(K)\). This is the existing common-flat image argument in the full local algebra. An empty local flat has rank zero.

Low affine rank. Let \(R=\dim\operatorname{span}\{\ell_j\}\). If \(R<H\), choose \(R\) actual inputs whose homogeneous parts form a basis; their complementary product is \(Q\). Subtract their constant linear combinations from every input, giving a legal tuple \(g'\) of functions only of \(z\). On the support of \(Q\), the original tuple equals \(g'\). Choose \(t\) binary code rows detecting every nonzero \(g'(z)\) at feature patterns of weight at most \(s\), including zero. The union bound is at most \((q+1)^s2^{-t}\le1/2\); express these rows in the original positions.

Every companion \(E'_j=P_{ m code}g'_j\), as a function of formal Boolean \(z\), vanishes on those patterns. In its unique multilinear representation, its coefficient at \(z_S\) is \[c_{j,S}=\sum_{T\subseteq S}E'_j(\mathbf1_T).\] Thus \(c_{j,S}=0\) for \(|S|\le s\). This is Boolean Möbius inversion, the same coefficient identity already proved in the binary-flat coefficient record. Consequently the tuple map factors through the sum of maps \(f\mapsto z_Sf\), \(|S|>s\), with scalar output vectors \((c_{j,S})_j\), followed by multiplication by \(Q\). The product \(z_S\) indicates the union affine system of rank \(R_S\). Rank subadditivity and (3) bound the full tuple by \((m+1)B_m(K)L_s\). No factor for the number of companions is introduced.

High affine rank. If \(R\ge H\), select \(H\) actual inputs with independent homogeneous parts, and let \(P\) be their complementary product. The companion map factors through multiplication by \(P\). Regard \[P(x,Z)=\prod_{j=1}^H(1+\ell_j(x)+F_j(Z))\] as a function of formal Boolean \(Z\), with coefficients polynomial in old coordinates. Its multilinear expansion is \[P(x,z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[q]}z_S\sum_{T\subseteq S}P(x,\mathbf1_T).\tag{4}\] For each \(T\), \(P(x,\mathbf1_T)\) is an indicator of \(H\) independent affine equations in the old coordinates. Hence \(z_SP(x,\mathbf1_T)\) is either zero or an affine-flat indicator whose rank is at least \(\max(H,R_S)\). Bound each term's multiplication image by (3) and sum the \(2^{|S|}\) terms for each \(S\). This gives \((m+1)B_m(K)U_H\), proving (2).

Degree scope. Expansion (4) can contain arbitrarily many terms and high-degree written products. It is used only to bound rank in the full local function algebra; it is not a source substitution or a sequence of PC lines. Every linear dependence among the actual small-degree companion columns is local-zero through \(K+(H+t+1)\delta\), so ordinary local normal reduction supplies a certificate at that degree. Weighting and absorption then give the stated quotient transfer. Neither an auxiliary projector nor a large expansion is replayed in the hard quotient. This is the same local-to-quotient principle as the preceding coded theorem. \(\square\)

Quotient rank source exclusion

Use the same fixed growing-expansion binary charged family, with \[\Delta=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil,\quad n\Delta/8\le m\le n\Delta,\quad D=\lfloor n\Delta/64\rfloor,\quad K=\lfloor m/1024\rfloor,\quad \log N=\Theta((\log n)^2).\] Keep \(h=\lceil(\log n)^5\rceil\), \(B=\lceil(\log n)^{11}\rceil\), and put \(d=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil\), \(r=\lceil16384\log n\rceil\). Fix \(A>0\) and an inventory bound \(1\le M\le N^A\). There are at most \(M\) complete old-affine bottom blocks and at most \(M\) parents, all with original accuracy \(h\). Every proper bottom span has rank at most \(d\); zero and unit spans are allowed. Parents are affine in old variables and genuine shared bottom products, never raw earlier coefficients or other parents.

For each parent supply a subspace \(U\) of homogeneous old-linear forms, \(\dim U\le c\le d\), with \(c\) a common integer upper bound. Let \(L_i\) be the homogeneous direction spaces of its nonconstant proper bottom products. Discard those with \(L_i\subseteq U\) from the following condition. Require, for every subset of the remaining feature indices, \[\dim\bigl((U+\sum_{i\in S}L_i)/U\bigr)\ge r|S|.\tag{5}\] The core and direction spaces may differ between parents. No edge-support restriction is imposed. Condition (5) is a supplied rank-growth hypothesis, not a property inferred from source size.

Corollary. For all sufficiently large \(n\), depending on \(A\), these complete sources have no degree-\(B\) PC or ordinary NS refutation. One weight of degree at most \(K\) and binary coefficient maps of degree at most \(T=\max(1,c)\) suffice, with full replay through \(K+TB\le D\). The preceding local-flat source exclusion is included by taking \(U=0\): disjoint edge supports imply the required direct-sum growth.

Rank-growth estimate. First suppose the features themselves have \(R_S\ge r|S|\). Necessarily \(q\le2m/r\). Since \(a\le\exp(-K/(2m))\) and \(K/m\ge1/2048\) eventually, \[a^r\le n^{-4},\qquad q a^{r/2}\le2m n^{-2}=o(1).\] For \(s\ge2eqa^r\), the first profile obeys \[L_s\le\sum_{j>s}\binom qj(a^r)^j \le e^{qa^r}(eqa^r/s)^s\le e\,2^{-s}\tag{6}\] eventually. This is the binomial tail with parameter \(a^r/(1+a^r)\), multiplied by \((1+a^r)^q\). For the other profile, use \(\max(H,R_S)\ge(H+r|S|)/2\) to get \[U_H\le a^{H/2}(1+2a^{r/2})^q\le e^2a^{H/2}.\tag{7}\] These are real bounds on dimensions, not real weights being reduced modulo two.

Condition and interpolate the core. Fix a homogeneous basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_{c_a}\) of the parent's core, \(c_a\le c\), and a linear complement to \(U\) in the old-linear space. For each \(\sigma\in\mathbb F_2^{c_a}\), impose the formal core values \(u_j=\sigma_j\). A basis of a bottom direction space can be chosen with a basis of \(L_i\cap U\) first. If those core equations disagree with \(\sigma\), the feature becomes zero. Otherwise the other equations project to independent directions in the chosen complement, giving an old affine-flat indicator \(z_i^\sigma\) with degree at most \(d\). On the core slice it equals the original feature. Core-contained features become constants. By (5), every remaining union of projected direction spaces has rank at least \(r|S|\). Inconsistent or identically-zero features may be removed.

Replace the features in the parent inputs by these projected indicators and constants, keeping the raw old-affine parts. The resulting actual polynomials \(g_j^\sigma\) still have degree at most \(d\) and agree with the original specialized inputs on that core slice. Apply the theorem and (6)–(7) to each branch with common parameters \[s=\lceil\log_2(16eM2^c(m+1))\rceil,\qquad H=\left\lceil\frac{2\log(16e^2M2^c(m+1))}{-\log a}\right\rceil,\] \[t=s\lceil\log_2(2m+1)\rceil+1.\tag{8}\] Using the upper bound \(2m\) for feature counts only adds padding to the code-row count. Each branch companion rank is at most \(B_m(K)/(8M2^c)\). Zero-feature branches are covered by the same theorem with \(q=0\).

Pad the branch matrices to the same number of rows, and interpolate each original coefficient entry with \[C(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb F_2^{c_a}}\chi_\sigma(x)C_\sigma, \qquad \chi_\sigma=\prod_{j=1}^{c_a}(1+u_j+\sigma_j).\tag{9}\] Each entry has ordinary old degree at most \(c_a\). On a Boolean point exactly one selector is one, so the actual mapped product and every companion agree there with the chosen branch matrix applied to that branch's inputs. Restriction to all core slices is injective on the local function algebra. The full companion map's rank is therefore at most the sum of its slice ranks, hence at most \(B_m(K)/(8M)\). Restricting a branch output cannot increase its rank, and the input remains \(V_K\); no degree-free change of the local edge coordinates is used. Unattainable core slices contribute zero. There is no claim of an efficiently enumerated table in (9).

One weight and original replay. All bottoms are assigned their full affine bases once, with constant coefficients and zero padding; their products are exactly the flats just used. Shared occurrences are coherent. The growing-family quotient has \(\dim[V_K]\ge B_m(K)/2\) eventually. The sum of all parent error ranks is at most \(B_m(K)/8\), so a degree-\(K\) weight \(W\notin J_D\) kills every mapped parent companion. To transfer that rank comparison, each actual mapped source generator of original degree \(e\le B\) has old degree at most \(Te\); its local-zero column relations have certificates through \(K+Te\). Thus the same rank comparison is valid in \(J_D\), not merely on the local cube.

As \(c\le d\) and \(\log M=O_A((\log n)^2)\), (8) gives \(H,s=O_A((\log n)^2)\), \(t=O_A((\log n)^3)\), so all rows fit \(h\) eventually. Every original two-level companion has ceiling at most \(2h^2+3h<B\); old widths fit too. Finally \(TB=O((\log n)^{13})=o(n\Delta)\), and \(K+TB<D\). Bottom companions are Boolean-zero, parent fields are Boolean-zero through \(2T\), and every actual image has its paid certificate. Replaying a degree-\(B\) PC proof under this one global map and multiplying its lines by \(W\) stays inside \(J_D\). The last line would kill \(W\). Ordinary NS has its same-degree PC replay. The graph, \(h,B\) and core-size allowance are independent of \(A\). \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For arbitrarily large \(m\), let \(v_j=x_1+\cdots+x_j\), \(1\le j\le m\), in the independent first indicator of each edge. This is an invertible linear system. Group consecutive \(v_j\)'s into blocks of size \(r\), and take their common-zero indicators as features. The direction spaces form a direct sum, so (5) holds with \(U=0\), while the supports of successive blocks are nested and extensively overlap. Setting all partners \(y_e=0\) leaves the full \(x\)-cube, so these flats are nonempty local objects. Parent inputs can include arbitrary old-affine terms and mixtures of all those features. Distinct parents may use different such dictionaries; old charged properness comes from the fixed graph, not these local points. For a positive core, include a basis of any \(c\)-dimensional core in the overall independent linear system and choose residual directions in a complement. These are genuine unbounded source instances with all original degrees inside the stated budget.

Degenerate cases. Empty feature families have \(L_s=0,U_H=a^H\). Zero or unit bottom spans specialize to constant products and are absorbed into the parent affine part. Core-contained features likewise become branch constants. Inconsistent unions contribute zero, and adding redundant defining equations does not change \(R_S\). Duplicating a feature can weaken the sufficient profile; identical feature functions can first be combined in the parent expressions with the same local certificates. The theorem does not assert a presentation-invariant optimal bound. If (5) is required for many copies without combining them, it correctly fails. The core-zero case uses constant matrices, and \(T=1\) by convention. No core of dimension comparable to \(m\), or unbounded-density family, is covered for free.

Coefficient orientation for the next test

The existing binary-flat coefficient lemma gives a direct oracle, not a new coding theorem. For constant binary row matrices and a formal feature vector \(Z\), write the union product as the zero indicator of \(AZ=b(x)\), and the two separate products together as the zero indicator of \(BZ=c(x)\), with \(B\) the stack of their actual row systems. Here the old-affine parts remain in \(b(x),c(x)\). For every feature subset \(S\), at each fixed old Boolean \(x\), the coefficient of their difference as a function of formal \(Z\) is \[\mathbf1[\operatorname{rank}A_S=|S|,\ b(x)\in\operatorname{im}A_S] +\mathbf1[\operatorname{rank}B_S=|S|,\ c(x)\in\operatorname{im}B_S]\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.\] This follows by applying lem:binary-flat-coefficients-matroid twice, with the actual affine constants retained. Overlapping input groups use the common registry once, as in lem:overlap-row-defect; naive symmetric difference would be wrong. If raw old-affine variables are expanded as extra formal features, this oracle can lose the useful affine-flat factorization, so the next estimate must keep that part factored. The oracle and a cancelled coefficient do not bound a quotient image by themselves.
