Source, survival and the finite statement

Use the binary one-hot form of the charged ternary graph pseudoideal \(J_D\), on a connected graph with \(n\) vertices, \(m\) edges, expansion \(\varepsilon\), maximum degree \(\Delta\), nonzero total charge, and \[ \max(4,2\Delta)\le D<\varepsilon n/8. \] Nonzero total charge identifies the intended inconsistent family; the exclusion argument itself only uses the proper quotient. Fix a spanning tree. Choose pairwise disjoint sets \(S_1,\ldots,S_s\) of \(d\ge1\) edges in its complement, with an arbitrary fixed label on each selected edge. Put \[ z_j=\prod_{e\in S_j}x_{e,a_e}. \] All labels are compatible because the supports are disjoint. Any product of these \(z_j\)'s of degree at most \(D\) survives in \(J_D\): its ternary character expansion has nonzero coefficients in distinct cut-code classes. A cut supported off the spanning tree is zero, since its vertex potential is constant on that tree. This is the existing tree-complement argument, not a global satisfying assignment of the charged system.

Let \(h\ge d\). Introduce one complete accuracy-\(h\) bottom block on the \(d\) affine inputs \((1-x_{e,a_e})_{e\in S_j}\). Select each once and zero-pad the other rows. The resulting actual bottom value is \(z_j\), every bottom companion is a Boolean consequence through \(d+1\), and coefficient domains vanish. Original product and companion costs remain \(2h\) and \(2h+1\).

There are at most \(M\ge1\) complete accuracy-\(h\) parents. Their original inputs are old affine polynomials plus constant binary combinations of the genuine bottom products. After the single bottom assignment, write \(G_a\) for a parent's actual polynomial input span, and \[ E_a=\{j\in[s]:z_j\in G_a\}. \] Membership here is literal constant-linear-combination membership, with its coefficients available for choosing a legal row. Assume each parent either has \(\dim G_a\le h\), contains one, or has \(|E_a|\ge h+1\). This is the exposure hypothesis. Plain positive readers of any subset of the bottom values satisfy it; arbitrary linear recodings of their input coordinates preserve it. It is not asserted for arbitrary mixed spans.

Let \(M_+\) be the number of parents left after the first two alternatives. If \(M_+=0\), set \(q=0\). Otherwise \(s\ge h+1\), and set \[ q=\min\!\left(s,\ \left\lceil\frac{s\ln M_+}{h+1}\right\rceil+1\right). \tag{1} \] The logarithm counts the inventory as a real number, not a field scalar.

Theorem (exposed reader cleanup). Under these assumptions, the complete two-level source has no PC refutation through any integer \(B\) satisfying \[ h(2h+1)+2h\le B,\qquad B+dq\le D. \tag{2} \] Ordinary NS certificates through \(B\) are excluded as well. One coherent constant coefficient assignment and a surviving old weight of degree at most \(dq\) remove all extension companions within \(D\).

Proof

Cover only the unresolved parents. Their sets \(E_a\) have size at least \(h+1\). If (1) gives \(q=s\), take all indices. Otherwise sample \(q\) indices independently and uniformly from \([s]\). A fixed \(E_a\) is missed with probability at most \[ \left(1-\frac{h+1}{s}\right)^q \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{q(h+1)}s\right). \] The sum of these probabilities is strictly below one by (1). Hence there is a set \(T\) of at most \(q\) distinct indices meeting every \(E_a\). Sampling repetitions can be discarded. Put \(W=\prod_{j\in T}z_j\), with \(W=1\) for the empty set. Its degree \(b=d|T|\le dq\le D\), and the tree-complement character argument proves \(W\notin J_D\).

Remove the small spans first. If \(1\in G_a\), select the corresponding constant input combination in one row, giving a zero product. If \(\dim G_a=r\le h\), choose a basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) of its actual inputs, represented by constant combinations in the original indexed positions. Use those \(r\) rows and zero-pad. Its product is \(\prod_{\ell=1}^r(1-u_\ell)\), so every input-times-product is a Boolean consequence through \((r+1)d\): express the input in the basis and use \(u_\ell(1-u_\ell)\), whose Boolean certificate fits \(2d\). All specialized parent inputs have degree at most \(d\), including the old affine parts. Empty tuples are harmless. After weighting by \(W\), the ceiling is at most \(b+(h+1)d\).

Clamp the large exposed spans. For each remaining parent choose \(j(a)\in T\cap E_a\). Its supplied constant representation selects \(z_{j(a)}\) as the first row sum; all later rows are zero. The actual parent product is \(1-z_{j(a)}\). Since \(W\) contains \(z_{j(a)}\) as a factor, \[ W(1-z_{j(a)})g_{a,i}=0\pmod{J_D} \] with old Boolean certificates through \(b+2d\). These are the whole actual companion tuples, not a selected scalar error. The same bottom values and parent coefficients are used in every occurrence. All fresh field equations vanish at their constant assignments.

Pay the full proof. Weighted bottom companions fit \(b+d+1\). Because \(d\le h\), every displayed Boolean certificate fits \[ b+h(2h+1)+2h\le b+B\le D. \] The original parents have input ceiling \(2h\), product cost \(h(2h+1)\) and companion cost \(h(2h+1)+2h\). These original costs, not their shorter specialized degrees, are covered in (2). Suppose a degree-\(B\) source PC refutation existed. Apply the one global constant substitution and multiply every line by \(W\). Old axioms and all extension images lie in \(J_D\). Linear steps persist; old-variable multiplication is allowed by bounded absorption through \(b+B\), and assigned-variable multiplication is scalar multiplication. The final line gives \(W\in J_D\), a contradiction. Every ordinary degree-\(B\) NS refutation is a PC derivation within that degree, so it is excluded too. \(\square\)

A genuine polynomial-inventory range

Fix a bounded-degree expander family and any \(0<\alpha<1/6\). Set \(h=\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\), \(B=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), and choose \(D=\Theta(n)\) inside the stated proper window. Allow any \(1\le d\le h\), any disjoint tree-complement supports as above, and \(M\le n^A\) for any fixed \(A\). Since \(sd\le m-n+1=O(n)\), the nonempty-cover case has \[ dq\le d+\frac{sd}{h+1}\ln M_+ +d =O(n^\alpha+n^{1-\alpha}\log n)=o(n). \] The first inequality uses \(\lceil x\rceil\le x+1\). Also the original companion ceiling is \(O(h^2)=o(n^{1/3})\). Thus (2) holds for all sufficiently large \(n\), with the threshold allowed to depend on \(A\). The fixed graph family does not depend on \(A\). Empty high-parent inventory needs no weight at all.

This does not require a common input across every parent. Nor does it fix every bottom value on the selected branch: unselected values may remain nonlinear and independent. Hence the all-input compression obstruction is not contradicted. No large rank fraction for \(W\) is needed for this terminal two-level replay. The existing sparse-weight lemma can give a fraction depending on its character support, but that is not promoted to an all-depth invariant.

The chain theorem covers many overlapping sources by a different hypothesis. Disjoint nonzero bottom input spaces form an antichain, so its number of chains can equal \(s\); its stated \(o(n/\log n)\) condition need not hold here, for example with \(d\) proportional to \(\log n\) and \(s\) proportional to \(n/\log n\). Conversely it permits parent mixtures without the present exposure assumption. The old direct-sum safe-reader theorem uses bit-PHP row geometry and its own type-count budget, not this charged graph's independent label products. No dominance across these families or stronger general source coverage is asserted.

What breaks for unrestricted mixtures

The proof needs an actually exposed predicate, not merely a nonzero projection of the parent span onto a feature coordinate. A tuple such as \((z_1+z_2,z_3+z_4,\ldots)\) need not contain any individual \(z_j\); selecting one of its coordinates does not force a chosen positive feature. Such mixtures are allowed by the current grammar. Extending coverage therefore requires a new argument for those unresolved spans, not an assumption that every parent can be put in positive-reader form.
