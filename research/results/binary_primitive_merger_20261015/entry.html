Primitive merger gap

Statement. Work in \(\mathbb F_2[z,y_1,y_2,y_3,w]\), with independent variables. Put

\[ B=z(z+1),\quad C_i=y_i(y_i+1),\quad E_i=(z+y_i)(z+y_i+1)=B+C_i, \qquad U=E_1E_2E_3,\quad V=C_1C_2C_3, \] \[ R=B^2+B(C_1+C_2+C_3)+C_1C_2+C_1C_3+C_2C_3. \]

For every \(M=2^t,\ t\ge0\), supply the three affine products

\[ Q_0=U^M,\qquad Q_1=V^M,\qquad Q_2=B^M w. \]

They are pairwise coprime, their largest common Frobenius power in all nonconstant factor multiplicities is one, and no pair sum is a product of binary affine polynomials. Their complete sum satisfies

\[ g=Q_0+Q_1+Q_2=B^M(R^M+w). \]

Its exact root order at each of zero and one is \(M\). With ordinary repeated wires, the triple and its quotient have size \(O(M+1)\); the quotient has unbounded-fanin binary depth at most six. Consequently common-gcd removal and global common-power deflation leave this supplied triple unchanged, and a next step requiring an affine-split residual pair cannot start. This concerns exactly those moves; subsum deflation, whole-sum identities and other division operations remain allowed alternatives.

Identity and orders. Expand \(\prod_{i=1}^3(B+C_i)\) and cancel \(V\). The result is \(U+V=BR\). Frobenius gives the displayed identity for \(g\). At both Boolean roots of \(B\), \(R=C_1C_2+C_1C_3+C_2C_3\). Hence \(R^M+w\) has nonzero specialization there, since \(w\) is independent and occurs with coefficient one. Neither root divides the quotient, proving both exact orders.

Coprimality and primitivity. The factors of \(U\) are \(z+y_i,z+y_i+1\); those of \(V\) are \(y_i,y_i+1\); those of \(B^Mw\) are \(z,z+1,w\). All are distinct affine primes across these lists. Thus all pair gcds, and in particular the common gcd, are one. The factor \(w\) occurs once. Therefore no power of two larger than one divides every supplied nonconstant factor multiplicity, for any \(M\). This uses formal polynomials, not their Boolean values.

The first pair is not split. The polynomial \(R\) is monic of degree four in \(z\), and it has no binary affine factor involving \(z\). To prove this, such a factor would be \(z+\lambda\), where \(\lambda=e+\sum_{i\in T}y_i+\epsilon w\), with \(e,\epsilon\in\mathbb F_2\) and \(T\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\). If \(\epsilon=1\), \(R(\lambda)\) has leading \(w^4\) coefficient one, since only \(B(\lambda)^2\) contributes that degree. It cannot vanish. If \(\epsilon=0\), set \(D=B(\lambda)=\sum_{i\in T}C_i\). Then

A nonconstant pivot-free factor of \(R\) is also impossible because its leading coefficient in \(z\) is one. In particular \(R\) is not an affine product. Unique factorization now shows that \(U+V=BR\) is not an affine product, and neither is \(Q_0+Q_1=(U+V)^M\): an irreducible non-affine factor persists under a positive power.

The other pairs are not split. For \(A=U^M\) or \(A=V^M\), consider \(A+B^Mw\) as a polynomial in the independent variable \(w\). It has degree one in \(w\). If it were an affine product, exactly one factor would contain \(w\), necessarily with coefficient one; the product of all remaining factors would be its leading coefficient \(B^M\). Its constant term would then be divisible by \(B^M\). The actual constant term is \(A\), which is coprime to \(B\), a contradiction since \(M\ge1\). This proves nonsplitting for both remaining pairs, including after pair-gcd removal, since those gcds are one.

This defeats the specified mandatory initial moves but leaves partial-power and whole-sum rewriting available; it is not a structural obstruction to cheap quotients.
