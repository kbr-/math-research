Prime-field residual normalization

Lemma. Fix a prime \(p\) and work over \(\mathbb F_p\) itself. Put \(B_j=x_j^2-x_j\). Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\) be arithmetic circuits of total gate/wire size \(s\) and depth at most \(\Delta\), on \(n\) variables. Gates are unbounded-fanin sums/products, without division or succinct powering. Define \[A=\prod_i(1-F_i^{p-1}),\quad A_i=\prod_{l\ne i}(1-F_l^{p-1}),\quad d_i=F_i^{p-2}\prod_{l<i}(1-F_l^{p-1}).\] At \(p=2\), the zero power is one. Any size-\(S\), depth-\(d\) general IPS certificate \(C(x,Y,Z)\), satisfying the formal identities \(C(x,0,0)=0\), \(C(x,F,B)=1\), gives a circuit \(Q(x,Z)\), linear in \(Z\), with \(Q(x,0)=0\), \(Q(x,B)=A\), of size polynomial in \(S+s+n+m\) and depth \(O(d+\Delta+1)\). Conversely such a circuit gives a certificate with input coefficients exactly \(d_i\), at polynomial size and depth \(O(\operatorname{depth}Q+\Delta+1)\). Constants in the polynomial bounds may depend on fixed \(p\). No PC-degree or multilinearity-in-\(x\) preservation is asserted.

Gate linearization. Write \(G=(F,B)\). At each gate \(g\) of \(C\), retain values \(a_g=g(x,G)\), \(b_g=g(x,0)\), and construct \(R_g(x,V)\), linear in fresh placeholders \(V\), with \(R_g(x,G)=a_g-b_g\). For placeholder leaves take \(V_l\); for old-variable and constant leaves take zero. Sums sum these circuits. At a product gate with ordered children \(g_1,\ldots,g_t\), put \[R_g=\sum_{j=1}^t R_{g_j}\prod_{l<j}a_{g_l}\prod_{l>j}b_{g_l}.\] Product telescoping proves the invariant. At the output, \(R(x,G)=1\), because \(C(x,0,0)=0\). All multipliers are independent of \(V\), so \(R\) is linear in the entire placeholder list. Store gate values once, and use at most \(O(t^2)\) wires per product gate. This gives polynomial size and depth \(O(d+\Delta+1)\). No extraction of a high homogeneous component is used.

Field defects. For every input circuit \(F\), construct a linear-placeholder \(K_F(x,Z)\) satisfying \(K_F(x,B)=F^p-F\). A constant in \(\mathbb F_p\) gives zero; a variable \(x_j\) gives \(Z_j\sum_{a=0}^{p-2}x_j^a\). At sums add the certificates, using Frobenius. At an ordered product of children \(a_1,\ldots,a_t\), use \[K_F=\sum_j K_{a_j}\prod_{l<j}a_l^p\prod_{l>j}a_l.\] Its specialization is \(\prod_la_l^p-\prod_la_l\). Sharing actual child values gives polynomial size and depth \(O(\Delta+1)\), with \(K_F(x,0)=0\). Extension constants are outside the statement because \(c^p-c\) need not vanish there.

Scale the linearized certificate. Set \[Q(x,Z)=R\bigl(x,(-A_iK_{F_i}(x,Z))_i,(AZ_j)_j\bigr).\] The substituted placeholders evaluate to \(AF_i\) and \(AB_j\), since \(-A_i(F_i^p-F_i)=AF_i\). Hence \[Q(x,B)=R(x,AF,AB)=A R(x,F,B)=A.\] Here linearity is proved in the first step, not assumed for the original IPS circuit. The inserted circuits have polynomial size and depth \(O(\Delta+1)\); grafting them adds that depth to \(R\). The resulting \(Q\) is linear in \(Z\). Conversely, literal product telescoping gives \(\sum_i d_iF_i=1-A\), so \(\sum_i d_iY_i+Q(x,Z)\) is a full IPS certificate. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. For \(p=2,F_1=x,F_2=1+x\), one has \(A=B_x\) and \(Q=Z_x\); the original certificate is \(Y_1+Y_2\). Unused variables and repeated equations give unbounded counts. This is a normalization witness, not a hard instance.

Degenerate cases. With no input equations, \(A=1\) and neither certificate exists on the nonempty Boolean cube. A unit input gives \(A=0\) and an immediate certificate; zero inputs are harmless. Repeated variables, repeated gates and cancellations preserve the identities and retain their actual circuit description costs. Existence of either certificate implies unsatisfiability, so a separate unsatisfiability hypothesis is unused and omitted.

What this changes and what it does not

The full clause-normalization proof was reread. It starts with a Hilbert-like certificate and literal inputs over any field, and has sharper additive constant-depth overhead. The present prime-field statement changes the inputs and removes the initial Hilbert-like restriction using standard gate telescoping. It does not subsume the earlier all-field scope. Its functional coefficient vector \((d_i)\) is cheap, while the formal Boolean correction \(Q\) remains the entire hardness task. Thus cheap inverses or first-violation coefficients cannot be reported as small full IPS certificates.

The converse also prevents attributing an extra universal restriction to the correction circuit merely from its general-IPS origin: every linear \(Q\) of the stated kind produces an IPS certificate with the claimed overhead. The homogeneous-component and field-coordinate barriers remain applicable to proposed extraction operations. This cycle constructs neither operation.
