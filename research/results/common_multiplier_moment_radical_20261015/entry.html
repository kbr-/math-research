Common moment radical

Setting. Let \(p\) be prime and \(J\le V_D=\mathbb F_p[x]_{\le D}\) a proper degree-\(D\) pseudoideal containing the old Boolean or prime-field domain consequences within that degree window. Fix \(w\in V_b\), an \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear subspace \(U\le V_K\), and errors \(E_1,\ldots,E_m\in V_e\), \(m\ge1\). All are fixed before choosing the functional or scalar coefficients. Assume

\[b+e+pK\le D.\tag{1}\]

Write \(T:U\to(V_D/J)^m\), \(u\mapsto([wE_i u])_i\), \(N=\ker T\), and \(r=\operatorname{rank}T\). For a symmetric \(p\)-linear form \(B\) on \(U\), its radical means \(\{u:B(u,u_2,\ldots,u_p)=0\text{ for all }u_2,\ldots,u_p\in U\}\), not just the diagonal zero set.

Statement. Over any finite extension \(L/\mathbb F_p\) with \(|L|>2r+1\), there exist a linear functional \(\mu:V_D\otimes L\to L\) annihilating \(J_L\), with \(\mu(1)=1\), and coefficients \(a_i\in L\), such that

\[B(u_1,\ldots,u_p)=\mu\!\left(w\Big(\sum_i a_iE_i\Big)\prod_{j=1}^p u_j\right),\qquad \operatorname{rad}B=N\otimes L.\tag{2}\]

Equivalently the first-factor flattening \(U_L\to(U_L^{\otimes(p-1)})^*\) has rank \(r\). For a fixed finite family of such data over the same \(J\), one \(\mu\) works for every member, with separate error coefficients, whenever \(|L|>1+2\sum_a r_a\) and each member satisfies its own reserve (1). These are sufficient field sizes. The functional is chosen from the full old annihilator space independently of a prospective successful multiplier, though it may depend on the fixed error data.

Proof: the easy radical inclusion. If \(u_1\in N_L\), every \(wE_i u_1\in J_L\). Multiplying by the other \(p-1\) arguments costs at most \(b+e+pK\). Scalar extension preserves degree intersections and paid pseudoideal multiplication, so all terms of (2) vanish. Thus \(N_L\subseteq\operatorname{rad}B\) for every \(\mu,a\). Every product in the form is an actual polynomial inside the window; no multiplication on an unrestricted quotient algebra is assumed.

Proof: a generic form has no extra radical. Choose a prime-field basis \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_q\) of \((V_D/J)^*\). Introduce independent indeterminates \(t_1,\ldots,t_m,z_1,\ldots,z_q\), let \(F=\mathbb F_p(t,z)\), and set \(\mu_z=\sum_\ell z_\ell\lambda_\ell\), \(E_t=\sum_i t_i E_i\). For any \(u=\sum_j c_j u_j\in U_F\) in a fixed prime-field basis of \(U\), domain reduction through \(pK\) gives

\[B_{t,z}(u,\ldots,u)=\sum_{i,\ell}t_i z_\ell\sum_j c_j^p\lambda_\ell([wE_i u_j]).\tag{3}\]

Indeed \(u^p=\sum_j c_j^p u_j^p\), and \(u_j^p-u_j\) is a domain consequence through \(pK\); multiplication by \(wE_i\) fits (1). Each inner sum in (3) is a \(p\)-th power in \(F\), because the displayed \(\lambda_\ell\)-values lie in \(\mathbb F_p\). The monomials \(t_i z_\ell\) are linearly independent over \(F^p\). Clear denominators in the \(p\)-th powers: their exponent vectors modulo \(p\) have one in the specified \(t_i\) and \(z_\ell\) positions and zero elsewhere, distinct for different pairs. Thus if (3) is zero, every inner sum is zero. Injectivity of Frobenius in a field gives \(\sum_j c_j\lambda_\ell([wE_i u_j])=0\) for every \(i,\ell\), and the dual basis then gives \(Tu=0\). A radical vector in particular has zero diagonal value, so it belongs to \(N_F\). Along with the easy inclusion this proves generic radical equality. In fact the diagonal zero set equals \(N_F\) over this imperfect generic field; this stronger fact will not be specialized.

Proof: finite specialization and normalization. In bases of \(U\) and its \((p-1)\)-fold tensor power, every flattening entry is bilinear in \(t,z\). Generic rank \(r\) gives a nonzero \(r\)-minor of total degree at most \(2r\). Properness makes \(\mu_z(1)\) a nonzero linear polynomial. Their product is nonzero and has total degree at most \(2r+1\). A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(s\) on a finite grid of size greater than \(s\) has a nonzero value, by induction using the univariate root bound. Select such a value and divide \(\mu\) by \(\mu(1)\). The minor stays nonzero and the easy radical inclusion holds at every specialization, so rank is exactly \(r\) and the radical is exactly \(N_L\). If \(r=0\), use the empty minor one. For a finite family take the product of one such minor for each member and the single normalization polynomial. The polynomial ring is a domain, so the product is nonzero despite sharing all moment parameters. This proves the claimed common field size and common functional.

Protected weights. Put \(L_0=\{u\in U:wu\in J\}\), and \(H=\dim U/L_0\). Paid multiplication gives \(L_0\subseteq N\). The form descends through \(L_0\) in every argument, by the same reserve, and its radical there is \(N/L_0\). A common surviving multiplier exists exactly when the flattening rank is less than \(H\). If a useful vector is found over \(L\), coefficient components in an \(\mathbb F_p\)-basis give an original-field useful vector in \(U\). This is an exact detection criterion, not an estimate that makes the rank small.

Binary interpretation. At \(p=2\), (2) is one symmetric bilinear moment form with precisely the actual error kernel as radical, at cost \(b+e+2K\). It uses one old functional and one error combination. The earlier common-moment adjoint uses unweighted input/output pairings through \(2(b+K+e)\) and supplies adjoint identities; this form has a cheaper stated reserve for kernel detection but supplies neither that adjoint nor its squared-operator formula. The prime retained rectangular pairing already uses the Frobenius mechanism at cost \(pK\) when no error weight is present. Here the error and weight are paid once, and the right-hand test space is generated by \(p-1\) elements of the actual allowed \(U\), not necessarily all of \(V_{(p-1)K}\).

Moment degree limits

Odd-prime bilinear control. For every odd prime \(p\), let \(D=2\), \(J=C_2(x^2-x,y^2-y,(x+y)^2)\), \(f=x+y\), and \(U=\langle f\rangle\). The three generators' quadratic leading parts are independent, since the coefficient of \(xy\) in the last is \(2\ne0\). Their span has no nonzero affine polynomial, so no nonzero legal variable multiplication is available through degree two; it is the actual PC closure. It is proper, also witnessed by \((x,y)=(0,0)\), and \(f\notin J\). With weight and single error both one, the actual action on \(U\) has rank one. But every old annihilator satisfies \(\mu(f^2)=0\), so its bilinear moment form on \(U\) is zero, over every scalar extension. Thus the uniform binary bilinear reserve \(2K\) cannot simply replace \(pK\) at odd primes. This is a satisfiable two-variable control, not a claimed sharpness theorem for every alternative rectangular construction.

Perfect-field diagonal control. For a finite perfect field \(L\) of characteristic \(p\), the diagonal of a moment form (2), on a base-field basis \(u_j\), has the shape \(\sum_j d_j c_j^p\), by the same field reduction. It is \((\sum_j d_j^{1/p}c_j)^p\), so its zero set has codimension at most one. If the flattening rank exceeds one, that zero set cannot equal the radical. The familiar binary special case is prop:perfect-binary-moment-rank, reread in full. Finite specialization in the theorem preserves the flattening minor, not the generic field's anisotropic diagonal detection. Testing only \(B(u,\ldots,u)=0\) after specialization is therefore unsound for finding a common multiplier.

Normalization control. On the one-bit Boolean quotient, \(U=\langle1,x\rangle\), \(w=E_1=1\), \(K=1,e=b=0,D=2,p=2\), a normalized binary functional has Gram matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\a&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whose determinant is zero for both \(a\in\mathbb F_2\). Over \(\mathbb F_4\), \(a\notin\mathbb F_2\) gives determinant \(a+a^2\ne0\). Thus even this old moment control may need a functional-value extension. It leaves source variable domains unchanged, and provides no nonzero annihilator of the unit error. The extension bound in the theorem is sufficient, not minimal.

Nonvacuity. Over every prime, in the one-bit Boolean quotient take \(U=\langle1,x\rangle\), \(w=1,E_1=x,K=1,e=1\), and \(D=p+1\). The joint action has rank one, its kernel is \(\langle1-x\rangle\), and that weight survives. Evaluation at \(x=1\) is an old normalized annihilator with \(\mu(x)=1\); coefficient one yields (2) with exactly that radical. Tensoring with any number of spectator Boolean variables preserves this witness. The odd-prime control has \(K=1,b=e=0\) but \(D=2

Degenerate cases. A zero error tuple, zero weight action or zero \(U\) gives rank zero and radical all of \(U\); no surviving conclusion is inferred when \(H=0\). Constant-domain \(K=0\) is permitted. Properness is used for normalization and would fail for \(J=V_D\). No unit is assumed in \(U\). The finite-family statement covers only fixed data specified before the functional; an unlisted later functional-dependent error choice is not automatically covered.
