11 October 2026 — One off-line zero in the density proof's Gram form: its exact spectrum, a bound for a solitary zero, and a two-pair cancellation showing the bound is not additive

Working proofs, and a refutation of an additive lower bound over pairs, in an abstract complete-grid model; nothing is proved about actual zeta zeros. First bridge test. Produced by Claude Code (claude-opus-5-5, low effort).

Question. The open statement is off-line exclusion; the missing implication is from the information the imported density proof controls (trace and Hilbert–Schmidt norm of its Gram form, positive-index counts) to the absence of one fixed zero with \(\Re\rho>1/2\). This cycle computes exactly what such a zero contributes to that form and asks which estimate would contradict it. The test ends with a conditional bridge with explicit premises or with a precise statement of the missing information.

New mathematics. Three elementary statements about the Gram form, proved below for every window and bandwidth. (1) A conjugate pair off the real axis contributes two nonzero eigenvalues, \(\kappa+1\) and \(-(\kappa-1)\), where \(\kappa\) grows like \(e^{\delta\Lambda}\) in the bandwidth \(\Lambda\) and the distance \(\delta\) from the line; its trace is the same as for two on-line zeros. (2) If the pair is the only one off the axis, the largest eigenvalue and the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the whole form are at least \(\kappa+1\). (3) For two pairs the bound is not additive: at height difference \(\pi/\Lambda\) their leading terms cancel up to a factor of order \((\delta\Lambda)^{-2}\). No statement about the zeros of \(\zeta\) follows without further premises.

Goal progress. Unchanged. The outcome of the test is the precise missing information, in two parts. First, at the bandwidth \(\Lambda=\log(T/2\pi)\) of the imported second-moment premise, the solitary bound gives \(\delta\le1/2+O(\log\log T/\log T)\), which every zero satisfies already: this bound, applied to a solitary pair on the complete grid, gives no constraint at \(\theta=1\); the premise might constrain zeros by other routes. By this route a constraint needs a second-moment bound at bandwidth \(\theta\log T\) with \(\theta>1\), which the import does not contain. Second, the passage from a solitary pair to several pairs is unproved. The only loss proved here is polylogarithmic (two pairs, a factor of order \((\delta\Lambda)^{-2}\)), which would not change the exponent; whether many close pairs can lose a power of \(T\) is untested. Neither part is supplied here.

General statement. prop:solitary-amplification, for every bandwidth \(\Lambda>0\), every real even window \(\varphi\in L^2\) supported in \([-\Lambda/2,\Lambda/2]\) and every finite conjugation-closed multiset of points with exactly one pair off the real axis.

Setting

Fix \(\Lambda>0\), a real number \(\alpha_0\), the grid \(\alpha_k=\alpha_0+2\pi k/\Lambda\) for \(k\in\mathbb Z\), and a real, even, nonzero \(\varphi\in L^2(\mathbb R)\) supported in \(I=[-\Lambda/2,\Lambda/2]\). Use \(\widehat f(z)=\int f(u)e^{-iuz}\,du\) for \(z\in\mathbb C\), and put \(N_\varphi=\Lambda\|\varphi\|_2^2\). For \(z\in\mathbb C\) let \(v_z=(\widehat\varphi(z-\alpha_k))_{k\in\mathbb Z}\). A configuration is a finite set \(Z\subset\mathbb C\) closed under complex conjugation with multiplicities \(m_z=m_{\bar z}\ge1\); its Gram form is \[G_Z={1\over N_\varphi}\sum_{z\in Z}m_zv_zv_z^{\mathsf T}.\] This is the complete-grid version of the form \(G\) of the density import, where \(\Lambda=L=\log(T/2\pi)\), \(z_\rho=\gamma-i(\beta-1/2)\), the partner \(1-\bar\rho\) has the conjugate \(z\), \(N_\varphi=aL^2\), and the grid is cut to \(0\le k<d\). Zeros on the line are real points; a zero with \(\beta=1/2+\delta\) is the point \(\gamma-i\delta\). The import's window \(\chi(L/2+u)\chi(L/2-u)\sqrt{\psi(u/L)}\) with \(\psi\) even is real and even, as assumed here.

Pair atom spectrum

Lemma (pair atom spectrum). lem:pair-atom-spectrum. In the setting above every \(v_z\) lies in \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\), and for all \(z,w\in\mathbb C\) \[v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w=\Lambda\,\widehat{\varphi^2}(z-w),\qquad v_z^{*}v_w=\Lambda\,\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-\bar z).\] Consequently, for \(z=\gamma-i\delta\) with \(\delta\ne0\), with \[\kappa(\delta)={\int\varphi(u)^2\cosh(2\delta u)\,du\over\int\varphi(u)^2\,du}>1,\] one has \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=N_\varphi\), \(v_z^*v_z=\kappa N_\varphi\), and there are orthonormal real vectors \(e_x,e_y\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\) with \[{1\over N_\varphi}\bigl(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+v_{\bar z}v_{\bar z}^{\mathsf T}\bigr)=(\kappa+1)\,e_xe_x^{\mathsf T}-(\kappa-1)\,e_ye_y^{\mathsf T}.\] For real \(z\) the vector \(v_z\) is real and \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}/N_\varphi\) is a rank-one orthogonal projection.

Proof. Put \(F_z(u)=\varphi(u)e^{-iuz}\in L^2(I)\) and \(\varepsilon_k(u)=\Lambda^{-1/2}e^{-i\alpha_ku}\). The \(\varepsilon_k\) are the standard Fourier basis of \(L^2(I)\) multiplied by the unimodular function \(e^{-i\alpha_0u}\), hence an orthonormal basis for \(\langle f,g\rangle=\int_If\bar g\). Since \(v_z(k)=\int F_z(u)e^{i\alpha_ku}\,du=\Lambda^{1/2}\langle F_z,\varepsilon_k\rangle\), Parseval gives \(v_z\in\ell^2\) and \[v_z^*v_w=\Lambda\sum_k\overline{\langle F_z,\varepsilon_k\rangle}\langle F_w,\varepsilon_k\rangle=\Lambda\langle F_w,F_z\rangle=\Lambda\int\varphi^2e^{-iuw}e^{iu\bar z}\,du=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-\bar z).\] Next, \(\overline{v_{\bar z}(k)}=\int\varphi(u)e^{iuz}e^{-i\alpha_ku}\,du\), and the substitution \(u\mapsto-u\) with \(\varphi\) even turns this into \(v_z(k)\). So \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\) and \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w=v_{\bar z}^*v_w=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(w-z)=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(z-w)\), the last step because \(\varphi^2\) is even. Now let \(z=\gamma-i\delta\). Then \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=\Lambda\widehat{\varphi^2}(0)=N_\varphi\), and \(z-\bar z=-2i\delta\) gives \(v_z^*v_z=\Lambda\int\varphi^2e^{-2\delta u}\,du=\Lambda\int\varphi^2\cosh(2\delta u)\,du=\kappa N_\varphi\), by evenness again. Since \(\cosh(2\delta u)>1\) for \(u\ne0\) and \(\varphi\ne0\), \(\kappa>1\). Write \(v_z=x+iy\) with real \(x,y\). Then \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=|x|^2-|y|^2+2i\,x\cdot y\) and \(v_z^*v_z=|x|^2+|y|^2\), so \(x\cdot y=0\), \(|x|^2=(\kappa+1)N_\varphi/2\) and \(|y|^2=(\kappa-1)N_\varphi/2>0\). As \(v_{\bar z}=x-iy\), \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+v_{\bar z}v_{\bar z}^{\mathsf T}=2(xx^{\mathsf T}-yy^{\mathsf T})\); take \(e_x=x/|x|\), \(e_y=y/|y|\). For real \(z\), \(v_z=\overline{v_z}\) is real with \(|v_z|^2=N_\varphi\). \(\square\)

The bilinear identity is the import's Poisson identity; what is added is its Hermitian companion at complex arguments and the resulting spectrum. Every hypothesis on \(\varphi\) is used: support in \(I\) for the basis expansion; realness in the Parseval step (\(\varphi^2\) rather than \(|\varphi|^2\)) and, with evenness, for \(\overline{v_z}=v_{\bar z}\); evenness also for the bilinear identity, which without it reads \(\Lambda\int\varphi(u)\varphi(-u)e^{-iu(z-w)}\,du\); and \(\varphi\ne0\) for \(\kappa>1\).

Solitary amplification

Proposition (solitary amplification). prop:solitary-amplification. Let \(Z\) be a configuration with \(p\) conjugate pairs \(\{z_j,\bar z_j\}\) off the real axis, \(z_j=\gamma_j-i\delta_j\), \(\delta_j>0\), with \(\kappa_j=\kappa(\delta_j)\) and vectors \(e_{x_j},e_{y_j}\) from the lemma.

  1. Trace. \(\operatorname{tr}G_Z=\sum_{z\in Z}m_z\), whatever the positions of the points.
  2. Negative index. \(G_Z\) has at most \(p\) negative eigenvalues.
  3. One pair against the others. For each \(j_0\), \[\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\ge e_{x_{j_0}}^{\mathsf T}G_Ze_{x_{j_0}}\ge m_{j_0}(\kappa_{j_0}+1)-\sum_{j\ne j_0}m_j(\kappa_j-1)\,(e_{x_{j_0}}\cdot e_{y_j})^2.\]
  4. Solitary pair. If \(p=1\), then \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\lambda_{\max}(G_Z)\ge m_1(\kappa_1+1)\).
  5. Truncated grid. Let \(\Pi\) be the coordinate projection onto any set of grid indices, \(p=1\), \(\kappa_1\ge2\), and suppose \(\|(1-\Pi)v_{z_1}\|^2\le\epsilon^2\|v_{z_1}\|^2\) with \(\epsilon\le1/4\). Then \(\|\Pi G_Z\Pi\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge\lambda_{\max}(\Pi G_Z\Pi)\ge\tfrac45m_1(\kappa_1+1)\).
  6. Growth. If \(\varphi^2\ge c>0\) almost everywhere on \(s\le|u|\le s'\) with \(0\le s<s'\), then \(\kappa(\delta)\ge c\,(s'-s)\,e^{2|\delta|s}/\|\varphi\|_2^2\).

Proof. (1) Each \(v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}\) has rank one and trace \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_z=N_\varphi\) by the lemma. (2) and (3) Group the sum by real points and pairs. The lemma gives \[G_Z=\sum_{z\in Z\cap\mathbb R}{m_z\over N_\varphi}v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}+\sum_{j=1}^pm_j\bigl[(\kappa_j+1)e_{x_j}e_{x_j}^{\mathsf T}-(\kappa_j-1)e_{y_j}e_{y_j}^{\mathsf T}\bigr],\] a real symmetric finite-rank operator. It is a positive semidefinite operator minus the positive semidefinite operator \(\sum_jm_j(\kappa_j-1)e_{y_j}e_{y_j}^{\mathsf T}\) of rank at most \(p\), so it is nonnegative on a subspace of codimension at most \(p\), which gives (2). Evaluating the quadratic form at the unit vector \(e_{x_{j_0}}\), dropping the nonnegative terms other than the \(j_0\) one and using \(e_{x_{j_0}}\cdot e_{y_{j_0}}=0\) gives (3). (4) is (3) with an empty sum, and \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) is the sum of the squared eigenvalues. (5) Write \(e_x,e_y,\kappa,m\) for the pair. Since \(|x|^2\ge|v|^2/2\) and, as \(\kappa\ge2\), \(|y|^2=(\kappa-1)N_\varphi/2\ge\kappa N_\varphi/4=|v|^2/4\), and \(|(1-\Pi)x|,|(1-\Pi)y|\le|(1-\Pi)v|\) coordinatewise, we get \(|(1-\Pi)e_x|^2\le2\epsilon^2\) and \(|(1-\Pi)e_y|^2\le4\epsilon^2\). Let \(u=\Pi e_x/|\Pi e_x|\), where \(|\Pi e_x|^2\ge1-2\epsilon^2\). The compressed on-line terms stay nonnegative, \(e_x\cdot\Pi e_x=|\Pi e_x|^2\), and \(e_y\cdot\Pi e_x=-((1-\Pi)e_y)\cdot((1-\Pi)e_x)\) because \(e_y\cdot e_x=0\). Hence \[u^{\mathsf T}G_Zu\ge m(\kappa+1)(1-2\epsilon^2)-m(\kappa-1){8\epsilon^4\over1-2\epsilon^2}\ge m(\kappa+1)\Bigl({7\over8}-{1\over28}\Bigr)\ge{4\over5}m(\kappa+1)\] for \(\epsilon\le1/4\). (6) \(\int\varphi^2\cosh(2\delta u)\,du\ge2c\int_s^{s'}\cosh(2\delta u)\,du\ge c(s'-s)e^{2|\delta|s}\), using both signs of \(u\) and \(\cosh t\ge e^{|t|}/2\). \(\square\)

What the trace sees. By (1) the trace premise of the density proof counts points and is blind to their distance from the line. All dependence on \(\delta\) sits in the second moment, through \(\kappa\).

Bandwidth threshold (a computation from the proposition, not a theorem about \(\zeta\)). Take \(\Lambda>4\), fixed \(\theta\) and bounded \(\eta\). For the density windows, \(\varphi^2(u)=\psi(u/\Lambda)\) for \(|u|\le\Lambda/2-1\) with \(\psi\ge\psi_{\min}=\cos(1/\sqrt2)>0.76\) there, so (6) with \(s=\Lambda/2-2\), \(s'=\Lambda/2-1\) gives \(\kappa\ge\psi_{\min}e^{\delta(\Lambda-4)}/\|\varphi\|_2^2\). By (4), a configuration with a solitary pair at distance \(\delta\) satisfies \[\delta\le{\log\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\log(\|\varphi\|_2^2/\psi_{\min})\over\Lambda-4}.\] Insert a bound \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le T^{1+\eta}\) at bandwidth \(\Lambda=\theta\log(T/2\pi)\), with \(\|\varphi\|_2^2=O(\Lambda)\): then \(\delta\le(1+\eta)/(2\theta)+O(\log\log T/\log T)\). The imported premise \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=(R+o(1))N\) with \(N\sim(T/2\pi)\log T\) is the case \(\theta=1\), \(\eta=o(1)\): it yields \(\delta\le1/2+O(\log\log T/\log T)\), which is no restriction, since every nontrivial zero has \(\delta<1/2\). Equally, a zero with \(\delta\) close to \(1/2\) does not contradict the imported premise by this route. A restriction on a solitary zero would need \(\theta>1\): a second-moment bound for test functions longer than \(\log T\). The import contains none. Whether its prime-side argument extends past \(\theta=1\) is not asserted either way: it requires reading the source's second-moment proof, which this cycle, spent on the zero side, did not do; it is listed under Sub-ideas.

Gaps in applying this to the imported form. The proposition concerns the complete grid and any finite configuration. The import's \(G\) uses the zeros in a window, the cut grid and a window that is smoothed, and its premise is an upper bound for the cut form; (5) transfers the lower bound to the cut form under a tail hypothesis \(\epsilon\le1/4\) that is not verified here for the import's window. For a single zero the outcome at \(\theta=1\) is vacuous regardless of these gaps. For many zeros it is not known to be: an aggregate version of part (4), if one held with small loss, would turn the imported premise into a zero-density bound, so the gaps and the many-pair question matter at \(\theta=1\) as well (see the reviewer's suggestions).

Two-pair cancellation

Proposition (two-pair cancellation). prop:two-pair-cancellation. Let \(\varphi\) be the indicator of \(I\), so \(N_\varphi=\Lambda^2\), \(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w/N_\varphi=\operatorname{sinc}((z-w)\Lambda/2)\) with \(\operatorname{sinc}x=\sin x/x\), and \(\kappa=\sinh b/b\) for \(b=\delta\Lambda\). For \(\delta>0\), real \(t\ne0\) and the configuration \(Z=\{\pm i\delta,\ t\pm i\delta\}\) with multiplicities one, \[\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=4+4\kappa^2+4\operatorname{sinc}^2(t\Lambda/2)+4\Re\operatorname{sinc}^2\bigl((t+2i\delta)\Lambda/2\bigr).\] At \(t=\pi/\Lambda\), with \(a=\pi/2\), \[\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=4+{16\over\pi^2}+4\kappa^2{3a^2b^2+a^4\over(b^2+a^2)^2}-{4(b^2-a^2)\over(b^2+a^2)^2}\le4+{16\over\pi^2}+{3\pi^2\kappa^2\over b^2}\quad(b\ge\pi/2).\] Each of the two pairs alone has \(\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=2\kappa^2+2\). Hence there is no constant \(c>0\) with \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge c\sum_j(\kappa_j^2+1)\) for all configurations: the ratio tends to zero along \(b\to\infty\).

Proof. For any configuration, \(\|G_Z\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\operatorname{tr}(G_Z^2)=N_\varphi^{-2}\sum_{z,w}m_zm_w(v_z^{\mathsf T}v_w)^2\), which here is \(\sum_{z,w\in Z}\operatorname{sinc}^2((z-w)\Lambda/2)\) by the lemma and \(\widehat{\varphi^2}(w)=2\sin(w\Lambda/2)/w\). Of the sixteen ordered pairs, four are diagonal and give \(1\) each. Four join a point to its conjugate, with difference \(\mp2i\delta\) and \(\operatorname{sinc}(\mp ib)=\sinh b/b=\kappa\). Four join points with equal imaginary parts, with difference \(\mp t\). The last four have the differences \(t+2i\delta\), \(-t-2i\delta\), \(t-2i\delta\), \(-t+2i\delta\), once each; \(\operatorname{sinc}\) is even and \(\operatorname{sinc}^2\) at conjugate arguments gives conjugate values, for a total of \(4\Re\operatorname{sinc}^2((t+2i\delta)\Lambda/2)\). At \(t\Lambda=\pi\): \(\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi/2)=4/\pi^2\) and \(\sin(a+ib)=\cosh b\), so the last term is \(4\cosh^2b\,\Re(a+ib)^{-2}=-4\cosh^2b\,(b^2-a^2)/(b^2+a^2)^2\). With \(\cosh^2b=\sinh^2b+1\) and \(\sinh^2b=\kappa^2b^2\), \[4\kappa^2-{4\kappa^2b^2(b^2-a^2)\over(b^2+a^2)^2}=4\kappa^2{(b^2+a^2)^2-b^2(b^2-a^2)\over(b^2+a^2)^2}=4\kappa^2{3a^2b^2+a^4\over(b^2+a^2)^2},\] which gives the closed form. For the inequality, \((3a^2b^2+a^4)/(b^2+a^2)^2\le3a^2/b^2\) because \((3a^2b^2+a^4)b^2\le3a^2(b^2+a^2)^2\), and \(4\cdot3a^2=3\pi^2\); the last term of the closed form is nonpositive for \(b\ge a\). \(\square\)

What the cancellation means. The solitary hypothesis in part (4) cannot be replaced by a sum over pairs at a constant cost. Two off-line zeros at the same distance \(\delta\) and height difference \(\pi/\Lambda\) reduce the leading \(\kappa^2\) by the factor \(3\pi^2/(4b^2)\), which is a power of \(\log T\) when \(b=\delta\theta\log T\). Such a loss does not change the exponent in the bandwidth threshold. An interval of unit height holds up to order \(\log T\) zeros, and the loss for many close pairs is not computed here. That it could reach a power of \(T\) is an untested hypothesis and no decision rests on it; computing it is the first sub-idea and the proposed next step.
