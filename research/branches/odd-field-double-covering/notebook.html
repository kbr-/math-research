Working mathematical context

Goal and completed part

The goal is \(f_q(n,k,1)=n(q-1)+q(k-1)-1\), for every fixed odd prime power q and \(k\ge2\) at sufficiently large n. Planes form a multiset, every nonzero point has at least k incidences, and zero at most \(k-1\). The k=2 result holds in every dimension. The high-origin theorem gives lower bounds \(D+q(k-1)\) and \(D+q(k-1)-1\) for origin orders \(k-1\) and \(k-2\), where \(D=n(q-1)\); matching covers are recorded.

Degree interface and obstruction

With \(F_i=x_i^q-x_i\), the unique expansion \(P=\sum_\beta F^\beta R_\beta\), \(\deg_{x_i}R_\beta<q\), preserves \(q|\beta|+\deg R_\beta\le\deg P\). Lowest grid jets determine the first nonzero layer. But for \(3\le k\le q\), the truncated lift of \(\Delta=\prod_i(1-x_i^{q-1})\) gives arbitrary polynomials of degree \(D+(q-1)(k-1)\), below target. Low-origin cases therefore need factor information.

Exact remaining ternary boundary

For \(k=q=3\), high origin orders already meet target \(2n+5\). A smaller cover must avoid zero and have exactly \(2n+4\) planes. It is equivalent to a full-support n-dimensional \(C\subseteq\mathbb F_3^{2n+4}\) with \(C\subseteq C^\perp\cap\mathbf1^\perp\) and \(C\cap\{0,1\}^{2n+4}=\{0\}\). The proof and infinite moment-only countercontrol must be read before relying on this formulation. No dimension bound for that exact class is established.

Controls and broader sufficient endpoint

Scalar-bundled triple covers have logarithmically growing excess by a Hamming argument; ordinary distance alone does not characterize general covers. Exact unpaired covers of size \(2n+4\) exist through \(n=5\). The local lift cannot iterate indefinitely because \(\Phi=B+2E\) decreases; larger/slack-sensitive replacements are outside that obstruction. Growth \(h_q(n,3)-D\to\infty\) for each fixed odd q remains a sufficient, not necessary, endpoint for all fixed \(k\). These qualifications distinguish the full goal from the ternary first test.

Gaps identified by formalization

No formalization has been assigned or performed for this thread. The recorded general arguments, finite classifications and code criterion are not Lean-verified. Their exact scopes and remaining gaps are linked above; no discrepancy has been discovered through formalization.

A first-jet degree bound, including the exceptional simple zero

Working lemma. Let \(q\) be any prime power, \(n\ge1\), and \(P\in\mathbb F_q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\). Suppose \(\operatorname{mult}_a(P)\ge2\) for every nonzero \(a\in\mathbb F_q^n\), where multiplicity is Hasse multiplicity. Put \(D=n(q-1)\). If \(P(0)\ne0\), then \(\deg P\ge D+q-1\). If \(\operatorname{mult}_0(P)=1\), then \(\deg P\ge D+q\). In particular multiplicity at most one at zero implies degree at least \(D+q-1\). No restriction on dimension or on the characteristic relative to the degree is imposed.

Degree-controlled division and the one-point selector. Set \(F_i=x_i^q-x_i\). Repeated monic division in each variable writes every polynomial of total degree \(d\) as

\[ P=R+\sum_{i=1}^n F_iH_i,\qquad \deg_{x_j}R<q\ (j\le n),\qquad \deg R\le d,\quad \deg H_i\le d-q. \]

The last bound follows directly during division: canceling a monomial divisible by \(x_i^q\) subtracts \(F_i\) times a monomial of degree at most \(d-q\), and the introduced term has lower degree. Iterating in all variables never increases total degree. If \(d<q\), the corresponding cofactors are zero. A polynomial of individual degrees below \(q\) is determined by its values on the full grid: induct on the number of variables, using the univariate root bound in the last variable. Thus the reduced representative of a function supported at zero is uniquely

\[ c\Delta,\qquad \Delta=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-x_j^{q-1}),\qquad c=P(0). \]

The polynomial \(\Delta\) has degree \(D\) and a nonzero coefficient at \(\prod_jx_j^{q-1}\). Reduction never increases degree, so every polynomial nonzero at zero and zero elsewhere has degree at least \(D\). This proves the needed one-point bound directly rather than assuming it for a new multiplicity algebra.

First derivatives retain the missing information. Since \(q=0\) in \(\mathbb F_q\), we have \(\partial_j F_i=-\mathbf1_{i=j}\). At every grid point \(a\), the division identity therefore gives

\[ \partial_iP(a)=c\,\partial_i\Delta(a)-H_i(a). \]

Order at least two is equivalent to vanishing of the value and all first derivatives over any characteristic: these are exactly the constant and degree-one coefficients of \(P(a+z)\). No higher ordinary derivatives or factorial divisions are used. All first derivatives above are zero at nonzero grid points.

Case 1: origin value nonzero. Here \(c\ne0\), and the value bound first gives \(d\ge D\). Suppose for contradiction that \(d<D+q-1\). Then \(\deg H_i\le D-2\), while

\[ \partial_i\Delta=x_i^{q-2}\prod_{j\ne i}(1-x_j^{q-1}) \]

is individually reduced and has degree exactly \(D-1\), with nonzero top coefficient. The polynomial \(H_i-c\partial_i\Delta\) vanishes at every nonzero grid point and has degree at most \(D-1\). By the one-point bound it must vanish at zero as well. Reduce it modulo the \(F_j\): its remainder must be zero by value uniqueness. But the remainder of \(H_i\) has degree at most \(D-2\) and cannot cancel the degree-\(D-1\) term of \(c\partial_i\Delta\). This is a contradiction. Negative degree bounds mean the zero polynomial, so the same reasoning includes \(q=2,n=1\).

Case 2: origin a simple zero. Now \(c=0\), so the value remainder \(R\) is zero. Some first derivative \(\partial_iP(0)\) is nonzero. The displayed derivative identity implies that \(H_i\) vanishes at every nonzero grid point and \(H_i(0)=-\partial_iP(0)\ne0\). Hence \(\deg H_i\ge D\) by the one-point bound. Since \(\deg H_i\le d-q\), we obtain \(d\ge D+q\). This closes the case not covered by a direct use of the nonvanishing-origin punctured bound.

Exact covering number

Working theorem. For every prime power \(q\ge2\) and every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \boxed{f_q(n,2,1)=(q-1)(n+1).} \]

Lower bound. Let the cover contain \(m\) proper affine hyperplanes, with repetitions allowed, and choose a nonconstant affine defining form \(L_r\) for each occurrence. The formal product \(P=\prod_{r=1}^mL_r\) is nonzero and has total degree exactly \(m\): its top homogeneous part is a product of nonzero linear polynomials in an integral domain. At a point \(a\), each incident form has order one and every other form has order zero; products add orders because their lowest homogeneous parts have nonzero product. Consequently \(P\) has order at least two at every nonzero point and order zero or one at the origin. The lemma gives \(m\ge n(q-1)+q-1\); if the origin is covered once it gives the stronger \(m\ge n(q-1)+q\). Repeated planes are counted correctly by this argument.

Matching construction. Take every plane \(x_i=c\), for \(i\le n\) and \(c\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), and every plane \(\sum_i x_i=c\), for \(c\in\mathbb F_q^\times\). There are \((q-1)(n+1)\) occurrences and none covers zero. A vector with at least two nonzero coordinates is covered by at least two coordinate planes. A vector with exactly one nonzero coordinate is covered once by a coordinate plane and once by the sum plane with that same nonzero value. These exhaust nonzero vectors. When \(n=1\), the two families coincide but their repeated occurrences give the required double coverage. This is the source's construction, not a new upper bound. The stronger simple-origin bound is also attained by adding any plane through zero to this cover.

Scope. This proves the branch's odd-prime-power target for every positive dimension, stronger than the sufficiently-large-dimension quantifier. It does not settle Question 4.2 for \(k\ge3\). The argument does not use the frontier theorem or claim novelty for degree-controlled division, one-point interpolation or the previously known uncovered-origin bound.

A dimension-uniform counterfamily to the polynomial relaxation

Working proposition. For every prime power \(q\), \(n\ge1\), and \(3\le k\le q\), there is a polynomial \(Q_k\) with \(Q_k(0)=1\), multiplicity at least \(k\) at every nonzero point, and

\[\deg Q_k\le n(q-1)+(q-1)(k-1)<n(q-1)+q(k-1)-1.\]

Thus the covering target cannot follow from these value and multiplicity hypotheses alone, even for arbitrarily large dimension. This does not assert that \(Q_k\) factors into affine polynomials.

Construction and proof. Put \(D=n(q-1)\), \(F_i=x_i^q-x_i\) and \(\Delta=\prod_i(1-x_i^{q-1})\). Write \(D^{[\beta]}\) for the Hasse derivative, the coefficient of \(z^\beta\) in translation by \(z\). Define the finite polynomial

\[Q_k(x)=\sum_{|\beta|<k}F(x)^\beta D^{[\beta]}\Delta(x).\]

Taylor's polynomial identity gives \(\Delta(x+F(x))=\sum_\beta F(x)^\beta D^{[\beta]}\Delta(x)\). Since \(x_i+F_i=x_i^q\), the left side is \(\Delta(x)^q\). At any nonzero grid point, \(\Delta^q\) has order at least \(q\ge k\). Every omitted term has a product of at least \(k\) grid polynomials \(F_i\), so has order at least \(k\) at every grid point. Subtraction proves the required orders of \(Q_k\). At zero, all terms except \(\beta=0\) vanish and \(\Delta(0)=1\). Finally, each nonzero summand has degree at most \(q|\beta|+D-|\beta|=D+(q-1)|\beta|\). Taking \(|\beta|\le k-1\) proves the bound; its deficit from the proposed covering degree is \(k-2>0\). This proof uses Hasse coefficients, so no factorial division or characteristic restriction was concealed.

Falsification scope. Taking \(k=3\) works over every odd finite field, in every dimension. Ordinary reduced-value idempotence was not the only obstacle: even retaining all jets below order three does not force the target among arbitrary polynomials. A new covering proof must use affine factorization or another genuine restriction of hyperplane products. The completed double-cover theorem is unaffected because its target corresponds to \(k=2\), where the degree deficit in this construction is zero.

Checks and route assessment

Exact Hasse-jet matrices over \((q,n)=(3,1),(3,2),(3,3),(5,2)\) confirmed a nonzero origin value is feasible at degree \(D+2(q-1)\), one below the triple-cover target. Every matrix rank and dimension is in the retained report; the general construction above, not finite rank evidence, proves the obstruction. No affine factorization was inferred from the test.

Contribution to multiplicity-sensitive-lower-bound. The proposed unrestricted polynomial implication is refuted uniformly in dimension. The covering problem remains open, and further jet calculations that ignore factorization cannot prove the missing case. Next isolate the cases with origin multiplicity \(k-1\) or \(k-2\), where degree-controlled grid-ideal division may already suffice, then focus the factor-sensitive obligation on the remaining origin orders. All living sections were reviewed; the working context now preserves this obstruction. 20 September 2026 — The two highest origin multiplicities are settled for every k

Working general degree bounds and a sufficient reduction; the remaining low-origin affine-factor case is open. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Obligation and stopping point. After the unrestricted polynomial bound failed, isolate precisely where factorization is needed. For arbitrary \(k\), handle origin orders \(k-1\) and \(k-2\), then state a sufficient general covering endpoint for lower origin orders. This tests the general mechanism rather than another finite family.

Sharp high-origin bounds

Working theorem. Let \(q\) be a prime power, \(n\ge1\), \(k\ge2\), \(D=n(q-1)\), and let \(P\) have order at least \(k\) at every nonzero point of \(\mathbb F_q^n\). If its order at zero is \(k-1\), then \(\deg P\ge D+q(k-1)\). If its order at zero is \(k-2\), then \(\deg P\ge D+q(k-1)-1\). Both bounds are attained by affine-factor covering products with that exact origin order.

Degree-controlled grid expansion. For \(F_i=x_i^q-x_i\), repeated monic division gives a unique finite expansion

\[P=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb N^n} F^\beta R_\beta(x),\qquad \deg_{x_i}R_\beta<q.\]

Indeed the products \(F^\beta x^r\), with \(0\le r_i<q\), have distinct leading monomials \(x^{q\beta+r}\), each with coefficient one, and every other monomial in such a product has lower total degree. Induction on degree gives spanning and uniqueness. The same triangular property gives \(q|\beta|+\deg R_\beta\le\deg P\) for every nonzero coefficient: terms of maximum weighted degree have distinct top monomials and cannot cancel.

If \(P\) has order at least \(s\) at every grid point, all coefficients with \(|\beta|<s\) are zero. Otherwise choose the smallest nonzero layer \(t<s\). A nonzero reduced \(R_\beta\) has a nonzero grid value somewhere by interpolation uniqueness. At any grid point \(a\), \(F_i(a+z)=-z_i+z_i^q\); the degree-\(t\) Taylor part of \(P(a+z)\) is \((-1)^t\sum_{|\beta|=t}R_\beta(a)z^\beta\). Distinct monomials cannot cancel, contradicting the assumed order at every point.

Top allowed origin order. Put \(s=k-1\). All lower layers vanish, and order at least \(s+1\) off zero says each reduced coefficient \(R_\beta\) at layer \(s\) is supported only at zero. Thus \(R_\beta=c_\beta\Delta\), where \(\Delta=\prod_i(1-x_i^{q-1})\). Exact origin order \(s\) makes some \(c_\beta\ne0\). Its degree is \(D\), so the weighted-degree bound gives \(\deg P\ge qs+D\).

Next allowed origin order. Put \(s=k-2\). Again the leading layer is \(R_\beta=c_\beta\Delta\), with a nonzero vector of constants. At a nonzero grid point, the degree-\(s+1\) Taylor coefficients must also vanish. For \(|\gamma|=s+1\), their identity is

\[R_\gamma(a)=\sum_{i:\gamma_i>0}c_{\gamma-e_i}\,\partial_i\Delta(a).\]

To verify signs, the leading \(F\)-layer contributes \((-1)^s\sum_i c_{\gamma-e_i}\partial_i\Delta(a)\), while the next layer contributes \((-1)^{s+1}R_\gamma(a)\). Corrections from \(F_i=-z_i+z_i^q\) multiply \(\Delta(a)=0\) at this order, including when \(q=2\). Higher layers have larger order. Both sides are individually reduced, so value uniqueness gives

\[R_\gamma=\sum_{i:\gamma_i>0}c_{\gamma-e_i}\partial_i\Delta+t_\gamma\Delta.\]

If some \(t_\gamma\ne0\), that coefficient has degree \(D\) and already gives a stronger bound. Otherwise choose \(c_\beta\ne0\) and \(\gamma=\beta+e_i\). The coefficient of \(\partial_i\Delta\) is nonzero, and the top monomials of the different \(\partial_j\Delta\) are distinct, of degree \(D-1\). Thus this \(R_\gamma\) has degree \(D-1\), giving \(\deg P\ge q(s+1)+D-1\). This proves the theorem for all characteristics, without factorial division.

Sharp covering controls. For origin order \(k-1\), use the single coordinate cover of size \(D\), plus \(k-1\) complete parallel partitions, each of size \(q\). For origin order \(k-2\), use the double-cover construction, plus \(k-2\) complete parallel partitions. Each partition adds one incidence to every point, including zero. Their sizes attain the stated bounds. Thus these cases need no further computation or structural assumption.

A sufficient endpoint for the remaining origin orders

Let \(h_q(n,3)\) be the minimum number of affine hyperplane occurrences covering every nonzero point at least three times while avoiding zero. A sufficient, potentially stronger-than-necessary statement for the full fixed-\(k\) problem is

\[h_q(n,3)-n(q-1)\longrightarrow\infty\quad(n\longrightarrow\infty),\quad\text{for each fixed odd }q.\]

Proof of sufficiency. Fix \(q,k\). The two highest allowed origin orders satisfy the target by the theorem above. For origin order \(s\le k-3\), remove the \(s\) hyperplanes through zero. The remaining \(m-s\) planes avoid zero and cover every nonzero point at least \(k-s\ge3\) times. If \(m<D+q(k-1)-1\), then \(m-s\le D+q(k-1)-2\). The stated divergence excludes that for all sufficiently large \(n\). The known upper construction gives equality. Only this implication is proved; the divergence premise is open here and is not claimed necessary.

For \(k=3\) the unresolved case is exactly an origin-avoiding triple cover. Proving merely \(h_q(n,3)\ge D+2q-1\) eventually would settle that case; proving divergence would settle all fixed \(k\). Do not insist on the stronger endpoint if a direct weaker route becomes available.

Review and assessment. Checked the normal-form basis, filtration, lowest nonzero layer, next-layer signs, extension-field identity and sharp constructions. The high-origin part of the general lower-bound obligation is discharged, but the uniform low-origin case remains genuinely factor-sensitive in light of the polynomial counterfamily. The next cycle should look for structural or coding bounds on zero-avoiding triple covers near the one-cover minimum; refining arbitrary polynomial jets cannot address that gap. 20 September 2026 — Coding gives growth for bundled covers, but small unpaired covers defeat that shortcut

Working restricted bound and exact finite counterexamples to universal bundling; no general large-dimension bound. Produced by Codex (gpt-6-astra, medium reasoning).

Question and stopping point. Test the sufficient zero-avoiding triple-cover endpoint using a precise code model, and falsify any inference that near-minimum covers must consist of complete scalar bundles. Stop at an applicable general bound or a demonstrated scope obstruction.

The exact code condition and the bundled case

Normalize each zero-avoiding plane to \(a_r\cdot x=1\), with nonzero \(a_r\), and form the matrix \(A\) with these rows. It has rank \(n\): a nonzero vector in its kernel would receive no covering. The associated linear code \(C=A\mathbb F_q^n\) has dimension \(n\). It describes an r-fold cover exactly when every nonzero codeword has at least r occurrences of each nonzero field symbol. Indeed the condition for symbol one is precisely incidence, and applying it to \(\lambda x\) gives the condition for every other symbol. Thus minimum Hamming distance at least \(r(q-1)\) is necessary but not the full condition.

Working restricted proposition. Suppose the cover is a multiset union of complete bundles \(b_i\cdot x=c\), one plane for each \(c\ne0\), for \(1\le i\le L\). Then \(m=(q-1)L\), and the number of coverings of x is the Hamming weight of \((b_i\cdot x)_{i\le L}\). Triple covering makes this a dimension-n, length-L code of distance at least three. Radius-one Hamming balls around its \(q^n\) words are disjoint, each containing \(1+L(q-1)\) vectors. Hence

\[q^n(1+L(q-1))\le q^L,\qquad m-n(q-1)\ge(q-1)\log_q(1+L(q-1)).\]

Since \(L\ge n\), this excess diverges. This proves the sufficient growth endpoint only for bundled covers. Replacing an arbitrary cover by all its scalar multiples can multiply the plane count by \(q-1\); it does not preserve an additive-constant excess. Fractional averaging likewise does not supply an integral bundled cover of the same size.

Why ordinary distance alone is too weak. Fix \(q>2\), C, and \(d_0=3(q-1)\). For all sufficiently large n there exist length \(m=(q-1)n+C\), dimension-n codes with distance at least \(d_0\) that contain the all-one vector. To see this, take that vector as one generator and choose the remaining n-1 generator vectors uniformly and independently. Every combination using at least one random generator is uniform on \(\mathbb F_q^m\). The union bound for a word of weight below \(d_0\) is at most

\[(q^n-q)q^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{d_0-1}\binom mj(q-1)^j=o(1).\]

The nonzero combinations using only the fixed generator have weight m; excluding weight zero among the other combinations also ensures rank n. Yet the all-one word contains no occurrence of another nonzero symbol, so these codes are not covers. This verifies the failure of the ordinary-distance surrogate in the relevant linear-size regime, rather than merely observing a missing converse.

Finite controls refute universal scalar pairing

Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the following lists of normals define planes \(a\cdot x=1\), one occurrence each. Direct integer incidence evaluation verifies that every nonzero vector is covered at least three times, while zero is uncovered:

n=3, m=10:
001, 002, 011, 021, 102, 112, 121, 201, 210, 221.
n=4, m=12:
0001, 0021, 0120, 0122, 0210, 0222,
1022, 1102, 1212, 2201, 2212, 2220.

Both sizes are \(2n+4\), one below the conjectured eventual triple-cover value \(2n+5\). They do not refute its sufficiently-large-n statement. They are not scalar-paired: for example the n=3 list contains 011 but not 022, and the n=4 list contains 0001 but not 0002. Since normalization to right side one is unique, this is a property of the actual hyperplane multiset, not its notation. Thus a universal all-dimension bundling assertion is false; an eventual structural theorem remains unproved.

Require both coverage preservation and a replenished input resource; a larger isolated cover is not the endpoint. The operation tested replaces two complete scalar pairs by six planes using their two forms and one new coordinate, adding two planes per dimension.

A coverage-preserving local replacement

Working proposition. Suppose a zero-avoiding triple cover over \(\mathbb F_3^n\) contains the pairs \(u=1,2\) and \(v=1,2\), counted with multiplicities. Remove those four occurrences, introduce a coordinate t, and add the six planes whose left sides are

\[t,\quad u+t,\quad u+2t,\quad u+v+2t,\quad u+2v+2t,\quad 2u+t,\]

each equal to one. Extend all other old planes independently of t. The result is a zero-avoiding triple cover in dimension \(n+1\), with two more planes.

Verification of the local rule. Let \(N(u,v,t)\) count the six new incidences, and let \(w(u,v)=\mathbf1_{u\ne0}+\mathbf1_{v\ne0}\), the number of removed incidences. When both u,v are nonzero, direct substitution gives \(N=2\) for every t. When exactly one is nonzero, the three counts as t varies are a permutation of \((4,1,1)\). When u=v=0, the counts for t=0,1,2 are \((0,3,3)\). These 27 exact substitutions prove \(N\ge w\) everywhere, with three coverings on the new axis away from zero. For a nonzero old vector the untouched planes plus the replacement preserve at least three coverings. On the new axis the new planes supply three. Zero remains uncovered. No rank assumption on u,v is needed for this implication.

Starting from the eight-plane triple cover in dimension two consisting of all four projective directions with both nonzero offsets, the rule gives exactly checked covers of sizes 10, 12 and 14 in dimensions 3,4,5. Their numbers of complete scalar pairs are respectively 3,2,1 (the seed has four). Full normal lists and integer incidences are retained in the iterate report. These examples remain below the conjectured eventual value in finitely many dimensions only.

Why this entire local domination scheme cannot iterate forever

Consider any six-plane replacement of the same type, not just the displayed gadget: each new equation is a linear combination of u,v,t equal to one, its coverage dominates w on all attainable triples, and it covers the new axis at least three times. Every new plane must have a nonzero t coefficient, since each plane can cover at most one of the two nonzero axis points and six total incidences are required.

Independent inputs: finite exhaustive certificate. If u,v are independent, there are exactly 18 normalized possible equations \(au+bv+ct=1\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb F_3\), \(c=1,2\). Enumerate all multisets of six equations: \(\binom{23}{6}=100947\). Exact integer evaluation of the 27 coverage requirements leaves 18 valid multisets, each with at most one complete scalar pair. The exhaustive program uses vectorized integer sums, and its full output lists every valid multiset and its pair count. No floating-point infeasibility claim is used. This finite classification is the computational component of the resource proof.

Dependent inputs: analytic check. The removed planes are two copies of the same scalar pair. Write \(x_a,y_a\) for multiplicities of \(au+t=1\) and \(au-t=1\), respectively. Axis coverage gives \(\sum x_a=\sum y_a=3\). For either nonzero u, summing over t gives six incidences, so domination of the removed count two forces exactly two at each t. At u=1 the equations are \(x_1+y_1=2,x_0+y_2=2,x_2+y_0=2\); at u=2 they are \(x_2+y_2=2,x_0+y_1=2,x_1+y_0=2\). Thus all y's are equal, all x's are equal, and the axis sums force every multiplicity to be one. This returns three distinct scalar pairs and no repeated pair. The 462-multiset enumeration independently confirms this unique solution.

Potential argument. For the full cover let b range over the multiplicities of its complete scalar bundles (the minimum multiplicity of the two opposite normalized normals). Put \(B=\sum b\), \(E=\sum\max(b-1,0)\), and \(\Phi=B+2E\). Independent inputs consume two pairs and return at most one, with no repeated output pair, so \(\Phi\) drops by at least one. Dependent inputs consume two copies of one pair and return three distinct pairs; B rises by one but E falls by at least one, so again \(\Phi\) drops by at least one. New planes have nonzero t coefficient and cannot form additional pairs with untouched old planes, whose t coefficient is zero. Invertible coordinate changes preserve scalar proportionality and pair multiplicities. Hence any sequence of these allowed replacements has length at most its initial \(\Phi\).

This rules out an infinite counterexample family obtained by this particular pointwise-domination scheme. It does not exclude replacements of larger groups, extra dependence on old coordinates, exploitation of slack in the full cover, or a different structural proof of the conjecture.

20 September 2026 — Critical ternary covers require a binary-avoiding self-orthogonal code

General claim under review. For every fixed odd prime power q and fixed \(k\ge2\), the target is \(f_q(n,k,1)=n(q-1)+q(k-1)-1\) at sufficiently large n. The full goal is not proved by the high-origin cases, scalar-bundled covers, finite exceptions or the local lift. Exact criterion at the ternary triple-cover boundary

Working proposition. An origin-avoiding ternary triple cover with at most \(2n+4\) planes exists if and only if there is a full-support n-dimensional linear subspace \(C\subseteq\mathbb F_3^{2n+4}\) satisfying

\[C\subseteq C^\perp,\qquad C\subseteq\mathbf1^\perp,\qquad C\cap\{0,1\}^{2n+4}=\{0\}.\]

Every such cover has exactly \(2n+4\) planes. Full support means that no coordinate is identically zero on C. Together with the high-origin theorem, this identifies the only possible way the ternary \(k=3\) target can fail in a fixed dimension. It does not give a dimension bound for these codes.

Necessity: degree saturation forces vanishing origin jets. Normalize the covering product as \(P=\prod_{r=1}^m(1-a_r\cdot x)\), so \(P(0)=1\) and \(\deg P=m\). Use the degree-filtered expansion in the previous proof, with \(F_i=x_i^3-x_i\), \(\Delta=\prod_i(1-x_i^2)\), \(D=2n\). Write \(b_i=\partial_iP(0)\). Its first layers are \(R_0=\Delta\) and \(R_{e_i}=\partial_i\Delta-b_i\Delta\). For \(|\gamma|=2\), vanishing of the off-origin second Hasse jets and reduced-value uniqueness imply

\[R_\gamma=D^{[\gamma]}\Delta-\sum_{i:\gamma_i>0}b_i D^{[\gamma-e_i]}\Delta+c_\gamma\Delta.\]

Here \(c_\gamma=D^{[\gamma]}P(0)\). To check the formula, the Hasse product rule gives \(D^{[\gamma]}\Delta-\sum_iD^{[\gamma-e_i]}R_{e_i}+R_\gamma=0\) off zero; the sum of derivatives of \(\partial_i\Delta\) is \(2D^{[\gamma]}\Delta\). Also \(F_i(a+z)=-z_i+z_i^3\) has no quadratic correction and \(\partial_i\Delta(0)=0\), giving the claimed origin constant.

If \(m\le D+4\), the coefficient bound is \(\deg R_\gamma\le D-2\). The degree-D term forces \(c_\gamma=0\). Taking \(\gamma=2e_i\), the degree-\(D-1\) term then forces \(b_i=0\), since \(\partial_i\Delta\) has a nonzero top coefficient. Finally \(D^{[2e_i]}\Delta\) has degree exactly \(D-2\), so \(m<D+4\) is impossible. Thus m=D+4, and the linear and quadratic homogeneous terms of P at zero vanish.

Let A have the normals \(a_r\) as rows. The linear term gives \(\sum_ra_r=0\). Writing \(L_r=a_r\cdot x\), the quadratic term is \(\sum_{r<s}L_rL_s=0\). Since 2 is invertible in \(\mathbb F_3\), the identity \(2\sum_{r<s}L_rL_s=(\sum_rL_r)^2-\sum_rL_r^2\) gives \(A^TA=0\). The cover ensures rank A=n, and its proper planes ensure full support. Thus \(C=A\mathbb F_3^n\) is self-orthogonal and orthogonal to the all-one vector. No nonzero word of C is binary: its negative would have no coordinate equal to one and hence would correspond to an uncovered nonzero input.

Sufficiency. Choose a basis of such a C and use its coordinate functionals as the rows of A. Full support gives proper zero-avoiding planes \(a_r\cdot x=1\), and rank n gives nonzero words on nonzero inputs. For any \(c\in C\), let \(N_1,N_2\) count its one and two coordinates. Orthogonality gives \(N_1-N_2=0\) and \(N_1+N_2=0\) modulo three. Hence both counts are multiples of three. The binary-avoidance condition, applied to c and -c, makes both positive whenever c is nonzero. Thus both are at least three, proving the triple covering. This is an exact condition, not a replacement by minimum distance alone.

Falsification of the simpler moment-only route

The two orthogonality conditions and full support alone allow unbounded dimension at exactly this length. For \(t\ge3\), use t blocks of length four, with \(U=(1,1,1,0)\) and \(V=(0,1,2,1)\). Let C consist of blocks \(u_iU+v_iV\) subject to \(\sum_i u_i=\sum_i v_i=0\). The block vectors are independent, so \(\dim C=2t-2=n\) and its length \(4t=2n+4\). We have \(U\cdot U=V\cdot V=U\cdot V=0\), so C is self-orthogonal. Coordinate sums equal \(\sum_i v_i=0\); each coordinate is nonzero on some allowed choice, so support is full. But taking u=1 in three blocks, all other u and all v zero, gives a nonzero binary word. Thus these are not covers. This explicit infinite control refutes an attempted dimension bound based solely on the moment equations.

Exact arithmetic confirms the new moment identities for all four lifted-cover witnesses and checks the control at t=3,4,5, including rank, full support and binary-word membership. The full report retains matrices and controls. The constructions above prove the general assertions; the checks do not establish nonexistence of the required binary-avoiding codes.
