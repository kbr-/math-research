A subset-coded old family and its exact projection

Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(n\ge2\), \(r\ge2\), \(m\ge r\), and choose \(n+1\) distinct \(r\)-subsets \(I_1,\ldots,I_{n+1}\) of \([m]\). Use fractional variables \(x_{u,j}\), \(u\in[m],j\in[n]\), and abbreviate \[ t_{a,j}=\prod_{u\in I_a}x_{u,j}. \] Define \(\Gamma_I\) by Booleanity, rows \(\sum_jt_{a,j}-1\), same-hole collisions \(t_{a,j}t_{b,j}\) for \(a\ne b\), and same-pigeon collisions \(t_{a,j}t_{a,k}\) for \(j\ne k\). Products are ordinary polynomials with their unreduced degree \(2r\). This is a selected-subset encoding, not a silent replacement of the full generalized-PHP generator list. The restriction \(r\ge2\) selects the intended logarithmic family; the core projection also works at \(r=1\).

It is unsatisfiable: each Boolean row of \(t\)'s must have an occupied hole; the two collision families make these choices a functional injection of \(n+1\) pigeons into \(n\) holes. Take \(r=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil\), \(m=2r\), and select \(n+1\) of the \(2^r\) sets choosing one element from each fixed pair. The family is fixed independently of proof-size exponents, has \(N=mn=O(n\log n)\) variables, and has a polynomial-size bounded-depth Boolean presentation of its equations. No new general Frege-to-source compiler is claimed for this presentation.

Let \(y_{a,j}\) be ordinary functional PHP with \(n+1\) pigeons and \(n\) holes. Define one linear substitution \[ \phi(x_{u,j})=\sum_{a:u\in I_a}y_{a,j}. \tag{1} \] Modulo its Boolean and same-hole exclusion axioms, \(\phi(t_{a,j})-y_{a,j}\) has an ordinary certificate through \(r\). To see this, expand the product: any term selecting different pigeons has a same-hole collision; a surviving same-pigeon term requires \(I_a\subseteq I_b\), hence \(a=b\) by equal cardinalities. Reduce \(y_{a,j}^r\) by Booleanity. Every reduction costs at most \(r\).

Consequently row images have certificates through \(r\), and each collision image follows from the corresponding ordinary collision through \(2r\), by substituting the two feature corrections and telescoping their product. Boolean images have certificates through two. Thus every degree-\(B\) PC refutation transfers through \(\max(B,2r)\), and the imported ordinary-PHP degree barrier applies to this family. The same direct generator certificates apply to NS with their original costs. This reuses Razborov's subset projection, with its selected row family and all generator images made explicit.

An affine dictionary must still be large

Lemma (subset feature inventory). Assume \[ 2r\le D\le\lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor. \tag{2} \] In the vector quotient by \(C_D(\Gamma_I)\), the span of \(1\) and all \(t_{a,j}\) has dimension \(n^2\). In particular, \(1\) and the \(n^2-1\) features \(t_{a,j}\) with \(j<n\) are independent. Suppose all those features are represented affinely using the original \(N=mn\) variables and \(R\) additional polynomial features \(Q_\ell(x)\), with constant coefficients and each representation error in \(C_D(\Gamma_I)\). Then \[ R\ge n^2-mn-1. \tag{3} \] The \(Q_\ell\) may be arbitrary polynomials; imposing small arity or expanded size cannot improve this lower bound.

Proof. If \(c+\sum a_{a,j}t_{a,j}\in C_D(\Gamma_I)\), apply (1). Since \(D\ge2r\), the generator transfer and feature corrections give \(c+\sum a_{a,j}y_{a,j}\in C_D(\mathrm{FPHP}_{n}^{n+1})\). The functional affine-consequence rigidity theorem, recovered from historical Theorem 9.8 with its verified stronger-base degree input, says precisely that this is a combination of ordinary row equations. Conversely every corresponding combination of the \(t\)-rows is an axiom combination in \(\Gamma_I\). Thus the only relations among the \(1+(n+1)n\) formal coordinates are the \(n+1\) independent row equations, giving dimension \(n^2\). Comparing the last-hole coefficients proves the displayed independent subfamily. This is the historical coordinate-quotient argument after an explicit hard-base transfer.

The classes of \(1,x_{u,j},Q_\ell\) span a space of dimension at most \(1+mn+R\). It contains the preceding \(n^2\)-dimensional span if all required affine representations exist, proving (3). Even if some \(Q_\ell\) have higher degree, the same dimension argument can be made in the full polynomial vector space modulo the linear subspace \(C_D\); it does not treat \(C_D\) as an ideal. Polynomial coefficients on the \(Q_\ell\), nonlinear feature use, or a different source-specific transformation are outside the affine-dictionary claim. \(\square\)

For \(m=O(\log n)\), (3) is \(\Omega(n^2)=\Omega(N^2/\log^2 N)\), exceeding \(N^{1+\epsilon(1-\eta)}\) for any fixed \(0<\epsilon,\eta<1\). Thus naming a full affine dictionary cannot meet the cited sparse-extension count, even after giving all original affine coordinates for free. Every XOR-gadget restriction that leaves one bit per pair preserves this conclusion on a logical pullback, by the earlier invertible-affine restriction lemma. No claim is made about arbitrary restrictions that change the logical instance.

Dahiya–Mahajan–Mouli's Definition 2.2 and Theorem 1.2 require nonrecursive definitions with an expanded monomial-size bound and the stated subquadratic count. Their later cleanup also pays for every affected hole, not only assigned ones. Those conditions cannot be replaced by arithmetic formula size or by (3)'s unrestricted dictionary. This is a limitation of that conversion, not source hardness.

For a tuple \(G_i(y_{\cdot,j})\), choose at each state where some \(G_i\ne0\) one nonzero input and its inverse as the corresponding coefficient. Interpolate these choices with the \(d_a\)'s to obtain affine \(\beta_i\). In characteristic two all choices are binary. Put them in the first coefficient row and set the other rows to zero. Every coefficient-field image \(\beta_i^2-\beta_i\) has its degree-two Boolean certificate. These are the already verified selectors; their field axioms are not omitted.

A complete shared source collapses to one affine level

Its inputs may be arbitrary polynomials in that column's original variables and earlier coefficient variables assigned to the same column. No other cross-column polynomial dependence or terminal-to-terminal dependence is included. Consequently, when \(r\) and the requested proof degree are polylogarithmic in \(n\) and terminal block inventory is polynomial, the source has no such refutation for sufficiently large \(n\), by the existing unary-affine endpoint. The stronger stored power range also applies whenever its exact hypotheses hold. The restriction \(m=O(\log n)\) was used for the earlier inventory comparison, not for this positive transfer. Proof: one global interpretation. First use (1). Process precursors in their acyclic order. Earlier coefficients already have affine interpretations in the designated ordinary column. Proof: terminal inputs and products. Substitute into each actual terminal input \(g_i\), obtaining \(G_i\); replace each \(H_b\) there by \(q_b\), obtaining an old-affine \(\widehat G_i\). The difference \(G_i-\widehat G_i\) has a base certificate through its original input cost \(d_i\). For the stated constant combinations of distinct whole products this is degree-adapted: after identical product occurrences are combined, every nonconstant product contributing a nonzero replacement has its own fresh coefficient variables in its top degree, so its original weight is at most \(d_i\). The target has ordinary functional PHP and exactly one old-affine complete terminal family, so the stated affine endpoint excludes polylogarithmic \(B\) at every fixed polynomial inventory exponent.
