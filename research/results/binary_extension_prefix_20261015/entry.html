Field-first certificate values

Lemma. Let \(r\ge1\), \(m\ge r\), and let binary division-free unbounded-fanin arithmetic circuits of joint gate/wire size \(s\), depth at most \(\delta\), compute \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\in\mathbb F_2[x_1,\ldots,x_r]\). Retain the full original ordered tuple with \(F_j=x_j^2+x_j\) for \(1\le j\le r\). Let a binary IPS circuit \(C(x,Y)\) of size \(S\), depth \(d\), actual joint degree \(D_C\), satisfy formally \(C(x,0)=0\), \(C(x,F)=1\). Put \(q=2^a\ge4\), \(e=q-1\), \(\delta_i=\deg F_i\) (use zero as a degree ceiling for zero inputs), and \(\Lambda=\sum_i\delta_i\). Define ordinary-power circuits \[ P=\prod_{i=1}^m(1-F_i^e),\qquad A(x,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^m F_i^{e-1}\prod_{j<i}(1-F_j^e)Y_i,\qquad C'=A+PC. \] Then \(C'\) is a binary IPS certificate for the same labeled tuple. It is Hilbert-like if \(C\) is. Its circuit size is \(O(S+s+mq+m^2)\), depth at most \(\max(d,\delta)+5\), and actual joint degree is at most \(D_C+e\Lambda\). If the supplied circuits are formulas, a formula construction has size \(O(S+qm(s+m)+1)\) and the same conservative depth bound. No succinct power gates, discarded field generators, or reset of original costs is used.

Fix a binary basis of \(\mathbb F_q\) whose first element is one. Boolean AND, OR and XOR gates below have unbounded fan-in, NOT has fan-in one, and size counts gates and wires. On the full scalar grid \((x,Y)\in\mathbb F_q^{r+m}\), the encoded value of \(C'\) has a nonuniform Boolean circuit of size \[ T=O\bigl(s+r q^2a^2+(r+m)^2a+1\bigr),\qquad \text{depth }\delta+O(1). \] The construction does not evaluate the arbitrary supplied certificate \(C\). It returns the encoded placeholder \(Y_i/F_i(x)\) for the first nonzero original equation. At a nonbinary old point that equation is a single-coordinate field equation. At a binary old point it is the first violated remaining input equation, whose value is one.

Formal identities and original circuit costs

With \(z_i=F_i^e\), ordinary product telescoping gives \(\sum_i z_i\prod_{j<i}(1-z_j)=1-P\). Thus \(A(x,F)=1-P\), while \(A(x,0)=0\). Substituting the two identities for \(C\) proves \(C'(x,0)=0\) and \(C'(x,F)=1\) as formal polynomials, over \(\mathbb F_2\) and after scalar extension. Placeholder linearity is preserved when present; it is not assumed for general IPS.

Share the input circuits and build each \(F_i^e\) and \(F_i^{e-1}\) with one product gate and the actual repeated input wires, costing \(O(mq)\). Complements add one layer. All prefixes use at most \(O(m^2)\) wires and one more layer. Each coefficient times its placeholder is one product. Flatten these summands of \(A\) and the separate product \(PC\) into one final unbounded sum gate. This realizes the claimed conservative depth without constructing a separate sum gate for \(A\). In a formula, copy each input subformula whenever it occurs in a prefix or power; each appears in at most \(m\) summands with at most \(q\) repetitions. This gives the displayed formula cost, with \(C\) appearing once.

Precisely, \(\deg P\le e\Lambda\) and the \(i\)-th summand of \(A\) has joint degree at most \(1+(e-1)\delta_i+e\sum_{j<i}\delta_j\le1+e\Lambda\). A certificate has \(D_C\ge1\), since a constant satisfying \(C(x,0)=0\) cannot specialize to one. Hence \(\deg C'\le\max(1+e\Lambda,D_C+e\Lambda)=D_C+e\Lambda\). These are upper bounds on actual joint degrees; cancellation can lower them but does not repay the original presentation budget.

Source-sensitive scalar evaluation

The tuple has no common zero over \(\mathbb F_q\), because such a zero would contradict the specialized identity \(C(x,F)=1\). At each point, \(F_i^e\) is zero or one, and consequently \(P(x)=0\). Since \(e-1=q-2>0\), a zero equation contributes zero to \(A\). If \(i\) is the first nonzero equation, its prefix is one and its coefficient is \(F_i(x)^{q-2}=F_i(x)^{-1}\); every later prefix is zero. This proves the scalar formula for every placeholder tuple. It does not make \(P\) the zero formal polynomial.

In the chosen basis, an encoded old coordinate is binary exactly when its higher \(a-1\) bits vanish. If some coordinate is nonbinary, let \(j\) be the first such coordinate. The field-first order makes \(j\) the first nonzero equation, since \(z^2+z=0\) over a field of characteristic two exactly at zero and one. Use the table \[ L(z,v)=\begin{cases}(z^2+z)^{-1}v&z\notin\{0,1\},\\0&z\in\{0,1\}.\end{cases} \] An explicit two-input lookup has at most \(q^2\) rows, \(2a\)-bit row comparisons and \(a\) output bits; \(O(q^2a^2)\) gates/wires and constant depth is conservative. This charges field representation, inverses and multiplication rather than treating them as free. Select the first nonbinary coordinate by prefix flags and take its lookup result.

If all coordinates are binary, evaluate the original input circuits using their first coordinate bits: addition is XOR and multiplication is AND, so the cost is \(O(s)\), depth \(\delta+O(1)\). Their outputs agree with the original polynomials on this branch and belong to \(\mathbb F_2\). The first nonzero remaining equation has value one, and some such equation exists by the certificate identity. Prefix flags select its encoded placeholder without field multiplication. Values computed by these binary circuits on the other branch are ignored. The flags, encoded selection and output XORs cost at most \(O((r+m)^2a+1)\) wires and constant depth beyond the input circuits. This proves \(T\); it is not a generic evaluator of binary arithmetic circuits on nonbinary inputs.

The exact Boolean probe consequence

For \(Q\) supplied nonadaptive scalar query circuits, producing encoded old points and placeholder tuples at total size \(E\) and depth \(\eta\), compose this evaluator separately at each query. Any Boolean outer probe of size \(U\), depth \(u\), whose output is unchanged when the given certificate is replaced by this exact \(C'\), has the same output at cost \(O(E+QT+U)\), depth at most \(\eta+\delta+u+O(1)\). The probe may also read its original Boolean input. This follows by ordinary Boolean circuit composition and the assumed invariance; it needs no algorithm computing \(C\). It applies to this scalar-query interface, not unrestricted access to the certificate's syntax or formal coefficients.

For \(s,r,m,q,E,Q,U\) polynomial in the original encoding length \(N\), and fixed \(\delta,\eta,u\), this is polynomial-size fixed-depth Boolean evaluation. It is not necessarily polynomial in a smaller Boolean input length: that requires these same quantities to be polynomial in that length. No claim is made for an arbitrary arithmetic outer probe over \(\mathbb F_q\); evaluating such a probe might itself be hard. Nor is \(C'\) claimed to belong to the exact matched Frege-proof image, to preserve a prescribed depth cutoff, or to preserve a prescribed joint-degree ceiling. Invariance and inclusion of both images are additional conditions when a method requires them. Formal division, coefficient extraction and the lower bound for every eligible certificate remain open.
