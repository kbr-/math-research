Ultimate Matching Conjecture

Prove or refute Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture (Conjecture 5.8 of The Status of my favorite problems): for \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\) there is a permutation \(\pi\) of \(\{1,\dots,N\}\) such that, for \(j=1,2\), \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\big(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\big)^{\alpha_j}\big]\le2N\), where \(X_1,\dots,X_N,Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) are independent and uniform on \([0,1]^2\), superscripts denote coordinates and \(L\) does not depend on \(N\).

Starting point

The source is Talagrand's survey The Status of my favorite problems (22 pages, PDF dated 1 October 2026; its hash is in research/references/redistribution.json), Section 5, pages 11–14; a personal copy and its text extraction are held in the ignored private folder. It defers all details and references to [T4]: Talagrand, Upper and lower bounds for stochastic processes: decomposition theorems, Ergeb. Math. Grenzgeb. (3) 60, Springer, 2021, not yet read here. Its introduction states that the conjecture has seen no progress since it was formulated. The thread starts from no earlier result of this repository.

Where we stand

The conjecture is open. The monograph reading fixed the statement from [T4]: \(L\) is universal, independent of the exponents; \(\alpha=\infty\) means the maximum bound; "with high probability" means probability tending to one. [T4] states it with evenly spread \(Y\); by the two-sample transfer that form implies the survey's two-sample goal with constant \(2L\), so it is a sufficient target. The common exponent cap shows, in the evenly-spread setting, that no pair with both exponents above \(4\) holds, so the conjecture cannot be strengthened on its diagonal there; off the diagonal the exponent sum \(1/2\) has no recorded lower-bound argument.

Reduction. The Hopf–Lax reduction proves that the conjecture at every admissible pair, endpoints included and with one universal constant, follows from one discrepancy bound: conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy, a bound \(\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}\) for the cell-averaged discrepancy over the class \(\int\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(|\partial_jG|)\le2\), \(\psi^*\) the conjugate of the exponential cost. Nothing indicates that it is easier than the conjecture: at \((2,\infty)\) it is a continuous counterpart of [T4]'s open Research Problem 17.2.2, The Leighton–Shor class test proves that the Leighton–Shor construction, scaled vertically by \((\log N)^{-|1/\alpha_1-1/\alpha_2|}\), gives \(D_N\gtrsim\sqrt N(\log N)^{3/4-(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2)/2}\) when \(|1/\alpha_1-1/\alpha_2|<1/2\): the reduced statement survives on the curve, where this is exactly its order \(\sqrt{N\log N}\), and is false for the class at every pair below the curve. In primal form, the dyadic flow bound shows that any flow between the smeared configurations bounds the matching cost, and the dyadic Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády flow gives the off-curve pair \((2,2)\) with probability tending to one; its per-point values are heuristically Gaussian, so this route needs a flow with lighter tails at every pair of the curve, presumably an adaptive one.

Known results. Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády and Leighton–Shor control the sum \(L\sqrt{N\log N}\) and the maximum \(L(\log N)^{3/4}/\sqrt N\) of the distances, both optimal; the conjecture at \((4,4)\) gives both at once (consequences), which is the survey's Problem 5.3, reported solved by [C5] (unread). Shor's theorem gives the first-coordinate sum with the second-coordinate maximum \(L\sqrt{\log N/N}\). In the evenly-spread setting [T4] proves the pair \((\alpha,\infty)\) for \(\alpha<1/2\) with constant \(K(\alpha)\) (Theorem 17.1.3) and records the isotropic pair \((2,2)\) only with probability \(9/10\) (Talagrand–Yukich); both lie off the curve. The three-dimensional analogue, exponent sum \(1\), is proved (Theorem 18.1.1 of [T4]).

Why the method stops. [T4] reduces Shor's theorem to a discrepancy bound for a class \(\mathcal H\) of anisotropic gradient-penalized functions, decomposes \(\mathcal H\) into Lipschitz classes \(\mathcal H_j\) with small support, and bounds the supremum of the sum by the sum of the suprema; "This entails a loss which seems to prevent reaching the correct value \(\alpha=2\)". The right chaining functional for \(\mathcal H\) is unknown.

Open statements

  1. Ultimate Matching Conjecture — open, the full target. The statement of the goal above (conj:ultimate-matching), for every pair \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in(0,\infty]\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), with one universal \(L\). Sufficient: the same with evenly spread \(Y\) (Research Problem 17.1.2 of [T4]), by the two-sample transfer. In that setting it cannot be strengthened on the diagonal, by the common exponent cap.
  2. Conjugate-class discrepancy — open, sufficient for the full target. conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy: there is \(L\ge132\) such that for every pair \(\alpha\in[2,\infty]^2\) on the curve, with probability tending to one every Lipschitz \(G\) with \(\int_\Omega\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(|\partial_jG|)\le2\) has \(\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}\) (notation in the working context). Implies the conjecture at every admissible pair by the Hopf–Lax reduction. Its order cannot be lowered at interior pairs of the curve (lem:leighton-shor-class-lower-bound); no upper-bound mechanism exists yet.
  3. Conjecture 5.5 — open, a special case. With high probability some matching has \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\big(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^1-Y^1_{\pi(i)}|\big)^2\big]\le2N\) and \(\max_i|X_i^2-Y^2_{\pi(i)}|\le L\sqrt{\log N}/\sqrt N\). The printed (5.1) places the square outside the exponential; the intended reading \(\exp(x^2)\) is the one consistent with Conjecture 5.8 and with Conjecture 17.1.4 of [T4], which is this case in the evenly-spread setting.
  4. Grid discrepancy — open, a route to the special case. Research Problem 17.2.2 of [T4] at \(\theta_1(x)=x(\log(3+x))^{1/2}\), \(\theta_2(x)=x\), which [T4] says would, with "significant extra work", prove Conjecture 17.1.4. The survey's Conjectures 5.6–5.7 use \(\theta(x)=x\log(x+3)\), a smaller class, whose sufficiency the survey calls "plausible" without the details; see the working context.

The remaining route

  1. Mechanism for the full conjecture. Find a discrepancy formulation of the anisotropic exponential-moment condition for general \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\), analogous to the one [T4] gives for Conjecture 5.5, and bound the size of the resulting function class; or find a construction showing the conjecture fails for some pair. The reduction to a discrepancy statement is done (Hopf–Lax reduction); what remains is to bound the conjugate class's discrepancy at Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády size without [T4]'s lethal sum-of-suprema loss, or to show that Leighton–Shor-type functions exceed it on the curve. Whether the exponent sum \(1/2\) is necessary off the diagonal is open.
  2. Close Conjecture 5.5. Raise the proved exponent from \(\alpha<1/2\) to \(2\), through Research Problem 17.2.2 or otherwise. The obstacle is the unknown geometry of \(\mathcal H\), the right functional for \(\gamma_2(\mathcal H,d_2)\).

Working mathematical context

Setting

Goal: \(X_1,\dots,X_N\) and \(Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) independent, uniform on \([0,1]^2\); \(X_i^j\) is the \(j\)-th coordinate; a matching is a permutation \(\pi\). Sufficient form ([T4]): \(Y\) evenly spread, that is \([0,1]^2\) covered by \(N\) rectangles of area \(1/N\) with disjoint interiors, each containing exactly one \(Y_i\) and inside \(B(Y_i,10/\sqrt N)\). \(L\) is universal, independent of \(N\) and of \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) ([T4]'s convention; \(K\) may depend on parameters). "With high probability" means probability tending to \(1\), no rate. \(d\) is Euclidean. Scale \(\sqrt{\log N/N}\). Call \(\pi\) \((L,\alpha)\)-good in coordinate \(j\) if \(\sum_i\exp[(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|)^\alpha]\le2N\), or for \(\alpha=\infty\) \(\max_i|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\le L\sqrt{\log N/N}\).

Proved tools

Two-sample transfer: good matchings of \(X\) and of \(Y\) to one common \(Z\) compose into a \((\max(2,2^{1/\alpha})L,\alpha)\)-good matching of \(X\) to \(Y\); evenly-spread theorems with failure \(\varepsilon_N\) give two-sample ones with failure \(2\varepsilon_N\). Consequences: \(\max_i\Delta^j_i\le L\sqrt{\log N}(\log2N)^{1/\alpha_j}/\sqrt N\); for \(\alpha_j\ge1\), \(\sum_i\Delta^j_i\le2L\sqrt{N\log N}\); at \((4,4)\) both Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády and Leighton–Shor bounds with \(4L\); at \((2,\infty)\) Shor's bounds with \(2L\). Common exponent cap: in the evenly-spread setting no pair in \((4,\infty]^2\) holds with probability tending to one, from Theorems 4.7.1 and 4.8.1 of [T4] by a reverse-Markov step. Hopf–Lax matching bound: for a separable convex finite cost \(C\) with \(C(0)=0\) and cell counts at least \(\lambda|Q|\) on a grid of side \(\delta\), \(\min_\pi\sum C(X_i-Y_{\pi(i)})\le\sup_g[\int g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-(1-2h)\lambda\int C^*(\nabla g)]+2hN\sum_j\Psi_j(\delta/h)\), \(\mu\) the cell-smeared measures. Reduction: costs \(\psi_\alpha(t/s)\), \(\psi_\alpha(t)=e^{t^\alpha}-1\), \(\psi_\infty(t)=N(t-1)_+\), \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\), grid side \(1/\lceil32/s\rceil\), \(L\ge132\), \(h=1/4\); then \(D_N\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}\) gives the pair. Replacing \(\psi^*\) by \(\max(\psi^*,x)\ge x\) changes \(D_N\) by at most a factor \(e\).

Discrepancy classes

Grid \(G=\{1,\dots,2^p\}^2\), \(2^{-p}\approx L^*\sqrt{\log N/N}\), \(n(\tau)\) evenly-spread points per cell with \(m_0\le n(\tau)\le2m_0\), law \(\mu(\{\tau\})=n(\tau)/N\). [T4]'s Research Problem 17.2.2: for which \(\theta_1,\theta_2\ge x\) does the class with \(\sum\theta_1(|h(k+1,\ell)-h(k,\ell)|)+\sum\theta_2(|h(k,\ell+1)-h(k,\ell)|)\le2^{2p}\) satisfy \(\mathbb E\sup_h\sum_{i\le N}(h(U_i)-\int h\,d\mu)\le K\sqrt{pm_0}\,2^{2p}\)? Shor's theorem uses horizontal increments at most \(1\) and vertical sum at most \(2^{2p}\) (Theorem 17.2.1, probability \(1-e^{-46p}\)); the duality step is Proposition 17.2.4. The survey's (5.2) has \(\theta(x)=x\log(x+3)\), uniform law, mean zero, and Conjecture 5.7 its Gaussian form \(L\sqrt p\,2^{2p}\). With \(\theta_1=\theta_2=x\) the best Gaussian bound is \(Lp^{3/4}2^{2p}\).

Question. The thread's goal is Conjecture 5.8 of the survey The Status of my favorite problems, which defers every detail to [T4], Talagrand's Upper and Lower Bounds for Stochastic Processes, 2nd edition, Springer 2021 (author-hosted at michel.talagrand.net/ULBSPRINGER.pdf, 727 pages; a personal copy and its text extraction are held in the ignored private folder; hash in research/references/redistribution.json). The missing implication for the goal is a matching construction for every admissible pair \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\); before any attempt, the exact statement to be proved has to be fixed. This entry reads the monograph's statements, settles the four ambiguities the setup recorded, and records with proofs the two elementary facts the setup used without proof. It ends when each ambiguity is settled from a read statement or recorded as open.

What the monograph states

Sources read. Read in the rendered or extracted text of [T4]: Section 4.3 (the evenly spread points and the duality Proposition 4.3.2), the statements of Theorems 4.5.1, 4.7.1 and 4.8.1, Section 4.9 and the Notes 4.10, all of Sections 17.1–17.3, Section 18.1 through Exercise 18.1.4 and its consequence, Appendix E.2, and the convention on the constants \(L\) and \(K\) in Chapter 2. The proofs of Theorem 17.1.3 (deferred to [T4]'s reference [129], The Generic Chaining, 2005), of Proposition 17.3.2 (reference [132]) and of the Talagrand–Yukich bound (reference [134]) are not in [T4] and were not read. The reviewer received bounded excerpts; the statements it could not see in them (the law \(\mu\), the support condition (17.40), the loss sentence after (17.42), the hypotheses of Theorem 18.1.1 and Proposition 18.1.3) were reread in the full text after the review, (17.40) from the rendered page.

The conjecture. Research Problem 17.1.2 of [T4] (page 550) asks to prove or disprove: for \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0\) with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), with high probability there is a matching \(\pi\) with \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big(\big(\sqrt{N/\log N}\,|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|/L\big)^{\alpha_j}\big)\le2N\) for \(j=1,2\). The normalization is the survey's. The setting differs: in [T4] the \(X_i\) are i.i.d. uniform on \([0,1]^2\) and the \(Y_i\) are nonrandom evenly spread points (Section 4.3: \([0,1]^2\) is covered by \(N\) rectangles of area \(1/N\) with disjoint interiors, each containing exactly one \(Y_i\) and contained in \(B(Y_i,10/\sqrt N)\)); the survey takes two independent i.i.d. samples. The survey's two-sample form is the thread's goal; the lemma below shows that the monograph's form implies it, so the monograph's form is a sufficient target, not a necessary one.

Ambiguity one: the constant. Settled. In [T4], "\(L\) denotes a universal constant", "a number that do[es] not depend on anything, while \(K\) might depend on some parameters, such as \(\alpha\)" (Chapter 2). Research Problem 17.1.2 writes \(L\), so the constant may not depend on \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\); the partial result writes \(K(\alpha)\). The survey agrees: "\(L\) is a universal constant (i.e. a number independent of \(N\))", and of its partial result it says "\(L\) is replaced by a number depending on \(\alpha\)".

Ambiguity two: the endpoint. Settled. [T4] states that for "\(\alpha_1=2,\alpha_2=\infty\)" one may interpret (17.3) for \(j=2\) as \(\sup_i|X_i^2-Y^2_{\pi(i)}|\le L\sqrt{\log N/N}\) (its (17.2)), and Conjecture 17.1.4 (Theorem 17.1.3 at \(\alpha=2\)) is that case; the survey's Conjecture 5.5 states the maximum bound directly. The text after Exercise 18.1.4 uses the convention \(|x|^\infty=0\) for \(|x|<1\), \(\infty\) otherwise; the strict inequality differs from the maximum bound's \(\le\) only by a change of constant.

Ambiguity three: the probability. Settled by the survey, which says after its Theorem 5.1: "Here and below … 'with high probability' means 'with probability going to 1 as \(N\to\infty\)'"; this governs its Conjecture 5.8, the goal. No rate is required. Research Problem 17.1.2 of [T4] says only "with high probability". The related proved theorems state explicit rates: \(1-LN^{-10}\) in Theorems 17.1.1, 17.1.3 and 18.1.1, \(1-L\exp(-(\log N)^{3/2}/L)\) in Theorem 4.7.1, and \(9/10\) in the Talagrand–Yukich bound (4.131); Theorem 4.5.1 bounds an expectation instead.

Ambiguity four: the discrepancy class. Recorded as a discrepancy between the sources, not settled. Research Problem 17.2.2 of [T4] asks for which \(\theta_1,\theta_2\ge x\) the class \(\mathcal H\) of \(h\) on \(G=\{1,\dots,2^p\}^2\) with \(\sum\theta_1(|h(k+1,\ell)-h(k,\ell)|)+\sum\theta_2(|h(k,\ell+1)-h(k,\ell)|)\le2^{2p}\) satisfies \(\mathbb E\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\sum_{i\le N}(h(U_i)-\int h\,d\mu)\le K\sqrt{pm_0}\,2^{2p}\), where the \(U_i\) are i.i.d. with the nonuniform law \(\mu(\{\tau\})=n(\tau)/N\) and \(m_0\le n(\tau)\le2m_0\). It names \(\theta_1(x)=x(\log(3+x))^{1/2}\), \(\theta_2(x)=x\): "A positive answer (and significant extra work) would allow one to prove Conjecture 17.1.4." The survey's (5.2) instead uses \(\theta(x)=x\log(x+3)\), the uniform law, and a bound in probability. Since \(x\log(x+3)\ge x(\log(3+x))^{1/2}\) for \(x\ge0\), the survey's class is contained in the monograph's; apart from the law of the sample and the form of the bound, its Conjecture 5.6 therefore asks less. [T4] attributes the route to Conjecture 17.1.4 only to the larger class, so the survey's claim that Conjecture 5.6 suffices for Conjecture 5.5 is not backed by the monograph's stated reduction; the survey itself footnotes it: "It might require some work to figure out all the details, but the claim is plausible!" Which \(\theta\) the survey intends is not decided here.

Known exponent pairs. In the monograph's setting: Theorem 17.1.3 gives the pair \((\alpha,\infty)\) for every \(0<\alpha<1/2\), with constant \(K(\alpha)\) and probability \(1-LN^{-10}\); the text says "We do not know how to prove Theorem 17.1.3 for \(\alpha\ge1/2\)". The Talagrand–Yukich bound (4.131), \(\frac1N\sum_i\exp(Nd(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})^2/(L\log N))\le2\) with probability at least \(9/10\), gives the isotropic pair \((2,2)\) in each coordinate with constant \(\sqrt L\), since \(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\le d(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\), but only with probability \(9/10\), not with high probability in the survey's sense. Both lie off the conjectured curve \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\). The simultaneous sum and maximum bound, the conjecture's consequence at \((4,4)\) below, is the survey's Problem 5.3, which the survey reports solved by a construction [C5] it says does not touch the real issues; [C5] was not read. Shor's Theorem 17.1.1 is the sum bound in coordinate one with the maximum bound in coordinate two.

Why the method stops. Section 17.3 of [T4] calls it the "lethal weakness": the class \(\mathcal H\) is decomposed as \(h=\sum_jh_j\) with \(h_j\) in classes \(\mathcal H_j\) of functions with horizontal increments at most \(1\), vertical increments at most \(2^j\), and at most \(2^{2p-j}\) points where \(h_j\ne0\) (Proposition 17.3.1, (17.39)–(17.40)), and the supremum over \(\mathcal H\) is bounded by the sum of the suprema over the \(\mathcal H_j\) (17.42). The text says "This entails a loss which seems to prevent reaching the correct value \(\alpha=2\)", and that the right functional for \(\gamma_2(\mathcal H,d_2)\) "can be discovered only by understanding the underlying geometry, which the author does not". Appendix E.2 adds: "Possibly, it is only a hard combinatorial problem to crack, to understand the geometry of certain classes of functions".

Dimension three. Theorem 18.1.1 of [T4] proves the three-dimensional analogue for every \(\varphi\) whose sublevel sets \(\{\varphi\le N_k\}\) are boxes \(S_k\) of volume \(2^{k+3}\), with a universal constant and probability \(1-LN^{-10}\); by Exercise 18.1.4 this includes \(\psi=\exp(\frac13\sum_j|x_j|^{\alpha_j})-1\) for \(\alpha_j\in(0,\infty]\) with \(\sum_j1/\alpha_j=1\), which the text calls the ultimate matching conjecture in that setting. Proposition 18.1.3 shows the volume growth is necessary there, by an empty box of volume about \(\log N/N\). The sections read give no corresponding lower-bound argument for the two-dimensional exponent sum \(1/2\); the survey says only that the powers of \(\log N\) in Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 are optimal. The diagonal case is proved below.

General statement. lem:two-sample-matching-transfer: for every \(N\ge2\), every exponent pair in \((0,\infty]^2\) and every point sets, matchings of both samples to one common point set compose into a matching of the two samples whose exponential moments are bounded with the constant multiplied by \(\max(2,2^{1/\alpha_j})\).

Two-sample matching transfer

Setting. Fix \(N\ge2\), \(L>0\) and \(\alpha\in(0,\infty]\). For finite sequences \(P=(P_i)_{i\le N}\), \(Q=(Q_k)_{k\le N}\) of points of \(\mathbb R^2\), a bijection \(\sigma\) of \(\{1,\dots,N\}\) and a coordinate \(j\), say that \(\sigma\) is \((L,\alpha)\)-good in coordinate \(j\) if \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\big(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|P_i^j-Q^j_{\sigma(i)}|\big)^{\alpha}\big]\le2N\) when \(\alpha<\infty\), and \(\max_i|P_i^j-Q^j_{\sigma(i)}|\le L\sqrt{\log N}/\sqrt N\) when \(\alpha=\infty\). Put \(\kappa(\alpha)=\max(2,2^{1/\alpha})\), with \(\kappa(\infty)=2\).

Statement. (1) Let \(X,Y,Z\) be sequences of \(N\) points and \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\) bijections such that \(\sigma_1\) is \((L,\alpha)\)-good for \((X,Z)\) and \(\sigma_2\) is \((L,\alpha)\)-good for \((Y,Z)\) in coordinate \(j\). Then \(\pi=\sigma_2^{-1}\circ\sigma_1\) is \((\kappa(\alpha)L,\alpha)\)-good for \((X,Y)\) in coordinate \(j\). (2) Consequently, suppose that for some pair \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\in(0,\infty]^2\), some \(L\) and some \(\varepsilon_N\), for i.i.d. uniform \(X\) of size \(N\) and some fixed evenly spread sequence \(Z\) of size \(N\) there is with probability at least \(1-\varepsilon_N\) a matching that is \((L,\alpha_j)\)-good in both coordinates. Then for two i.i.d. uniform samples \(X,Y\) of size \(N\), with no independence between them assumed, there is with probability at least \(1-2\varepsilon_N\) a matching that is \((\kappa(\alpha_j)L,\alpha_j)\)-good in coordinate \(j\) for \(j=1,2\).

Proof. (1) Write \(c=\sqrt N/(L\sqrt{\log N})\), \(a_i=|X_i^j-Z^j_{\sigma_1(i)}|\) and \(b_k=|Y_k^j-Z^j_{\sigma_2(k)}|\). Since \(\sigma_2(\pi(i))=\sigma_1(i)\), the triangle inequality gives \(|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\le a_i+b_{\pi(i)}\). If \(\alpha=\infty\), then \(\max_i(a_i+b_{\pi(i)})\le2L\sqrt{\log N}/\sqrt N\). If \(\alpha<\infty\), we claim \((c(a+b)/\kappa)^\alpha\le\frac12((ca)^\alpha+(cb)^\alpha)\) for \(a,b\ge0\). For \(\alpha\ge1\), \(\kappa=2\) and this is convexity of \(t\mapsto t^\alpha\). For \(\alpha<1\), \(\kappa^\alpha=2\) and \((a+b)^\alpha\le a^\alpha+b^\alpha\) (subadditivity of the concave function \(t^\alpha\) with value \(0\) at \(0\)). Applying the increasing function \(\exp\) and then the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, \(\exp((c(a+b)/\kappa)^\alpha)\le\big(e^{(ca)^\alpha}e^{(cb)^\alpha}\big)^{1/2}\le\frac12\big(e^{(ca)^\alpha}+e^{(cb)^\alpha}\big)\). Since \(t\mapsto\exp((ct/\kappa)^\alpha)\) is nondecreasing, summing over \(i\) gives \(\sum_i\exp\big[\big(\frac{c}{\kappa}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\big)^\alpha\big]\le\frac12\sum_ie^{(ca_i)^\alpha}+\frac12\sum_ie^{(cb_{\pi(i)})^\alpha}\le\frac12(2N)+\frac12(2N)=2N\), where the second sum runs over all \(k=\pi(i)\) because \(\pi\) is a bijection. As \(c/\kappa=\sqrt N/(\kappa L\sqrt{\log N})\), this is the claim. (2) Let \(Z\) be the fixed evenly spread points of the hypothesis. Each of \(X\) and \(Y\) is an i.i.d. uniform sample of size \(N\), so each event "some matching to \(Z\) is good in both coordinates" has probability at least \(1-\varepsilon_N\); their intersection has probability at least \(1-2\varepsilon_N\) by the union bound, whatever the joint law of \(X\) and \(Y\). On it, (1) applied in each coordinate with the same \(\pi=\sigma_2^{-1}\circ\sigma_1\) gives the claim. \(\square\)

Scope. Only the direction from evenly spread points to two samples is proved. The converse, deducing the monograph's form from the survey's, is not addressed. Exponential matching consequences

Statement. Let \(N\ge2\), \(L>0\), and let \(\pi\) be \((L,\alpha_j)\)-good for \((X,Y)\) in coordinate \(j\), with \(\alpha_j\in(0,\infty]\), for any point sequences; write \(\Delta_i^j=|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\). (a) \(\max_i\Delta_i^j\le L\sqrt{\log N}\,(\log 2N)^{1/\alpha_j}/\sqrt N\), which is at most \(2L(\log N)^{1/2+1/\alpha_j}/\sqrt N\) when \(\alpha_j\ge1\). (b) If \(\alpha_j\ge1\), then \(\sum_i\Delta_i^j\le2L\sqrt{N\log N}\). (c) If \(\pi\) is good in both coordinates for the pair \((4,4)\), then with \(d\) the Euclidean distance, \(\sum_id(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\le4L\sqrt{N\log N}\) and \(\max_id(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\le4L(\log N)^{3/4}/\sqrt N\): one matching meets the bounds of both the Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády and the Leighton–Shor theorems. (d) If \(\pi\) is good in both coordinates for the pair \((2,\infty)\), it meets both bounds of Shor's theorem with constant \(2L\). (e) For every pair with \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), the product of the two bounds in (a) is \(L^2(\log N)(\log2N)^{1/2}/N\).

Proof. Write \(t_i=\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}\Delta_i^j\). If \(\alpha_j=\infty\), (a) is the definition. Otherwise every summand \(e^{t_i^{\alpha_j}}\) is at least \(1\), so each is at most \(2N-(N-1)\le2N\), whence \(t_i\le(\log2N)^{1/\alpha_j}\). For \(N\ge2\), \(\log2N\le2\log N\), so \((\log2N)^{1/\alpha_j}\le2^{1/\alpha_j}(\log N)^{1/\alpha_j}\le2(\log N)^{1/\alpha_j}\) when \(\alpha_j\ge1\). For (b), \(t\le\max(1,t^{\alpha_j})\le e^{t^{\alpha_j}}\) for \(t\ge0\) and \(\alpha_j\ge1\), so \(\sum_it_i\le2N\); if \(\alpha_j=\infty\), \(\sum_i\Delta_i^j\le N\max_i\Delta_i^j\le L\sqrt{N\log N}\). For (c), \(d\le\Delta^1+\Delta^2\); add (b) for both coordinates, and add (a) at \(\alpha_j=4\). For (d), (b) at \(\alpha_1=2\) gives the sum bound in coordinate one with \(2L\) and the definition at \(\alpha_2=\infty\) gives the maximum bound. For (e), the exponents of \(\log2N\) add to \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\). \(\square\)

Scope. These are the deductions [T4] sketches after Research Problem 17.1.2 and the survey after Conjecture 5.8, with explicit constants.

Common exponent cap

Statement. In the monograph's setting (\(X\) i.i.d. uniform on \([0,1]^2\), \(Y=Y^{(N)}\) any evenly spread sequence for each \(N\)), let \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in(4,\infty]\) and \(L'>0\). Then it is false that, with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\), some matching is \((L',\alpha_j)\)-good in coordinate \(j\) for \(j=1,2\). Hence on the diagonal \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2\) the conjecture's exponent \(4\), the point where \(1/\alpha_1+1/\alpha_2=1/2\), cannot be increased.

Proof. This argument was proposed by the reviewer and checked here. Let \(M=\inf_\pi\max_id(X_i,Y_{\pi(i)})\) and \(a_N=(\log N)^{3/4}/\sqrt N\). Theorem 4.7.1 of [T4] gives a universal \(L_1\) with \(P(M>L_1a_N)\le L_1\exp(-(\log N)^{3/2}/L_1)\); Theorem 4.8.1 gives a universal \(L_2\) with \(\mathbb EM\ge a_N/L_2\). Always \(M\le\sqrt2\). Let \(G=\{M\le L_1a_N\}\). Then \(\mathbb E[M1_G]\ge a_N/L_2-\sqrt2P(G^c)\ge a_N/(2L_2)\) for all large \(N\), since \(\exp(-(\log N)^{3/2}/L_1)\) decays faster than every power of \(N\) while \(a_N\ge N^{-1/2}\). With \(b=a_N/(4L_2)\), \(\mathbb E[M1_G]\le b+L_1a_NP(M>b)\), so \(P(M>a_N/(4L_2))\ge1/(4L_1L_2)\) for all large \(N\). Now suppose the matching statement held. Put \(\beta=\max_j1/\alpha_j<1/4\). On its event, part (a) of the consequences lemma (with \(\alpha_j\ge1\)) and \(d\le\Delta^1+\Delta^2\) give \(M\le4L'(\log N)^{1/2+\beta}/\sqrt N\), which is below \(a_N/(4L_2)\) once \((\log N)^{1/4-\beta}>16L'L_2\). So for large \(N\), \(P(M>a_N/(4L_2))\) is at most the failure probability, which tends to zero, contradicting the bound \(1/(4L_1L_2)\). \(\square\)

Scope. The cap concerns the monograph's evenly-spread form only: excluding the survey's two-sample form would need a two-sample Leighton–Shor lower bound, or the converse of the transfer lemma, neither available here. Empty boxes do not supply one, by a heuristic count, not a proof: the largest empty axis-parallel box has area about \(\log N/N\) (the count in the informal proof of Lemma 18.1.2 of [T4], in dimension two), and a point of \(Y\) at its centre violates both coordinate bounds only if its sides exceed \(2L\sqrt{\log N}(\log2N)^{1/\alpha_j}/\sqrt N\), an area at least \(4L^2\log N/N\), whatever the exponent sum. Degenerate cases. \(N=1\) makes \(\log N=0\) and the normalization undefined; the first two lemmas assume \(N\ge2\), and the cap concerns large \(N\) only. Question. The missing implication for the goal is a construction, for each admissible pair \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\), of a matching with both exponential moments bounded. [T4] proves the special case of Shor's theorem by reducing the matching to a discrepancy bound through linear-programming duality (Proposition 4.3.2) and a regularity step (Proposition 17.2.4, proved in Section B.6, read in this cycle). The question is whether that reduction extends to exponential costs, and to which class of functions. The test ends with a proved reduction or with the step where it fails.

From the regularity step to a semigroup. Lemmas B.6.1 and B.6.2 of [T4] bound the variation of the inf-convolution \(h(k,\ell)=\inf\{u(r,s)+|k-r|:|\ell-s|\le1\}\) by \(\sum n(\tau)(u(\tau)-h(\tau))\), using that the horizontal cost is linear and the vertical cost is \(0\) within distance \(1\) and infinite beyond. For a convex cost \(\Psi\) the corresponding quantity is the conjugate: if \(u(x)=Mx\) on the line, then \(u(x)-\inf_r[u(r)+\Psi(|x-r|)]=\sup_d(Md-\Psi(|d|))=\Psi^*(M)\) for \(M\ge0\). The lemma below replaces the discrete regularity estimate by a Hopf–Lax interpolation, in which this conjugate appears as the penalty \(C^*(\nabla g)\).

General statement. lem:hopf-lax-matching-bound: for every separable convex finite cost vanishing at \(0\) and every two \(N\)-point configurations in the unit square whose cell counts on some square grid are all at least \(\lambda\) times the cell area, the minimal matching cost is at most the supremum over Lipschitz \(g\) of the cell-averaged discrepancy of \(g\) minus \((1-2h)\lambda\int C^*(\nabla g)\), plus a cell-size error \(2hN\sum_j\Psi_j(\delta/h)\).

Hopf–Lax matching bound

Setting. \(\Omega=[0,1]^2\). \(\Psi_1,\Psi_2:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) are convex, nondecreasing, finite, with \(\Psi_j(0)=0\). \(C(z)=\Psi_1(|z_1|)+\Psi_2(|z_2|)\) for \(z\in\mathbb R^2\); \(C\) is convex, finite, \(C\ge0\), \(C(0)=0\). Its conjugate \(C^*(\xi)=\sup_q(q\cdot\xi-C(q))=\Psi_1^*(|\xi_1|)+\Psi_2^*(|\xi_2|)\), with \(\Psi^*(x)=\sup_{t\ge0}(xt-\Psi(t))\), is convex with values in \([0,\infty]\). \(\mathcal Q\) is the partition of \(\Omega\) into \(k^2\) squares of side \(\delta=1/k\), each half-open \([\cdot,\cdot)\times[\cdot,\cdot)\) except that the squares of the last row and column contain their top or right edges. Points \(X_1,\dots,X_N\) and \(Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) lie in \(\Omega\); \(a(Q)\) and \(b(Q)\) count the \(X_i\) and the \(Y_j\) in \(Q\); \(\mu_X\) and \(\mu_Y\) are the measures with density \(a(Q)/|Q|\) and \(b(Q)/|Q|\) on each \(Q\). Assume \(a(Q),b(Q)\ge\lambda|Q|\) for every \(Q\), with \(\lambda>0\).

Statement. For every \(h\in(0,1/2)\), \[\min_\pi\sum_{i\le N}C(X_i-Y_{\pi(i)})\le\sup_g\Big[\int_\Omega g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-(1-2h)\lambda\int_\Omega C^*(\nabla g)\,dx\Big]+2hN\big(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h)\big),\] the supremum over Lipschitz functions \(g\) on \(\Omega\) (\(\nabla g\) exists almost everywhere; a term \(\int C^*(\nabla g)=\infty\) counts as \(-\infty\)).

Proof. Duality. By Proposition 4.3.2 of [T4] with \(c_{ij}=C(X_i-Y_j)\), the minimum equals \(\sup\sum_i(w_i+w'_i)\) over families with \(w_i+w'_j\le C(X_i-Y_j)\). Fix such a family and put \(f(y)=\max_i[w_i-C(X_i-y)]\) for \(y\in\Omega\); \(f\) is Lipschitz on \(\Omega\), \(C\) being convex and finite, hence Lipschitz on the compact \([-1,1]^2\). Then \(w'_j\le\min_i[C(X_i-Y_j)-w_i]=-f(Y_j)\).

Hopf–Lax functions. For \(t>0\) and \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) set \(Q_t(x)=\min_{y\in\Omega}[f(y)+tC((x-y)/t)]\), a minimum of a continuous function on a compact set. (i) \(Q_1(X_i)\ge w_i\), since \(f(y)\ge w_i-C(X_i-y)\). (ii) For \(t,s>0\), \(x,q\in\mathbb R^2\): \(Q_{t+s}(x+sq)\le Q_t(x)+sC(q)\). Indeed, if \(y\) attains \(Q_t(x)\), then \(\frac{x+sq-y}{t+s}=\frac t{t+s}\cdot\frac{x-y}t+\frac s{t+s}\,q\), and convexity gives \((t+s)C(\frac{x+sq-y}{t+s})\le tC(\frac{x-y}t)+sC(q)\). (iii) For \(y'\in\Omega\), \(Q_s(y')\le f(Y_j)+sC((y'-Y_j)/s)\), taking \(y=Y_j\). (iv) On \(U\times[h,1-h]\), with \(U=(-1,2)^2\supset\Omega\), \((x,t)\mapsto Q_t(x)\) is Lipschitz. In \(x\): for \(x\in U\), \(y\in\Omega\), \(t\ge h\), the point \((x-y)/t\) lies in \([-2/h,2/h]^2\), so each \(x\mapsto tC((x-y)/t)\) has the Lipschitz constant \(\Lambda\) of \(C\) on that square, and so has their minimum \(Q_t\). In \(t\): (ii) with \(q=0\) gives \(Q_{t+s}\le Q_t\). Conversely, if \(y\) attains \(Q_{t+s}(x)\) and \(z=x-y\), put \(x'=y+\frac t{t+s}z\), a convex combination of \(x\in U\) and \(y\in\Omega\subset U\), so \(x'\in U\); then \(Q_{t+s}(x)=f(y)+tC(\frac z{t+s})+sC(\frac z{t+s})\ge Q_t(x')\ge Q_t(x)-\Lambda|x-x'|\ge Q_t(x)-3\Lambda s/h\), using \(C\ge0\), \((x'-y)/t=z/(t+s)\) and \(|x-x'|=\frac s{t+s}|z|\le3s/h\).

Hamilton–Jacobi inequality. By Rademacher's theorem, \(Q\) is differentiable at almost every \((x,t)\in\operatorname{int}\Omega\times(h,1-h)\). At such a point, (ii) with \(s\downarrow0\) gives \(\partial_tQ+q\cdot\nabla_xQ\le C(q)\) for every \(q\), so \(C^*(\nabla_xQ)\le-\partial_tQ\); in particular \(C^*(\nabla_xQ)\) is finite there.

Interpolation. Let \(\rho_t=\frac{(1-h-t)\mu_Y+(t-h)\mu_X}{1-2h}\) for \(t\in[h,1-h]\), with density at least \(\lambda\) on \(\Omega\), and \(F(t)=\int_\Omega Q_t\,d\rho_t\). \(F\) is Lipschitz, and for almost every \(t\), by Fubini \(Q\) is differentiable at \((x,t)\) for almost every \(x\), so dominated convergence (difference quotients in \(t\) bounded by \(3\Lambda/h\)) gives \(F'(t)=\int\partial_tQ_t\,d\rho_t+\frac1{1-2h}\int Q_t\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\le-\lambda\int_\Omega C^*(\nabla Q_t)\,dx+\frac1{1-2h}\int Q_t\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)\), using \(C^*\ge0\). Integrating over \([h,1-h]\), an interval of length \(1-2h\), \(F(1-h)-F(h)\le\sup_g[\int g\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y)-(1-2h)\lambda\int C^*(\nabla g)]\), each \(Q_t\) being Lipschitz on \(\Omega\).

Smearing. By (i) and (ii) with \(t=1-h\), \(s=h\), \(q=(X_i-x)/h\): \(w_i\le Q_1(X_i)\le Q_{1-h}(x)+hC((X_i-x)/h)\) for every \(x\); averaging over \(x\) in the cell of \(X_i\), whose points differ from \(X_i\) by at most \(\delta\) in each coordinate, and summing, \(\sum_iw_i\le\int Q_{1-h}\,d\mu_X+hN(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h))\). By (iii) with \(s=h\), \(-f(Y_j)\le-Q_h(y')+hC((y'-Y_j)/h)\); averaging over \(y'\) in the cell of \(Y_j\), \(\sum_jw'_j\le-\int Q_h\,d\mu_Y+hN(\Psi_1(\delta/h)+\Psi_2(\delta/h))\). The sum of the two main terms is \(F(1-h)-F(h)\). Taking the supremum over dual families gives the statement. \(\square\)

Scope. The lemma is deterministic and uses the points only through the cell counts. Superlinearity of the cost is not needed: with \(C(z)=|z_1|+|z_2|\) it gives a smeared Kantorovich–Rubinstein bound over functions with \(|\partial_jg|\le1\). Only this direction is proved: a matching of small cost need not give a small value of the right-hand side. The continuous form, for densities bounded below by \(\lambda\), appears to be, by the reviewer's unverified recollection, the dual of the flux bound for the linear interpolation of the two measures (Benamou–Brenier), with the semigroup proof of Bobkov, Gentil and Ledoux; neither source was read in this cycle. What is written here is that bound for point configurations, through cell smearing and the finite duality of [T4]; no novelty is claimed.

Exponential matching from discrepancy

Setting. For \(\alpha\in[1,\infty)\) put \(\psi_\alpha(t)=e^{t^\alpha}-1\), and for \(N\ge2\) put \(\psi_\infty(t)=N(t-1)_+\); each is convex, nondecreasing, finite and \(0\) at \(0\). Their conjugates \(\psi_\alpha^*(x)=\sup_{t\ge0}(xt-\psi_\alpha(t))\) include \(\psi_\infty^*(x)=x\) for \(0\le x\le N\) and \(+\infty\) for \(x>N\). For \(L>0\) put \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\), \(k=\lceil32/s\rceil\), \(\delta=1/k\), and use the grid \(\mathcal Q\) of side \(\delta\). For points \(X,Y\) let \(\mu_X,\mu_Y\) be as above and define \[D_N=\sup\Big\{\int_\Omega G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y):G\text{ Lipschitz},\ \int_\Omega\big(\psi^*_{\alpha_1}(|\partial_1G|)+\psi^*_{\alpha_2}(|\partial_2G|)\big)dx\le2\Big\}.\]

Statement. Let \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[1,\infty]\), \(L\ge132\), \(X\) i.i.d. uniform on \(\Omega\) and \(Y\) evenly spread, and suppose \(P(D_N\le\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N})\to1\). Then with probability tending to one there is a matching \(\pi\) that, for \(j=1,2\), satisfies \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp[(\frac{\sqrt N}{L\sqrt{\log N}}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|)^{\alpha_j}]\le2N\) when \(\alpha_j<\infty\) and \(\max_i|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|\le2L\sqrt{\log N/N}\) when \(\alpha_j=\infty\). Proof. Apply the bound with \(\Psi_j(t)=\psi_{\alpha_j}(t/s)\) and \(h=1/4\). Then \(C^*(\xi)=\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(s|\xi_j|)\), since \(\Psi_j^*(x)=\psi_{\alpha_j}^*(sx)\).

Leighton–Shor class lower bound

Setting. As in cor:exponential-matching-discrepancy-reduction: \(X\) i.i.d. uniform on \(\Omega=[0,1]^2\), \(Y\) evenly spread, \(L\ge132\), \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\), grid side \(\delta=1/\lceil32/s\rceil\), \(\psi_\alpha\) and \(\psi^*_\alpha\) for \(\alpha\in[1,\infty]\), and \(D_N=\sup\{\int G\,d(\mu_X-\mu_Y):\int_\Omega\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(|\partial_jG|)\le2\}\). Write \(\beta_j=1/\alpha_j\in[0,1]\).

Statement. There is a universal constant \(L_1\) such that for all \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in[1,\infty]\) with \(|\beta_1-\beta_2|<1/2\) and all \(L\ge132\), with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\), \[D_N\ge\frac{\sqrt N\,(\log N)^{3/4-(\beta_1+\beta_2)/2}}{4L_1}.\] Consequently: (a) for every pair below the curve (\(\beta_1+\beta_2<1/2\), which forces \(|\beta_1-\beta_2|<1/2\)), \(D_N/\sqrt{N\log N}\to\infty\) in probability, so the bound \(\frac L2\sqrt{N\log N}\) fails for every \(L\); (b) for every pair on the curve other than the endpoints \((2,\infty)\) and \((\infty,2)\), \(D_N\ge\sqrt{N\log N}/(4L_1)\) with probability tending to one, so the order \(\sqrt{N\log N}\) in conj:conjugate-class-discrepancy cannot be lowered there. The same order may already follow, at every pair, from the Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády lower bound for the functions with \(|\partial_jG|\le1\), which the class contains, if that bound survives the cell smearing; this baseline was not checked, so (b) is not claimed as the lemma's specific contribution.

Proof. By the symmetry of the setting under exchanging the coordinates (the evenly spread definition, the uniform law and the grid are symmetric), assume \(\beta_1\ge\beta_2\). Put \(\kappa=(\log N)^{-(\beta_1-\beta_2)}\in((\log N)^{-1/2},1]\).

Step 1: a scaled Leighton–Shor excess. We claim: with probability tending to one there is a function \(f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\), piecewise linear, with \(f(0)=f(1)=0\), \(\int_0^1f'^2\le\kappa^2\), \(|f'|\le\kappa\sqrt r\) with \(r\le\log N\), and \[\operatorname{card}\{i:X_i\in S(f)\}\ge N\lambda(S(f))+\sqrt{\kappa N}(\log N)^{3/4}/L_1,\qquad S(f)=\{(x,y)\in\Omega:y\le f(x)\}.\] This is (4.119) of [T4] with every tent multiplied by \(\kappa\), and its proof is the monograph's with that factor carried. Take \(f=\kappa\sum_{k\le r}f_k\), \(f_k=\sum_{\ell\le2^{ck}}x_{k,\ell}\tilde f_{k,\ell}\), \(\tilde f_{k,\ell}=f_{ck,\ell}/\sqrt r\), \(x_{k,\ell}\in\{0,1\}\), with \(r\) the largest integer such that \(2^{cr}\le N^{1/100}\). By (4.83)–(4.84), \(\int f'^2=\kappa^2\sum x_{k,\ell}2^{-ck}/r\le\kappa^2\); \(f\ge0\), \(f(0)=f(1)=0\); by (4.88) each \(\tilde f_{k,\ell}\) has slope \(\pm1/\sqrt r\) on disjoint supports within a level, so \(|f'|\le\kappa r/\sqrt r=\kappa\sqrt r\), and \(f\le\kappa\sum_k2^{-ck-1}/\sqrt r\le1\). In the inductive step the regions \(R_\ell\) now have \(\lambda(R_\ell)=\kappa2^{-2c(q+1)}/(4\sqrt r)\) (by (4.121) scaled), the favorable threshold becomes \(\sqrt{N\lambda(R_\ell)}/L^*=\sqrt\kappa\,2^{-c(q+1)}\sqrt N/(2r^{1/4}L^*)\), and (4.128) still applies because \(N\lambda(R_\ell)\ge\kappa N^{49/50}/(4\sqrt{\log N})\to\infty\), using \(\kappa\ge(\log N)^{-1/2}\). The counting of possible \(g\), the Poissonization and the choice of \(c\) do not involve \(\kappa\). [T4]'s bound on the failure probability at level \(q\) is of order \(2^{-2^{cq+1}}\), which is not small at the lowest levels, so it gives "overwhelming" probability rather than probability tending to one. Every chosen region has \(\delta_\ell\ge0\), so each level contributes a nonnegative excess whether or not its favorable count succeeds; we use the favorable count only at the levels \(r/2\le q<r\), whose total failure probability is at most \(\sum_{q\ge r/2}2^{-2^{cq+1}}\to0\) (with the Poissonization error at those levels, which also tends to zero as \(N\lambda(R_\ell)\to\infty\)). So with probability tending to one the excess is at least \(\frac r2\cdot\sqrt{\kappa N}/(16r^{1/4}L^*)=\sqrt{\kappa N}r^{3/4}/(32L^*)\); with \(r\ge\log N/(L'c)\) this is the claim for a universal \(L_1\). (Repair proposed by the reviewer and checked here.)

Step 2: the test function. Let \(w=\delta\), \(A=(\log N)^{-\beta_2}/2\) and \(G(x,y)=A\min\{1,\max\{0,(f(x)+w-y)/w\}\}\). \(G\) is Lipschitz, \(G=A\) on \(S(f)\), \(G=0\) where \(y\ge f(x)+w\), and on the band \(B=\{f(x)<y<f(x)+w\}\cap\Omega\), of area at most \(w\), \(\partial_2G=-A/w\) and \(\partial_1G=Af'(x)/w\); elsewhere \(\nabla G=0\) almost everywhere. Put \(M=A/w\); for large \(N\), \(M\le N\) and \(M\kappa\sqrt r\le N\).

Step 3: the penalty. From the previous entry, \(\psi^*_\alpha(x)\le x\max(1,(\log(1+x))^{1/\alpha})\) for \(\alpha<\infty\), and \(\psi^*_\infty(x)=x\) for \(x\le N\). Since \(\max(1,(\log(1+x))^{\beta})\le(2\log N)^\beta\le2(\log N)^\beta\) for \(x\le N\), \(N\ge2\), \(\log N\ge1\) and \(\beta\le1\), \[\int\sum_j\psi^*_{\alpha_j}(|\partial_jG|)\le w\,\psi^*_{\alpha_2}(M)+w\int_0^1\psi^*_{\alpha_1}(M|f'(x)|)dx\le2A(\log N)^{\beta_2}+2A(\log N)^{\beta_1}\int_0^1|f'|\le2A\big((\log N)^{\beta_2}+\kappa(\log N)^{\beta_1}\big)=4A(\log N)^{\beta_2}=2,\] using \(\int|f'|\le(\int f'^2)^{1/2}\le\kappa\).

Step 4: the discrepancy. With probability tending to one every cell has \(a(Q)\le2N\delta^2\) (upper Chernoff tail \(P(a(Q)\ge2N\delta^2)\le e^{-N\delta^2/3}\le N^{-5}\), \(N\delta^2\ge16\log N\), and at most \(N\) cells). General statement. lem:flow-matching-bound: for every separable convex finite cost, every two \(N\)-point configurations and any smearings of them by probability measures supported within \(\ell^\infty\)-distance \(\delta_X\), \(\delta_Y\) of the points, with densities at least \(\lambda\) on the unit square, and every bounded vector field \(\Phi\) whose weak divergence carries one smeared measure to the other, the minimal matching cost is at most \((1-2h)\lambda\int C(\Phi/((1-2h)\lambda))\) plus the smearing error.

Flow matching bound

Setting. \(\Omega\), \(\Psi_j\), \(C\), \(C^*\) as in lem:hopf-lax-matching-bound. Points \(X_i,Y_j\in\Omega\), \(i,j\le N\). For each \(i\) a probability measure \(\nu_i\) on \(\Omega\) supported in \(\{x:|x-X_i|_\infty\le\delta_X\}\), and for each \(j\) a probability measure \(\nu'_j\) supported in \(\{y:|y-Y_j|_\infty\le\delta_Y\}\); \(\nu_X=\sum_i\nu_i\) and \(\nu_Y=\sum_j\nu'_j\) are absolutely continuous, with densities at least \(\lambda>0\) on \(\Omega\) (the proof differentiates \(\int Q_t\,d\rho_t\) using differentiability of \(Q\) at Lebesgue-almost every point). Fix \(h\in(0,1/2)\) and put \(\lambda'=(1-2h)\lambda\).

Statement. (1) \(\min_\pi\sum_iC(X_i-Y_{\pi(i)})\le\sup_g[\int g\,d(\nu_X-\nu_Y)-\lambda'\int_\Omega C^*(\nabla g)]+hN\sum_{k=1,2}(\Psi_k(\delta_X/h)+\Psi_k(\delta_Y/h))\), the supremum over Lipschitz \(g\) on \(\Omega\). (2) If \(\Phi:\Omega\to\mathbb R^2\) is bounded and measurable with \(\int_\Omega\nabla g\cdot\Phi\,dx=\int g\,d(\nu_X-\nu_Y)\) for every Lipschitz \(g\), then \(\min_\pi\sum_iC(X_i-Y_{\pi(i)})\le\lambda'\int_\Omega C(\Phi/\lambda')\,dx+hN\sum_{k=1,2}(\Psi_k(\delta_X/h)+\Psi_k(\delta_Y/h))\).

Proof. (1) is the proof of lem:hopf-lax-matching-bound with one change in the smearing step: averaging \(w_i\le Q_{1-h}(x)+hC((X_i-x)/h)\) against \(\nu_i\) instead of the uniform measure on the cell of \(X_i\), and \(-f(Y_j)\le-Q_h(y')+hC((y'-Y_j)/h)\) against \(\nu'_j\); every point of the support of \(\nu_i\) differs from \(X_i\) by at most \(\delta_X\) in each coordinate, so, \(\Psi_k\) being nondecreasing, the errors are \(hN\sum_k\Psi_k(\delta_X/h)\) and \(hN\sum_k\Psi_k(\delta_Y/h)\); the interpolation uses \(\rho_t\) between \(\nu_Y\) and \(\nu_X\), whose density is at least \(\lambda\). (2) For Lipschitz \(g\), by the identity and the Fenchel–Young inequality \(\xi\cdot q\le C^*(\xi)+C(q)\) at \(\xi=\nabla g(x)\), \(q=\Phi(x)/\lambda'\): \(\int g\,d(\nu_X-\nu_Y)=\lambda'\int\nabla g\cdot\frac\Phi{\lambda'}\le\lambda'\int C^*(\nabla g)+\lambda'\int C(\Phi/\lambda')\). So each term of the supremum in (1) is at most \(\lambda'\int C(\Phi/\lambda')\). \(\square\)

Dyadic square flow

Statement. Let \(S=[0,\rho]^2\) (up to translation) with children \(S_{a,b}\), \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) (\(a\) the horizontal and \(b\) the vertical half), and let \(e_{a,b}\) be reals with \(\sum e_{a,b}=0\). There is a bounded field \(\Phi^S\) on \(S\), linear in \((e_{a,b})\), with \(|\Phi^S|\le6\max|e_{a,b}|/\rho\) and \(\int_S\nabla g\cdot\Phi^S=\sum_{a,b}\frac{e_{a,b}}{|S_{a,b}|}\int_{S_{a,b}}g\) for every Lipschitz \(g\) on \(S\); each coordinate of \(\Phi^S(x)\) is \(\sum_{a,b}\beta_{a,b}(x)e_{a,b}\) with \(|\beta_{a,b}(x)|\le2/\rho\).

Proof. Let \(E_b=e_{0,b}+e_{1,b}\), so \(E_0+E_1=0\). Vertical part: \(V(x,y)=(0,v(y))\) with \(v(y)=-\int_0^yr\), \(r=E_b/(\rho^2/2)\) on the half \(b\); \(v(0)=v(\rho)=0\), \(|v|\le|E_0|/\rho\). Horizontal part on each half \(b\): with \(d_{a,b}=e_{a,b}-E_b/2\) (\(d_{0,b}+d_{1,b}=0\)), \(H(x,y)=(k_b(x),0)\), \(k_b(x)=-\int_0^x\frac{d_{a(x'),b}}{\rho^2/4}dx'\); \(k_b(0)=k_b(\rho)=0\), \(|k_b|\le2|d_{0,b}|/\rho\). For Lipschitz \(g\), integrating by parts in \(y\) on each vertical segment and in \(x\) on each horizontal segment, with the boundary values zero, gives \(\int\nabla g\cdot(V+H)=\int g\,(r+\sum_{a,b}\frac{d_{a,b}}{\rho^2/4}1_{S_{a,b}})=\sum\frac{e_{a,b}}{|S_{a,b}|}\int_{S_{a,b}}g\). Bounds: \(|E_0|\le2\max|e|\) and \(|d_{a,b}|=|e_{0,b}-e_{1,b}|/2\le\max|e|\), so \(|\Phi^S|\le(2+2)\max|e|/\rho\le6\max|e|/\rho\); \(\beta_{a,b}(x)\) denotes the explicit coefficients of \(e_{a,b}\) in \(v\) and \(k_b\) (the representation is not unique since \(\sum e=0\)), which are at most \(1/\rho\) in absolute value, hence at most \(2/\rho\). \(\square\)

Square-exponential matching by the dyadic flow

Statement. There is a universal \(L_0\) such that for every \(L\ge L_0\), if \(X_1,\dots,X_N\) are i.i.d. uniform on \(\Omega\) and \(Y\) is evenly spread, then with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\) there is a matching \(\pi\) with \(\sum_{i\le N}\exp\big[\frac{N}{L^2\log N}|X_i^j-Y^j_{\pi(i)}|^2\big]\le2N\) for \(j=1,2\). This is the conjecture's conclusion at the pair \((2,2)\), which lies off the curve (\(1/2+1/2=1\)).

Proof. Parameters. \(s=L\sqrt{\log N/N}\), \(\Psi_j(t)=\psi_2(t/s)\), \(\psi_2(u)=e^{u^2}-1\), \(h=1/4\). Let \(J\) be the least integer with \(2^{-J}\le s/32\), \(\delta=2^{-J}\ge s/64\); for large \(N\), \(J\le\log N\) and \(N\delta^2\ge L^2\log N/4096\ge16\log N\) for \(L\ge256\). Smear each \(X_i\) uniformly over its level-\(J\) dyadic cell (\(\delta_X=\delta\)) and each \(Y_j\) uniformly over its rectangle \(R_j\) from the definition of evenly spread (\(\delta_Y=20/\sqrt N\)); then \(\nu_Y=N\cdot\text{Lebesgue}\) on \(\Omega\).

Events. \(m(S)\) is the number of \(X_i\) in a dyadic square \(S\). \(E_1\): every dyadic square \(S\) of level \(j\le J\) has \(N|S|/2\le m(S)\le2N|S|\). By the multiplicative Chernoff bounds and \(N|S|\ge16\log N\), each fails with probability at most \(2N^{-2}\), and there are at most \(2\cdot4^J\le N\) squares for large \(N\), so \(P(E_1)\to1\). On \(E_1\), \(\nu_X\) has density at least \(N/2\), so \(\lambda=N/2\) and \(\lambda'=N/4\).

Error. \(\Psi_j(\delta/h)=\psi_2(4\delta/s)\le\psi_2(1/8)<0.02\) and \(\Psi_j(4\cdot20/\sqrt N)=\psi_2(80/(L\sqrt{\log N}))\to0\); the error term is at most \(\frac14N\cdot2(0.02+0.02)=0.02N\) for large \(N\).

The flow. For a dyadic square \(S\) of level \(j<J\) with children \(S_c\), let \(e_c=m(S_c)-m(S)/4\) and \(\Phi_j=\sum_S\Phi^S\) (dyadic square flow, the squares of a level being disjoint up to boundaries, where the normal components vanish). With \(\Phi=\sum_{j<J}\Phi_j\), the identities telescope: \(\int\nabla g\cdot\Phi=\sum_{j<J}\sum_S\sum_c\frac{e_c}{|S_c|}\int_{S_c}g=\int g\,d\nu_X-N\int g=\int g\,d(\nu_X-\nu_Y)\), since the level-\(J\) smearing of \(X\) has density \(m(\text{cell})/|\text{cell}|\) and \(m(\Omega)=N\). By the flow bound (2), the minimal matching cost is at most \(\frac N4\int_\Omega C(4\Phi/N)+0.02N\le\frac N2\int_\Omega\psi_2(4|\Phi|/(Ns))\,dx+0.02N\), using \(C(z)\le2\psi_2(|z|/s)\). With \(Ns=L\sqrt{N\log N}\), it suffices that \(I:=\int_\Omega\psi_2(4|\Phi|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))\le1/2\) with probability tending to one: then the cost is below \(0.27N\), and each coordinate's sum of exponentials is at most \(N+0.27N\le2N\).

Martingale tail. Fix \(x\) and write \(S_j(x)\) for its level-\(j\) square, \(\rho_j=2^{-j}\). Let \(\mathcal F_j\) be generated by the counts of all dyadic squares of level at most \(j\). Given \(\mathcal F_j\), the points in each level-\(j\) square are i.i.d. uniform in it, so each coordinate of \(\Phi_j(x)=\sum_c\beta_c(x)e_c\) is a sum over the \(m(S_j(x))\) points of \(S_j(x)\) of the independent centred variables \(\beta_{c(\text{point})}(x)-\bar\beta\), each in an interval of length at most \(4/\rho_j\). By Hoeffding's lemma, given \(\mathcal F_j\) it is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy \(\sigma_j^2=4m(S_j(x))/\rho_j^2\), which is at most \(8N\) on \(E_1\); \(\sigma_j^2\) is \(\mathcal F_j\)-measurable and \(\Phi_j(x)\) is \(\mathcal F_{j+1}\)-measurable. For a coordinate \(B\) of a partial sum \(\sum_{j_0\le j<J}\Phi_j(x)\), the process \(\exp(\theta B_n-\frac{\theta^2}2\sum_{j_0\le j<n}\sigma_j^2)\) is a supermartingale, so \(P(|B|\ge t,\ \sum\sigma_j^2\le V)\le2e^{-t^2/(2V)}\); with \(V=8NJ\le8N\log N\) and both coordinates, \(P(|\sum_{j_0\le j<J}\Phi_j(x)|\ge t,E_1)\le4e^{-t^2/(32N\log N)}\). The same holds conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{j_0}\) with \(E_1\) replaced by \(E_{1,T}\) (defined below) for \(x\in T\): the supermartingale starts at level \(j_0\) under \(P(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{j_0})\), and every square \(S_j(x)\), \(j\ge j_0\), lies in \(T\), so \(\sum\sigma_j^2\le V\) on \(E_{1,T}\).

Coarse levels. Let \(j_0=\lfloor(\log N)^{1/4}\rfloor\), \(A=\sum_{j<j_0}\Phi_j\), \(B=\sum_{j_0\le j<J}\Phi_j\). \(E_2\): every child excess at levels \(j<j_0\) has \(|e_c|\le\sqrt{m(S)j_0}\). By Hoeffding \(P(|e_c|>\sqrt{mj_0})\le2e^{-2j_0}\), and there are at most \(8\cdot4^{j_0}\) pairs, so \(P(E_2^c)\le16(4/e^2)^{j_0}\to0\). On \(E_1\cap E_2\), \(|\Phi_j|\le6\sqrt{2N\rho_j^2j_0}/\rho_j\), so \(|A|\le6\sqrt2\,j_0^{3/2}\sqrt N\) and \(8|A|/(L\sqrt{N\log N})\le70j_0^{3/2}/(L\sqrt{\log N})\to0\) uniformly in \(x\).

Fine levels. By convexity of \(\psi_2\), \(I\le\frac12\psi_2(8|A|_\infty/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))+\frac12\sum_TZ_T\), where \(T\) runs over the \(4^{j_0}\) squares of level \(j_0\) and \(Z_T=\int_T\psi_2(8|B|/(L\sqrt{N\log N}))\,1_{E_{1,T}}\), \(E_{1,T}\) being the part of \(E_1\) that concerns subsquares of \(T\). Given \(\mathcal F_{j_0}\), the \(Z_T\) are independent. Put \(U=8|B(x)|/(L\sqrt{N\log N})\); the tail above gives \(P(U\ge u,E_{1,T})\le4e^{-u^2L^2/2048}\), so for \(L^2>4096\), \(\mathbb E[\psi_2(U)1_{E_{1,T}}]=\int_0^\infty2ue^{u^2}P(U\ge u,E_{1,T})\,du\le\int_0^\infty8ue^{-(L^2/2048-1)u^2}du=4/(L^2/2048-1)=:\eta\), and, using \(\psi_2(U)^2\le e^{2U^2}-1\), \(\mathbb E[\psi_2(U)^21_{E_{1,T}}]\le\int_0^\infty4ue^{2u^2}4e^{-u^2L^2/2048}du=8/(L^2/2048-2)=:\eta_2\). Hence \(\mathbb E[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_{j_0}]\le|T|\eta\) and \(\mathbb E[Z_T^2\mid\mathcal F_{j_0}]\le|T|^2\eta_2\) (Cauchy–Schwarz over \(T\)), so \(\sum_TZ_T\) has conditional mean at most \(\eta\) and conditional variance at most \(4^{-j_0}\eta_2\to0\). By Chebyshev, \(\sum_TZ_T\le2\eta\) with probability tending to one. For \(L\) large enough that \(\eta\le1/8\), on \(E_1\cap E_2\) and that event, \(I\le\frac12\psi_2(o(1))+\eta\le1/2\) for large \(N\). \(\square\)

What the dyadic flow cannot give. Heuristic, unproved: \(\Phi(x)\) is a martingale over about \(\frac12\log_2N\) levels whose increments have conditional variance of order \(N\), so it is approximately Gaussian with variance of order \(N\log N\), with Gaussian lower tails up to \(t\) of order \(\sqrt{\log N}\) standard deviations. Then \(\int\psi_\alpha(4|\Phi|/(Ns))\) contains \(\exp((ct/L)^\alpha-Ct^2)\) at such \(t\), which grows without bound for \(\alpha>2\). The cost is separable, so one coordinate with exponent above \(2\) suffices: the dyadic flow is too costly at every pair of the curve, the endpoint \((2,\infty)\) included (there \(\psi_\infty\) requires a sup-norm bound that Gaussian tails also violate). So this route needs a flow whose value at a typical point has tails lighter than Gaussian at the scale \(\sqrt{N\log N}\); nothing here shows that the conjecture itself needs one, the flow bound running in one direction.

Degenerate cases. Flow bound: \(\Phi=0\) is admissible exactly when \(\nu_X=\nu_Y\), and then the bound is the smearing error, as for \(X=Y\) in lem:hopf-lax-matching-bound. A field with \(C(\Phi/\lambda')\) infinite somewhere is excluded only if \(C\) is infinite; here \(C\) is finite. Theorem: as \(N\to\infty\) with \(L\) fixed; \(N=1\) is excluded by \(\log N>0\). The dyadic square flow with all \(e_c=0\) is \(0\). A square with one child holding all the excess (\(e=(3m/4,-m/4,-m/4,-m/4)\)) gives a large field, within the bound \(6\max|e|/\rho\).

New mathematics. The Hopf–Lax bound gives a primal criterion: the matching cost is at most the conjugate-free cost \(\lambda'\int C(\Phi/\lambda')\) of any flow carrying the smeared configurations to each other. Through the dyadic Ajtai–Komlós–Tusnády flow, treated as a vector martingale over the dyadic levels, this proves the conjecture's conclusion at the off-curve pair \((2,2)\) coordinatewise with probability tending to one.
