Rank residual order

Lemma. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) variable matrix and \(B\) an \(n\times m\) variable matrix, with \(m>n\ge1\). Put \(r=m-n\), \(F_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(AB)_{ij}\), \(P=\prod_{i,j}(1+F_{ij})\), and \(I=(x^2+x:x\text{ is an entry variable})\). Then \[ P\in I^{r+1}\setminus I^{r+2}. \] Its minimum local vanishing order over the full Boolean cube is \(r+1\). This is not a degree bound for an original proof.

Lower-order proof. At a Boolean point \(a\), let \(C=A(a)B(a)\) and \(E=I_m+C\). Then \(\operatorname{rank}E\ge m-\operatorname{rank}C\ge r\), so \(E\) has at least \(r\) nonzero entries. Each contributes a vanishing factor of \(P\). If there are at least \(r+1\), the assertion follows. If there are exactly \(r\), rank \(r\) forces distinct occupied rows and columns. The fixed space \(\ker(I_m+E)=\ker C\), of dimension at least \(r\), is contained in \(\operatorname{im}E\), of dimension \(r\). They are equal, so \(E^2=E\). An off-diagonal occupied position \(E_{ij}\) would be the only entry of row \(i\); column \(j\) being occupied at \(i\ne j\) forces \(E_{jj}=0\), hence \((E^2)_{ij}=0\), a contradiction. Thus \(E\) is diagonal with \(r\) ones.

Consequently \(C\) is diagonal of rank \(n\), and \(A(a),B(a)\) have full inner rank. At every deleted diagonal index, the zero row of \(C\) forces the corresponding row of \(A(a)\) to vanish, and its zero column forces the column of \(B(a)\) to vanish. That factor \((AB)_{ii}\) has order two. The product therefore has order \(2r\ge r+1\).

Sharpness. Set row \(A_i=e_i\) for \(i<n\), and \(A_i=e_n\) for \(i\ge n\). Set column \(B_j=e_j\) for \(j<n\), and \(B_j=0\) for \(j\ge n\). Then \(C=\operatorname{diag}(I_{n-1},0_{r+1})\). The only vanishing factors are diagonals \(i=n,\ldots,m\), with distinct linear parts \(dB_{n,i}\). All other factors have constant one. The leading form is \(\prod_{i=n}^m dB_{n,i}\), of degree \(r+1\).

Global ideal proof. Boolean division gives \(I=\bigcap_a\mathfrak m_a\), where the finite Boolean-point maximal ideals are pairwise comaximal. Their \(q\)-th powers remain comaximal: if \(u+v=1\), expand \((u+v)^{2q-1}\); every term belongs to one of the two \(q\)-th powers. The Chinese remainder product/intersection identity gives \(I^q=\bigcap_a\mathfrak m_a^q\). The local bounds prove the claim. Every factor of \(P\) is nonzero, so \(P\ne0\) in the polynomial ring. \(\square\)

Nonvacuity. The sharp Boolean point works for every \(m>n\ge1\), including \(n=1\). For \(m=2n\) the order is \(n+1\), at unbounded variable count. For \(m=n+1\) it is two.

Degenerate cases. At \(n=0\), a diagonal factor is identically zero; this case is excluded. At \(m\le n\), a Boolean factorization of identity exists and the residual can be one, so the claim does not extend there.

Controls. At any fixed Boolean point, the leading form of \(P\) is the product of the leading forms of its degree-at-most-two factors. It therefore has an explicit \(O(m^2n)\)-size constant-depth circuit. No high-component extraction from an arbitrary supplied circuit is assumed. Also write \(b_j=x_j^2+x_j\) and \(Q=\sum_jq_jZ_j\), \(Q(x,b)=P\). Adding \(b_2Z_1+b_1Z_2\) replaces \((q_1,q_2)\) by \((q_1+b_2,q_2+b_1)\) without changing the certificate. For \(r\ge2\), at most one tuple can have all coefficients in \(I^r\), because \(b_2\notin I^2\). Thus an arbitrary representative need not have deep coefficients, even when \(P\) does.

Binary oracle closure gap

Corollary. A sum/product circuit over \(\mathbb F_2\), with scalar oracle gates for polynomials that vanish on every binary input, has the same Boolean output after replacing every oracle gate by zero, with no increase in size or depth. This allows nested calls: induction through the circuit keeps every scalar argument in \(\mathbb F_2\), where the oracle returns zero.

In particular, for an \(r\times r\) symbolic matrix \(X\), set \(f_r=\det_r(X)(x_{11}^2+x_{11})\). It is nonzero, has degree \(r+2\), belongs to the ideal of \(r\)-minors, and vanishes on the binary cube. There cannot be circuits of uniformly fixed depth and polynomial size in \(r\), over \(\mathbb F_2\) itself with \(f_r\) scalar oracle gates, computing \(\det_t\) or \(\det_t^{2^{e_r}}\), where \(t=\lfloor r^{1/3}\rfloor\), for arbitrary \(e_r\ge0\). Replacing oracles by zero gives the binary determinant function, since Frobenius powers fix Boolean values. Taking \(r=t^3\) contradicts the recorded determinant-function lower bound, whose explicit MOD3 reduction was reread. This is its attributed consequence, not a new general circuit lower bound.

Actual certificate application. For any linear \(Q(x,Z)=\sum_jq_j(x)Z_j\) with \(Q(x,b)=P\) above, \(P\in I^2\) makes every first derivative vanish at every Boolean point. Differentiating the identity gives \(\partial_jP(a)=q_j(a)\), since \(b'_j=1\). Thus every \(q_j(a)=0\), and \(Q(a,Z)=0\) for all binary \(Z\). This is the coefficient-trace identity applied to the exact order. The scalar-oracle obstruction therefore covers every complete linear correction certificate for this rank residual. It does not cover white-box transformations of the circuit.

Nonvacuity and scope. The displayed \(f_r\) is a nonzero product in a domain for all \(r\ge1\), giving unbounded examples. Extension-field arguments or constants, encoded vector operations, division undefined at Boolean points, and growing depth are excluded. This does not contradict the cited characteristic-zero/large-field ideal-closure theorems or their possible small-characteristic large-field extensions.
