Closing the actual error kernel under translations is too strong

Proposition (orbit kernel saturation). Let \(E\notin J_D\), \(\deg E\le e\), and \(K+e\le D\). Write \(s\ge1\) for the number of nonzero cut-code character coordinates of \(R_D(E)\). There is a subgroup \(H\le\mathcal Z\) of dimension at most \(\lceil\log_3s\rceil\) such that \[ Q_K\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{z\in H}Q_D, \qquad [f]\longmapsto\bigl(R_D((T_zE)f)\bigr)_{z\in H} \] is injective. In particular its rank is \(h(K)\) and \(|H|\le3^{\lceil\log_3s\rceil}<3s\). The common kernel over the full circulation group is zero. Equivalently, the kernel of multiplication by \(E\) contains no nonzero \(\mathcal Z\)-invariant subspace of \(Q_K\). The statement applies to a component \(E=wRg_i\) of an actual weighted error tuple; it translates the entire component, including \(w\).

Proof. Collect \(E\) in the charged quotient as \(E'=\sum_{j=1}^s c_jm_{\beta_j}\), choosing representatives from its support of degree at most \(e\). Distinct \(\beta_j\) have distinct cut-code classes, and all coefficients are nonzero. Replacing \(E\) by \(E'\), or translating this replacement, leaves the displayed maps unchanged by bounded absorption. Fix \(\beta_1\).

If \(s=1\), take \(H=0\). Otherwise choose \(q=\lceil\log_3s\rceil\) independent uniform samples \(z_1,\ldots,z_q\) from \(\mathcal Z\), allowing linear dependence between the samples, and let \(H\) be their span. For each \(j\ne1\), the nonzero quotient class \(\beta_j-\beta_1\) gives a nonzero linear functional on \(\mathcal Z\). Its probability of vanishing on every sample is \(3^{-q}\). The union bound is \((s-1)3^{-q}<1\). Hence some choice separates \(\beta_1\) from every other support label on \(H\), and \(\dim H\le q\).

The character projector on that subgroup gives \[ \frac1{|H|}\sum_{z\in H}\omega^{-z\cdot\beta_1}T_zE' =c_1m_{\beta_1}. \] Here \(|H|\) is odd, so its image is one in \(F\) and is invertible. The orthogonality identity follows by summing a nontrivial character along one direction where it is not one. If \(f\) lies in the common kernel of all orbit maps, multiplying this equality by \(f\) shows \(R_D(m_{\beta_1}f)=0\).

Multiplication by a single character is injective from \(Q_K\) in this window: choose one degree-at-most-\(K\) representative per input cut-code coordinate. Translating its exponent by \(\beta_1\) keeps all terms through \(K+e\le D\), sends distinct input cosets to distinct output cosets and introduces only nonzero phases. This is the single-character case of the existing sparse-column proof; no multiplication by an inverse outside the window is needed. Thus \([f]=0\), proving injectivity.

For the invariant-kernel formulation, if a subspace \(U\subseteq\ker M_E\) is invariant under all \(T_z\), then for \(f\in U\), applying \(T_z\) to \(E T_{-z}f\in J_D\) gives \((T_zE)f\in J_D\). The full orbit's kernel is zero, so \(U=0\). \(\square\)

This is a consequence of Fourier separation and the already proved nonzero translated columns. It is not an uncertainty principle imported from a positive measure, and it does not assert a rank lower bound on the original single map. For a binary error, all orbit maps are binary in the one-hot coordinates; extending scalars to \(F_4\) preserves their rank, so the injectivity also holds over \(\mathbb F_2\). The projector is used only to prove rank; its \(F_4\) coefficients are not legal ENS row choices.

Orbit size is not automatically affordable: \(s\) may be exponential in the error degree, so even the smaller subgroup does not supply polynomial source inventory. More basically, adjoining these translated errors is not authorized by the original source. When an original common kernel exists, this extra demand can erase it.

A complete nonlinear source already tests the mismatch

Take \(d\ge2\) edges \(T\) outside a spanning tree, with \(2d\le D\), and put \(z=\prod_{e\in T}x_{e,0}\). No cut vector is supported on \(T\), so all \(3^d\) Fourier coordinates of this conjunction are distinct in \(Q_D\). A complete bottom block on \((1+x_{e,0})_{e\in T}\), with accuracy \(h\ge d\), has the legal constant assignment selecting each input once and zero-padding the rest; its product is exactly \(z\), and every bottom companion vanishes by local Booleanity. A parent block reads the single nonlinear input \(z\). Set its rows initially to zero, so its complete companion tuple consists of \(E=z\).

With the already valid bottom weight one, the degree-\(d\) multiplier \(f=1+z\) survives, because its \(3^d-1\) nonconstant character coordinates are nonzero, and \(zf=0\) through degree \(2d\). But all circulation translates of \(z\) include the label conjunctions on every assignment to \(T\): the restriction \(\mathcal Z\to\mathbb F_3^T\) is surjective, since its dual has kernel \(\mathcal B\cap\mathbb F_3^T=0\). Those conjunctions sum to one. Thus their common multiplier kernel is zero in every allowed window. An invariant-kernel repair loses a perfectly valid multiplier on a complete nonlinear source.

This is deliberately a calibration, not a hard source: choosing the parent's first row to select \(z\) makes its product \(1+z\) and its companion zero, with no new weight. Original bottom product/companion charges are \(2h,2h+1\), and for parent accuracy \(h\) they are \(h(2h+1)\) and \(h(2h+1)+2h\). They remain in any replay; the smaller displayed identities concern the constant specialization only. This positive choice prevents interpreting a bad zero prefix as an all-choice obstruction.

What genuine input covariance would give

Lemma (invariant input removal). Let binary polynomials \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\) have degree at most \(\delta\ge1\), with \(3\delta\le D\). Suppose their binary image span \(G\) in the descended charged quotient is invariant under every circulation. For a complete ENS block on this tuple with accuracy \(h\ge2\), there are legal constant binary rows for which every specialized companion belongs to \(J_D\) through degree \(3\delta\). If \(G\ne0\), the first two rows already make the specialized product zero modulo \(J_D\) through \(2\delta\); all remaining rows may be zero. If \(G=0\), all-zero rows suffice to kill the companions. Original degrees \(h(\delta+1)\) and \(h(\delta+1)+\delta\) are not reduced by this assignment.

Proof. If \(G=0\), each input already belongs to \(J_D\); set the product to one. Otherwise extend \(G\) to \(F_4\). The full circulation action is diagonal with distinct characters on the reached cut-code coordinates. Character projection of a nonzero vector therefore puts a character representative \(m_\beta\), of degree at most \(\delta\), in this extended input span. If the coordinate is zero in the quotient group, it is a nonzero scalar multiple of the constant coordinate. Thus one belongs to the extended span, and, since one is binary, to the original binary image span. A legal first row then equals one modulo \(J_D\), giving the conclusion.

For a nonzero quotient label, the binary involution squares coefficients and exchanges \(U_e,V_e\). It sends \(m_\beta\) to \(m_{-\beta}\), also of degree at most \(\delta\). Hence the extended span contains both. Its two fixed vectors \[ u=m_\beta+m_{-\beta},\qquad v=\omega m_\beta+\omega^2m_{-\beta} \] belong to the binary image span. To see descent directly, write coefficients of any representation as \(a_i+\omega b_i\), with \(a_i,b_i\in\mathbb F_2\). A vector fixed by the binary involution has \(\sum_i b_ig_i=0\) in the quotient, and hence equals \(\sum_i a_ig_i\). Choose binary row combinations \(q_1,q_2\) representing \(u,v\), respectively, through degree \(\delta\).

Writing \(m=m_\beta\), local-domain reduction gives \(m^2=m_{-\beta}\), \(m_{-\beta}^2=m\), and \(m m_{-\beta}=1\) through degree \(2\delta\). Since \(\omega+\omega^2=1\), direct expansion gives \[ (1+u)(1+v) =1+\omega^2m+\omega m_{-\beta} +\omega m^2+\omega^2m_{-\beta}^2+(\omega+\omega^2)m m_{-\beta}=0. \] Replacing \(u,v\) by \(q_1,q_2\) uses their degree-\(\delta\) differences and cofactors of degree at most \(\delta\), so \((1+q_1)(1+q_2)\in J_D\) through \(2\delta\). Every companion is its product with a degree-\(\delta\) input and fits \(3\delta\). All coefficient-domain equations specialize to zero. \(\square\)

The three local character values give trace pairs \((0,1),(1,1),(1,0)\), never \((0,0)\); the algebraic proof above supplies the degree certificate rather than merely an evaluation argument. This is an application of the ordinary binary descent and Fourier character decomposition. Invariance of a nonzero subspace contained in the input span would also suffice. It is not necessary that every input be invariant individually.

The nonlinear calibration has input span \(\langle z\rangle\), which is not invariant: translates of \(z\) are distinct label conjunctions. Enlarging that span to its orbit would give new row choices and change the original block. Thus the positive lemma does not cover even every easily removable block, much less supply unrestricted source coverage. With a nonconstant common weight, its invariant span cannot be obtained merely by translating the old quotient and pretending that \(w\) is fixed.
